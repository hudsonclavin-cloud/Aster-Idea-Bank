Spin Wobble

A tether spinning in orbit does not keep a steady tip speed. The orbit's gravity gradient brakes the spin as the tether swings toward horizontal and hands the speed back as it comes vertical again. Skyhook's glossary records this as an observation from its lumped-mass model. Here it is in closed form, from params/rev_a.json alone, and held against the program's own twin.

How it is computed

A rigid tether of any mass distribution along a straight line, spinning in the plane of a circular orbit, with θ its angle from the local vertical measured in the frame that turns with the orbit at n = √(μ/R³). The gravity-gradient torque gives the pitch equation θ″ = −3n² sin θ cos θ, so θ′² + 3n² sin²θ stays constant. The tip's speed relative to the centre of mass is L(n + θ′): the spin relative to the orbiting frame plus the frame's own turn. Spun up to tip speed v₀ at angle θ₀, θ′(θ₀) = v₀/L − n.

The tip is fastest at the vertical and slowest at the horizontal. The time between verticals is ∫dθ/θ′ over half a turn, a complete elliptic integral, and Gauss's arithmetic–geometric mean gives it exactly: the rotation-mean of θ′ is AGM(θ′max, θ′min). To first order in n/ω the dip is 1.5n²/(ω(ω − n)), not the 1.5n²/ω² the model's docstring states: the first integral holds for the spin relative to the orbiting frame, and the tip speed is measured in inertial space.
