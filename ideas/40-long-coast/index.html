Long Coast

Idea 39 drew the transfer as a single arc about the Sun, from the Earth's centre to where Kepler puts the asteroid. Here it is flown: out of the parking orbit on a hyperbola, with the Earth, the Moon, the Sun and all eight planets pulling the whole way, to where the asteroid will really be. Then it is navigated, the way JPL and KinetX do it: the Deep Space Network ranges, Dopplers and interferometers the spacecraft, the knowledge of where it is grows and shrinks, and each trajectory-correction manoeuvre (TCM) costs what the injection error, the navigation error and the last manoeuvre's own error make it cost.           How this works

          The forces and the integrator

          Dormand-Prince 5(4) with the 6×6 state transition matrix integrated alongside. The Sun (Aster's GM), the Earth (Aster's GM, DE440's J2 about the ICRF pole), the Moon and Mercury to Neptune (DE440 GMs), each with its indirect term in this Sun-centred frame, and the Sun's relativistic (Schwarzschild) term. Within a million kilometres of the Earth the state is integrated relative to it, against the ephemeris Earth's own acceleration: the same problem, without the 1e-5 m/s an hour that heliocentric round-off costs near the periapsis. Planets: Chebyshev series fitted to JPL Horizons (Aster's daily file for the Earth, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars; fetched records for Jupiter to Neptune). Sunlight, when an area is given: a flat absorber facing the Sun, L☉/(4πcr²) from NASA's Sun fact sheet, no eclipses.

          The departure and the aim

          Idea 39's v-infinity is flown from a periapsis on Aster's 6,578.137 km parking radius at its departure epoch, in the least-inclined plane through it; or idea 37's planned impulsive departure; or the state idea 38 actually flew. "Re-aim the injection" solves for the v-infinity that reaches the target at idea 39's arrival epoch through every force (Newton on the STM, converged to 1 km): what a mission's launch target would be. "Correct at TCM-1" flies the departure as given and puts the correction in TCM-1. The target is Aster's own elements integrated through the same forces (no Yarkovsky) or, for comparison, idea 39's two-body position.

          The navigation analysis

          A linear covariance analysis (as Orion EM-1's, AAS 15-758): the trajectory's dispersion and the navigation knowledge are carried along the reference by its STMs. The injection error starts both (Orion EM-1's ICPS accuracy, U V W). Every Doppler point (each node of a pass), range point (one a pass) and Delta-DOR point (one per baseline a session, Goldstone-Canberra and Goldstone-Madrid, both antennas above 10°) is a Kalman update of the knowledge, from DSS-14, 43 and 63 turned by the Earth rotation angle. Each TCM nulls the estimated arrival miss (fixed-time position targeting), designed on the data up to its cutoff; its delta-v is Gaussian with the estimate's covariance mapped through the targeting matrix, and its mean and 99th percentile come from 4,000 seeded samples. Gates' four-term model adds the execution error to the knowledge. Unmodelled accelerations and Lucy's momentum dumps (0.4 mm/s, 3σ, every 4 days) add process noise. The budget totals the per-TCM 99th percentiles, as Orion EM-1's tables do.

          What it does not do

          No light time, no station-dependent biases or media errors (DSN's numbers fold them into the data weight), no solar-plasma blackout near conjunction (module 41), no SRP or other estimated parameters, no optical navigation or target ephemeris uncertainty (module 42), no flyby targeting. TCMs are impulsive; the deterministic correction is fixed-time position targeting, not a B-plane target with free arrival time.
