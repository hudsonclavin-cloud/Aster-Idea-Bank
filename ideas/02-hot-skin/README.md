# Hot Skin
> At the fin template's own declared 700 m/s at 3,000 m on a standard day, PartForge's `isa()` puts the stagnation bound at 239.4 °C and the turbulent adiabatic wall at 213.1 °C against 6061-T6's 170 °C service limit, and every kelvin of hot day adds a degree to both; the page screens all 21 flight materials this way, refuses a bare Mach number (Mach 2 reads 210.4 °C at 3 km and 125.6 °C at 25 km), and never says PASS.

**Kind:** explorable
**For:** PartForge
**Run:** open `ideas/02-hot-skin/index.html` in a browser (plain classic scripts, so `file://` works; no build, no network; `?v=700&alt=3000&m=PEEK&dt=20&src=source&ref=sim%20run%203` or `?mach=2` preload a condition). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/02-hot-skin/heat.test.mjs` (Node built-ins only, under a second) and `node ideas/02-hot-skin/ui-check.mjs` (headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright, about ten seconds; `--shots` also writes screenshots); `tools/check.mjs` runs both, the second through this folder's `check.mjs`. `node ideas/02-hot-skin/build-data.mjs /path/to/Partforge` re-derives `snapshots/partforge.js` from a checkout; `--check` says whether the committed copy is stale.
**Made by:** Pitot

## What it is

A screen for aerodynamic heating, Stage 1 of PartForge's idea 20. You give it an airspeed in m/s (the speed relative to the air, not over the ground), a geometric altitude in metres, the day as an ISA offset in kelvin (0, the standard day, unless you say otherwise) and one of the 21 `FLIGHT_MATERIALS`, and it computes, with PartForge's own 1976 Standard Atmosphere function run unmodified:

- the free-stream temperature T∞ (the standard-atmosphere value plus the offset) and the speed of sound, and from them the Mach number (derived; Mach is never an input);
- the **stagnation temperature** T₀, what a leading edge or nose tip can at most reach from heating by the air;
- the **recovery temperatures** T_r for a turbulent and a laminar boundary layer, what a flat skin can at most reach with free-stream conditions at its edge;
- the margin of each against the material's `service_max_c`, printed beside that row's confidence (`likely` or `recall`), the table's own note on the row, and the table's warning that the column is judgement, not a rating;
- the speed at which each bound would reach the limit at that altitude, when that speed is under the 2,000 K ceiling described below, and the day (ISA offset) at which T₀ would reach the limit at that speed, when that day is one the page accepts (−90 to +50 K); otherwise it says that no accepted day brings T₀ to the limit, or that T₀ is over it on every one.

The verdict is always **SCREEN**, qualified as *bound exceeds the limit*, *recovery under, stagnation over*, *bound within 25 °C of the limit*, or *bound under the limit*. PartForge's evaluation says to pass when T_r plus a margin is under `service_max_c` (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md:561`); this page deliberately does not. A bound under a limit would justify a pass if the limit and the inputs were evidence, and here they are not: 14 of the 21 limits are `recall` and the column is documented as judgement, the speed may be a tool default (`inputs_src`) or a ground speed where airspeed is meant, the atmosphere is a standard day unless an offset is declared, and the bound covers heating by the air only, not sun soak on the pad or motor-case and plume heat. It is also not a prediction: a rocket's hot phase lasts seconds, and conduction into the part and radiation keep the skin below the bound. The first line of the page says what the bound covers.

Underneath, a chart draws the three bounds against speed at the chosen altitude and day with the material's limit as a dashed line and the current speed marked, and a table runs all 21 materials at the current condition, each row with its note behind a *note* toggle. It opens on the condition the shipped fin template declares (`v_max_m_s` 700, `alt_m` 3000, 6061-T6, `inputs_src` default), and a button returns to it. The page carries PartForge's provenance rule for loads inputs: a speed the tool proposed makes the margin "arithmetic, not evidence", in the app's own words, and choosing *source* asks for the citation (`inputs_ref`); without one the page demotes it to *default*, as PartForge's parser does.

The page refuses rather than guesses. Type *Mach 2* and it explains that T₀ − T∞ tracks V²/2c_p, not Mach, gives the gap between two altitudes as a formula, and shows the same Mach number at 3 km (210.4 °C) and 25 km (125.6 °C), 84.8 °C apart. Type *Mach 10* and it refuses the same way but works the example at Mach 2 and says why: at 3 km Mach 10 would put the perfect-gas T₀ past the 2,000 K ceiling, and no temperature past the ceiling is ever printed (the example can show up to Mach 5.67 on a standard day). Type *700 m* and it asks whether you meant m/s; type km/h and it asks for m/s rather than converting. Ask for 90 km and it says the ISA table ends at 86 km. Ask for 2,000 m/s, or 10,000,000, and it says the perfect-gas T₀ would pass 2,000 K, where oxygen begins to dissociate and these equations stop describing air, and it names a speed to stay below, rounded down so the printed figure is itself inside (1,854.6 m/s at sea level and 1,865.1 m/s at 3 km on a standard day; a hot day lowers both). Give a day offset outside −90 to +50 K and it says the offset is the day's temperature minus the standard one (a 35 °C day at sea level is ISA+20), not the temperature itself. Name a material it does not know in the URL (`?m=`) and it refuses with the 21 it does know, rather than quietly showing 6061-T6; give it a material with no `service_max_c` (none ships today) and it says there is no limit to measure against. Every refusal is structured: a code (`invalid_input`, `not_found`, `insufficient_data`, `out_of_envelope`), the reason, and what would help. The figures it derives keep to the same envelope. For 304 at the fin condition it does not print ISA+560.6 K as the day on which T₀ would meet the 800 °C limit; it says that no accepted day does. For 6061-T6 at 1,500 m/s and 3 km it does not print ISA−945.3 K (a free stream at −676.6 K); it says T₀ is over the limit on every accepted day. For Graphite's 2,000 °C it names no crossing speed, because T₀ reaches the ceiling first.

## Why I made it

PartForge's roadmap evaluation (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md` at `0974a62`, last changed `680546e` 2026-09-18) names this as the fourth of "The five to build first" (`:251-252`): *"Idea 20 is 40 lines, has no free parameters, its three worked numbers reproduce against the repo's own `isa()` and `ISA_GAMMA`, and `service_max_c` already ships for all 21 materials."* The scoring row is `:69` (value 3, 4 days, **BUILD NOW**, Stage 1 only), the appendix entry `:559-568`, and the trap it must close is wrong-number risk 6 (`:300-302`): a bare Mach reads ~128 °C "at apogee" where the same vehicle at 3 km sees ~210 °C, "an 80 °C unsafe-LOW read that passes a PA12 or G10 part". The "Do not build" table replaces idea 8, thermal stress from ΔT, with exactly this: "a 1.5-d gate comparing declared temperature against the shipped `service_max_c`" (`:230`). In the code: `ISA_GAMMA` at `index.html:637`, `isa()` at `:663`, `flutterVelocity()` (the only consumer of the atmosphere today) at `:740`, the loads whitelist `/^(hoop|flutter|thread|bolt_shear)$/` at `:4733`, the `inputs_src` / `inputs_ref` normalisation at `:4738-4740`, the loads evaluator loop at `:5745`, and `dbMaterialThermal()` at `:5014` returning `service_max_c` that nothing compares a temperature to. `data/tables/materials_thermal.json` has the 21 rows; `tests/templates/fin.scad` declares the worked condition.

Two deviations from the evaluation's Stage 1, stated here so nobody has to find them. First, the evaluation's method line passes a part when T_r plus a margin is under `service_max_c` (`:561`); this page never passes, for the reasons in *What it is*. Second, the evaluation's Stage 1 is "adiabatic T0/T_r as an 'aeroheat' loads check + flutter-G cross-link lint" (`:559`), and the **flutter-G cross-link lint is not built**: nothing here warns that a flutter row's shear modulus is a room-temperature figure (the fin template's 6061-T6 flutter row uses `FLIGHT_MATERIALS`' G = 26.0 GPa, `index.html:936`) while the bound at the same condition puts the skin up to 239 °C. It needs the flutter row and the aeroheat row side by side in PartForge's loads loop, which this page does not have; it is listed under *Where it would land*.

What I weighed:

- **Stage 2, a transient skin temperature** (Eckert's reference-enthalpy heat-transfer coefficient plus lumped capacitance on a bevelled edge). It is what a designer actually wants, and it is what the evaluation says to hold: it "would print a confidently wrong LOW skin temperature; do not ship it without a validated case" (`:566`). A number that can be wrong in the unsafe direction, with no case to validate against in the checkout, has no business in a bank that promises every number traces somewhere. Not built, and the page says it is Stage 1 only.
- **Patching the check into PartForge's loads evaluator.** Out of bounds for this bank, and the evaluation says the check "cannot work at all" there until the `row.ok` guard is amended (item 3 of the build-first list). So this is the pure function, its tests and its presentation, built beside the repository, with the landing spelled out below.
- **A CLI over `headless/core.gen.mjs`.** Possible, and the headless core already carries `isa()`. But the comparison is a picture (three curves, one line, where do they cross) and the "what if" is the point; a page that runs from a file serves a club member at a laptop better than a command does. The verbatim extraction gives the page the same `isa()` the headless core has.
- **Thermal stress from ΔT (idea 8).** Refuted by the evaluation's skeptics: ΔT is the one input nobody can measure or look up, and the retry loop rewards inventing one that passes.

This won because the arithmetic is closed-form, has no fitted coefficient, reproduces the repository's own test vectors to the digit, and closes a named trap; and because the table it reads was added for this comparison and nothing in the shipped app makes it.

## How it works

**Where the inputs come from.** `build-data.mjs` reads a PartForge checkout and writes `snapshots/partforge.js`, a classic-script module carrying (1) `index.html` lines 620–684 **verbatim**, found by text marker, not line number: the ISA header comment, `ISA_G0`, `ISA_R`, `ISA_R0`, `ISA_GAMMA`, `ISA_LAYERS`, `ISA_H_MAX`, `ISA_BASE_P` and `function isa()`; (2) `{name, kind}` for each `FLIGHT_MATERIALS` key (lines 935–999); (3) the whole of `snapshots/materials_thermal.json`, read from this folder, not the repository, so the embedded rows can only differ from the snapshot if someone edits one by hand; (4) the FLIGHT block's `material` and `loads` from `snapshots/fin.scad`, likewise; (5) provenance: commit `0974a62`, the sha256 of `index.html` and the sidecars' recorded sha256 of the two data files. The JSON and the template are byte-for-byte copies made with `tools/snapshot.mjs`, each with its `.provenance.json` sidecar (`readMatchesCommitted: true`); `partforge.js` has a `--sidecar-only` sidecar pointing at `index.html`. `build-data.mjs --check` compares **content**, not commit metadata: it re-derives from the checkout and reports stale only if the verbatim ISA block, the `{name, kind}` map or the embedded data differ, so a later PartForge commit that leaves them alone (a docs change, or an edit that moves line numbers) is not drift, while an edit to `isa()` or a renamed material is; when `index.html` is byte-for-byte the file the module records, it also demands a byte-identical rebuild. The page fetches nothing: all three snapshots load as scripts, so `file://` works.

**The arithmetic** (`heat.js`, pure, no DOM). With V in m/s, altitudes in geometric metres, temperatures in kelvin until the last step:

- `{T_K, a_m_s, clamped} = isa(alt_m)` from the repository. [Certain] It is their function; the tests reproduce their published vectors (below).
- The day: T∞ = T_ISA + ΔT, the same offset at every altitude, and a = √(γ R T∞), the formula `isa()` itself uses; with ΔT = 0 (the default) T∞ and a are `isa()`'s own values, untouched. PartForge's `isa()` has no offset argument, so the offset is added here, after it. A uniform offset is the usual way a hot or cold day is stated; a real day's profile varies with height, which this does not model. The page accepts −90 to +50 K, a band that brackets recorded surface air temperatures (the 56.7 °C Death Valley record is about ISA+42 K at sea level; the −89.2 °C Vostok record at 3,488 m is about ISA−82 K by `isa()`) [Likely, the records from memory]; outside it the page refuses, because an offset of +60 is far likelier to be an ambient temperature typed in the wrong box than a day.
- M = V / a. [Certain] V is the **airspeed**, the speed relative to the air, not over the ground. The model cannot tell the two apart, so the page labels the field *Airspeed, relative to the air* and the bound caveat repeats it. A ground-relative v_max (a sim that reports speed over the ground) flown into a headwind w reads low by ((V + w)² − V²)/2c_p: 7.0 K per 10 m/s at 700 m/s, since (710² − 700²)/(2 × 1004.685) = 7.02 K [Certain, algebra]. That is the same unsafe-low direction as an unstated hot day, only smaller. A tailwind errs high. The bound caveat prints the figure at the current speed.
- T₀ = T∞ · (1 + (γ−1)/2 · M²), γ = `ISA_GAMMA` = 1.4. [Certain] The energy equation for a calorically perfect gas; the standard text is Anderson, *Modern Compressible Flow*, eq. 3.28 in the editions I know, cited from memory.
- c_p = γR/(γ−1) = 1.4 × 287.05287 / 0.4 = 1004.685 J/(kg·K), from `ISA_R`. [Certain] Then T₀ − T∞ = V²/(2c_p) *identically*: (γ−1)/2 · V²/(γRT∞) · T∞ = (γ−1)V²/(2γR). The page prints both as a cross-check, and a test holds them to 1e-9 relative over 300 random conditions; at 700 m/s the rise is 243.858 K either way. Because the rise does not depend on T∞, **at a fixed speed every bound moves one for one with the day**: on an ISA+ΔT day T₀, both T_r and every margin shift by exactly ΔT. [Certain] At the fin condition PEEK's +10.6 °C margin against T₀ is gone on any day warmer than ISA+10.6 K; at ISA+11 PEEK reads *recovery under, stagnation over* (−0.4 °C) and at ISA+20 its T₀ margin is −9.4 °C. A summer day at an inland launch site is commonly ISA+15 to +30 K near the ground (35 °C at 1,400 m is ISA+29 by `isa()`) [Likely], so the standard-day reading is not the conservative one, and the page says so beside every standard-day result.
- T_r = T∞ · (1 + r · (γ−1)/2 · M²), the adiabatic-wall (recovery) temperature, with the flat-plate recovery factors r = Pr^½ (laminar) and Pr^⅓ (turbulent). [Likely] These are Eckert's results as given in Schlichting, *Boundary-Layer Theory*, and in Anderson; I know them from textbooks, not from the original papers. r = 1 is the stagnation point. T_r here uses **free-stream** conditions at the boundary-layer edge. Behind a shock the edge is hotter, T_e > T∞, total temperature is unchanged, and the local recovery temperature T_e + r(T₀ − T_e) exceeds the free-stream one by exactly (1 − r)(T_e − T∞), which can never take it past T₀. [Certain, algebra] For the fin condition I worked the weak oblique shock off a sharp wedge (the θ-β-M relation and the shock temperature ratio, γ = 1.4, not coded in the page): 2.5 K for a 5° half-angle, 5.2 K for 10°, 8.1 K for 15°, against a ceiling of T₀ − T_r = 26.3 K [Likely: the wedge is an assumed geometry]. So T₀ is the bound that holds at every point; T_r is the flat-skin estimate the bands use, and the *stagnation* band's text says that a skin behind a shock can sit above it.
- Pr = 0.71 for air near 300 K. [Likely] Property tables put it at 0.707 at 300 K and 0.684 at 500 K (Incropera & DeWitt, Table A.4, from memory), so between about 0.68 and 0.72 over 250–500 K. It is the **only constant in the equations not read from the repository** (the 800 K flag, the 2,000 K ceiling, the 25 °C *near* band and the −90 to +50 K offset band are thresholds on what is flagged, refused or named, not terms in the arithmetic), it is named `PR_AIR` in one place, and the evaluation's guard asked for exactly this ("derive r from a named Pr constant instead of hardcoding 0.845/0.896", `:567`). It gives r = 0.8426 (laminar) and 0.8921 (turbulent). Using 0.70 instead moves the turbulent recovery temperature at the fin condition by about 1 °C.
- margin = `service_max_c` − T (°C), one for each bound; the screen band uses two of them: *over* if the turbulent recovery bound exceeds the limit, *stagnation* if only T₀ does, *near* if T₀ is within 25 °C under it, *under* otherwise. The 25 °C is a presentation threshold, not physics, and the page says so in the table's legend.
- Speed at which a bound meets the limit: V = √(2 c_p (T_limit − T∞) / r), the inversion of the line above [Certain]. It is null in two cases. One is when T∞ already exceeds the limit. The other is when the speed is past the one at which T₀ reaches the 2,000 K ceiling at that altitude, because the page refuses there. Only Graphite's 2,000 °C is that high: its T₀ crossing at 3 km would be about 2,007 m/s against a ceiling speed of 1,865.18 m/s, and the page says *none under the ceiling*.
- The day on which T₀ meets the limit at the current speed. By the one-for-one shift, the T₀ margin is gone at ΔT + margin [Certain, algebra; the label covers only the shift]. Whether that sum is a day is a separate question, and the page answers it with its own band: it names the sum only when it lies inside −90 to +50 K and T₀ on that day is still under the ceiling. Inside the band T∞ is at least 186.9 − 90 = 96.9 K, because the coldest air `isa()` returns is the 86 km top. Outside it the sum is algebra, not a day. 304 at the fin condition would need ISA+560.6 K, and 6061-T6 at 1,500 m/s and 3 km ISA−945.3 K, a free stream at −676.6 K. In such cases the page prints no figure and says instead that the bound is under the limit on every accepted day (304) or over it on every one (6061-T6). Near the ceiling, the range it speaks for ends where T₀ reaches 2,000 K: for Graphite at 1,865 m/s and 3 km, that is ISA+0.3 K.

**Flags that print beside every result:** the bound caveat (always), which says the bound is for heating by the air alone through the stated day, that T₀ bounds every point and T_r uses free-stream conditions, that sun soak and motor or plume heat are outside it, and that the speed is airspeed, with the headwind figure at the current speed; *standard day* when no offset is given, or *offset* naming the day when one is. Each of these names the day on which this material's T₀ margin is gone (or, for a bound already over, the day it must be colder than) when that day is in the band, and otherwise says that no accepted day brings T₀ to the limit, or that T₀ is over it on every one; *demoted* when `inputs_src` is `source` with no `inputs_ref`, as PartForge does it (`index.html:4740`); *provisional* when `inputs_src` is `default`, in the app's wording ("arithmetic, not evidence"), because a low guessed speed passes a polymer part silently (`:566`, risk 2); the row's confidence, with `recall` in the warning colour; and *hot* when T₀ exceeds 800 K, because with constant γ the stagnation temperature is overstated there, by a few percent just above 800 K and by more the hotter it gets (c_p of air rises with temperature once vibration is excited) [Likely], which keeps the bound conservative. The table's note on the selected row prints under the figures, and every row's note sits behind its *note* toggle in the table; most notes describe the expansion coefficient, not the limit, and the page says that too.

**The envelope.** Three edges are refused as `out_of_envelope` rather than computed. The ISA table's: `isa()` clamps outside −5 km to 86 km geometric, and a clamped altitude would be a number for the wrong place. The day's: outside −90 to +50 K. And the perfect-gas picture's: when the constant-γ T₀ would exceed **2,000 K**, around where oxygen begins to dissociate (Anderson, *Hypersonic and High-Temperature Gas Dynamics*, the regimes for air at 1 atm: vibration excited above about 800 K, O₂ dissociation from about 2,000 K, N₂ from about 4,000 K; lower pressure lowers all three; cited from memory, and 2,000 K is the more conservative of the 2,000 and 2,500 K onsets different texts give) [Likely], c_p, γ and the recovery factors all depend on temperature and pressure and T₀ = T∞(1 + 0.2M²) is no longer a bound on anything, so the model refuses and names the speed at which T₀ reaches the ceiling at that altitude and day, V = √(2c_p(2000 K − T∞)): 1,854.65 m/s at sea level, 1,865.18 m/s at 3 km and 1,908.69 m/s at the top of the table on a standard day, 1,838.05 m/s at 3 km at ISA+50. The refusal prints the speed to stay below rounded **down** to 0.1 m/s (1854.6, 1865.1), so the printed figure is itself one the page computes. Nothing the page can show is above the ceiling: the 1,500 m/s slider tops out at T₀ ≈ 1,408 K at sea level on a standard day (about 1,490 K at −5 km and ISA+50, the hottest air the page accepts), the chart's speed axis stops at the ceiling speed, and the Mach refusal's worked example uses the typed Mach number only when that Mach is under the ceiling at both example altitudes (up to Mach 5.67 on a standard day, set by 3 km); above that it works Mach 2 and says why, and a row past the ceiling carries no speed and no temperature at all. The figures derived from a condition keep to the same envelope: a limit-crossing speed past the ceiling speed is not named, and neither is a day outside the band or a day on which T₀ would pass the ceiling at that speed. The hint under the day field prints the day's own temperature only for an offset inside the band, so a typed −300 does not show "this day is −304.5 °C".

Three reviews found the edges. The first typed 1,000,000 m/s into the speed field and froze the tab: with no ceiling, the chart's speed axis went to 1,250,000 m/s, its temperature range to 7.8 × 10⁸ K, and its grid loop, with the step capped at 500 °C, stroked 1,555,214 lines synchronously inside the input handler; and below the hang, at 99,999 m/s and 40 km, the page printed "T₀ 4976561.7 °C" to a tenth of a degree. The ceiling, a grid step chosen from the range (about six lines whatever the numbers) and the capped axis fixed that, and `ui-check.mjs` times each refusal at under a second from the keystroke. The second review found the same defect one path over: the Mach refusal's worked example ran the perfect-gas T₀ at whatever Mach was typed, so *Mach 10* printed 5,368.7 °C at 3 km beside a refusal that says no temperature is printed, and *Mach 99999* printed 537,307,650,584.6 °C; it also found that *700 m* was read as Mach 700. Both are closed above, and a test scans the Mach refusal at seven Mach numbers up to 10³⁰⁰ on three days for any T₀ past 2,000 K. The third review found the same class in a figure the second fix had added. The day on which T₀ meets the limit was printed as ΔT + margin, whatever that came to: 6061-T6 at 1,500 m/s and 3 km read "ISA−945.3 K" (a free stream at −676.6 K), and 304 at the fin condition read "ISA+560.6 K", offsets the page itself refuses as input. That figure is now named only inside the band, and a test scans 3,150 conditions for any printed offset outside it. Checking the other derived figures for the same defect turned up Graphite's limit-crossing speed (about 2,007 m/s, past the ceiling) and the day hint's "−304.5 °C"; both are now withheld. The third review also pointed out that nothing said V is airspeed; the field, the bound caveat and this README now say so.

Against 6061-T6's 170 °C (`likely`), the fin condition's margins are −69.4 °C (T₀), −43.1 °C (turbulent) and −31.0 °C (laminar): **the template's declared flight condition puts the adiabatic-wall bound above the alloy's service temperature**, and the bound reaches 170 °C at 592.1 m/s (stagnation) and 626.9 m/s (turbulent recovery) at that altitude. Read with the caveat: the bound is a ceiling for a steady flight, the template's speed is `inputs_src` default, and 170 °C is where T6 temper "starts to draw back", in the table's words, not where the part fails. What it means is that a Mach 2 aluminium fin is a case for Stage 2, not a case this screen can clear. The evaluation's "~128 °C at apogee" is Mach 2 near 26.3 km geometric by the same `isa()` (127.96 °C); at 25 km it is 125.6 °C, which is what the page pins, and the gap to the 3 km figure, 84.8 °C, is the "80 °C unsafe-low read". At a fixed Mach the gap between two altitudes is (T∞,low − T∞,high)(1 + 0.2M²), so it grows with M²; the refusal states that formula instead of a size.

**The table at the fin condition:** on a standard day, 13 materials *over* (the three aluminium alloys (6061, 7075, 2024), brass, copper, all four laminates, Ultem, PA12, CF-nylon, Delrin), none *stagnation*, one *near* (PEEK at 250 °C, +10.6 °C vs T₀) and seven *under* (304, 316, 17-4PH, 4130, Ti-6Al-4V, Inconel 718, graphite). On a hotter day the bands move one row at a time: past ISA+10.6 K PEEK is *stagnation*, past ISA+35.6 K 17-4PH-H900 comes within 25 °C (*near*), and past ISA+36.9 K PEEK is *over*; the other rows keep their bands up to the +50 K the page accepts. Fourteen of the 21 limits are `recall`, seven `likely`; the row's word prints beside every margin.

**The chart** is Canvas 2D: three 2 px lines in a colour set validated for colour-vision deficiency in both light and dark modes (blue, orange, aqua, with a legend and direct labels at desktop width), the limit as a dashed ink line labelled with the material and its confidence, the current speed as a vertical rule with dots, and a crosshair readout under the pointer or finger. The speed axis runs to at least 1,000 m/s and a quarter beyond the current speed, rounded up to 250, but never past the speed where T₀ reaches the 2,000 K ceiling at that altitude and day (a dotted rule marks the cap when it engages); the grid steps on both axes are chosen from the range (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 × 10ⁿ), so the drawing does the same work whatever is typed. The axis label names the day when it is not standard. The table colours only the numbers that are negative and tags each row with a word, never colour alone.

**The page.** `heat.js` loads as a classic script (`window.HotSkin`) and with `require()`; `app.js` draws from it. Inputs are native (text, number, range, select, and a details toggle per table note), so they work by keyboard and touch; the verdict and refusal regions are `aria-live`; colours are CSS variables redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. ## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The atmosphere is PartForge's.** `isa()` runs as the repository wrote it, from text copied by marker and compared character for character with `index.html` at the recorded commit, and it reproduces the vectors the repository's own test file publishes. If the ISA block or the materials change in a later commit, `build-data.mjs --check` and the tests say so; a commit that leaves them alone does not trip them.
- **[Certain] The stagnation arithmetic.** T₀ = T∞(1 + 0.2M²) with γ = 1.4 is the perfect-gas energy equation; the V²/2c_p identity is exact and tested; the hand-worked case agrees to 1e-9 K. The fin condition's 239.4 °C is right for what it is.
- **[Likely] The recovery factors.** Pr^½ and Pr^⅓ are the standard flat-plate results and Pr = 0.71 is a fair value for air between 250 and 500 K; the turbulent recovery temperature moves about 1 °C per 0.01 of Pr at the fin condition. These are textbook results I did not re-derive and the one external constant in the equations; both are labelled on the page.
- **[Certain] T₀ is an upper bound on adiabatic-wall temperature from aerodynamic heating, for the atmosphere stated, and nothing more.** The ISA standard day unless an offset is declared; T₀ bounds every point on the part; T_r assumes free-stream edge conditions and behind a shock is exceeded by (1 − r)(T_e − T∞), never past T₀. It is the temperature the skin would reach with no heat going anywhere, and it counts only heat from the air: sun soak on the pad, motor-case conduction and plume heating are outside it and can add to it. A fin that is at Mach 2 for ten seconds, conducting into a tab and an airframe and radiating, never gets to the bound; a fin that sat on a black pad in the sun starts warmer than the standard day assumes. The page can say "the bound is above the limit"; it cannot say the part is too hot, and it says neither "pass" nor "fail". A margin here is a reason to do Stage 2, or to look at the declared speed and day, not a verdict on the part.
- **[Certain] On a hot day every bound is higher by the offset.** With the speed fixed, T₀ − T∞ does not depend on T∞, so the shift is exact; the page shows it and takes the day as an input.
