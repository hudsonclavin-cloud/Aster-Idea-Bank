# Hot Skin
> At the fin template's own declared 700 m/s at 3,000 m, PartForge's `isa()` puts the stagnation bound at 239.4 °C and the turbulent adiabatic wall at 213.1 °C against 6061-T6's 170 °C service limit; the page screens all 21 flight materials this way, refuses a bare Mach number (Mach 2 reads 210.4 °C at 3 km and 125.6 °C at 25 km), and never says PASS.

**Kind:** explorable
**For:** PartForge

## What it is

A screen for aerodynamic heating, Stage 1 of PartForge's idea 20. You give it a speed in m/s, a geometric altitude in metres and one of the 21 `FLIGHT_MATERIALS`, and it computes, with PartForge's own 1976 Standard Atmosphere function run unmodified:

- the free-stream temperature T∞ and speed of sound, and from them the Mach number (derived; Mach is never an input);
- the **stagnation temperature** T₀, what a leading edge or nose tip can at most reach;
- the **recovery temperatures** T_r for a turbulent and a laminar boundary layer, what a flat skin can at most reach;
- the margin of each against the material's `service_max_c`, printed beside that row's confidence (`likely` or `recall`) and the table's own warning that the column is judgement, not a rating;
- the speed at which each bound would reach the limit at that altitude.

The verdict is always **SCREEN**, qualified as *bound exceeds the limit*, *recovery under, stagnation over*, *bound within 25 °C of the limit*, or *bound under the limit*. It is never PASS, because an adiabatic wall is an upper bound on skin temperature, not a prediction of it: a rocket's hot phase lasts seconds, and conduction into the part and radiation keep the skin below the bound. The first line of the page says so.

Underneath, a chart draws the three bounds against speed at the chosen altitude with the material's limit as a dashed line and the current speed marked, and a table runs all 21 materials at the current condition. It opens on the condition the shipped fin template declares (`v_max_m_s` 700, `alt_m` 3000, 6061-T6, `inputs_src` default), and a button returns to it. The page also carries PartForge's `inputs_src` vocabulary: a speed the tool proposed makes the margin "arithmetic, not evidence", in the app's own words.

The page refuses rather than guesses. Type *Mach 2* and it explains that T₀ − T∞ tracks V²/2c_p, not Mach, and shows the same Mach number at 3 km (210.4 °C) and 25 km (125.6 °C), 84.8 °C apart. Type km/h and it asks for m/s rather than converting. Ask for 90 km and it says the ISA table ends at 86 km. Ask for 2,000 m/s, or 10,000,000, and it says the perfect-gas T₀ would pass 2,000 K, where oxygen begins to dissociate and these equations stop describing air, and it names the speed at which that happens at this altitude (1,855 m/s at sea level, 1,865 m/s at 3 km) instead of printing a temperature. Give it a material with no `service_max_c` (none ships today) and it says there is no limit to measure against. Every refusal is structured: a code (`invalid_input`, `not_found`, `insufficient_data`, `out_of_envelope`), the reason, and what would help.

## Why I made it

PartForge's roadmap evaluation (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md` at `0974a62`, last changed `680546e` 2026-09-18) names this as the fourth of "The five to build first" (`:251-252`): *"Idea 20 is 40 lines, has no free parameters, its three worked numbers reproduce against the repo's own `isa()` and `ISA_GAMMA`, and `service_max_c` already ships for all 21 materials."* The scoring row is `:69` (value 3, 4 days, **BUILD NOW**, Stage 1 only), the appendix entry `:559-568`, and the trap it must close is wrong-number risk 6 (`:300-302`): a bare Mach reads ~128 °C "at apogee" where the same vehicle at 3 km sees ~210 °C, "an 80 °C unsafe-LOW read that passes a PA12 or G10 part". The "Do not build" table replaces idea 8, thermal stress from ΔT, with exactly this: "a 1.5-d gate comparing declared temperature against the shipped `service_max_c`" (`:230`). In the code: `ISA_GAMMA` at `index.html:637`, `isa()` at `:663`, `flutterVelocity()` (the only consumer of the atmosphere today) at `:740`, the loads whitelist `/^(hoop|flutter|thread|bolt_shear)$/` at `:4733`, the loads evaluator loop at `:5745`, and `dbMaterialThermal()` at `:5014` returning `service_max_c` that nothing compares a temperature to. `data/tables/materials_thermal.json` has the 21 rows; `tests/templates/fin.scad` declares the worked condition.

What I weighed:

- **Stage 2, a transient skin temperature** (Eckert's reference-enthalpy heat-transfer coefficient plus lumped capacitance on a bevelled edge). It is what a designer actually wants, and it is what the evaluation says to hold: it "would print a confidently wrong LOW skin temperature; do not ship it without a validated case" (`:566`). A number that can be wrong in the unsafe direction, with no case to validate against in the checkout, has no business in a bank that promises every number traces somewhere. Not built, and the page says it is Stage 1 only.
- **Patching the check into PartForge's loads evaluator.** Out of bounds for this bank, and the evaluation says the check "cannot work at all" there until the `row.ok` guard is amended (item 3 of the build-first list). So this is the pure function, its tests and its presentation, built beside the repository, with the landing spelled out below.
- **A CLI over `headless/core.gen.mjs`.** Possible, and the headless core already carries `isa()`. But the comparison is a picture (three curves, one line, where do they cross) and the "what if" is the point; a page that runs from a file serves a club member at a laptop better than a command does. The verbatim extraction gives the page the same `isa()` the headless core has.
- **Thermal stress from ΔT (idea 8).** Refuted by the evaluation's skeptics: ΔT is the one input nobody can measure or look up, and the retry loop rewards inventing one that passes.

This won because the arithmetic is closed-form, has no fitted coefficient, reproduces the repository's own test vectors to the digit, and closes a named trap; and because the table it reads was added for this comparison and nothing in the shipped app makes it.

## How it works

**Where the inputs come from.** `build-data.mjs` reads a PartForge checkout and writes `snapshots/partforge.js`, a classic-script module carrying (1) `index.html` lines 620–684 **verbatim**, found by text marker, not line number: the ISA header comment, `ISA_G0`, `ISA_R`, `ISA_R0`, `ISA_GAMMA`, `ISA_LAYERS`, `ISA_H_MAX`, `ISA_BASE_P` and `function isa()`; (2) `{name, kind}` for each `FLIGHT_MATERIALS` key (lines 935–999); (3) the whole of `snapshots/materials_thermal.json`, read from this folder, not the repository, so the embedded rows can only differ from the snapshot if someone edits one by hand; (4) the FLIGHT block's `material` and `loads` from `snapshots/fin.scad`, likewise; (5) provenance: commit `0974a62`, the sha256 of `index.html` and the sidecars' recorded sha256 of the two data files. The JSON and the template are byte-for-byte copies made with `tools/snapshot.mjs`, each with its `.provenance.json` sidecar (`readMatchesCommitted: true`); `partforge.js` has a `--sidecar-only` sidecar pointing at `index.html`. `build-data.mjs --check` re-derives and compares, and a test runs it. The page fetches nothing: all three snapshots load as scripts, so `file://` works.

**The arithmetic** (`heat.js`, pure, no DOM). With V in m/s, altitudes in geometric metres, temperatures in kelvin until the last step:

- `{T_K, a_m_s, clamped} = isa(alt_m)` from the repository. [Certain] It is their function; the tests reproduce their published vectors (below).
- M = V / a. [Certain]
- T₀ = T∞ · (1 + (γ−1)/2 · M²), γ = `ISA_GAMMA` = 1.4. [Certain] The energy equation for a calorically perfect gas; the standard text is Anderson, *Modern Compressible Flow*, eq. 3.28 in the editions I know, cited from memory.
- c_p = γR/(γ−1) = 1.4 × 287.05287 / 0.4 = 1004.685 J/(kg·K), from `ISA_R`. [Certain] Then T₀ − T∞ = V²/(2c_p) *identically*: (γ−1)/2 · V²/(γRT∞) · T∞ = (γ−1)V²/(2γR). The page prints both as a cross-check, and a test holds them to 1e-9 relative over 300 random conditions; at 700 m/s the rise is 243.858 K either way.
- T_r = T∞ · (1 + r · (γ−1)/2 · M²), the adiabatic-wall (recovery) temperature, with the flat-plate recovery factors r = Pr^½ (laminar) and Pr^⅓ (turbulent). [Likely] These are Eckert's results as given in Schlichting, *Boundary-Layer Theory*, and in Anderson; I know them from textbooks, not from the original papers. r = 1 is the stagnation point.
- Pr = 0.71 for air near 300 K. [Likely] Property tables put it at 0.707 at 300 K and 0.684 at 500 K (Incropera & DeWitt, Table A.4, from memory). It is the **only constant in the equations not read from the repository** (the 800 K flag, the 2,000 K ceiling and the 25 °C *near* band, below, are thresholds on what is flagged, refused or how a band is named, not terms in the arithmetic), it is named `PR_AIR` in one place, and the evaluation's guard asked for exactly this ("derive r from a named Pr constant instead of hardcoding 0.845/0.896", `:567`). It gives r = 0.8426 (laminar) and 0.8921 (turbulent). Using 0.70 instead moves the turbulent recovery temperature at the fin condition by about 1 °C.
- margin = `service_max_c` − T (°C), one for each bound; the screen band uses two of them: *over* if the turbulent recovery bound exceeds the limit, *stagnation* if only T₀ does, *near* if T₀ is within 25 °C under it, *under* otherwise. The 25 °C is a presentation threshold, not physics, and the page says so in the table's legend.
- Speed at which a bound meets the limit: V = √(2 c_p (T_limit − T∞) / r), the inversion of the line above; null when T∞ already exceeds the limit. [Certain]

**Flags that print beside every result:** the bound caveat (always); *provisional* when `inputs_src` is `default`, in the app's wording ("arithmetic, not evidence"), because a low guessed speed passes a polymer part silently (`:566`, risk 2); the row's confidence, with `recall` in the warning colour; and *hot* when T₀ exceeds 800 K, because with constant γ the stagnation temperature is overstated there, by a few percent just above 800 K and by more the hotter it gets (c_p of air rises with temperature once vibration is excited) [Likely], which keeps the bound conservative.

**The envelope.** Two edges are refused as `out_of_envelope` rather than computed. The ISA table's: `isa()` clamps outside −5 km to 86 km geometric, and a clamped altitude would be a number for the wrong place. And the perfect-gas picture's: when the constant-γ T₀ would exceed **2,000 K**, around where oxygen begins to dissociate (Anderson, *Hypersonic and High-Temperature Gas Dynamics*, the regimes for air at 1 atm: vibration excited above about 800 K, O₂ dissociation from about 2,000 K, N₂ from about 4,000 K; lower pressure lowers all three; cited from memory, and 2,000 K is the more conservative of the 2,000 and 2,500 K onsets different texts give) [Likely], c_p, γ and the recovery factors all depend on temperature and pressure and T₀ = T∞(1 + 0.2M²) is no longer a bound on anything, so the model refuses and names the speed at which T₀ reaches the ceiling at that altitude, V = √(2c_p(2000 K − T∞)): 1,854.65 m/s at sea level, 1,865.18 m/s at 3 km, 1,908.69 m/s at the top of the table. Nothing the page can show is above it: the 1,500 m/s slider tops out at T₀ ≈ 1,408 K, and the chart's speed axis stops at the ceiling speed. This edge exists because the first review typed 1,000,000 m/s into the speed field and froze the tab: with no ceiling, the chart's speed axis went to 1,250,000 m/s, its temperature range to 7.8 × 10⁸ K, and its grid loop, with the step capped at 500 °C, stroked 1,555,214 lines synchronously inside the input handler; and below the hang, at 99,999 m/s and 40 km, the page printed "T₀ 4976561.7 °C" to a tenth of a degree (I reproduced both numbers from the old arithmetic). Now the grid step is chosen from the range (about six lines whatever the numbers), the axis is capped, and anything past the ceiling is a refusal in well under a second (38–60 ms per keystroke for 10⁴ to 10⁸ m/s in a fresh headless page at 390 and 1280 px).

Against 6061-T6's 170 °C (`likely`), the fin condition's margins are −69.4 °C (T₀), −43.1 °C (turbulent) and −31.0 °C (laminar): **the template's declared flight condition puts the adiabatic-wall bound above the alloy's service temperature**, and the bound reaches 170 °C at 592.1 m/s (stagnation) and 626.9 m/s (turbulent recovery) at that altitude. Read with the caveat: the bound is a ceiling for a steady flight, the template's speed is `inputs_src` default, and 170 °C is where T6 temper "starts to draw back", in the table's words, not where the part fails. What it means is that a Mach 2 aluminium fin is a case for Stage 2, not a case this screen can clear. The evaluation's "~128 °C at apogee" is Mach 2 near 26.3 km geometric by the same `isa()` (127.96 °C); at 25 km it is 125.6 °C, which is what the page pins, and the gap to the 3 km figure, 84.8 °C, is the "80 °C unsafe-low read".

**The table at the fin condition:** 13 materials *over* (the three aluminium alloys (6061, 7075, 2024), brass, copper, all four laminates, Ultem, PA12, CF-nylon, Delrin), none *stagnation*, one *near* (PEEK at 250 °C, +10.6 °C vs T₀) and seven *under* (304, 316, 17-4PH, 4130, Ti-6Al-4V, Inconel 718, graphite). Fourteen of the 21 limits are `recall`, seven `likely`; the row's word prints beside every margin.

**The chart** is Canvas 2D: three 2 px lines in a colour set validated for colour-vision deficiency in both light and dark modes (blue, orange, aqua, with a legend and direct labels at desktop width), the limit as a dashed ink line labelled with the material and its confidence, the current speed as a vertical rule with dots, and a crosshair readout under the pointer or finger. The speed axis runs to at least 1,000 m/s and a quarter beyond the current speed, rounded up to 250, but never past the speed where T₀ reaches the 2,000 K ceiling at that altitude (a dotted rule marks the cap when it engages); the grid steps on both axes are chosen from the range (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 × 10ⁿ), so the drawing does the same work whatever is typed. The table colours only the numbers that are negative and tags each row with a word, never colour alone.

**The page.** `heat.js` loads as a classic script (`window.HotSkin`) and with `require()`; `app.js` draws from it. Inputs are native (text, number, range, select), so they work by keyboard and touch; the verdict and refusal regions are `aria-live`; colours are CSS variables redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. Nothing is stored, nothing is fetched, and the URL is read once on load.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The atmosphere is PartForge's.** `isa()` runs as the repository wrote it, from text copied by marker and compared character for character with the checkout, and it reproduces the vectors the repository's own test file publishes. If `index.html` changes, `build-data.mjs --check` and the verbatim test say so.
- **[Certain] The stagnation arithmetic.** T₀ = T∞(1 + 0.2M²) with γ = 1.4 is the perfect-gas energy equation; the V²/2c_p identity is exact and tested; the hand-worked case agrees to 1e-9 K. The fin condition's 239.4 °C is right for what it is.
- **[Likely] The recovery factors.** Pr^½ and Pr^⅓ are the standard flat-plate results and Pr = 0.71 is a fair value for air between 250 and 500 K; the turbulent recovery temperature moves about 1 °C per 0.01 of Pr at the fin condition. These are textbook results I did not re-derive and the one external constant in the equations; both are labelled on the page.
- **[Certain] It is an upper bound and nothing more.** The adiabatic wall temperature is the temperature the skin would reach with no heat going anywhere. A fin that is at Mach 2 for ten seconds, conducting into a tab and an airframe and radiating, never gets there. The page can say "the bound is above the limit"; it cannot say the part is too hot, and it says neither "pass" nor "fail". A margin here is a reason to do Stage 2, or to look at the declared speed, not a verdict on the part.
- **[Likely] Above about 800 K the constant-γ T₀ is high, and increasingly so,** because c_p of air rises with temperature once vibration is excited: a few percent just above 800 K, growing toward the ceiling (from the property tables I remember, c_p of air at 2,000 K is about a quarter above its 300 K value, which would put the overstatement of T₀ near the ceiling in the region of 15 percent [Speculative: remembered table values, not computed here]). The bound stays conservative throughout; at the fin condition (512 K) the effect is negligible. The page flags the regime; the number is not corrected.
- **[Likely] The 2,000 K ceiling is a textbook regime limit, not a measured boundary.** O₂ dissociation onset for air at 1 atm is given as about 2,000 K in Anderson and as 2,500 K elsewhere, from memory; at altitude the onset is lower. The ceiling is the more conservative round number, and above it the page refuses rather than printing a perfect-gas T₀ that means nothing. Everything the controls can reach stays under it; only a typed speed can hit it.
- **[Certain] The limit column is weak and the page says so every time.** `materials_thermal.json`'s own notes: "service_max_c is judgement, not a rating", "every value is recall; nothing here was fetched or measured", and the metals are marked `likely` only because handbooks agree.
