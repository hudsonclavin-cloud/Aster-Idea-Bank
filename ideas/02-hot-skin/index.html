Hot Skin

The stagnation and recovery temperature of air at an airspeed and altitude are an upper bound on how hot heating by the air can make a part's skin, on the day stated (the ISA standard day unless you set an offset), not a prediction of how hot it does get. Sun on the pad and motor heat are not in it. This page measures that bound against each PartForge flight material's service_max_c. The verdict is SCREEN, never a pass: the limits are judgement and the speed may be a guess.

Condition

The only speed input, and it is airspeed: speed relative to the air, not over the ground. A ground speed into a headwind reads low, by 7.0 K per 10 m/s of headwind at 700 m/s. Mach is derived. Type Mach 2 to see why a bare Mach number is refused. Where T0 would pass 2,000 K (1,854.6 m/s at sea level on a standard day) the page refuses: the perfect-gas picture ends there.

    Margins in °C on the day stated: positive means the bound sits under the limit, and a hotter day takes the offset off every margin. over: even the turbulent recovery temperature exceeds the limit. stagnation: only stagnation points (leading edge, nose tip) exceed it. near: the stagnation bound is within 25 °C of the limit. under: the bound is below the limit; the limit itself is still judgement, not a rating. Confidence: likely = nominal, agreeing across handbooks; recall = model recall, not cited. note opens the table’s own remark on the row (most are about expansion, not the limit).
