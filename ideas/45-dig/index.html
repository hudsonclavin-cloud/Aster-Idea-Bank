Dig

Idea 44 left the spacecraft standing on the asteroid, weighing a few tens of millinewtons. This page puts a mining plant there: bucket drums that dig without pushing it off, a mirror and an oven that bake water out of the regolith, and the Sun at that distance, rising and setting over that spot. It says how much water comes out each day, what limits it, and how sure that is.

            How this works

            What the rock gives up

            The body's spectral class comes from JPL's Small-Body Database: Apophis is Sq (Binzel et al. 2009), Bennu B (Clark et al. 2011). The S complex is matched to ordinary chondrites, whose water was measured in Itokawa's returned grains (Jin and Bose 2019: 160 to 510 ppm in the bulk silicate, held in pyroxene and olivine); the page counts all 510 ppm, an upper bound, because how much of it an oven releases is not measured there. The C complex is matched to CM chondrites: Suttle et al. (2024) heated chips of Murchison in a thermogravimetric balance with a mass spectrometer and measured the water each run gave off above 200 °C (below that it is terrestrial water the meteorite soaked up on Earth, left out as the authors do). Six runs: 500 or 750 °C, 2 to 20 °C a minute, argon or vacuum. The chips differ: 4.0 to 9.7 wt% by 750 °C.

            What the heat costs

            Hess's law over Robie and Hemingway's tables (USGS Bulletin 2131): the water is carried as serpentine (chrysotile) that breaks down into forsterite, enstatite and steam; the rest of the rock is heated as forsterite. Reactants at the start temperature, products at the oven's peak; the heat is the difference of their enthalpies, whatever the path. Breaking the serpentine costs 4.38 MJ per kg of water, 79% more than boiling free water; heating the rock costs more than that when the water is a few per cent, and everything when it is parts per million. Heat recovered from the spent rock can preheat the next batch.

            What the Sun delivers

            Where the asteroid is comes from JPL Horizons (ICRF, every 2 days); the flux is the Sun's luminosity (NSSDCA, as idea 40 uses it) over 4π r². Whether the Sun is up at the site comes from idea 43's spin, unmodified: Bennu's IAU rotation, or Apophis tumbling by Euler's equations from NAIF's CK samples (called every 2 h, with a cubic Hermite in between). A Sun-tracking mirror feeds a cavity oven; the oven's opening, the mirror's area over the concentration, radiates σT⁴ at the oven's temperature, so too little concentration means no heat goes in at all. The tests hold the Sun's elevation at Nightingale to JPL Horizons' own Bennu model.

            Digging without pushing off

            NASA's IPEx and RASSOR excavators dig with pairs of bucket drums turning in opposite directions so their cutting forces cancel. The page keeps a fraction of one drum's force uncancelled (the imbalance), uses idea 44's regolith resistance (its fit to OSIRIS-REx's contact, 69 to 165 Pa) as the pressure on the cut face, and adds the momentum of the regolith thrown into the drums at their tip speed (8.5 times the cut speed, IPEx). Against it is the spacecraft's weight on the ground. Too deep a cut lifts it off: the page cuts the depth back, or holds it down with thrusters at idea 44's cost per newton-hour if there is propellant.

            Day by day

            Each day the plant processes the least of three amounts: what the day's heat can bake, what the oven can cycle while the Sun is up (batches heated at the run's rate), and what the drums can dig while the Sun is up. Water is that times the run's water fraction. The handoff gives module 46 the mean, the range and every day.

            What it does not do

            No terrain shadows (the horizon is the site plate's plane; a mask is offered); no heat lost or kept overnight; no kinetics beyond the measured runs; carbonates and sulfides are released but their heats of reaction are left out; no anchoring; no electrical power budget for the drums, the condenser or the radiators; the release curve of Bennu's own returned sample is not in this folder.
