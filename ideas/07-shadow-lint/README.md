# Shadow Lint
> For every number or name a PartForge FLIGHT loads row feeds its engineering check, says whether the declared geometry can see it. Over 13 files from the PartForge checkout, 99 inputs feed the checks and 44 of them have no geometric shadow (16 loads, pressures, speeds, altitudes and strengths; 18 bolt grades, materials and shank-in-shear choices named in text; five lengths, four thread sizes and one bolt count). Given an attempt and its retry, it flags an input that moved the way that makes a check easier while the code outside comments stayed byte-identical, the retry failure mode PartForge's own evaluation says nothing detects today.

**Kind:** linter
**For:** PartForge
**Run:** from the bank root, `node ideas/07-shadow-lint/shadow-lint.mjs ideas/07-shadow-lint/snapshots/bulkhead.scad` (one file: the shadow table) or `node ideas/07-shadow-lint/shadow-lint.mjs ideas/07-shadow-lint/snapshots/motor-retainer.scad ideas/07-shadow-lint/snapshots/motor-retainer.retry.scad` (two files: the attempt diff); `--json` prints an envelope in the shape of PartForge's headless CLI, `--help` the usage; exit 0 no findings, 1 findings, 2 refused, 3 usage. `example.txt` is saved output of seven runs. `ideas/07-shadow-lint/index.html` shows the same findings in a browser and lints pasted text (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). Tests: `node --test ideas/07-shadow-lint/shadow.test.mjs` (28 tests, Node built-ins, about 2 s) and `node ideas/07-shadow-lint/ui-check.mjs` (31 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). `build-data.mjs` re-derives the excerpts under `snapshots/` and `findings.js` from a PartForge checkout.
**Made by:** Sciagraph

## What it is

PartForge's flight grade works like this: the model writes a `.scad` file with a commented `FLIGHT` block that declares critical extents, turned diameters, bores, hole patterns, gauges and *loads rows*; the app compiles the file, measures the declared geometry on the mesh (its "CMM"), and recomputes each loads row's engineering check (Lamé hoop stress, fin flutter, thread strip, bolt shear) from the numbers in the row. A failing row goes back to the model as a retry prompt that ends "correct the inputs if they were wrong; keep the check."

That sentence is the problem this tool is about. The CMM measures a bore. Nothing measures `force_N`. When a bolt-shear row fails, the model's cheapest fix is to lower `force_N` from 20,000 to 12,000, leave the geometry byte-identical, and pass. Shadow Lint does two things about it:

1. **One file.** For every key of every loads row it says what PartForge's evaluator does with it, and for every input the check reads it prints a class and a witness:
   - `WITNESSED`: the value equals a declared dimension of compatible unit within that dimension's tolerance (`plate_t_mm 12.7` against the critical "plate thickness" 12.70 ±0.05; `n 8` against a circle of 8 holes whose Ø6.40 is a clearance for M6; `size #8-32` against a Ø3.45 hole, which is the 75 % tap drill 3.454 for #8-32).
   - `DERIVABLE`: an arithmetic combination of declared rows (`plate_t_mm 3.2` is a shear-pin hole's span 74.65 → 71.45; `engagement_mm 10` is a tap-drill hole's span 2.7 → 12.7; `ri_mm 40` is the Ø80 pocket bore over two).
   - `UNWITNESSED`: nothing declared can see it. For `force_N`, `P_MPa`, `v_max_m_s`, `alt_m`, the strengths, a bolt `grade`, a `material` named in text and a shank put in the shear plane that is by nature; for a length it means every candidate missed, and the nearest one is named with its distance.
   - `POLICY`: `sf_min` on a hoop, flutter or bolt-shear row, a declared acceptance threshold rather than a physical quantity; a retry can lower it with no geometric consequence, so it is listed and diffed, but not counted as an invented physical input.

   The file's **invention surface** is the list of unwitnessed inputs: the numbers and names a retry can change with no geometric consequence. Beside the classes the table lists the **gaps**: a key the row lacks, or declares as something the evaluator cannot read as a number, and what the evaluator does instead (refuses the row; assumes 1 bolt, sea level or an 800 MPa bolt; or, for a missing `plate_t_mm`, fails the row with a bearing SF of 0, Finding F7). Keys the check never reads (`sf_min` on a thread row, `threads_in_shear`, a `grade` overridden by `bolt_sigma_y_MPa`, unknown numeric keys) are listed as **not read** and counted nowhere. A number written as a string (`"force_N":"12000"`) is read as `+value`, as the evaluator reads it, and says so.

   The witnesses are only as good as the declaration. The endcap is PartForge's own dry run, kept *because* the CMM fails it: its O-ring groove is declared as a bore down the axis (`tests/dryrun/cases.json`: "the groove is declared as a bore down the axis", expected failures include `Bore "o-ring groove bottom diameter"` and `Outside diameter "shoulder OD"`, and "declare it as an od"). Its hoop row's `t_mm 2.77 = (101.50 − 95.96)/2` comes out `DERIVABLE`, but 2.77 is the groove depth (the file's own NOTE says so), and at z 11.15–15.9 the annulus between Ø95.96 and Ø101.5 is what the groove removes, not a wall. `DERIVABLE` there shows only that the row's arithmetic agrees with a declaration known to be wrong. The tool now prints a caveat on such walls and radii: a bore whose axial span lies strictly inside a coaxial OD's span is the signature of an external groove declared as a bore.

2. **Two files.** Given an attempt and its retry it strips every comment (SPEC, FLIGHT, KIND and NOTE text all live in comments and the model rewrites them on every retry), normalises whitespace and compares the code; matches loads rows by check and name and diffs every key; recomputes PartForge's contract floor (`flightCounts`); and judges which way each changed key went, by how the evaluator reads it. A move that makes a check easier (a load, pressure or speed lowered, the altitude raised, the threshold lowered, a strength or grade raised, more bolts, a thicker section), a witnessed input that loses its witness, a key whose loss makes the row refuse, and a material renamed are **suspicious**. A move the other way, a key added whose absence the evaluator refused (the recorded first-attempt failure: no bolt grade), a load raised to a motor's certified peak, and added provenance are **expected corrections**. With byte-identical code, each suspicious move is an `input_moved_geometry_unchanged` finding; a lost category is a `floor_loss` finding; expected corrections are listed, not flagged. Declaration changes (a nominal, a station, a hole count) are listed as information, because the CMM re-measures those against the mesh.

A file with no `FLIGHT` block, with malformed JSON, or with no loads rows is a structured refusal (`not_found` / `invalid_input` / `insufficient_data`, a reason and what would help), never an empty table. "Witnessed" means a *declared* dimension matches: PartForge's CMM is what measures that declaration against a mesh, and nothing here measures a mesh. The tool names the row the app would measure. It cannot know that an unwitnessed number is wrong; it can only say that nothing checks it. It reports and never rewrites.

## Why I made it

**The open item.** PartForge's roadmap evaluation (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md` at `0974a62`) names this twice as a structural hole:

- Wrong-number risk 9 (`:313`): "Every failing loads row lands in `g.fails` and is handed back to the model with 'correct the inputs if they were wrong.' For any input with no geometric shadow … the model's cheapest fix is to edit the number. **The geometry stays byte-identical and nothing detects it.**" The same sentence recurs in the guard for the bonded-joint idea (`:651`: "Nothing measures engage_mm … the geometry stays byte-identical and nothing detects it").
- Shared infrastructure **D** (`:131–133`), "Provenance that means something", first bullet: "**Measured-input cross-reference.** A join from a loads entry to the CMM-measured `od`/`bores`/`critical-extent` rows by name or station. It does not exist: the evaluator reads `l.ri_mm` and `l.t_mm` straight off the declaration." It is scheduled inside build-first item 3 (`:249`), and the completeness critic (`:351`) adds that "a first-class refusal state" is "assumed everywhere, specified nowhere".

The code agrees with the document. `parseFlight` (`index.html:4672`) copies every loads key off the declaration (`:4735`); the evaluator reads `l.P_MPa`, `l.ri_mm`, `l.t_mm` (`:5756–5757`), `l.root_mm … l.v_max_m_s` (`:5762–5767`), `l.engagement_mm` (`:5771–5773`), `l.force_N` (`:5781`), `l.grade` (`:5804–5808`) and `l.plate_t_mm` (`:5810`) directly; `row.provisional` (`:5820`) depends on `inputs_src`, not on anything measured; both retry hints say "correct the inputs if they were wrong" (`:5751`, `:5778`); the contract floor (`:5840–5843`, counts from `flightCounts` at `:4823`) forbids *fewer* entries but says nothing about changed values; `flightContradictions` (`:4847`) checks critical nominals against SPEC part sizes only. The headless `check` envelope prints the loads rows as `check, sf, sf_min, inputs, provisional` (`headless/partforge.mjs:103`), no shadow. `headless/llm.mjs design()` (`:141`) keeps the attempts of a run in memory (`:142`, returned in the envelope at `:185`) and `cli.mjs --out` writes only the chosen code, so nothing on disk compares attempt *n* with attempt *n* − 1. The recorded Gemini flight rings (`docs/runs/2026-10-06-gemini-flight-x5/README.md`) show the loop in action: runs 1–5 each failed attempt 1 and passed attempt 2 (runs 1, 2, 4 and 5 on a missing bolt grade, run 3 on a lightening-hole pattern that did not match the drawn holes); run 6, made after the doctrine fix `98df2a2`, passed on attempt 1; only the chosen file of each run was kept.

**Alternatives weighed.**

- *Witness against the mesh, not the declaration.* The honest ideal, and the app's own CMM already does half of it. It needs `openscad-wasm` (14 MB, not vendorable here, and neither OpenSCAD nor the wasm is installed in this sandbox) and would duplicate `measureExtent`/`measureBore`. Foundation D's own wording is a join "by name or station" to the rows the CMM measures, which is a declaration-level join; that is what this does, and the README says "declared" wherever it matters. The endcap shows the price: a declaration-level witness can agree with a declaration the CMM rejects.
- *Patch PartForge's retry loop.* The right landing, not something a deposit may do, and the detection is more useful as a step a harness can call on any two files than as a line inside `design()`.
- *Per-input provenance* (D's second bullet: `inputs_src` is one field per row). A schema change for PartForge to make; the shadow table shows exactly which inputs such a field would have to cover.
- *A geometry digest from measured extents* (foundation H says a text hash of the `.scad` is the wrong record key because comments change on every retry). For a record store H is right. For attempt-versus-retry the question is literally "is the code outside comments byte-identical", so a comment-stripped text comparison is the correct instrument here, and it is what the evaluation's sentence asks for.
- *Flag every moved input with byte-identical geometry* (this deposit's first version). It would have failed the dominant recorded correction: four of the five first attempts in `docs/runs/2026-10-06-gemini-flight-x5/` failed on a missing bolt grade, and the fix (add `"grade":"8.8"`) changes no geometry. The verdict now follows the direction the evaluator reads, and the test suite replays exactly that correction.

This won because it is the smallest thing that answers the evaluation's sentence and refuses when it cannot.

## How it works

**Fixtures.** Thirteen `.scad` files copied from the PartForge checkout at `0974a62` with `tools/snapshot.mjs` (sidecars under `snapshots/`; every copy's read blob matched the committed blob): six of the seven shipped templates in `tests/templates/` (`av-sled`, `bulkhead`, `centering-ring`, `coupler`, `fin`, `motor-retainer`; `nosecone` carries `loads: []` and would only repeat the av-sled refusal), the three dry-run cases in `tests/dryrun/` (`endcap` and `retainer`, kept because the CMM fails them, and `retainer-fixed`, "hand-corrected from retainer.scad" per `cases.json`), and four of the six recorded Gemini rings (`ring1`, `ring3`, `ring4`, `ring6-after-doctrine-fix`; rings 2 and 5 repeat ring 1's shape of declaration). One more file is synthetic: `motor-retainer.retry.scad`, the template with exactly one edit inside the FLIGHT block, `"force_N":20000` → `"force_N":12000`, and code outside comments byte-identical (`build-data.mjs` asserts both; a test re-checks; its sidecar says `derived`). Every total in this README is over the 13 real files unless it says otherwise.

**Parser.** `shadow.js` parses the FLIGHT block the way `index.html` `parseFlight()` (`:4672–4750`) does, field for field in every category it returns (critical, od, bores, holes, revolve, gauges, loads) and in the dropped and demoted counts: strip `// ` from the block, `JSON.parse`, cap each category at 24 entries, drop a critical without an axis or a positive nominal and tolerance, drop a round feature whose `from_mm` equals `to_mm`, default `pos_tol_mm` to `tol_mm`, keep a loads row only for `hoop|flutter|thread|bolt_shear`, copy its keys through `/^[a-z_]+$/i` (numbers stay numbers, everything else becomes a string cut to 40 characters), normalise `inputs_src`, and demote a `source` claim without a reference to `default` on every critical, od, bore, hole, revolve and loads entry. It leaves out `mfg` and the provenance summary, which no check reads. `build-data.mjs` cuts PartForge's own parser out of `index.html` by regex anchors (`^const FLIGHT_AXIS`, `^function provenanceSummary`, `^function flightCounts`) inside its `/* DECL-BEGIN */ … /* DECL-END */` block (`:4646–4859`, the block PartForge's `tests/run.mjs` extracts whole) into `snapshots/flight-decl.js`, unedited, and a test runs both parsers over every fixture and a set of edge cases and demands identical output on all of those fields, plus identical `flightCounts`.

**How each key is read** (`SPEC` in `shadow.js`, from the evaluator's loads loop, `index.html:5745–5828`):

| check | key | kind | witnessed or derivable by | when absent or not readable | easier when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hoop | `P_MPa` | pressure | — | refused (`:5756`) | lower |
| hoop | `ri_mm` | radius | derivable: a bore's d/2, within tol/2 | refused | lower |
| hoop | `t_mm` | thickness | an extent; derivable: a wall or a span | refused | higher |
| flutter | `root_mm`, `tip_mm`, `span_mm` | planform | an extent (never a span) | refused (`:5762`; `tip_mm` may be 0) | no single direction |
| flutter | `sweep_mm` | planform | an extent | assumed: ε 0.25 (`:5764`, `:751`) | no single direction |
| flutter | `thick_mm` | thickness | as `t_mm` | refused | higher |
| flutter | `v_max_m_s` | speed | — | refused (`:5769`) | lower |
| flutter | `alt_m` | altitude | — | assumed: sea level (`:5764`) | higher |
| thread | `size` | thread | a hole or bore at a tap-drill diameter for it | refused (`parseThreadSize`, `:797`) | smaller |
| thread | `engagement_mm` | engagement | derivable: a hole or bore span (never an OD), preferring a tap-drill row | refused (`:5771`) | higher |
| thread | `bolt_sigma_u_MPa` | strength | — | assumed: 800 MPa (`:5773`) | lower (strip must beat the bolt) |
| thread | `internal_material` | material, by name | — | the row's material (`:5772`) | cannot be ordered here |
| bolt_shear | `size` | thread | a hole at a clearance diameter for it | refused | larger |
| bolt_shear | `n` | count | a hole row with that count **and** a clearance diameter for `size` | assumed: 1 bolt (`:5810`) | higher |
| bolt_shear | `force_N` | force | — (a named motor adds a data witness) | refused (`:5781`) | lower |
| bolt_shear | `plate_t_mm` | thickness | as `t_mm` | the row fails with bearing SF 0 (F7) | higher |
| bolt_shear | `grade` | grade, by name | — (its σy is read from `GRADE_SY`) | refused unless `bolt_sigma_y_MPa` (`:5806`) | higher σy |
| bolt_shear | `bolt_sigma_y_MPa` | strength | — | the grade is used (`:5805`) | higher |
| bolt_shear | `shear_plane` | shear plane, by name | — | threads in the shear plane (`:5809`) | to "shank" |
| bolt_shear | `motor` | (a data floor) | listed under other keys | no floor | adding it is harder |
| hoop, flutter, bolt_shear | `sf_min` | policy | — | assumed: 2.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 (`:5759`, `:5767`, `:5812`) | lower |
| any | `material` | material, by name | — | the part's material (`:5749`) | cannot be ordered here |
| thread | `sf_min` | not read | — | the check fixes 1.0 (`:5775`, F6) | — |
| bolt_shear | `threads_in_shear` | not read | — | it arrives as a string (F1) | — |
| any | any other numeric key | not read | — | — | — |

The directions follow from the evaluator's formulas: σ ∝ P and F; the Lamé factor grows with `ri` at fixed `t`; bearing goes as F/(d·t); the flutter speed goes as a·√(P₀/P) (`flutterVelocity`, `:740`), which grows with altitude in the ISA model; the thread check passes when strip strength ≥ bolt strength, so a stronger bolt or a larger thread (strip/bolt ∝ Le/d) makes it harder. The flutter planform does not move the check one way (a longer root lowers the aspect ratio but also t/c), so any planform move the geometry cannot show is suspicious. A number read with `+value` follows the evaluator exactly: `"12000"` is 12000, `"abc"` is no number, `n 4.4` is read as 4.

**Candidates** from a parsed declaration (all mm):

| candidate | from | value | tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| extent | each `critical` row | `nominal_mm` | its `tol_mm` |
| span | each `od`, `bores`, `holes` row | \|`from_mm` − `to_mm`\| | its `tol_mm` |
| wall | each `od` × `bores` pair on the same axis and `at_mm`, with `od.d > bore.d`, whose axial ranges overlap | (`od.d_mm` − `bore.d_mm`)/2 | mean of the two `tol_mm` |
| radius | each `bores` row | `d_mm`/2 | `tol_mm`/2 |
| count | each `holes` row | circle `n`, list length, or 1 | exact |

The overlap condition matters: on the endcap the flange OD (z 0–5.4) and the O-ring groove bore (z 11.15–15.9) are coaxial but share no stretch of axis, so no wall is formed from them; the shoulder OD (5.4–25.4) and that bore do, and that pair gives the 2.77 mm the hoop row declares. Because the bore's span lies strictly inside the shoulder OD's span (it opens at neither end of the shoulder), the wall and the radius built on it carry the groove caveat; the doctrine itself says an external O-ring groove "is an od entry over the groove's band" (`index.html:1456`). No other fixture raises the caveat.

A length matches when |value − candidate| ≤ tolerance (+10⁻⁹, so a value exactly one tolerance away still matches in floating point). "Clearance for a size" means the hole diameter lies between the thread's major diameter and its loosest clearance, each widened by the hole's tolerance; "tap drill" means within the hole's tolerance of a tap-drill diameter. Those diameters come from `snapshots/fasteners_metric.excerpt.js` (all 12 rows of `data/tables/fasteners_metric.json`: `d_mm`, `tap_drill_mm`, `tap_drill_stainless_mm`, the three ISO 273 clearances) and `fasteners_un.excerpt.js` (all 16 rows of `fasteners_un.json`: `major_mm`, the 75 % and 50 % tap drills, the three B18.2.8 clearances). Every row keeps its `confidence` and the witness text prints it: four UN rows are `recall` (`#2-56`, `#12-24`, `1/2-13`, `1/2-20`), the fixtures touch only `#2-56`, and the coupler's witness says `(fasteners_un recall)`. The excerpts carry the tables' `license_note`, and the per-row `source` text, identical across all 12 metric rows and 15 of the 16 UN rows, is kept once as `rows_source` (the one UN row that differs keeps its own). A bolt grade's yield comes from `snapshots/grade_sy.excerpt.js`, the evaluator's own `GRADE_SY` line (`index.html:5804`) cut verbatim and parsed (8.8 → 640, 12.9 → 1100, A2-70 → 450 MPa, …); a grade not in it is a gap the evaluator refuses. When a bolt_shear row names a `motor`, the `force_N` note adds what the evaluator would do (`:5786–5793`): hold `force_N` to the certified peak in `motor_perf.json`. `snapshots/motor_perf.excerpt.js` holds the one row the fixtures name, Loki L2050 (`max_thrust_N` 3331, `max_thrust_src` "certified", confidence `likely`), matched as `dbMotorPerf` matches (`:4907–4916`); the note calls that a data witness, not a geometric one, and says whether the declared value equals, exceeds or falls below the peak (below, the evaluator refuses the row, `:5792`). A motor outside the excerpt is reported as not in the excerpt.

**The two-file diff.** `geometryText()` removes `/* … */` blocks and `//` comments, trims and collapses whitespace per line and drops blank lines; the two texts are compared for identity and, when they differ, an LCS over lines gives removed/added counts. Loads rows are matched by (`check`, `name`); every key of the union is compared (numbers to 10⁻⁹, strings exactly), and each changed key gets its class before and after and a verdict from `judgeMove()`:

| change | verdict |
|---|---|
| a number moved the "easier" way of the table above, judged on what the evaluator reads (so `n` removed is 8 → 1 bolt, harder; `alt_m` removed is 3000 → 0 m, harder) | suspicious |
| a geometric input (thickness, radius, planform, engagement, count, thread size) lost its witness | suspicious |
| a key the evaluator refuses without was removed; a grade it does not know; the motor dropped; a material renamed; a flutter planform input moved | suspicious |
| a check row removed | suspicious (and a `floor_loss`) |
| a number moved the "harder" way; `force_N` raised to the named motor's certified peak | expected |
| a key added whose absence the evaluator refused (a grade, a thread size, a load) | expected |
| a geometric input gained a witness | expected |
| `inputs_src` promoted or an `inputs_ref` added (provenance never decides pass or fail, `:5818`; the reference is not verified here) | expected |
| the evaluator reads the same number (`"8"` → 8), or does not read the key | no effect |

Declaration rows are matched by name and their nominal, diameter, tolerance, stations, count and bolt circle compared. The floor is `flightCounts` before and after, with lost categories named exactly as `index.html:5840–5843` would fail them. Findings: `floor_loss` per lost category; and, only when the geometry text is identical, `input_moved_geometry_unchanged` per suspicious move. With changed geometry every move is listed with its verdict and none is a finding: that is an ordinary retry.

**Exit codes and the envelope.** As `headless/cli.mjs`: 0 / 1 / 2 / 3. One file never exits 1: its table is information. `--json` wraps the result as `{ ok, tool, value, units, confidence: 'derived', provenance, assumptions, validity_envelope, summary, refusal? }` (`headless/partforge.mjs:41`), with the four excerpts' repo, path and commit in `provenance`. `confidence` is `derived` from PartForge's own vocabulary (`measured | derived | cited`, `headless/mcp.mjs:36`): the classification is computed from the declaration text, and nothing here measures anything.

**The page.** `index.html` loads `shadow.js`, `findings.js` and the four excerpts as classic scripts: a fixture selector with the shadow table (class badges always carry the word, never only a colour; gaps and keys not read have their own badges), a pair selector with what each pair is, the diff with a verdict per moved key, and its findings, and two text areas that run the same code on pasted files ("Load the synthetic retry pair" fills them with a minimal declaration that reproduces the edit, since a `file://` page cannot fetch the `.scad` snapshots). Light and dark follow `prefers-color-scheme`; at 390 px each input stacks into a card.

## Tests

`shadow.test.mjs` (28 tests, Node built-ins, about 2 s):

- **Snapshots.** All 19 files under `snapshots/` still hash to their sidecar's `writtenSha256`; copies equal their source bytes and matched the committed blob; derived files are marked `derived`. `findings.js` deep-equals a fresh computation over the snapshots, pairs with their relation. The synthetic retry differs from the template in exactly one line, by `"force_N":20000 → 12000`, with identical geometry text.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The classification is exactly what the `SPEC` and candidate tables say, over the declaration PartForge's own parser produces.** The parser agreement test runs PartForge's `parseFlight` beside this one on every fixture; the witnesses quote the rows and the arithmetic; every fixture number in this README is produced by the tests. Each line of `SPEC` cites the evaluator line it encodes; the directions are argued above from the evaluator's formulas, and I checked the flutter, hoop, thread and bolt-shear directions by running PartForge's own `hoopStress`, `flutterVelocity`, `threadCheck` and `boltShear` (copied out of `index.html` into a scratch file, not into this folder) at values taken from the fixtures.
- **[Certain] Nothing here measures a mesh, and a witness is only as good as the declaration.** `WITNESSED` and `DERIVABLE` mean a declared dimension matches. The app's own tests expect the seven templates to pass the CMM (`tests/flight-templates-cases.json`, `fails: []`, run by `headless/test/parity.test.mjs`) and expect the endcap and retainer dry runs to fail it (`tests/dryrun/cases.json`). The endcap is the case in point: its `t_mm 2.77` derives from a groove row the CMM rejects, and 2.77 is a groove depth, not a wall. The groove caveat catches that one shape of wrong declaration (a bore enclosed by a coaxial OD section); it would not catch a wrong critical nominal, which would witness a loads input just as well.
- **[Certain] The tool cannot tell an invented number from a right one.** `force_N 20000` on the motor retainer is the doctrine's own illustrative default (the evaluation's foundation B says so); `force_N 3331` on the rings is a certified peak the app itself verified against its table. Both are `UNWITNESSED`, because that is a statement about geometry, not about truth. The motor note is the one place the tool reaches for data, and it says "a data witness, not a geometric one".
- **[Certain] The synthetic retry is synthetic.** `motor-retainer.retry.scad` was made by this folder's `build-data.mjs`, and the README says so wherever it appears. No recorded PartForge run keeps a failing attempt beside its passing one (`design()` keeps attempts in memory), so there is no real attempt pair in the checkout; retainer → retainer-fixed is a hand correction, and ring1 → ring6 are two independent runs, labelled as such.
- **[Likely] The rules are a reasonable first set, not a proven one.** Unit-compatible pairs only, within the declared tolerances, with walls requiring axial overlap and counts requiring a size-compatible diameter: on the fixtures this produced no coincidental match (ring1's eight bolts never witness its 8.0 mm plate, nor the plate its bolts; the centering ring's four lightening holes never witness its four M4 bolts), but it did produce one witness I cannot defend physically, the endcap's 2.77 "wall", which is why the caveat exists. Whether the rules hold on files from other models is untested here.
- **[Likely] Direction is the right axis for a retry verdict, and the record is consistent with it.** In `docs/runs/2026-10-06-gemini-flight-x5/`, runs 1–5 failed attempt 1, four of them on a missing bolt grade and run 3 on a declared hole pattern that did not match the drawn holes (a mismatch the CMM finds by measuring the mesh); run 6 passed on attempt 1. (The runs README's "5 of 6" also counts a 2026-10-05 run that is not in the directory.) Of the four rings kept here, ring1 and ring4 failed attempt 1 on the grade, ring3 on the holes, and ring6 passed first time. Adding the grade is the expected correction, and the replay test shows it passes without a finding. What the record does not contain is a single retry that changed only a number, so the false-positive rate of the suspicious verdict on real retries is unmeasured. A model that rewrites one comment or re-indents the code defeats the byte-identity test trivially; the comment stripping and whitespace normalisation cover the common cases, a semantic equivalence of OpenSCAD code they do not.
- **[Speculative] What a maintainer would do with the invention surface.** My guess is that it becomes a line on the manufacturing sheet ("3 of the 9 check inputs have no geometric witness", the bulkhead's count) and a reason to require `inputs_ref` for them. That is a design question for PartForge, not something this tool settles.
- **Rough.** Station arithmetic across two features (the endcap's 25.4 − 8.0 = 17.4 face plate) is deliberately not a rule, because pairwise station differences across ten features produce too many coincidences; the fixture that needs it comes out unwitnessed with the nearest candidate named. Materials named in text cannot be ordered (no `FLIGHT_MATERIALS` excerpt), so any material change with identical geometry is suspicious. The thread-size direction uses the fastener excerpt's major diameters; a size outside it cannot be ordered. Comment stripping assumes `//` and `/* */` never occur inside OpenSCAD string literals. Loads rows are matched by check and name, so a retry that renames a row shows as one row removed (suspicious) and one added. The line diff is an LCS count, not a readable diff. The browser page cannot read the `.scad` snapshots from `file://`, so its "load sample" fills in a minimal declaration rather than the real file. The PartForge vocabulary (`certain / likely / recall`) is printed on witnesses from the tables; the bank's `[Certain]/[Likely]/[Speculative]` is used only in this README.
- **Fidelity.** An idea-bank prototype. It has not been through PartForge's geometry gate (no file here was compiled or CMM-measured in this sandbox), its data-provenance pipeline (`tools/db/`, `tests/flight-db*.test.mjs`) or a skeptic review, and it is not wired into the retry loop or the doctrine.

**Deviations from the judged proposal, and why.**

- A fourth class, `POLICY`, for `sf_min` on the checks that read it. Lowering the threshold is the cheapest fix of all and must be diffed; counting it as an invented *physical* input would overstate the invention surface.
- Inputs read by name (`grade`, `material`, `internal_material`, `shear_plane`) are classified, because they move the same strengths and areas as the numeric keys; gaps and keys not read are listed beside the classes, because the evaluator's defaults are numbers nobody declared.
- `size` (a string) is classified, through excerpts of the two fastener tables, and `grade` through the evaluator's `GRADE_SY` line. The proposal said "no tables"; without them `n 2` on the bulkhead would have matched three hole rows with two positions each, and nothing could name the right one. The excerpts keep `confidence` and the `recall` row is printed as recall.
- Walls require axial overlap; the proposal's "(od − bore)/2" would have produced a 15.5 mm "wall" between the endcap's flange and a groove 6 mm above it. Walls and radii on a bore enclosed by an OD section carry the groove caveat.
- The diff judges direction instead of flagging every moved key, so the recorded correction (a missing grade added) is not a finding.
- One-file mode is informational (exit 0); the proposal's "exit 0/1/2" is kept with 1 reserved for pair-mode findings. The count mismatch the proposal wanted "reported" is reported inside the `n` row (unwitnessed, with the clearance-size rows named), not as a separate finding.
- Fixtures: nosecone dropped (no loads rows), `retainer.scad` added (the only real before/after pair), rings 2 and 5 dropped, ring 6 added (the one first-attempt pass in the record); the motor row, the fastener excerpts and `GRADE_SY` added.
- The evaluator citation is `:5745–5828` with the per-check lines given above; the proposal's `:5779–5845` starts at the bolt-shear branch.
