# Stack Ledger
> A one-dimensional tolerance stack over PartForge's own fit, stock and expansion tables, run through the shipped `dbFit()` and `dbFitThermal()` verbatim: the worst case can FAIL or CLEAR but never PASS (nothing here measures the gap), RSS is a footnote (two ±0.05 contributors give 0.0707, 29 % narrower, and no basis at N=2), and both shipped templates stack to REFUSED as written, because their mating half is a NOTE with no tolerance, their finish is "bare or type II", and a fibreglass bore has no hoop CTE.

**Kind:** prototype
**For:** PartForge
**Run:** open `ideas/06-stack-ledger/index.html` in a browser (plain classic scripts, so `file://` works; no build, no network; `?example=<id>` preloads one of the nine worked stacks). Command line, from the bank root: `node ideas/06-stack-ledger/stack-ledger.mjs --example bulkhead-declared` (`--list` names the examples; a path to your own stack JSON instead of `--example`; `--json` for the result object; `--repo /path/to/Partforge` reads the three tables live from a checkout, and `--repo … --embedded` reads the app's own embedded copy of them from its `index.html`; `--help`; exit 0 CLEARS, 1 FAIL, 2 refused or not gated, 3 usage). Tests: `node --test ideas/06-stack-ledger/stack.test.mjs` (36 tests, Node built-ins only, about 7 s) and `node ideas/06-stack-ledger/ui-check.mjs` (71 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `--shots` writes screenshots). `node ideas/06-stack-ledger/build-data.mjs /path/to/Partforge` re-derives the two derived snapshots; `--check` says whether they are stale.

## What it is

You describe one gap as a signed chain of contributors, in JSON, and the page (or the command line, same kernel) prints a ledger:

- the **worst-case** minimum and maximum of the gap, and a verdict against the requirement you declared, on that alone: FAIL when the worst case leaves the requirement, CLEARS when it stays inside. CLEARS is a screening verdict, never a pass: every contributor is declared or looked up and nothing here measures the gap, so the ledger says so and names any tool default or recall row the clear rests on;
- underneath, the **RSS** band as information: centred on the sum of the mid-limit means (not on the nominal sum), with its N and the words "no statistical basis below N=4" whenever that is true, and how much narrower it is than the worst case of the same contributors;
- a **thermal** shift, when declared, evaluated at both ends of the temperature range through PartForge's own `dbFitThermal()`, entering the worst case and kept out of the RSS; the RSS band is then also printed with that shift added at both ends, so it can be set beside the worst case like for like;
- a per-contributor table with each one's absolute limits, signed band, and where its numbers came from, with the confidence word the data carries (`likely` or `recall` for a table row, `source` / `user` / `default` for a declaration);
- **refusals**: a contributor the tables cannot source is refused with a code, the table's own reason, and what would help. A stack with any refusal prints no gap at all, because a partial sum would be a placeholder number; so does a stack whose sums overflow.

Six kinds of contributor:

| kind | what it reads | limits |
|---|---|---|
| `declared` | a dimension you type: `nominal_mm`, `plus_mm`/`minus_mm` (or `tol_mm`), PartForge's `tol_src` and `tol_ref`, `dir` | nominal − minus … nominal + plus |
| `fit` | an ISO 286 fit, hole `["H",7]` over shaft `["g",6]` at `size_mm`, through `dbFit()` | the clearance band; nominal 0 |
| `iso` | one ISO 286 feature, an H hole or a lettered shaft, through `dbFit()` | that feature's limits |
| `stock` | an as-supplied surface of a `stock.json` row: bar `od`, plate `thickness`, tube `od` or `id` | `dim_min_mm`/`dim_max_mm` or `id_min_mm`/`id_max_mm` |
| `coating` | a build-up per surface, `per_side_mm` as a value or `[min, max]`, on 1 or 2 surfaces, with a `src` | surfaces × per side; closes the gap by default |
| `thermal` | `(α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT` over `dT_K: [low, high]` through `dbFitThermal()`; the ten laminate, polymer and graphite rows whose table value is a placeholder are refused unless `hoop_cte` supplies a value with its source | the two ends of the range; nominal 0 |

Nine worked stacks ship, picked from a menu. Two are PartForge's own templates exactly as they declare themselves (`tests/templates/bulkhead.scad`, `coupler.scad`): both are REFUSED, three and two contributors respectively. One is the bulkhead again with the missing declarations supplied (the coupler band labelled illustrative): with the Type II anodise the template's `mfg.finish` names, it interferes by 0.030 mm, and the RSS band would have told you 0.008 mm of clearance; bare, it clears at 20 °C, but across the pad-to-altitude range only if the fibreglass coupler's hoop CTE lies between 16.8 and 25.9 µm/m·K, a number nobody has measured. One is a 6061 plug in a 304 bore at Ø101.6 H7/g6, FAIL by 0.0008 mm at the hot end of the pad-to-altitude range. One is the same plug in a G10 coupler, refused twice: the G10 expansion figure is an in-plane placeholder, and an H7 bore in a composite coupler is a fit class PartForge's own doctrine rules out. One is a 1/4 in cold-finished bar used unmachined as a pin in an H7 hole, FAIL both ways on the row's +0.038/−0.076 band. One is a 54 mm motor tube in a centring ring, H8/f7, CLEARS. One is a recall stock row claimed as a cited source, refused. One is the evaluation's two ±0.05 teaching example. `example.txt` is the command line's real output for eight runs over seven of them, one against the live checkout's tables and one against the app's embedded copy.

Nothing here is measured on a mesh, and the page says so on every ledger: every contributor is declared or looked up, and the mating half is by definition not in any one file.

## Why I made it

The evaluation's build-first list, item 5 (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md:254-255`): "Tolerance stack-up (idea 30) … `fits.json` derives IT grades and fundamental deviations from the standard's own formulas with five disagreeing values withheld; `stock.json` carries asymmetric supply tolerances; `dbFitThermal()` … already computes `(α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT` with a confidence label — use it, do not write a second one-sided thermal term. Gate on worst case; print RSS as information only, centred on the sum of mid-limit means rather than on G." The appendix row (`:77`) marks it BUILD NOW and "best in the group"; the method, hook, needs, guard and wrong-number risks are at `:662-669`. Two of "what the 50 missed" feed it: #7 (`:269`), the Type II anodise allowance that "silently invalidates every fit idea 30 and 38 compute", and #10 (`:272`), cold soak. In the repository: `fits.json` notes (6) anodise and (7) thermal; `dbFit()` at `index.html:4997`, `dbMaterialThermal()` at `:5014`, `dbFitThermal()` at `:5018`; in `dbHints()`, the thermal sentence at `:5465`, the fit sentence at `:5477` and its refusals at `:5480`; `parseFlight()` at `:4672`, `mfgSheet()` at `:6671`, `icdSheet()` at `:6741`. PartForge has the single-fit limits and the single thermal term; nothing sums contributors, nothing reads a stock tolerance into a gap, nothing sizes a coating, and there is no `stacks` declaration.

What I weighed:

- **Idea 38's pairwise assembly mode** (`:744-752`), which would resolve both halves of a joint from two files and a CMM. The evaluation sequences it after an angular datum primitive and the cross-row reference exist. This is the declared-contributor half the evaluation says can stand on its own.
- **PASS on the worst case.** The proposal printed PASS when the worst case fitted, and so did this deposit's first round. The evaluation's Needs line for idea 30 (`:666`) asks for "a screening-only row type that can never print PASS", because there is no assembly mode and "nothing can measure the resulting gap"; idea 38's guard (`:752`) lets a joint row print PASS only "when BOTH mating dimensions are tol_src 'user' or 'source' with a measurement cited — otherwise COMPUTED". A stack here can never meet that, so the verdict is FAIL or CLEARS, and a CLEARS lists what it rests on.
- **Resolving contributors by name to the file's `critical` / `od` / `bores` entries** (`:664`, "so the stack cannot disagree with the drawing"). That needs the `stacks` array in `parseFlight()` and lives in the app. Here the contributors are explicit JSON; the worked examples read the templates' FLIGHT blocks and NOTE lines from the snapshots instead of retyping them, and a test checks that they do.
- **Writing the thermal term** (the proposal's `ΔD = (α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT`). The evaluation says not to, twice. So the three DB functions are copied out of `index.html` by text marker and run unmodified; the kernel wraps them, it does not rewrite them.
- **Monte Carlo, Bender, a 2-D loop.** All ruled out at `:664`; Bender's 1.5 factor would be a number I retype from memory, which this bank forbids.
- **The cold-soak O-ring term** (#10). Not buildable from the repository: `orings_as568.json` at `0974a62` has no low-temperature text at all (see the findings), so the kernel refuses an elastomer contributor by having no kind for it rather than inventing one.

This won because the arithmetic is closed-form, the data already ships with its confidence, the one function the evaluation names is run as written, and the thing it demonstrates on PartForge's own templates (that neither can be stacked as declared, and that "bare or type II" is a 0 to 0.050 mm ambiguity on a 0.10 mm design clearance) is a finding, not a feature.

## How it works

**The chain.** A stack is `{gap_name, contributors: [...], require: {min_mm, max_mm}}`, units mm. Each contributor resolves to absolute limits `[low_i, high_i]` about a nominal `n_i` and carries a direction `s_i = ±1` (+1 opens the gap, −1 closes it; required for `declared`, `iso` and `stock`, +1 by default for `fit` and `thermal`, −1 by default for `coating`). The signed band is `[min(s·low, s·high), max(s·low, s·high)]`. Then

- worst case: `G_min = Σ signed low_i`, `G_max = Σ signed high_i`, `G_nom = Σ s_i·n_i`; the verdict compares `G_min` and `G_max` with `require` and nothing else: FAIL outside, CLEARS inside;
- RSS, over the manufacturing kinds only (`declared`, `fit`, `iso`, `stock`, `coating`): half-band `√Σ((high_i − low_i)/2)²`, centred on `Σ signed mid_i`; the ledger prints the centre's offset from `G_nom`, the ratio of the RSS width to the worst-case width of the same contributors (and that worst-case band), N, and the N=4 caveat. The "false comfort" note compares the RSS low end with the worst-case minimum of those same contributors, so the deterministic thermal shift is never reported as statistical narrowing;
- thermal: the signed bands of the `thermal` contributors summed separately and printed as a deterministic shift. Since `ΔD` is linear in `ΔT`, its extremes over a declared range are at the ends, so evaluating `dbFitThermal()` at `dT_K[0]` and `dT_K[1]` is exact. With a thermal term the RSS band is also printed plus that shift at both ends (`RSS low + thermal low … RSS high + thermal high`), the band to set beside the worst case, and that is the dashed bar on the number line;
- any limit or sum that is not a finite number (an overflow) refuses the contributor or the stack with `invalid_input`; no verdict is printed on it.

The proposal wrote the worst case as `Σ dir·low` and `Σ dir·high`; that is wrong for a −1 contributor with asymmetric limits (it would give 0.4 where the answer is 0 in the test's example), so the kernel uses the signed band and a test pins the difference.

**What each kind reads.** `fit` and `iso` call the verbatim `dbFit(mm, 'H', g_h, letter, g_s)` and take its `clearance`, `hole` or `shaft`. When it returns `null` the kernel diagnoses why in the table's own words, the way `dbHints()` does at `index.html:5480`: above 500 mm or at 1 mm and below, off the table (`fits.json` LIMITS (1)); p/r/s/t/u, the delta-rule letters the table deliberately omits (LIMITS (2)); `js`, which needs no table (LIMITS (4)) and is refused with the IT/2 value to declare instead; a hole letter other than H; a grade the step does not carry; and the five values withheld at 1–3 mm (IT9–IT12 and the n deviation), refused by lookup in the table's `disputed` list, quoting its "formula gives 22 um, the published table gives 25 um". `k` outside grades 4–7 is accepted with zero deviation and a note, as `dbFit()` enforces.

`stock` reads a row of the excerpt (ten rows; all 192 with `--repo`), takes `dim_min_mm`/`dim_max_mm` or `id_min_mm`/`id_max_mm`, prints `tolerance_confidence` and the row `confidence`, carries the row's `disputed` entry and, for tubes, the `ovality_individual_dia_tol_mm` figure as a note, and refuses `tol_src: "source"` on any row whose tolerance or row confidence is `recall`. Run over the app's embedded copy of the table (`--embedded`), four of those columns are missing (see the findings); the kernel then says so in a note at each gap instead of failing or guessing: the row confidence prints in place of `tolerance_confidence` (never the stronger of the two at `0974a62`: 48 rows are recall by row and likely by tolerance, none the other way round), the nominal ID is `dim_mm − 2·wall_mm` (equal to `id_nominal_mm` on all 113 tube and pipe rows of the full table), and the ovality figure and the disputed values are pointed to in the file.

`coating` needs a positive `per_side_mm`; without one it is refused, and the refusal quotes `fits.json` note (6), "roughly 0.005 to 0.025 mm per surface on a Type II coating", as prose to cite, not a row.

`thermal` calls the verbatim `dbFitThermal()` through a one-call material override so a user-supplied `hoop_cte` with a `src` flows through the shipped formula and prints as `user`. Without one, ten of the 21 rows are refused as either half of a joint (`cte_placeholder`), because the table itself does not offer their value as a number to gate a fit on: its LIMITS (1) says "the laminates and polymers vary so much with layup, weave, resin, fill and print direction that a single number is a placeholder". The refusal quotes that and the row's own note: the four laminates (`G10-FR4` in-plane only, `CF-laminate` along the fibres, `Fiberglass-laminate` "Hoop and axial α differ", `Phenolic` "Never machine a fit to this number"), the two printed polymers whose notes name the print direction (`Ultem-9085`, `CF-Nylon`), `Graphite` ("α depends on grain direction"), and the other three polymers (`PEEK`, `PA12-SLS`, `Delrin-POM`) under LIMITS (1). What is accepted is exactly the eleven rows PartForge's own `tests/flight-db.test.mjs:143` treats as metals, and every row its `:146` says "depends on layup or print direction" is in the refused set. When one side is a user value and the other a recall row, the contributor stays `recall`. A thermal contributor that names a composite or phenolic tube (the four laminates) also refuses every `fit`, and every `iso` feature on that side, in the same stack (`fit_on_composite`), quoting `fits.json` LIMITS (5), "THESE ARE FOR MACHINED METAL. A composite or phenolic tube is not round, not constant, and not to an IT grade", and PartForge's doctrine at `index.html:1454`, "for anything mating with a composite or phenolic tube, do not use a fit class at all". A hoop CTE lifts the first refusal, not the second; the way through is a measured bore as a `declared` contributor.

`declared` mirrors `parseFlight()` (`index.html:4660-4700`): `tol_src` is `source | user | default`, any other word is `default`, and `source` without a `tol_ref` is demoted to `default` with a note. A nominal with no tolerance, or ±0, is refused: "a nominal is not a limit".

**Where every number comes from.** `snapshots/fits.json` and `snapshots/materials_thermal.json` are whole-file copies of `data/tables/` at `0974a62` with `tools/snapshot.mjs` sidecars; `snapshots/bulkhead.scad` and `snapshots/coupler.scad` likewise. `snapshots/stock_excerpt.json` is ten rows of `stock.json`, verbatim, by key, with the table's `license_note`, `columns` and `notes` (derived by `build-data.mjs`, sidecar pointing at `stock.json`). `snapshots/partforge.js` is the module the page and the tests load: `index.html` lines 4994–5024 verbatim (the comment that introduces `dbFit()` through the closing brace of `dbFitThermal()`, found by text marker, sha256 recorded), a `dbRows()` shim that serves the folder's tables, the three JSON snapshots embedded, the two templates' header comments, parameters and FLIGHT blocks, and the commit and sha256 of every source (sidecar pointing at `index.html`). The shim is not the shipped `dbRows()`: it serves the full table files, where the app serves the projection `tools/db/embed.mjs` writes; it spreads a table's hoisted `shared` fields back into each row as `index.html:4992` does, so `--embedded` can hand it that projection, and its comment says all this. The kernel never contains a table value. The examples take the bulkhead OD (146.20 ± 0.05, `tol_src default`) from the template's FLIGHT `critical` entry, the coupler OD (149.30 ± 0.05) from its `od` entry, the assumed coupler and airframe IDs (146.30, 149.60) from the NOTE lines by regular expression, the finish strings from `mfg.finish`, and the 6061 expansion from the table; the hoop-CTE window in the bulkhead example's description is computed from those. The numbers that are this idea's own are labelled as such where they appear: the illustrative ±0.03 mm coupler band in the second bulkhead example, the ±0.05 bore in the recall example, the `require` values of the examples, the pad-to-altitude range `[−60, +20]` K (the 40 °C pad and −40 °C altitude of `materials_thermal.json`'s note, relative to ISO 286's 20 °C reference; `dbHints()` uses −60 for the drop), and the N=4 threshold under which RSS is called baseless, a rule of thumb [Likely]. The page's footer lists the same.

**Worked numbers the page shows (all recomputed by the tests).** Bulkhead with declarations: coupler 146.270/146.330 (+), bulkhead 146.150/146.250 (−), anodise 0.010 to 0.050 diametral (−): worst case −0.030 to 0.170, nominal 0.100; RSS over N=3: centre 0.070 (−0.030 from the nominal sum, the coating's mean) ± 0.062 → 0.008 to 0.132, 38 % narrower than the worst case of the same three, and its low end 0.038 above their worst-case minimum; FAIL because the minimum is 0.030 below zero. Drop the coating and the minimum is +0.020: CLEARS at 20 °C, resting on the template's tool-default bulkhead tolerance. The thermal term was left out because the coupler's hoop CTE is unknown, and it decides the bare case: `0.020 + (α − 23.6)·10⁻⁶·146.2·ΔT ≥ 0` at both ΔT = +20 and −60 holds only for α from 16.76 to 25.88 µm/m·K. At the ends of the evaluation's 10–30 hoop range (`:751`) the bare minimum is −0.0198 (α 10) and −0.0361 (α 30); at G10's in-plane 14 it is −0.0081; at the fibreglass row's 22 it is +0.0153. Plug in a 304 bore: H7/g6 at Ø101.6 is 0.012 to 0.069 (IT7 35 µm, IT6 22 µm, g −12 µm); `dbFitThermal(101.6, '304', '6061-T6', dT)` is (17.3 − 23.6) × 10⁻⁶ × 101.6 × dT, −0.0128 at +20 K and +0.0384 at −60 K; worst case −0.0008 to 0.1074, FAIL; the RSS at N=1 is the fit's own band, 0 % narrower, and plus the thermal shift it is −0.0008 to 0.1074 again, so there is no false-comfort note. Bar as pin: hole 6.350/6.365 (IT7 15 µm), bar 6.274/6.388; gap −0.038 to 0.091, the maximum 1.8 times the 0.050 allowed. Motor tube in ring: H8/f7 at Ø54 is 0.030 to 0.106 (IT8 46, IT7 30, f −30); 6061 on 6061 is exactly zero; CLEARS. Two ±0.05: worst ±0.100, RSS 0.0707, 29 % narrower. The H7/g6 mean offset the evaluation calls "roughly 0.03 mm" is 0.0295 mm at Ø50 (its own worked size) and 0.0465 mm at Ø150; the proposal applied the 0.03 to Ø150, and the README and tests now say which is which. `materials_thermal.json`'s note says a 6061 plug in a G10 coupler "gains about 0.1 mm" from a 40 °C pad to −40 °C; `dbFitThermal(101.6, 'G10-FR4', '6061-T6', −80)` gives 0.0780 mm, and the kernel refuses that pair anyway until a hoop CTE is supplied, so the figure is pinned through the function directly.

**Page and command line.** `stack.js` is pure (no DOM, no clock, no network), loads as `window.StackLedger` and with `require()`, and exports `stackUp()` and `ledgerText()`; the `<details>` at the foot of the page and the CLI print the same text. `app.js` draws the verdict, four figures, an inline-SVG number line (thick bar worst case, dashed bar RSS plus any thermal shift, shaded requirement window, ticks at zero and the nominal; hidden if the requirement window is too wide to draw), the RSS and thermal lines, the refusals, the table and the notes, and recomputes as you edit the JSON; bad JSON shows a parse error and keeps the last ledger. The command line is `stack-ledger.mjs`, Node built-ins only, with `headless/cli.mjs`'s exit codes (0 pass, 1 fail, 2 refused, 3 usage) except that 0 is CLEARS, since a stack never passes. `--repo` swaps the snapshot tables for a checkout's through the module's `useTables()`, and `--embedded` swaps in the FLIGHT_DB_DATA literal between `index.html`'s DB-DATA markers (lines 4875–4893 at `0974a62`); the verbatim functions are the same either way. Tests assert that the nine examples give identical ledgers through `--repo` and identical verdicts, limits and refusals through `--embedded`.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The fit and thermal numbers are PartForge's.** `dbFit()`, `dbMaterialThermal()` and `dbFitThermal()` run as the repository wrote them, from text compared character for character with the checkout, over tables copied byte for byte, and they reproduce the numbers the repository's own test file publishes. The kernel adds the chain, the signs, the RSS line and the refusals; it adds no table value.
- **[Certain] The worst-case arithmetic.** Signed bands summed; the hand-worked chains agree to 10⁻⁹ mm; a −1 contributor is handled correctly where the proposal's formula was not; an overflow is refused, not gated.
- **[Certain] Nothing here prints PASS.** A stack clears or fails on its worst case and is labelled a screening verdict, as the evaluation's `:666` asks; a CLEARS names the tool defaults and recall rows it rests on. Whether a club reads "CLEARS (screening)" as weaker than "PASS" is [Speculative].
- **[Certain] The RSS line is information and never gates,** is centred on the mid-limit means, prints its N, excludes the thermal shift, and is compared with the worst case of the same contributors (with the thermal shift added back at both ends when there is one). [Likely] N=4 as the line below which RSS has no statistical basis is the rule of thumb the evaluation's wrong-number risk 1 (`:668`) implies ("at N=2 or 3 the RSS number has no statistical basis at all"), not a theorem; the real conditions are independence, centring and normality, which a student biasing every cut to the safe side violates at any N, and the page says so beside the number.
- **[Certain] What is refused is what the tables refuse, with the tables' words:** the five withheld values, the delta-rule letters, sizes off the table, every expansion figure the table calls a placeholder (LIMITS (1)) or ties to a direction (its row notes) as either half of a thermal pair, a fit class on a composite or phenolic tube (LIMITS (5) and the doctrine), recall rows as sources, coatings without a size, nominals without limits.
