## What it is

You describe one gap as a signed chain of contributors, in JSON, and the page (or the command line, same kernel) prints a ledger:

- the **worst-case** minimum and maximum of the gap, and PASS or FAIL against the requirement you declared, on that alone;
- underneath, the **RSS** band as information: centred on the sum of the mid-limit means (not on the nominal sum), with its N and the words "no statistical basis below N=4" whenever that is true, and how much narrower than the worst case it is;
- a **thermal** shift, when declared, evaluated at both ends of the temperature range through PartForge's own `dbFitThermal()`, entering the worst case and kept out of the RSS;
- a per-contributor table with each one's absolute limits, signed band, and where its numbers came from, with the confidence word the data carries (`likely` or `recall` for a table row, `source` / `user` / `default` for a declaration);
- **refusals**: a contributor the tables cannot source is refused with a code, the table's own reason, and what would help. A stack with any refusal prints no gap at all, because a partial sum would be a placeholder number.

Six kinds of contributor:

| kind | what it reads | limits |
|---|---|---|
| `declared` | a dimension you type: `nominal_mm`, `plus_mm`/`minus_mm` (or `tol_mm`), PartForge's `tol_src` and `tol_ref`, `dir` | nominal − minus … nominal + plus |
| `fit` | an ISO 286 fit, hole `["H",7]` over shaft `["g",6]` at `size_mm`, through `dbFit()` | the clearance band; nominal 0 |
| `iso` | one ISO 286 feature, an H hole or a lettered shaft, through `dbFit()` | that feature's limits |
| `stock` | an as-supplied surface of a `stock.json` row: bar `od`, plate `thickness`, tube `od` or `id` | `dim_min_mm`/`dim_max_mm` or `id_min_mm`/`id_max_mm` |
| `coating` | a build-up per surface, `per_side_mm` as a value or `[min, max]`, on 1 or 2 surfaces, with a `src` | surfaces × per side; closes the gap by default |
| `thermal` | `(α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT` over `dT_K: [low, high]` through `dbFitThermal()`; `hoop_cte` lets you supply a laminate's hoop value with its source | the two ends of the range; nominal 0 |

Nine worked stacks ship, picked from a menu. Two are PartForge's own templates exactly as they declare themselves (`tests/templates/bulkhead.scad`, `coupler.scad`): both are REFUSED, three and two contributors respectively. One is the bulkhead again with the missing declarations supplied (the coupler band labelled illustrative): bare, the worst case clears; with the Type II anodise the template's `mfg.finish` names, it interferes by 0.030 mm, and the RSS band would have told you 0.008 mm of clearance. One is a 6061 plug in a 304 bore at Ø101.6 H7/g6, FAIL by 0.0008 mm at the hot end of the pad-to-altitude range. One is the same plug in a G10 coupler, refused for want of a hoop CTE. One is a 1/4 in cold-finished bar used unmachined as a pin in an H7 hole, FAIL both ways on the row's +0.038/−0.076 band. One is a 54 mm motor tube in a centring ring, H8/f7, PASS. One is a recall stock row claimed as a cited source, refused. One is the evaluation's two ±0.05 teaching example. `example.txt` is the command line's real output for six of them, including one run against the live checkout.

Nothing here is measured on a mesh, and the page says so on every ledger: every contributor is declared or looked up, and the mating half is by definition not in any one file.

## Why I made it

The evaluation's build-first list, item 5 (`docs/roadmap-evaluation.md:254-255`): "Tolerance stack-up (idea 30) … `fits.json` derives IT grades and fundamental deviations from the standard's own formulas with five disagreeing values withheld; `stock.json` carries asymmetric supply tolerances; `dbFitThermal()` … already computes `(α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT` with a confidence label — use it, do not write a second one-sided thermal term. Gate on worst case; print RSS as information only, centred on the sum of mid-limit means rather than on G." The appendix row (`:77`) marks it BUILD NOW and "best in the group"; the method, hook, guard and wrong-number risks are at `:662-669`. Two of "what the 50 missed" feed it: #7 (`:269`), the Type II anodise allowance that "silently invalidates every fit idea 30 and 38 compute", and #10 (`:272`), cold soak. In the repository: `fits.json` notes (6) anodise and (7) thermal; `dbFit()` at `index.html:4997`, `dbMaterialThermal()` at `:5014`, `dbFitThermal()` at `:5018`; the `dbHints()` fit sentence and its refusals at `:5465` and `:5480`; `parseFlight()` at `:4672`, `mfgSheet()` at `:6671`, `icdSheet()` at `:6741`. PartForge has the single-fit limits and the single thermal term; nothing sums contributors, nothing reads a stock tolerance into a gap, nothing sizes a coating, and there is no `stacks` declaration.

What I weighed:

- **Idea 38's pairwise assembly mode** (`:744-752`), which would resolve both halves of a joint from two files and a CMM. The evaluation sequences it after an angular datum primitive and the cross-row reference exist. This is the declared-contributor half the evaluation says can stand on its own.
- **Resolving contributors by name to the file's `critical` / `od` / `bores` entries** (`:664`, "so the stack cannot disagree with the drawing"). That needs the `stacks` array in `parseFlight()` and lives in the app. Here the contributors are explicit JSON; the worked examples read the templates' FLIGHT blocks and NOTE lines from the snapshots instead of retyping them, and a test checks that they do.
- **Writing the thermal term** (the proposal's `ΔD = (α_hole − α_shaft)·D·ΔT`). The evaluation says not to, twice. So the three DB functions are copied out of `index.html` by text marker and run unmodified; the kernel wraps them, it does not rewrite them.
- **Monte Carlo, Bender, a 2-D loop.** All ruled out at `:664`; Bender's 1.5 factor would be a number I retype from memory, which this bank forbids.
- **The cold-soak O-ring term** (#10). Not buildable from the repository: `orings_as568.json` at `0974a62` has no low-temperature text at all (see the findings), so the kernel refuses an elastomer contributor by having no kind for it rather than inventing one.

This won because the arithmetic is closed-form, the data already ships with its confidence, the one function the evaluation names is run as written, and the thing it demonstrates on PartForge's own templates (that neither can be stacked as declared, and that "bare or type II" is a 0 to 0.050 mm ambiguity on a 0.10 mm design clearance) is a finding, not a feature.

## How it works

**The chain.** A stack is `{gap_name, contributors: [...], require: {min_mm, max_mm}}`, units mm. Each contributor resolves to absolute limits `[low_i, high_i]` about a nominal `n_i` and carries a direction `s_i = ±1` (+1 opens the gap, −1 closes it; required for `declared`, `iso` and `stock`, +1 by default for `fit` and `thermal`, −1 by default for `coating`). The signed band is `[min(s·low, s·high), max(s·low, s·high)]`. Then

- worst case: `G_min = Σ signed low_i`, `G_max = Σ signed high_i`, `G_nom = Σ s_i·n_i`; the verdict compares `G_min` and `G_max` with `require` and nothing else;
- RSS, over the manufacturing kinds only (`declared`, `fit`, `iso`, `stock`, `coating`): half-band `√Σ((high_i − low_i)/2)²`, centred on `Σ signed mid_i`; the ledger prints the centre's offset from `G_nom`, the ratio of the RSS width to the worst-case width of the same contributors, N, and the N=4 caveat;
- thermal: the signed bands of the `thermal` contributors summed separately and printed as a deterministic shift. Since `ΔD` is linear in `ΔT`, its extremes over a declared range are at the ends, so evaluating `dbFitThermal()` at `dT_K[0]` and `dT_K[1]` is exact.

The proposal wrote the worst case as `Σ dir·low` and `Σ dir·high`; that is wrong for a −1 contributor with asymmetric limits (it would give 0.4 where the answer is 0 in the test's example), so the kernel uses the signed band and a test pins the difference.

**What each kind reads.** `fit` and `iso` call the verbatim `dbFit(mm, 'H', g_h, letter, g_s)` and take its `clearance`, `hole` or `shaft`. When it returns `null` the kernel diagnoses why in the table's own words, the way `dbHints()` does at `index.html:5480`: above 500 mm or at 1 mm and below, off the table (`fits.json` LIMITS (1)); p/r/s/t/u, the delta-rule letters the table deliberately omits (LIMITS (2)); `js`, which needs no table (LIMITS (4)) and is refused with the IT/2 value to declare instead; a hole letter other than H; a grade the step does not carry; and the five values withheld at 1–3 mm (IT9–IT12 and the n deviation), refused by lookup in the table's `disputed` list, quoting its "formula gives 22 um, the published table gives 25 um". `k` outside grades 4–7 is accepted with zero deviation and a note, as `dbFit()` enforces. `stock` reads a row of the excerpt (ten rows; all 192 with `--repo`), takes `dim_min_mm`/`dim_max_mm` or `id_min_mm`/`id_max_mm`, prints `tolerance_confidence` and the row `confidence`, carries the row's `disputed` entry and, for tubes, the `ovality_individual_dia_tol_mm` figure as a note, and refuses `tol_src: "source"` on any row whose confidence is `recall`. `coating` needs a positive `per_side_mm`; without one it is refused, and the refusal quotes `fits.json` note (6), "roughly 0.005 to 0.025 mm per surface on a Type II coating", as prose to cite, not a row. `thermal` calls the verbatim `dbFitThermal()` through a one-call material override so a user-supplied hoop CTE with a `src` flows through the shipped formula and prints as `user`; without it, the four rows whose own notes say their value is in-plane, along-fibre, or not for fits (`G10-FR4`, `CF-laminate`, `Fiberglass-laminate`, `Phenolic`) are refused as either half of a joint, quoting the note. `declared` mirrors `parseFlight()` (`index.html:4660-4700`): `tol_src` is `source | user | default`, any other word is `default`, and `source` without a `tol_ref` is demoted to `default` with a note. A nominal with no tolerance, or ±0, is refused: "a nominal is not a limit".

**Where every number comes from.** `snapshots/fits.json` and `snapshots/materials_thermal.json` are whole-file copies of `data/tables/` at `0974a62` with `tools/snapshot.mjs` sidecars; `snapshots/bulkhead.scad` and `snapshots/coupler.scad` likewise. `snapshots/stock_excerpt.json` is ten rows of `stock.json`, verbatim, by key, with the table's `license_note`, `columns` and `notes` (derived by `build-data.mjs`, sidecar pointing at `stock.json`). `snapshots/partforge.js` is the module the page and the tests load: `index.html` lines 4994–5024 verbatim (the comment that introduces `dbFit()` through the closing brace of `dbFitThermal()`, found by text marker, sha256 recorded), a `dbRows()` shim that serves the folder's tables, the three JSON snapshots embedded, the two templates' header comments, parameters and FLIGHT blocks, and the commit and sha256 of every source (sidecar pointing at `index.html`). The kernel never contains a table value. The examples take the bulkhead OD (146.20 ± 0.05, `tol_src default`) from the template's FLIGHT `critical` entry, the coupler OD (149.30 ± 0.05) from its `od` entry, the assumed coupler and airframe IDs (146.30, 149.60) from the NOTE lines by regular expression, and the finish strings from `mfg.finish`. The numbers that are this idea's own are labelled as such where they appear: the illustrative ±0.03 mm coupler band in the second bulkhead example, the ±0.05 bore in the recall example, the `require` values of the examples, the pad-to-altitude range `[−60, +20]` K (the 40 °C pad and −40 °C altitude of `materials_thermal.json`'s note, relative to ISO 286's 20 °C reference; `dbHints()` uses −60 for the drop), and the N=4 threshold under which RSS is called baseless, a rule of thumb [Likely].

**Worked numbers the page shows (all recomputed by the tests).** Bulkhead with declarations: coupler 146.270/146.330 (+), bulkhead 146.150/146.250 (−), anodise 0.010 to 0.050 diametral (−): worst case −0.030 to 0.170, nominal 0.100; RSS over N=3: centre 0.070 (−0.030 from the nominal sum, the coating's mean) ± 0.062 → 0.008 to 0.132, 38 % narrower than the worst case of the same three; FAIL because the minimum is 0.030 below zero; drop the coating and the minimum is +0.020, PASS. Plug in a 304 bore: H7/g6 at Ø101.6 is 0.012 to 0.069 (IT7 35 µm, IT6 22 µm, g −12 µm); `dbFitThermal(101.6, '304', '6061-T6', dT)` is (17.3 − 23.6) × 10⁻⁶ × 101.6 × dT, −0.0128 at +20 K and +0.0384 at −60 K; worst case −0.0008 to 0.1074, FAIL. Bar as pin: hole 6.350/6.365 (IT7 15 µm), bar 6.274/6.388; gap −0.038 to 0.091. Motor tube in ring: H8/f7 at Ø54 is 0.030 to 0.106 (IT8 46, IT7 30, f −30); 6061 on 6061 is exactly zero. Two ±0.05: worst ±0.100, RSS 0.0707, 29 % narrower. The H7/g6 mean offset the evaluation calls "roughly 0.03 mm" is 0.0295 mm at Ø50 (its own worked size) and 0.0465 mm at Ø150; the proposal applied the 0.03 to Ø150, and the README and tests now say which is which. `materials_thermal.json`'s note says a 6061 plug in a G10 coupler "gains about 0.1 mm" from a 40 °C pad to −40 °C; `dbFitThermal(101.6, 'G10-FR4', '6061-T6', −80)` gives 0.0780 mm, and the kernel refuses that pair anyway until a hoop CTE is supplied, so the figure is pinned through the function directly.

**Page and command line.** `stack.js` is pure (no DOM, no clock, no network), loads as `window.StackLedger` and with `require()`, and exports `stackUp()` and `ledgerText()`; the `<details>` at the foot of the page and the CLI print the same text. `app.js` draws the verdict, four figures, an inline-SVG number line (thick bar worst case, dashed bar RSS, shaded requirement window, ticks at zero and the nominal), the RSS and thermal lines, the refusals, the table and the notes, and recomputes as you edit the JSON; bad JSON shows a parse error and keeps the last ledger. The command line is `stack-ledger.mjs`, Node built-ins only, with exit codes copied from `headless/cli.mjs` (0 pass, 1 fail, 2 refused, 3 usage), and `--repo` swaps the snapshot tables for a checkout's through the module's `useTables()`; the verbatim functions are the same either way, and a test asserts the nine examples give identical ledgers both ways.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The fit and thermal numbers are PartForge's.** `dbFit()`, `dbMaterialThermal()` and `dbFitThermal()` run as the repository wrote them, from text compared character for character with the checkout, over tables copied byte for byte, and they reproduce the numbers the repository's own test file publishes. The kernel adds the chain, the signs, the RSS line and the refusals; it adds no table value.
- **[Certain] The worst-case arithmetic.** Signed bands summed; the hand-worked chains agree to 10⁻⁹ mm; a −1 contributor is handled correctly where the proposal's formula was not.
- **[Certain] The RSS line is information and never gates,** is centred on the mid-limit means, prints its N, and excludes the thermal shift. [Likely] N=4 as the line below which RSS has no statistical basis is the rule of thumb the evaluation's wrong-number risk 1 (`:668`) implies ("at N=2 or 3 the RSS number has no statistical basis at all"), not a theorem; the real conditions are independence, centring and normality, which a student biasing every cut to the safe side violates at any N, and the page says so beside the number.
- **[Certain] What is refused is what the tables refuse,** with the tables' words: the five withheld values, the delta-rule letters, sizes off the table, laminates as either half of a thermal pair, recall rows as sources, coatings without a size, nominals without limits. The refusal path is the only thing that makes the templates' examples honest, and it is tested at every kind.
- **[Certain] Both shipped templates stack to REFUSED as written.** That is an observation about the declarations, not a defect in the parts: a NOTE saying "measure your coupler" is the right instruction, and the stack is the place where its consequence (no limits, no gap) becomes visible.
- **[Likely] The "declarations supplied" bulkhead example is fair but chosen.** Its ±0.03 mm coupler band is illustrative and labelled so in the contributor's name, its `tol_ref` and the menu; a real fibreglass coupler measured at three clockings may be wider, which only makes the worst case worse. The lesson it carries (anodise closes a 0.10 design clearance by up to 0.050) does not depend on that band; the PASS-when-bare does.
- **[Speculative] A club would declare stacks as JSON.** The evaluation wants contributors resolved by name to the drawing's own entries so the stack cannot disagree with the drawing. This prototype makes you write the chain out, which is more honest and more work. Whether the ledger's "what would help" lines change what a student declares, I cannot test here.
- **What is rough.**
  - No Bender, no Monte Carlo, no 2-D or 3-D loop, no name-resolution to FLIGHT entries, no angular or positional contributors. One gap at a time.
  - The thermal kind is the differential diametral term only (`dbFitThermal()`'s form). An axial stack's `α·L·ΔT` per part is the one-sided term the evaluation says not to write, and there is no kind for it; declare it, with a source, as a `declared` contributor if you must.
  - `iso` holes are H only (as `dbFit()` is); `js` is refused with the value to declare rather than computed.
  - Tube `stock` rows use the mean-diameter `id_min_mm`/`id_max_mm`; the individual-diameter ovality the column warns about is carried as a note, not folded into the band, because the table gives it as an OD figure and I would be deriving a bore number the table does not state.
