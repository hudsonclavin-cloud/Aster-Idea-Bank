# Signal
> Listen to idea 40's spacecraft every day from launch to the end of a stay at the asteroid: its range and one-way and round-trip light time, where it sits against the Sun, which Deep Space Network complex hears it, and what a real spacecraft's radio closes at through the DSN's own published antenna model. It reproduces Juno's published link budget to +0.06 dB and the Deep Impact and Juno rate tables to 0.014 and 0.06 dB at the rates that fly, while Dawn's own 2009 station data came in 0.55 to 1.19 dB louder than the model says (mean +0.87 dB), which is the number to remember. On the chain's Apophis cruise Dawn's radio closes its top 123.8 kbps every day (18.5 dB over Dawn's own published threshold at arrival, 5.73 min one way, 11.46 min round trip) and the trouble is the opposite of distance: 61 days overload a 34-m receiver (80 a 70-m). An Eros transfer puts 13 X-band days (5 at Ka) behind the Sun.

**For:** Aster

Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/41-signal/signal.test.mjs` (16 tests, about 11 s; one reads idea 40's committed `handoff.json`) and `node ideas/41-signal/ui-check.mjs` (53 checks in headless Chromium; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). ## What it is

Idea 41, module 11 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 40 flew the cruise through every planet and navigated it, and its README left two things to this module: no light time, and no solar-plasma blackout near conjunction. This page takes idea 40's flown trajectory and, for every day from the departure to the end of a stay at the asteroid, works out what the ground hears. You can:

- **choose the trajectory**: idea 40's six presets (each transfer picked by idea 39's kernel and flown by idea 40's with its defaults; Apophis is the chain's), or import idea 40's handoff, which idea 40's own kernel re-flies and re-checks; set **how long to stay** at the asteroid (the asteroid is flown on by idea 40's propagator, so a stay at Apophis rides through its April 2029 Earth encounter);
- **choose the radio**: Dawn's, Juno's or the Deep Impact flyby spacecraft's, from JPL's published telecom summaries; change its **power** and **dish diameter** (gain scales at the preset's own aperture efficiency), **X or Ka band**, **ranging on or off**, the mission's **own rate table** or **any rate** up to the DSN decoder's limit, and a pointing loss;
- **choose the ground**: one 34-m beam-waveguide antenna per complex (DSS-26, 36, 56) or the 70-m antennas (DSS-14, 43, 63), the **weather** (the DSN's cumulative distribution, 0 to 0.90), the **lowest elevation**, the **margin**, and the **passes a week** and their **length** (idea 40's 3 a week of 8 h by default);
- **read** at arrival: distance, one-way light time, command round trip, Sun-Earth-probe angle, Pt/No, the data rate and its margin, data per pass and per week, and over the mission the data returned, the blacked-out days and the days the receiver is overloaded (at the 34-m or the 70-m antenna's own published limit), each with a confidence chip; and every refusal;
- **watch** the range (with light time on the second axis), the Sun-Earth-probe angle against the conjunction thresholds, Pt/No against each rate's need, the rate, and the hours each complex sees the spacecraft; **tap any day** for its full design control table, line by line as JPL lays one out, with every rate in the table and whether it closes;
- **list the conjunctions**: when, how close, how many days blacked out, superior or inferior, and the corona's ranging delay at the closest;
- **check it against the oracles**: JPL's published Juno, Dawn and Deep Impact link budgets and rate tables, Dawn's own station monitor data from June 2009, the DSN handbook's worked examples, and JPL Horizons;
- **export the handoff** for module 42 (copy, download, link).

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 41: "Talking to the spacecraft all mission: Earth range, one-way light time, a deep-space link budget and data rate, solar-conjunction blackouts", taking the trajectory and handing on a comms timeline. Idea 40's README, *What is rough*: "no light time … no solar-plasma degradation or conjunction blackout (module 41)", and *Where it would land*: "module 41 (Signal) takes `arrival_state` and the cruise for its Earth-range and conjunction timeline".
- **Two of Aster's own kernels are exactly this** (`strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md` at `62ddb17`): **K4**, the one-way light-time chip, row 6 and §6.2 to §6.3, whose erratum (2026-10-12) says the first implementation used heliocentric distance ÷ c, was cut, and must be reworked as Earth-to-body distance at the displayed epoch, inheriting the body's fidelity and refusing when the state is missing; and **K9**, the solar-conjunction flag on a mission timeline, row 14 ("Sun–Earth–probe angle below a stated threshold"). This module does both and validates them against Horizons. At the chain's arrival, heliocentric distance ÷ c would say 8.67 min; the Earth-to-Apophis light time is 5.73 min.
- **What a person decides with it.** How big a dish and how much power a spacecraft needs to return a given volume from a given asteroid; whether 34-m passes suffice or 70-m time must be bought; how many passes a week; X or Ka; when the blackouts fall, so critical events (arrival, touch-and-go) avoid them; how long a command and its answer take at the asteroid, which sets how autonomous the approach must be (module 42).

Alternatives weighed. (1) *A textbook link equation with round numbers for the DSN* (a 34-m dish at "68 dBi", "25 K"). Rejected: the DSN publishes its antennas' gain and noise against elevation and weather (810-005 Appendix A), and JPL publishes the link budgets its missions flew; with those the model can be checked, not just plausible. (2) *Port a telecom tool* (JPL's Telecom Forecaster Predictor is not public). **Built: the DSN handbook's own model and the missions' own radios**, checked against the forecaster's published outputs. (3) *Light time as distance ÷ c at one instant.* Rejected: that is what Aster's K4 erratum warns against in another form; the light-time iteration is what Horizons defines and costs nothing. (4) *A blackout below a fixed SEP* (MRO's 5°, DS1's 3°). Kept as the caution line; **built: the 810-005 module 106 scintillation model**, whose m ≥ 0.3 frame-error threshold is band-dependent (2.19° at X, 0.98° at Ka), and DS1's real conjunction as a check on it.

## How it works

**Geometry** (`signal.js` `downleg()`, `roundTrip()`). Idea 40's reference cruise (2-hour nodes, heliocentric ICRF, TDB) and, after the arrival, the asteroid flown on from idea 40's target state by idea 40's `propagate()` (every 6 h, every 15 min within 3 million km of the Earth), interpolated by cubic Hermite (idea 39's interpolant, on node times by bisection). The signal received at the Earth's centre at t left the spacecraft at t − τ, τ = |r_sc(t − τ) − r_E(t)| / c, iterated to 1 ns: JPL Horizons' "1-way down-leg light-time", Newtonian. The round trip is an up-leg to the spacecraft and a down-leg back. The Sun-Earth-probe angle is measured at the Earth; the ray's closest approach p to the Sun gives the elongation asin(p / r_E) the corona sees (the same as SEP for a spacecraft beyond the Sun; larger for one nearer the Sun, where the ray stops short of it). c = 299,792,458 m/s (SI exact); the Earth from idea 39's fit of Aster's Horizons file.

**The day's pass** (`passesOf()`, `dayRow()`). At 00:00 UTC each day, each complex's elevation over ±12 h in 10-min steps (idea 40's `elevation()`: ITRF93 coordinates from 810-005 module 301 Table 2, turned by the Earth rotation angle, with the spacecraft's geocentric vector interpolated across the day, so parallax near the Earth is right). The complex that culminates highest that day takes one pass of the chosen length centred on its culmination, inside its arc above the limit (10° by default: module 301's low-power transmit limit). The link is computed at the pass's lowest elevation, so one rate holds for the whole pass.

**The ground** (`dsnRx()`), 810-005 module 104 Rev. Q Appendix A (34 m) and module 101 Rev. I (70 m): vacuum gain G(el) = G0 + 20 log(f/f0) − G1 (el − γ)², f0 8425 MHz (X), 32050 MHz (Ka), 8420 MHz (70-m X); antenna-microwave noise T1 + T2 e^(−a·el); atmosphere loss A = Azen / sin(el) with Azen per complex, band and weather from Table A-5 (module 105 Rev. E values), L = 10^(A/10), noise Tp (1 − 1/L) with Tp = 255 + 25·CD, and the cosmic background 2.725 K / L. Pointing loss 0.1 dB (Table 7, CONSCAN). Recommended maximum signal −85 dBm at the 34-m HEMT input (104 Table 7) and −80 dBm at the 70-m LNA input (101 Table 2); more is flagged as an overload and refused (`saturation`). For the Deep Impact oracle only, DSS-14 also carries its S/X-configuration parameters (101 Tables A-1 and A-3: G0 74.35 dBi, G1 0.000365, γ 44.19°; T1 12.57 K, T2 7.11 K, a 0.065).

**The Sun and the corona.** The Sun's noise in a 34-m beam pointed θ from its centre, module 105 Rev. E eqs. (10)-(11): 5000 e^(−6.6θ) K (0.35° to 0.75°) or 100 e^(−1.4θ) K at X, 1400 e^(−5.1θ) and 86 e^(−1.4θ) at Ka, zero beyond 4°; inside 0.35° "likely in excess of 300 K" (refused); for the 70-m antennas only a curve (Figure 19), so refused within 4°. Scintillation, module 106 Rev. B eq. (7): m = e^(−a1 x) + a2 + a3 x + a4 x², x = ε − εt (X: εt 1.35°, a 2.0, 0.14, −0.03, 0; Ka: 0.68°, 4.0, 0.07, −0.25, 0.002), capped at 1 as the module's Figures 5 and 6 draw it; a day with m ≥ 0.3 is blacked out ("frame errors have been observed to significantly increase when the scintillation index reaches values of 0.3 and above"). Group delay, eqs. (1)-(2): Ne(r) = 2.21e14 (r/R0)^−6 + 1.55e12 (r/R0)^−2.3 m⁻³ integrated along the ray (24-point Gauss–Legendre after x = p tan φ), delay = 1.3446e−19 STEC / f(GHz)² µs. The minus signs pdftotext drops were read off the rendered pages (eqs. 2 and 7) or from continuity (eqs. 10-11 at 0.75°).

**The radio** (`radio()`, `linkBudget()`), from the JPL DESCANSO summaries (articles 13, 16, 9): Dawn 8435.370372 MHz, 1.524-m HGA 39.60 dBi, 100 W (50.00 dBm), 0.41 dB circuit loss, turbo (3568, 1/6) needing Eb/No 0.10 dB plus 0.33 dB of losses, rates 10 bps to 123,751.4 bps (Table 4-2), and one published threshold: 52.30 dB-Hz at 123,751.4 bps with ranging on (Table 5-2 line 21); Juno 8404.135802 MHz, 2.5 m, 44.70 dBi, 44.40 dBm, turbo (8920, 1/6) at −0.10 dB plus 0.32 dB, 17 turbo rates with their published required Pt/No (Tables 3-2, 3-3); Deep Impact 8435.370371 MHz, 1 m, 35.60 dBi, 42.70 dBm behind 3.76 dB of circuit loss, RS + (7, 1/2) at 2.31 dB plus 0.30 dB, 27 rates with published thresholds (Table 3-1; with ranging on at its low index the data side is the table's +0.30 dB, and the carrier side, which no source prints, is assumed to be −0.30 dB as well). EIRP = Pt − circuit loss + gain − pointing; space loss 20 log(4πdf/c); Pr = EIRP − space loss − atmosphere + DSN gain − 0.1 − polarization; N0 = k T; residual carrier at modulation index θ: carrier cos²θ, data sin²θ (with the preset's published ranging suppression when ranging is on); the carrier loop needs 10 dB of SNR in its bandwidth (module 202 eq. 3). A rate closes when Pt/No exceeds its need (the published threshold where the article prints one, otherwise rate + required Eb/No + losses − data suppression; and never less than the carrier's need) by the margin. "Any rate" solves for the highest rate at the radio's top modulation index, up to the DSN decoder limit (module 208: 1 Mbps for turbo 1/6, 13 Mbps for (7, 1/2)). Data returned = each day's rate × pass length × passes a week / 7.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain as measured] The link arithmetic and the DSN model reproduce JPL's own.** Juno's DCT to +0.06 dB in Pt/No with the antenna-microwave noise exact; the published rate tables to 0.014 dB (Deep Impact, ≥ 1 kbps) and 0.06 dB (Juno with ranging, ≥ 99.7 kbps). Deep Impact's 70-m DCT to +0.19 dB with DSS-14 in the S/X configuration its header names (system noise to 0.04 K). Where they differ the cause is named: the DCTs' mean-valued totals, an atmosphere model 0.4 K apart, 0.20 dB of DSS-14 gain at 24° between the 2005 DCT and today's S/X parameters, and rate-dependent losses (subcarrier, symbol sync, radio loss) the model does not carry, which make it 0.8 to 3.3 dB optimistic below about 1 kbps and 0.21 dB optimistic at Dawn's top rate. That is why the page uses published thresholds wherever an article prints them, Dawn's one included.
- **[Certain as measured; Likely as an estimate for another spacecraft] The gap to reality is about +0.9 dB.** On five real Dawn passes the stations measured 0.55 to 1.19 dB more carrier than this model predicts from Dawn's published design values (zero off-point, the logged range and elevation, average clear weather); the symbol SNR, which does not depend on the modulation index's tolerance, is −0.06 to +0.61 dB. A design built on this model has that much in hand on Dawn's evidence; the measured noise temperatures (23.8 to 30.9 K) also show 2 to 7 K of weather and hardware at DSS-25 and 26 that today's published model (21.9 to 22.3 K) does not (at DSS-24, diplexed, the model is 5.3 K high).
- **[Certain as measured] Light time and SEP are Horizons' to the precision that matters.** 26 µs and 0.009°; the chain's Apophis within 10 km of Horizons until its 2029 encounter (287 km after it, idea 40's propagator through a 38,000 km pass). Aster's two-body Apophis (87,301 km off at arrival, idea 40's finding) would move the light time by at most 0.29 s.
- **[Likely] The conjunction model is conservative.** Module 106's m ≥ 0.3 blacks X band out below 2.19°; DS1 had telemetry back on 20 Nov 2000, at 1.90° by Horizons, while sequenced at 40 bps (its rate below 3°). The article ran both X and Ka through the conjunction and does not say which band carried that telemetry or with what margin, so this is a hint, not a measurement. Margin buys back some of the blackout; the model does not credit it.
- **[Speculative] Scintillation for a spacecraft between the Earth and the Sun.** Module 106's eq. (7) is fitted to the Sun-Earth-probe angle of spacecraft beyond the Sun. For a ray that stops short of the Sun this page evaluates it at the ray's equivalent elongation asin(p / |r_E|), its own construction: it reduces to the SEP for superior geometry (tested) but has no published check for inferior geometry. None of the presets' conjunctions is inferior.
- **[Certain as numbers] The chain.** With Dawn's radio, the 34-m subnet and idea 40's 3 passes of 8 h a week, the Apophis mission is never link-limited: 123.8 kbps every day, 18.5 dB over Dawn's published threshold at arrival, 1.03e12 bits in the cruise and 5.55e11 in a year's stay. The binding constraints are elsewhere: 61 days when the HGA overloads a 34-m receiver (the first 46 days, to 17 Aug 2026, and 7 to 21 April 2029, around Apophis's pass of the Earth, which a spacecraft staying at the asteroid would ride; on the 70-m antennas, 80 days against their −80 dBm), the passes bought, and the 11.46-minute round trip at arrival. Those overload days still count toward the data returned, at the rate the link closes: the refusal says the HGA must give way to a low-gain antenna or a low-gain receiver mode then, which this module does not model. With "any rate", the same link would carry 1 Mbps (the decoder's limit). Apophis comes no closer than 6.0° to the Sun (8 Sept 2028), so no conjunction; Eros does (0.082°, 13 X-band days).
- **What is rough or cut.**
  - *No command uplink*: none of the sources gives an HGA receive gain and receiver noise temperature; refused, not guessed.
  - *One radio at a time*, always Earth-pointed on its HGA: no low-gain antennas, attitude constraints, or switching to a low-gain antenna when the HGA overloads (the refusal says so).
  - *Deep Impact with ranging on*: its table's +0.30 dB covers the data side; the carrier side at the 0.185-rad index is published nowhere and is assumed to be −0.30 dB too (conservative for the carrier loop).
  - *Ka band* scales the X-band dish by f² at the same efficiency and the DSN's nominal 32,050 MHz: none of these spacecraft sent Ka telemetry (refused as `ka_band`).
  - *The Sun's noise* is the 1996 solar-minimum upper envelope, not scaled to the solar cycle (module 105 eq. 12 would need NOAA's flux predictions); the 70-m curve is not digitised.
  - *Stations*: one 34-m per complex (DSS-26, 36, 56), turned by the Earth rotation angle only (no precession: 0.09° of elevation on average), one pass a day at the best complex, no arraying or DSN scheduling; the link at the pass's lowest elevation.
  - *No tolerances*: design values only (no mean − 2σ), no Shapiro delay, light time to the Earth's centre (a station is up to 21 ms nearer).
  - *Size*: 636 KB as the checker counts (619 KB before the review added its tests, snapshot lines and this record), over the 500 KB budget and under the 650 KB limit: idea 40's kernel and its ephemerides are 336 KB. For that reason the sample handoff is built from idea 40's preset (36 KB) rather than idea 40's committed sample, which carries ideas 31 to 39 inside it (112 KB); both give the same cruise to the metre and both import (tested).
