## What it is

Aster's catalog colours and "min C3" values come from `tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json`: for each of 41,906 bodies, the five cheapest cells of a Lambert screen over a lattice of departure dates and flight times. A lattice has edges, and a minimum that sits on an edge is a minimum of the *sampled* surface, not necessarily of the body's transfer landscape. This study counts where the 41,905 stored minima land and how many sit on each of the four edges, with the evidence the cache itself holds about whether the surface is still falling there.

It is a census, not a solver. From the cache's own `metadata` it derives the lattice (nothing is typed: the page's "the grid spans …" sentence is computed, and a test fails if a year or date appears in the page source), checks that every one of the 209,525 stored windows sits on that lattice, and then counts:

- **Where the minima land.** A departure-year × flight-time heatmap of each body's cheapest window (and, toggled, of all five), with the year and flight-time totals as marginals, and a per-departure-date line over all 783 dates. The first date carries 218 minima, 4.4× the interior median of 49 per date; the last carries 165, 3.4×. The opening pile-up is wider than one date: 2026, 2027 and 2028 carry 4,268, 3,474 and 3,188 minima against a median interior year of 2,528, and 2040 carries 3,397.
- **The edges.** 2,530 bodies (6.0%) have their minimum at the 182-day floor, 218 (0.5%) on the first departure date, 165 (0.4%) on the last, 34 (0.1%) at the 1,802-day ceiling; 2,936 on any edge, 11 on two at once. For each, whether the cache records the slope at the edge: the next cell inward is among the body's five windows for 113 of the 218, 87 of the 165, 28 of the 34, and 1 of the 2,530. Where a chain of three or more stored cells runs inward it rises at every step in every case (44 of 44, 38 of 38, 9 of 9).
- **Who sits there**, by orbit class, absolute magnitude, synodic period with Earth, PHA flag and the cache's co-orbital flag. The floor share falls monotonically with H (14.9% of bodies brighter than H 18, 0.2% of those fainter than 28); 24 of the 36 Halley-type comets sit at the ceiling and 20 of 38 interior-Earth objects at the floor; the edge rate is nearly flat across synodic-period bands (5.4–10.3%), and the 130 cache-flagged co-orbitals are *less* often on an edge (4.6%) than the rest (7.0%).
- **Basins.** How many distinct basins a body's five windows span under the proposal's rule (same basin within 2 columns and 1 row), with two looser rules beside it: most bodies spread their five over three or more.
- **The porkchop view's tail.** The view's own grid ends at 2040-01-01 (`GRID_PARAMS.depEndJD`, read from `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:196` at build time), the cache's lattice at 2040-12-27: 3,397 bodies have their catalog minimum in the tail the view never shows and 5,644 have at least one of their five windows there. Slice 17's F2 finding measured this incidence as 0 in a five-body sample.
- **Fifteen sample bodies** a reader can check by hand: each one's five windows on the whole lattice and in a zoom around its minimum, with column, row, basin, edge flags and verdict, from 433 Eros (the hand-worked example in the tests) to the one body with no windows at all, shown as a refusal.
- **What it cannot say**, as structured refusals: the slope at the floor (recorded for 1 body in 2,530), how far beyond an edge a true minimum lies (no cell exists there), whether any sampled minimum is a body's true best window, the 210 bodies without H, and the body the propagator failed on.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** H0, horizon extension past 2040, is "Hudson's top stated ask" (`strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:52` in §2, `:59` in §3) and its gate was re-stated on 2026-09-21 (§6.1, `:104-118`): not payload, but "the honesty boundary" and a compute cost of ~2.33× (1.80e9 → ~4.21e9 solves). Nobody had counted how many bodies the current edge actually touches, in which direction, or whether the cache can say so. `STATUS.md:410-425` (Slice 19 item 1) and `SLICE_17_FOUNDING.md:716-719` (S17-F2) both record that the cache and the porkchop view screen different spans and that a cached minimum in the eleven-month tail is undisplayable, with an incidence measured on five bodies; DEC-17-2's span clause (`SLICE_17_FOUNDING.md:120-135`) says such rows must be flagged. The counts those items need did not exist. `mcp/README.md:56-71` ("Advertised span vs accepted span") records the same family of edge trouble on the MCP side.
- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *Re-solve Lambert beyond the grid for a sample of edge bodies* and measure the true minima. That is H0's own compute, needs the TypeScript solver compiled out of the protected core, and would turn a census into a claim about off-grid physics; killed, and the page refuses that question instead (`out_of_envelope`).
  - *The year histogram alone*, which is what the session probe the proposal cites produced. It conflates the strict edge effect (minima on the very first or last date) with the three-year opening ramp and the two-year closing one, and says nothing about the flight-time edges, which turn out to dominate.
  - *A per-body list of the 2,936 edge bodies* as data. About 100 KB, and it would be read as a verdict list ("these bodies are clipped") when the cache supports that reading for 229 of them and not the other 2,718. The census ships counts, the verdict split and fifteen inspectable samples instead.
  - *A "clipped" flag computed from the five windows' monotonicity*, as the proposal phrased it. For two points the condition is automatic (the neighbour always costs more than the minimum, or it would be the minimum), so it was made honest: *likely clipped* needs the inward neighbour to be among the five (and a rising chain when longer), *undetermined* otherwise, both counts shown.

  This won because it answers the H0 question in the kernel's own terms (how many bodies does the edge touch, and what does the cache know about them), finds a larger edge nobody had named, and gives S17-F2 its number, all without inventing a single off-grid value.

## How it works

**Inputs and provenance.** `snapshots/metadata.json` is the cache's `metadata` object, copied with `tools/snapshot.mjs --pick metadata` from Aster `tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json` at commit `62ddb17` (sidecar: cache sha256 `bd27b9bd…`, 34,499,477 bytes, read bytes equal the committed blob). `edge-census.mjs` reads the whole cache (34 MB) and the catalog `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.json` (sha256 `f88028d2…`, which equals the cache's own `provenance.catalogFixtureSha256`: the cache was built from this catalog, tested), extracts `GRID_PARAMS` from `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts` (`depStartJD` line 195, `depEndJD` line 196, the derived span label at line 203) and `AU_KM` from `tools/build/precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:81` by regex with their line numbers, and writes `snapshots/edge-census.json` (52 KB; sidecar `--sidecar-only` pointing at the cache). `build-data.mjs` wraps that and the metadata as `snapshots/edge-census.data.js` for `file://`. No per-body record is copied except the fifteen samples' five windows.

**The lattice [Certain].** The precompute walks departures from `screeningWindow.startUtc` in steps of `departureGridSpacingDays` while `t <= endUtc`, and flight times from `tofMinDays` in steps of `tofGridSpacingDays` while `d <= tofMaxDays` (both loops inclusive at day granularity; the test checks the loop text when the checkout is present). So nCols = ⌊(end − start)/spacing⌋ + 1 = ⌊5478/7⌋ + 1 = 783 and the last departure is start + 782·7 d = 2040-12-27, four days before the metadata's `endUtc` 2040-12-31, which is not a lattice point; nRows = ⌊(1826 − 182)/30⌋ + 1 = 55 and the last flight time is 1,802 days, not the metadata's 1,826. The reconciliation that pins this: 783 × 55 × 41,905 bodies with windows = 1,804,638,825 = `metadata.totalSolves` exactly (the precompute counts a solve only after a successful propagation, and the one `propagator_failed` body contributed none). Every stored `launchDate` is start + 7k days and every `tofDays` is 182 + 30k: 209,525 windows, 0 violations, 783 distinct launch dates (every column is used), 44 distinct flight times; the cache's `minC3Date`/`minC3TofDays`/`minC3` name the first stored window for every body and the five are ascending for every body. (The TDB − UTC date-line issue `mcp/README.md` records for the MCP does not reach the cache's date strings: the precompute converts back with the same offset.)

**Cells and edges [Certain].** column = (launchDate − startUtc)/7 d, row = (tofDays − 182)/30 d; a window that does not land on the lattice is reported as a named violation, never rounded. Edges: column 0 (first departure date), column 782 (last), row 0 (floor), row 54 (ceiling). A body "is on an edge" when its cheapest stored window is (found by C3, not by position). 433 Eros, by hand: 2026-01-01 → 2032-01-01 is 2,191 days and June 10 is day 161 of 2032, so 2,352 days = 336 weeks exactly: column 336, row (272 − 182)/30 = 3; its other windows are columns 335, 340, 698 and 699.

**Slope evidence [Certain as arithmetic, Likely as an inference].** For a minimum on an edge, walk inward along the same row (departure edges) or column (flight-time edges) through the body's other stored cells: columns 1, 2, … for the first date, 781, 780, … for the last, rows 1, 2, … for the floor. If the inward neighbour is stored it costs more than the minimum (else it would be the minimum), so the sampled surface is falling toward the edge; if the chain has three or more cells, "monotone" means it rises at every step. Verdict *likely clipped* [Likely]: neighbour stored and chain monotone, the minimum of the surface is at or beyond the edge; the slope quoted is that one step in km²/s² (first date: median 0.386, n 113; last: 0.519, n 87; ceiling: 7.111, n 28; floor: 0.751, n 1). Verdict *undetermined* [Speculative]: the other four windows lie in other basins and no slope is recorded; the cache cannot say. All 91 chains of three or more are monotone. **Interior alternative:** 2,616 of the 2,936 edge bodies also store a window in another basin that is on no edge, costing a median 0.623 km²/s² more than the edge minimum (0.0002–32.5); 320 store none.

**Basins [Certain].** Two windows are in the same basin when their cells are within `maxColGap` columns and `maxRowGap` rows; basins are the connected components (union–find), so the count does not depend on window order (tested over all 120 permutations of each sample). Proposal's rule (2, 1): 1 basin 3,848 bodies, 2: 6,464, 3: 10,155, 4: 10,601, 5: 10,837. Under (4, 2): 13,665 / 14,492 / 8,788 / 3,921 / 1,039; under (8, 2): 17,780 / 15,908 / 5,970 / 1,854 / 393. Eros has 3 under the first rule and 2 under the second (column 340 is four columns from 336).

**Catalog bands [Certain].** `orbitClass`, `H` (bands < 18, 18–22, 22–25, 25–28, ≥ 28, missing), `pha`, and a synodic period with Earth from `elements.aKm`: T = (a/AU)^1.5 years, S = 1/|1/T − 1| (Kepler's third law in AU and years; a = 1 AU gives ∞), with AU = 149,597,870.7 km read from the precompute script. Closed-form checks: Mars at 1.524 AU gives 780 days, Venus at 0.7233 AU 584 days. Per-class totals plus the one body without windows reproduce the catalog's own `classDistribution` row by row; the H-missing row plus that body reproduces `missingAbsoluteMagnitudeCount` 210; the co-orbital row is the 130 the precompute script asserts (`precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:368`, OQ-7).

**The view tail [Certain].** `minimaAfter` counts bodies whose cheapest stored window's `launchDate` is later than the view's `depEndUtc`; since the first lattice date of 2040 is 2040-01-05, this equals the 2040 year total, 3,397 (a test recounts it from the per-column vector). `anyWindowAfter` (5,644) counts bodies with any of the five there.

**The page.** Inline SVG built with DOM calls (`textContent`, never `innerHTML`), sized to its real pixel width and redrawn on resize; colours are CSS tokens on `:root` redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, so a scheme change needs no redraw. The heatmap is a one-hue sequential ramp in five classes (light `#86b6ef → #104281`, dark `#184f95 → #b7d3f6`, both validated with the dataviz skill's `validate_palette.js --ordinal` against the card surfaces `#fcfcfb` / `#1a1a19`; zero is the surface with a hairline), class thresholds derived from the data's maximum; the series colour is slot 1 blue and edges are slot 2 orange (the pair validated in both modes, CVD ΔE 24.7 light / 26.8 dark). Marginal bars and the basin columns are ≤ 24 px with a 4 px rounded data end; a legend is present wherever two things are drawn; every chart has a table-view twin; tooltips follow hover, tap and keyboard (arrow keys, Home/End, Escape). At 390 px the edge, group and inspector tables re-render as key/value blocks or two-line rows so nothing scrolls sideways.

## Honest assessment

What works:
- **[Certain] The lattice and the counts.** Derived from metadata, verified against all 209,525 stored windows, and pinned to the cache's own `totalSolves`; recounted independently in Python. Every number on the page is in `snapshots/edge-census.json`, and the CLI reproduces that file from the checkout byte for byte.
- **[Certain] The floor is the binding edge.** 2,530 minima at 182 days against 383 on the two departure edges and 34 at the ceiling. This was not in the proposal, which assumed the horizon was the question.
- **[Certain] The view tail.** 3,397 bodies' catalog minima and 5,644 bodies' stored windows lie after the porkchop view's last departure; S17-F2's five-body incidence of 0 was a small sample.
- **[Likely] The edge is still falling for the 229 likely-clipped bodies.** The inward cell is stored and costs more, and every longer chain rises monotonically. For the 2,718 undetermined bodies the cache simply holds no slope; the page says so and does not guess.
- **[Likely] The pile-up is not the near-co-orbital drift story.** If slowly drifting bodies near 1 AU were the cause, the edge rate would climb with synodic period and the co-orbital flag would concentrate it; it is nearly flat (5.4–10.3%) and the flagged co-orbitals are below average.

What is rough:
- **[Speculative] Why the floor share falls with H.** The monotone trend across the five H bands is measured; the cause is not. A reading: faint bodies enter the catalog because they were seen close to Earth, which puts a cheap interior window on the lattice, while bright, long-known bodies on wider orbits reach their cheapest single-revolution transfer at short flight times. The census does not test this.
- **[Speculative] What lies below 182 days.** For 2,529 of the floor bodies nothing in the cache says whether a shorter flight is cheaper; one Lambert solve per body at a shorter flight time (about 2,530 solves against 1.8e9) would settle it. That is the single most useful follow-up and this study deliberately does not do it.
- **[Certain] "Likely clipped" is a statement about the sampled surface's slope at the edge, not an estimate of the off-grid minimum.** The slope quoted is one lattice step; no extrapolation is made. The all-chains-monotone result is strong evidence that the surfaces are smooth at 7-day spacing, which is all it is.
- **[Certain] Basin counts depend on the rule.** The proposal's (2, 1) rule gives the histogram shown; two looser rules move most bodies to one or two basins. The page shows all three and names the rule in every label.
- **[Likely] The year-level excess cannot be split cleanly** into "truncation" and "the catalog is richest near its selection epoch" (the catalog's `closeApproachWindow` is 2026-05-01 to 2026-07-30; many small bodies were found near their 2026 approaches). The strict per-date edge counts (218 and 165) are clean; the three-year opening ramp is not attributed.
- **[Certain] Static data.** The page reads a snapshot of the cache at `62ddb17`; if Aster regenerates the cache, `edge-census.mjs` must be re-run and the live-checkout tests will say so (the committed-blob check fails first).
- The 15-sample inspector is the hand-check surface; there is no per-body lookup for the other 41,890 bodies, by design (see alternatives). The desktop edge table's eight columns are wide; at 390 px it becomes key/value blocks. No real person has used the page.

**Deviations from the judged proposal, and why.**
- The `main.ts:637` literal motivation is gone (F1): it was already fixed when the proposal was judged, as the judges' notes say; the study keeps the honest half (the two surfaces still cover different spans) and tests that the label is derived.
- "Last departure 2040-01-01" is the porkchop *view's*; the cache's is 2040-12-27. Both are read from the checkout at build time with file and line, and the tail count is the difference between them.
- "Roughly a thousand bodies may have their true minimum before or after the grid" is not stated.
