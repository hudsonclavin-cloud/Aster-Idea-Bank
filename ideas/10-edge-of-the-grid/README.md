# Edge of the Grid
> Of the 41,905 bodies Aster's screening cache holds windows for, 2,936 (7.0%) have their cheapest window on an edge of the 783 × 55 departure-by-flight-time lattice (2,530 at the 182-day flight-time floor, 218 on the first departure date, 165 on the last, 34 at the ceiling); the departure edges and the ceiling show a pile-up against the next line in and the floor does not, and 3,397 bodies (8.1%) have a cached minimum after 2040-01-01, the porkchop view's last departure, so the view can never show it.

## What it is

Aster's catalog colours and "min C3" values come from `tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json`: for each of 41,906 bodies, the five cheapest cells of a Lambert screen over a lattice of departure dates and flight times. A lattice has edges, and a minimum that sits on an edge is a minimum of the *sampled* surface, not necessarily of the body's transfer landscape. This study counts where the 41,905 stored minima land, how many sit on each of the four edges, and what the five stored windows can and cannot say about the surface there.

It is a census, not a solver. From the cache's own `metadata` it derives the lattice (nothing is typed: the page's "the grid spans …" sentence is computed, and a test fails if a year or date appears in the page source), checks that every one of the 209,525 stored windows sits on that lattice, and then counts:

- **Where the minima land.** A departure-year × flight-time heatmap of each body's cheapest window (and, toggled, of all five), with the year and flight-time totals as marginals, and a per-departure-date line over all 783 dates. The first date carries 218 minima, 4.4× the interior median of 49 per date; the last carries 165, 3.4×. The opening excess is wider than one date: 2026, 2027 and 2028 carry 4,268, 3,474 and 3,188 minima against a median interior year of 2,528, and 2040 carries 3,397.
- **The edges, compared fairly.** 2,530 bodies (6.0%) have their minimum at the 182-day floor, 218 (0.5%) on the first departure date, 165 (0.4%) on the last, 34 (0.1%) at the 1,802-day ceiling; 2,936 on any edge, 11 on two at once. The edges differ in length (a departure edge has 55 cells, a flight-time edge 783), so the page shows minima per edge cell (first date 3.96, last date 3.00, floor 3.23, ceiling 0.04) and each edge against the next three lines in: first date 218 then 154, 149, 149; last date 165 then 131, 113, 121; ceiling 34 then 0, 1, 0; floor 2,530 then 2,772, 6,280, 10,319. Three edges show a pile-up; the floor shows none.
- **The inward rise.** Every edge minimum is the cheapest solved cell of the whole lattice, so the surface falls toward it by construction. What the stored windows add is how steeply it rises one step in: recorded where the inward neighbour is among the five (229 edge instances), bounded below by the spread of the five where it is not (2,718). For the 91 stored chains of three or more cells running inward, a three-point quadratic puts the vertex beyond the edge for 41, inside the lattice within half a step for 44, and has no minimum on the inward side for 6.
- **Who sits there**, by orbit class, absolute magnitude, synodic period with Earth, PHA flag and the cache's co-orbital flag. The floor share falls monotonically with H (14.9% of bodies brighter than H 18, 0.2% of those fainter than 28); 24 of the 36 Halley-type comets sit at the ceiling and 20 of 38 interior-Earth objects at the floor; the edge rate is nearly flat across synodic-period bands (5.4–10.3%), and the 130 cache-flagged co-orbitals are *less* often on an edge (4.6%) than the rest (7.0%).
- **Basins**, under four rules: the proposal's box (same basin within 2 columns and 1 row), two looser boxes, and the proposal's reach sheared along constant arrival dates. Under the box, 31,593 bodies spread their five over three or more basins; under the arrival-aware rule 32,859 have one or two. The difference is the lattice's aspect ratio (below), and the page says so.
- **Another stored window off the edge**: whether an edge body also stores a window in another basin that is on no edge, under each rule (2,616 under the box, 2,121 along arrival dates).
- **The porkchop view's tail.** The view's own grid ends at 2040-01-01 (`GRID_PARAMS.depEndJD`, read from `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:196` at build time), the cache's lattice at 2040-12-27: 3,397 bodies have their catalog minimum in the tail the view never shows and 5,644 have at least one of their five windows there. Slice 17's F2 finding measured this incidence as 0 in a five-body sample.
- **Twenty sample bodies** a reader can check by hand: each one's five windows on the whole lattice and in a zoom around its minimum, with column, row, basin, edge flags and the rise evidence, from 433 Eros (the hand-worked example in the tests) to the one body with no windows at all, shown as a refusal.
- **What it cannot say**, as structured refusals: what lies below the flight-time floor, how far beyond an edge a true minimum lies, whether any sampled minimum is a body's true best window, the 210 bodies without H, and the one body the propagator could not take (a hyperbolic orbit).

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** H0, horizon extension past 2040, is "Hudson's top stated ask" (`strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:52` in §2, `:59` in §3) and its gate was re-stated on 2026-09-21 (§6.1, `:104-118`): not payload, but "the honesty boundary" and a compute cost of ~2.33× (1.80e9 → ~4.21e9 solves). Nobody had counted how many bodies the current edge actually touches, in which direction, or what the cache can say about them. `STATUS.md:410-425` (Slice 19 item 1) and `SLICE_17_FOUNDING.md:716-719` (S17-F2) both record that the cache and the porkchop view screen different spans and that a cached minimum in the eleven-month tail is undisplayable, with an incidence measured on five bodies; DEC-17-2 (`SLICE_17_FOUNDING.md:120-137`, span clause `:133-137`) says the compare view "must flag any such row". The counts those items need did not exist. `mcp/README.md:56-71` ("Advertised span vs accepted span") records the same family of edge trouble on the MCP side.
- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *Re-solve Lambert beyond the grid for a sample of edge bodies* and measure the true minima. That is H0's own compute, needs the TypeScript solver compiled out of the protected core, and would turn a census into a claim about off-grid physics; killed, and the page refuses that question instead (`out_of_envelope`), sizing the probes that would answer it.
  - *The year histogram alone*, which is what the session probe the proposal cites produced. It conflates the strict edge effect (minima on the very first or last date) with the three-year opening ramp and the two-year closing one, and says nothing about the flight-time edges.
  - *A per-body list of the 2,936 edge bodies* as data. About 100 KB, and it would be read as a verdict list ("these bodies are clipped") which the cache cannot support for any of them. The census ships counts, distributions and twenty inspectable samples instead.
  - *A "clipped" flag computed from the five windows' monotonicity*, as the proposal phrased it. For two points the condition is automatic (the neighbour always costs more than the minimum, or it would be the minimum). The first build redefined it as "the inward neighbour is among the five", which round-1 review showed is just as automatic in another way: the five are the body's cheapest cells of the whole lattice, so whether the neighbour made the five depends on how steep the rise is. That verdict is gone. What remains is what the data distinguishes: the rise recorded or bounded, and for three-cell chains the quadratic vertex under a stated model.

  This won because it answers the H0 question in the kernel's own terms (how many bodies the edge touches, and what the cache knows about them), puts the flight-time edges beside the departure edges on a fair footing, and gives S17-F2 its number, all without inventing a single off-grid value.

## How it works

**Inputs and provenance.** `snapshots/metadata.json` is the cache's `metadata` object, copied with `tools/snapshot.mjs --pick metadata` from Aster `tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json` at commit `62ddb17` (sidecar: cache sha256 `bd27b9bd…`, 34,499,477 bytes, read bytes equal the committed blob). `edge-census.mjs` reads the whole cache (34 MB) and the catalog `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.json` (sha256 `f88028d2…`, which equals the cache's own `provenance.catalogFixtureSha256`: the cache was built from this catalog, tested), and extracts by regex, with line numbers: `GRID_PARAMS` from `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts` (`depStartJD` line 195, `depEndJD` line 196, the derived span label at line 203), `AU_KM` from `tools/build/precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:81`, and the best-window insertion bound `if (insertAt >= 5)` from the same script's line 132. It writes `snapshots/edge-census.json` (sidecar `--sidecar-only` pointing at the cache); `build-data.mjs` wraps that and the metadata as `snapshots/edge-census.data.js` for `file://`. `snapshots/slice18-neighbours.json` is a `--pick` excerpt of Slice 18's `tools/slice18-research/c3-drift-sensitivity-results.json` (the five bodies' best cells and their four re-solved neighbours), used only by the tests. No per-body record is copied except the twenty samples' five windows.

**The lattice [Certain].** The precompute walks departures from `screeningWindow.startUtc` in steps of `departureGridSpacingDays` while `t <= endUtc`, and flight times from `tofMinDays` in steps of `tofGridSpacingDays` while `d <= tofMaxDays` (both loops inclusive at day granularity; the test checks the loop text when the checkout is present). So nCols = ⌊(end − start)/spacing⌋ + 1 = ⌊5478/7⌋ + 1 = 783 and the last departure is start + 782·7 d = 2040-12-27, four days before the metadata's `endUtc` 2040-12-31, which is not a lattice point; nRows = ⌊(1826 − 182)/30⌋ + 1 = 55 and the last flight time is 1,802 days, not the metadata's 1,826. The reconciliation that pins this: 783 × 55 × 41,905 bodies with windows = 1,804,638,825 = `metadata.totalSolves` exactly (the precompute counts a solve only after a successful propagation, and the one `propagator_failed` body contributed none). Every stored `launchDate` is start + 7k days and every `tofDays` is 182 + 30k: 209,525 windows, 0 violations, 783 distinct launch dates (every column is used), 44 distinct flight times; the cache's `minC3Date`/`minC3TofDays`/`minC3` name the first stored window for every body, the five are ascending for every body, and every body with windows stores the full five. (The TDB − UTC date-line issue `mcp/README.md` records for the MCP does not reach the cache's date strings: the precompute converts back with the same offset.)

**Cells and edges [Certain].** column = (launchDate − startUtc)/7 d, row = (tofDays − 182)/30 d; a window that does not land on the lattice is reported as a named violation, never rounded. Edges: column 0 (first departure date), column 782 (last), row 0 (floor), row 54 (ceiling). A body "is on an edge" when its cheapest stored window is (found by C3, not by position). 433 Eros, by hand: 2026-01-01 → 2032-01-01 is 2,191 days and June 10 is day 161 of 2032, so 2,352 days = 336 weeks exactly: column 336, row (272 − 182)/30 = 3; its other windows are columns 335, 340, 698 and 699. Because the edges differ in length, each edge is also reported per edge cell (count ÷ 55 for a departure edge, ÷ 783 for a flight-time edge) and against the minima counts on the next three lines in, from the per-column and per-row vectors. An edge carrying more minima than the line beside it is a pile-up [Certain as a count]; whether that pile-up is truncation is not shown by counts [Speculative].

**The inward rise.** The precompute offers every solved cell to `maybeInsertBestWindow` (`precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:127-139`, called at `:287`), which keeps the five cheapest. So the cheapest stored window is the minimum over the whole sampled lattice, and every other solved cell costs at least as much: the surface falls toward an edge minimum by construction [Certain], which says nothing about clipping. One step inward along the same row (departure edges) or column (flight-time edges):
- *rise recorded* [Certain] when the inward neighbour is among the five: its C3 minus the minimum. First date median 0.386 km²/s² (n 113), last date 0.519 (n 87), floor 0.751 (n 1), ceiling 7.111 (n 28).
- *rise bounded* [Likely] when it is not: the neighbour then cost at least the dearest stored window, so the rise is at least c3[max] − c3[min]. First date median ≥ 0.719 (n 105), last date ≥ 1.075 (n 78), floor ≥ 1.754 (n 2,529, range 0.011–41.486), ceiling ≥ 17.655 (n 6). This is exact wherever the neighbour's Lambert solve converged; the cache does not record the cells where one failed (Izzo returns `ok: false` on degenerate geometry or no convergence, and the precompute then skips the cell), hence Likely rather than Certain. Slice 18 re-solved about 2.7 million cells with zero solver failures (`C3_DRIFT_SENSITIVITY.md:141`).

The bounded rises are the steeper ones (all edges: median ≥ 1.710 against 0.532 recorded): a shallow rise lets the neighbour into the five, a steep one keeps it out. The split therefore sorts edges by steepness, not by strength of evidence, and the page presents both distributions side by side without a verdict.

**The quadratic vertex [Likely, under a local-quadratic model].** For a chain of three or more stored cells running inward, c0, c1, c2 at x = 0, 1, 2 lattice steps, the parabola through them has curvature k = c2 − 2c1 + c0 and, when k > 0, its vertex at x* = ½ − (c1 − c0)/k. x* < 0 is beyond the edge; 0 ≤ x* < ½ is inside the lattice within half a step (with c1 > c0 the vertex cannot lie further in); k ≤ 0 is a straight or concave chain with no minimum on the inward side. Over the 91 chains: beyond 41 (first date 19, last 14, ceiling 8), inside 44 (22, 21, 1), none 6 (3, 3, 0). More generally, under such a model a minimum on the edge cell with a dearer neighbour puts the continuous minimum along that line within half a lattice step of the edge or beyond it; that is all the edge position itself says. All 91 chains also rise at every stored step.

**Constant-arrival-date valleys.** A porkchop valley often runs along a nearly constant arrival date (departure + flight time). On this lattice one 30-day flight-time row is 30 ÷ 7 ≈ 4.3 departure columns, so the same valley one row over sits 4 or 5 columns away. The census looks for it at the flight-time edges: one row in and floor(4.3) − 1 … ceil(4.3) + 1 = 3 … 6 dates away (earlier above the floor, later below the ceiling). 1,137 of the 2,530 floor bodies store a window there, a median 0.862 km²/s² dearer than the floor minimum; 1,316 store some window one row up. The valley runs toward the floor at later departures, which is the direction a sub-floor probe should follow (sized under "What it cannot say"). None of the 34 ceiling bodies stores one.

**Basins [Certain as arithmetic].** Two windows are in the same basin when the rule calls their cells close; basins are the connected components (union–find), so the count does not depend on window order (tested over all 120 permutations of each sample). Box rules: within `maxColGap` columns and `maxRowGap` rows. The arrival-aware rule: arrival dates within `maxColGap` departure steps (|Δcol·7 + Δrow·30| ≤ 14 days) and rows within `maxRowGap`; it joins a valley's diagonal and, unlike a box, separates the same date one row apart (30 days apart in arrival). Histograms (1 to 5 basins): box (2, 1) 3,848 / 6,464 / 10,155 / 10,601 / 10,837; box (4, 2) 13,665 / 14,492 / 8,788 / 3,921 / 1,039; box (8, 2) 17,780 / 15,908 / 5,970 / 1,854 / 393; arrival (2, 1) 16,832 / 16,027 / 6,565 / 2,023 / 458. Eros has 3 under the first rule and 2 under (4, 2) (column 340 is four columns from 336).

**Another stored window off the edge.** For each edge body and each rule, the cheapest stored window outside the minimum's basin that is itself on no edge: box (2, 1) 2,616 with one (median 0.623 km²/s² dearer, 0.0002–32.53) and 320 without; box (4, 2) 2,230 / 706; box (8, 2) 2,004 / 932; arrival (2, 1) 2,121 / 815 (median 0.736). Many of the box's "other basins" are the same valley one row over: of the 2,616, the round-1 reviewer counted 1,086 within (8, 2) of the minimum, most at offsets (−4, +1) and (−5, +1).

**Catalog bands [Certain].** `orbitClass`, `H` (bands < 18, 18–22, 22–25, 25–28, ≥ 28, missing), `pha`, and a synodic period with Earth from `elements.aKm`: T = (a/AU)^1.5 years, S = 1/|1/T − 1| (Kepler's third law in AU and years; a = 1 AU gives ∞), with AU = 149,597,870.7 km read from the precompute script. Closed-form checks: Mars at 1.524 AU gives 780 days, Venus at 0.7233 AU 584 days. Per-class totals plus the one body without windows reproduce the catalog's own `classDistribution` row by row; the H-missing row plus that body reproduces `missingAbsoluteMagnitudeCount` 210; the co-orbital row is the 130 the precompute script asserts (`precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:368`, OQ-7).

**The view tail [Certain].** `minimaAfter` counts bodies whose cheapest stored window's `launchDate` is later than the view's `depEndUtc`; since the first lattice date of 2040 is 2040-01-05, this equals the 2040 year total, 3,397 (a test recounts it from the per-column vector). `anyWindowAfter` (5,644) counts bodies with any of the five there.

**The page.** Inline SVG built with DOM calls (`textContent`, never `innerHTML`), sized to its real pixel width and redrawn on resize; colours are CSS tokens on `:root` redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, so a scheme change needs no redraw. The heatmap is a one-hue sequential ramp in five classes (light `#86b6ef → #104281`, dark `#184f95 → #b7d3f6`, both validated with the dataviz skill's `validate_palette.js --ordinal` against the card surfaces `#fcfcfb` / `#1a1a19`; zero is the surface with a hairline), class thresholds derived from the data's maximum; the series colour is slot 1 blue and edges are slot 2 orange (the pair validated in both modes, CVD ΔE 24.7 light / 26.8 dark). Marginal bars and the basin columns are ≤ 24 px with a 4 px rounded data end; a legend is present wherever two things are drawn, and the column chart names its dashed interior-median line in the legend rather than on the data; every chart has a table-view twin; tooltips follow hover, tap and keyboard (arrow keys, Home/End, Escape). The inspector's basin labels are placed to avoid each other and the dots, and its captions wrap to the chart's width. At 390 px the edge, rise, group and inspector tables re-render as key/value blocks or two-line rows so nothing scrolls sideways. A few things are computed on the page from the census and the lattice rather than stored: the sizes of the floor probe and the forward extension in the refusals, which edges pile up against their next line, and today's date (to say that the first departure date is past).

## Honest assessment

What works:
- **[Certain] The lattice and the counts.** Derived from metadata, verified against all 209,525 stored windows, and pinned to the cache's own `totalSolves`; recounted independently in Python. Every number on the page is in `snapshots/edge-census.json` or computed on the page from it, and the CLI reproduces that file from the checkout byte for byte.
- **[Certain] Where the edge minima sit, compared fairly.** 2,530 at the floor, 218 and 165 on the departure edges, 34 at the ceiling, with per-cell values and the next lines in. The departure edges and the ceiling carry more minima than the line beside them (218 against 154, 165 against 131, 34 against 0); the floor carries fewer (2,530 against 2,772, then 6,280 and 10,319).
- **[Certain] The view tail.** 3,397 bodies' catalog minima and 5,644 bodies' stored windows lie after the porkchop view's last departure; S17-F2's five-body incidence of 0 was a small sample.
- **[Likely] The inward-rise bounds.** For the 2,718 edge instances whose neighbour is not stored, the rise is at least the spread of the five (floor median ≥ 1.754 km²/s²), exact wherever the neighbour's solve converged. Slice 18's independent re-solve of five bodies confirms the premise on 13 unstored neighbours.
- **[Likely] The pile-up is not the near-co-orbital drift story.** If slowly drifting bodies near 1 AU were the cause, the edge rate would climb with synodic period and the co-orbital flag would concentrate it; it is nearly flat (5.4–10.3%) and the flagged co-orbitals are below average.

What is rough:
- **[Speculative] Whether any edge truncates minima, and how many.** The counts show pile-ups at three edges and none at the floor; neither is a measurement of truncation. The first build called the floor "the binding edge" from raw counts; round-1 review showed that the floor is simply the longest edge and that minima crowd toward short flight times (the row profile peaks at 272 days), and the claim is withdrawn.
- **[Likely, under a local-quadratic model] The vertex split.** 41 beyond, 44 inside, 6 without a vertex, over only 91 chains: the departure edges and the ceiling. No floor body stores a chain of three, so the model says nothing there.
- **[Speculative] What lies below 182 days.** For 2,529 floor bodies the cache bounds the rise but holds no cell beyond. 1,137 store a window on the constant-arrival-date line running toward the floor, so a single shorter flight at the same date would not settle it; the page sizes a 50,600-solve probe that follows the line (152 and 122 days, ten departure dates) and says it would bound, not settle. This study does not run it.
- **[Speculative] Why the floor share falls with H.** The monotone trend across the five H bands is measured; the cause is not. A reading: faint bodies enter the catalog because they were seen close to Earth, which puts a cheap interior window on the lattice, while bright, long-known bodies on wider orbits reach their cheapest single-revolution transfer at short flight times. The census does not test this.
- **[Certain] Basin counts depend on the rule, and the proposal's box is a poor fit for this lattice.** A 30-day row is 4.3 departure columns, so the box splits constant-arrival-date valleys; under the arrival-aware rule most bodies have one or two basins. The arrival-aware rule has its own blind spot (it separates the same date one row apart), so the page shows all four rules and names the rule in every label.
- **[Likely] The year-level excess cannot be split cleanly** into "truncation" and "the catalog is richest near its selection epoch" (the catalog's `closeApproachWindow` is 2026-05-01 to 2026-07-30; many small bodies were found near their 2026 approaches). The strict per-date edge counts are clean; the three-year opening ramp is not attributed.
- **[Certain] Static data.** The page reads a snapshot of the cache at `62ddb17`; if Aster regenerates the cache, `edge-census.mjs` must be re-run and the live-checkout tests will say so (the committed-blob check fails first).
- The 20-sample inspector is the hand-check surface; there is no per-body lookup for the other 41,886 bodies, by design (see alternatives). No real person has used the page.
