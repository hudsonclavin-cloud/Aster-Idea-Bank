# Two Seconds at the Bottom
> At the bottom of its swing the Rev A tether's lower tip pulls away from a vehicle matched to it at 20.34 m/s² (2.07 g), so a passive vehicle stays inside ±10 m for 1.98 s and inside ±1 m/s for 0.10 s; the founding document's 3–10 s window is a station-keeping bill of 61–203 m/s at 2.07 g, computed from `params/rev_a.json` alone.

**Kind:** explorable
**For:** Skyhook
**Run:** open `ideas/09-two-seconds-at-bottom/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network, no build). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/09-two-seconds-at-bottom/catchwindow.test.mjs` (20 tests, Node built-ins, under 1 s) and `node ideas/09-two-seconds-at-bottom/ui-check.mjs` (69 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). `node ideas/09-two-seconds-at-bottom/build-data.mjs --check` confirms the data wrapper is current.
**Made by:** Plumbline

## What it is

Skyhook's founding document asks the catch to hold "relative position within meters and relative velocity within ~1 m/s at the moment of capture, sustained through a window of 3–10 seconds" (`docs/project_instructions.md:124`, §4.3). Its glossary says the window's duration is "set by tether length, tip speed, and the vehicle's trajectory accuracy" (`docs/glossary.md:11-12`), and the program site's Monte Carlo takes the window as a slider (`tools/sims/skyhook_program_site.html:1336`). Nothing in the repository computes it. This page does, from the tether's own kinematics, and prices what it costs to make it longer.

You pick a tier from the Rev A canon (2.5 km/s reference, 2.8 km/s cislunar option, 3.5 km/s Phase 3), a half-length (canon 320 km, movable), a capture envelope (±10 m by default), a velocity tolerance (±1 m/s by default) and a held window (3 or 10 s at a tap). The page returns:

- the **relative acceleration** between the lower tip and a ballistic vehicle matched to it at the bottom of the swing, a_rel = v_tip²/L − μ/R² + μ/(R−L)², and the tip's own inertial acceleration;
- the **passive windows**: how long the vehicle stays inside the envelope (±√(2e/a_rel)) and inside the velocity tolerance (±v_tol/a_rel), which of the two binds, the speed at which it leaves the envelope, and the best any passive trajectory can do (graze the far edge: 4√(e/a_rel), arriving at 2√(a_rel e));
- what the 3 s and 10 s requirements would **need passively** (envelope ±a_rel(T/2)²/2, edge speed a_rel T/2) and what they **cost held**: thrust at a_rel for the whole window, Δv = a_rel T, as a share of the catch speed, and the equivalent if a reel at the tip pays instead;
- a sketch of the bottom of the swing in the centre-of-mass frame, a two-panel timeline of separation and relative speed with the envelope and tolerance as bands, the passive window against half-length with the §4.3 line drawn, a tier table, a provenance table in which every number names its key or its equation, the Earth-rotation convention question, and what the rigid rotor leaves out, quantified from the twin's golden run;
- structured refusals, never a placeholder: a missing canon, a non-positive input, a half-length that puts the tip at or below the ground. A tip below 150 km is computed and flagged, because a vehicle there is not ballistic.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** `docs/project_instructions.md:48` (§2.2) requires every design iteration's parameter sheet to carry "rendezvous window duration"; `params/rev_a.json` has no such field (grep `window` finds nothing). `docs/glossary.md:11-12` names what sets the window but gives no formula or number; the site's glossary (`skyhook_program_site.html:1434-1435`) defines the catch window as "The 3–10 second interval", which is the requirement restated as a definition. §5.2 (b) and (d) (`:167`) say "the launch vehicle does all the matching" with "margin propellant" and that the envelope is "engineered to be tens of meters wide with multi-second windows by shaping tip trajectory and using the smart grapple's own terminal maneuvering", unquantified. The Monte Carlo (`:1318-1400`) scatters errors against a window literal (`mc-w`, `:1336`, default ±3 s) and FAQ Q2 (`docs/objections_faq.md:12`) leans on it. "Multi-second precision rendezvous at hypersonic closing geometries" is one of the program's six named hard parts (`project_instructions.md:286`, `handoff.md`). The number that makes the hard part quantitative, the relative acceleration at the catch, appears nowhere.

- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *A full rendezvous simulation* with a guided vehicle on a climbing arc, lateral offsets, a tracking law and dispersions. It would need a vehicle model, a guidance law and error budgets, none of which exist in the repository; every input would be invented, and the output would be a capture probability nobody could check. Killed by the honesty rule that a kernel which must invent numbers does not ship.
  - *Re-derive the Monte Carlo with coupled errors* (timing, position and velocity are not independent for a passive vehicle; see Findings). Useful, but it inherits every invented dispersion of the original and answers "what fraction latches", not "what the window is".
  - *A Python function in `physics/skyhook_physics.py`* with a `pytest` pin. That is where this should land, but a deposit here cannot write there, and the explorable form is what makes the point visible: the band the vehicle leaves in a second, the parabola, the 3 s line the curve never reaches.
  - *This.* A rigid rotor and a ballistic vehicle give a closed form in three terms, every one of them traceable to a canon key, that turns a prose requirement into a derived number and a bill. It is the smallest thing that is true, and an exact two-body integration sits beside it to show the closed form is not an approximation that matters.

## How it works

**Constants.** Everything comes from `snapshots/`, copied with `tools/snapshot.mjs` from Skyhook at `34935cb`: `rev_a.json` whole (sha256 `d0828c1e…`), `skyhook_physics.py` whole (read-only evidence, not executed) and `tools/twin/golden.json` whole. `build-data.mjs` wraps them as `snapshots/canon.data.js` (`window.SKYHOOK_CANON` / `module.exports`) so `index.html` can read them from `file://`: the parsed `rev_a.json` and its sidecar; `R_EARTH_KM = 6371.0` (line 40), `G_STD = 9.80665` (line 41) and EQ-3a's `v_earth_rot = 0.465` literal (line 109) regexed from the physics-module snapshot with their line numbers; the twin's t = 0 node states and 600 s means. A test re-derives the wrapper byte for byte and checks every snapshot still hashes to its own sidecar. `index.html` and `app.js` carry no tether constant as static text; a test greps for 21 of them.

**Geometry (SI inside the kernel; the canon is in km).** μ = `orbital.mu_earth_m3s2` = 3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s². CoM altitude h = `orbital.com_altitude_km` = 610 km; half-length L = `geometry.tether_half_length_km` = 320 km; tip speeds from `kinematics.v_tip_kms` (2.5), `kinematics.cislunar_revenue_gated_option.v_tip_kms` (2.8) and `kinematics.phase3_growth_option.v_tip_kms` (3.5). The CoM radius is R = R_E + h = 6981 km with R_E the physics module's 6371 (the radius `tools/twin/physics.js:21`, the golden run and the file's own "r_tip = 7301 km" C3 notes use). The file's `orbital.com_radius_km` is 6988 (idea 01's finding F1); the page discloses it and the kernel can run with it: a_rel moves from 20.336 to 20.334 m/s², 0.01 %, so nothing here depends on which radius is right. Tip radius R − L = 6661 km, tip altitude 290 km = `geometry.tip_altitude_lower_km`.

**The scene [Certain for the equations].** A rigid rotor spinning at ω = v_tip/L = 7.8125×10⁻³ rad/s (period 2πL/v_tip = 13.40 min, the file's 13.4) about a CoM on a circular orbit, v_orb = √(μ/R) = 7556.3 m/s. At the bottom of the swing the lower tip moves at v_orb − v_tip = 5056.311 m/s; the twin's golden run starts from exactly this state (`golden.json` `checkpoints.0.state[0..3]` = [6 661 000 m, 0, 0, 5056.311 m/s]), and the kernel reproduces it to 10⁻¹² relative. A ballistic vehicle at the apex of its arc is matched to the tip there: same place, same velocity.

**The three terms.** The tip's inertial acceleration at the bottom is centripetal minus the hub's gravity, ω²L − μ/R² = 19.531 − 8.179 = 11.352 m/s² outward (ω²L = v_tip²/L is the file's `tip_acceleration_g` 1.99 g, EQ-3c; g₀ = 9.80665 from the module). The vehicle's is gravity at the tip radius, μ/(R−L)² = 8.984 m/s² inward. Tip minus vehicle, radial:

  a_rel = v_tip²/L − μ/R² + μ/(R−L)² = 19.531 − 8.179 + 8.984 = **20.336 m/s² = 2.074 g**.

The gravity-gradient part, μ/(R−L)² − μ/R² = 0.805 m/s², is what the linearised 2μL/R³ = 0.750 would give to 7 %; the kernel uses the exact difference. Both accelerations are radial at the match instant, so to first order the separation is a_rel t²/2 and the relative speed a_rel t.

**Passive windows.** Inside ±e: |t| ≤ √(2e/a_rel) = 0.9917 s for e = 10 m, 1.983 s in all, leaving at √(2 a_rel e) = 20.2 m/s. Inside ±v_tol: |t| ≤ v_tol/a_rel = 0.0492 s for 1 m/s, 0.098 s in all, by which time the separation is v_tol²/(2a_rel) = 2.5 cm. So the velocity tolerance binds, by a factor of twenty. The longest any passive trajectory can stay inside ±e (enter at the top of the band falling, turn at the bottom, leave at the top) is 4√(e/a_rel) = 2.80 s, arriving at 2√(a_rel e) = 28.5 m/s. Scaling: t_pos ∝ √e and 1/√a_rel, t_vel ∝ v_tol and 1/a_rel.

**What §4.3 would need, and what it costs.** A passive window of total duration T needs an envelope of ±a_rel (T/2)²/2 and meets the vehicle at a_rel T/2: for 3 s, ±22.9 m at 30.5 m/s; for 10 s, ±254 m at 102 m/s. (The site's catch sketch injects "a 30 m/s along-track mismatch" as deliberately bad, `:790`; that is 1.48 s of free fall here.) Held instead, the vehicle must thrust at a_rel for the whole window: Δv = a_rel T = 61.0 m/s for 3 s and 203.4 m/s for 10 s, 1.3 % and 4.4 % of the 4.591 km/s ground-relative catch speed, at a thrust-to-weight of 2.07. Who pays is a design choice (the vehicle's engine, the grapple's own thrusters, or a reel at the tip paying line in and out by a_rel (T/2)²/2, 254 m, at up to a_rel T/2, 102 m/s, for 10 s); the bill a_rel × T is physics.

**Against half-length.** a_rel falls as v_tip²/L shrinks, so the window grows as √L: 0.80 s in all at L = 50 km, 1.98 s at 320 km, and with the tip at sea level (L = h = 610 km, a_rel = 11.89 m/s²) 2.59 s inside ±10 m and 0.17 s inside ±1 m/s. No half-length that keeps the tip above the ground reaches a passive 3 s at any of the three tip speeds. The curve is monotonic over the whole range (tested).

**Tiers.** At the canon L: cislunar a_rel = 25.30 m/s² (2.58 g), ±0.889 s / ±0.040 s, 253 m/s per 10 s; Phase 3 a_rel = 39.09 m/s² (3.99 g), ±0.715 s / ±0.026 s, 391 m/s per 10 s. The tip accelerations reproduce the file's 1.99 / 2.5 / 3.9 g at the file's precision and the ground-relative speeds its 4.59 and 3.59 km/s (the file states none for the cislunar tier; it would be 4.29).

**Exact check.** The closed forms are the t → 0 limit. `catchwindow.js` also integrates the real scene: the CoM on a circular Kepler orbit, the tip on a rigid rotor about it (x = R cos Ωt − L cos ωt, y = R sin Ωt − L sin ωt), the vehicle under μ/r² from the matched state by RK4 at 2 ms, and the exact tracking thrust |a_tip − g(r_tip)| integrated by Simpson's rule over a symmetric window. Separation lags a_rel t²/2 by 2×10⁻⁶ at 1 s, 4×10⁻⁵ at 5 s and 1.8×10⁻⁴ at 10 s (1016.6 m against 1016.8); the exit time from ±10 m is 0.99171 s against 0.99170; the exact hold Δv for 10 s is 203.355 m/s against 203.360, and the thrust magnitude moves by 7×10⁻⁵ over ±5 s. The page prints the exit time and the exact Δv next to the closed forms.

**Earth rotation.** EQ-3a (`skyhook_physics.py:95-110`) subtracts a literal 0.465 km/s "at equator": ground-track speed 7556.3 − 2500 − 465 = 4591 m/s, the file's 4.59. The one external constant in this deposit is Earth's sidereal rotation period, 23.9345 h, with the equatorial radius 6378.137 km and volumetric mean radius 6371.000 km, from the NASA NSSDC Earth Fact Sheet (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html, read 2026-10-26, rows quoted verbatim in the kernel). ω_E = 7.2921×10⁻⁵ rad/s gives 465.1 m/s at the equatorial surface, which is where EQ-3a's literal comes from, and 485.7 m/s at the tip radius, so the tip's airspeed in a co-rotating atmosphere is 4571 m/s, 21 m/s less than the ground-track figure. Ground-track is the bookkeeping for what a launch site hands the vehicle; airspeed is the number for drag and heating. The parameter sheet does not say which it means; the page reports both and leaves the choice to a verification memo.

**What the rigid rotor ignores, quantified.** `golden.json` records the twin's mean tip speed over 600 s as 2462.64 m/s, 1.49 % below the 2500 m/s it starts with at the vertical: the gravity-gradient modulation the glossary names (`docs/glossary.md:23-24`, "~±3 %", "depresses its rotation-averaged value ~1.5-2 %"), and the rigid-dumbbell estimate 3Ω²/(2ω²) = 2.9 % peak-to-trough agrees. The spin is fastest when the tether is vertical, which is the catch, so the canon's v_tip is the bottom-of-swing value and 20.34 is the consistent number. If v_tip were instead the rotation mean, the tip would pass the bottom at ~2538 m/s and a_rel would be 20.93 m/s² (+2.9 %), shortening every window by 1.4 %. Elasticity, damping and the catch transient are not modelled; the twin's dynamic load factor is a different question.

**Drawing.** Inline SVG sized to its rendered width (the viewBox is set to the element's pixel width and redrawn on resize), so labels stay 11–12 px on a phone. Colours: series 1 blue for position and the envelope, series 2 orange for velocity and the tolerance, the dataviz reference palette's first two slots, validated in both modes (light `#2a78d6 #eb6834`, dark `#3987e5 #d95926`; worst adjacent CVD ΔE 24.7 / 26.8, all checks pass); the held window is a neutral wash; status chips carry an icon and a word. Crosshair and one tooltip listing both series on hover, tap, or focus with the arrow keys; every value is also in the tables. The sketch stretches the vertical (stated, ×20 at desktop width) because the tip rises 295 m over 27 km of travel.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The equations.** For a rigid rotor on a circular orbit in a point-mass field, the tip's acceleration at the vertical instant is ω²L − μ/R² outward, a ballistic vehicle's is μ/(R−L)² inward, and the relative acceleration is their difference; separation a_rel t²/2 and relative speed a_rel t follow, and an exact integration of the same scene agrees to 2×10⁻⁴ at 10 s. The hold Δv is a_rel T by the same token. The closed forms are checked against hand-worked values, the canon's stated numbers and the twin's golden state.
- **[Certain] At Rev A, a passive vehicle matched at the bottom of the swing is outside ±1 m/s after ±0.05 s and outside ±10 m after ±0.99 s**, and no half-length that keeps the tip above the ground gives a passive 3 s inside ±10 m at any of the three tip speeds. The founding document's 3–10 s is therefore a station-keeping requirement, not a property of the geometry.
- **[Likely] The rigid-rotor numbers describe the Rev A tether to a few percent.** The twin's golden run says the tip speed is modulated by ~1.5 % about its mean and that v_tip is the bottom-of-swing value; elasticity and the catch transient are not in this page at all. A real catch is timed, so the instantaneous value at the vertical is the right one, but a_rel could sit anywhere in 20.3–20.9 m/s² depending on which value a parameter sheet means by v_tip.
- **[Likely] The bill can be paid by someone other than the vehicle.** The reel and grapple columns are the same kinematics with a different actor; whether a tip reel can run at 100 m/s line speed, or a grapple can carry 200 m/s of Δv, is engineering this page does not touch.
- **[Likely] The Earth-rotation conventions.** The arithmetic is exact; the fact-sheet constants are a published reference read on the date given, labelled [Likely] rather than [Certain] because I quote them from a web page, not the IERS conventions. Which convention the program means is for its verification memo.
- **[Speculative] That a 2.07 g hold is how the catch will actually be flown.** A guided vehicle would not hold station symmetrically about apex; it might arrive early and low and fly up the tip's arc, or the grapple might do the last metres. The Δv for any such profile over the same window is still of order a_rel T, but the profile, the margins and the abort logic are design work.
- **[Certain] What is out of scope** and said so on the page: off-apex and climbing vehicles, lateral offsets, atmosphere (the tip at 290 km is flagged clear; below 150 km the ballistic idealisation is marked wrong rather than extrapolated), grapple reach, latch mechanics, reel dynamics, tether elasticity, damping.
- **Rough.** The sketch's vertical exaggeration is a drawing choice and changes with the window; the "best passive" grazing bound is stated, not drawn; the ±3 s default of the Monte Carlo is not reproduced here; the tier table's phone layout is CSS-stacked rather than redesigned; the 150 km scope line is this prototype's choice, not a Skyhook number; sliders are checked by keyboard and number entry in `ui-check.mjs`, not by a synthetic touch drag (native range inputs do not respond reliably to Playwright's touch drag), while buttons, radios and the charts are tapped.
- **Fidelity.** An idea-bank prototype. It has not been through Skyhook's Rule 1 canon process (constants come from a snapshot, not an import through `physics/skyhook_physics.py`), a verification memo, or the `pytest tests/` suite (pytest is not installed in this sandbox and nothing here was run against it).

**Findings about the source repo** (observed in the checkout at `34935cb`; not fixed there):

- **F1. The parameter sheet lacks the field its own instructions require.** `docs/project_instructions.md:48` (§2.2) lists "rendezvous window duration" among the kinematics every design iteration must document; `params/rev_a.json` has no window, catch or relative-acceleration key. [Certain] (gap).
- **F2. Two glossaries, neither derives the window.** `docs/glossary.md:11-12` says duration is "set by tether length, tip speed, and the vehicle's trajectory accuracy"; the program site's glossary (`tools/sims/skyhook_program_site.html:1434-1435`) defines the catch window as "The 3–10 second interval", the §4.3 requirement restated as a definition. For a passive vehicle the length-and-speed dependence is a_rel = v_tip²/L − μ/R² + μ/(R−L)², and the 3–10 s is a hold, not a window. [Certain]
- **F3. The Monte Carlo's errors are independent where the kinematics couple them.** `skyhook_program_site.html:1366-1369` draws lateral position, closing velocity and timing errors independently and passes a trial that is inside all three limits; the window is a literal (`mc-w`, `:1336`, default ±3 s) and the timing error's default 1σ is 1.2 s (`mc-st`, `:1328`). For a passive vehicle a timing error t is not free: it is a_rel t²/2 of position and a_rel t of velocity, so 1.2 s is 14.6 m and 24 m/s at Rev A, outside the tool's own ±10 m and 1 m/s. The independence holds only for a vehicle that holds station through the window, which the site does not state and which costs the 2.07 g / 203 m/s computed here; FAQ Q2 (`docs/objections_faq.md:12`) cites the tool's ">99 %" without that condition.
