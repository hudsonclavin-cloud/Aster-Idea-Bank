Two Seconds at the Bottom

At the bottom of its swing the lower tip of a rotating tether is being pulled up by the tether while a vehicle matched to it keeps falling. This page computes how long a passive vehicle at the apex of its arc stays inside the capture envelope, from Skyhook's Rev A constants alone, and prices the alternative: holding station with the tip for the founding document's 3 to 10 seconds.

What the founding document asks for, priced

"Rendezvous closing accuracy requirement: relative position within meters and relative velocity within ~1 m/s at the moment of capture, sustained through a window of 3–10 seconds" — docs/project_instructions.md:124 (§4.3). Section 5.2 (b) and (d): "the launch vehicle does all the matching" and "the capture envelope is engineered to be tens of meters wide with multi-second windows by shaping tip trajectory and using the smart grapple's own terminal maneuvering" (:167).

A window is not a property of the geometry. For a passive vehicle it is set by the relative acceleration above; to get the stated duration passively the envelope would have to be far wider than "meters" and the grapple would meet the vehicle at tens of metres per second. Otherwise something supplies for the whole window: the vehicle's engine, the grapple's own thrusters, or a reel paying line out from the tip.

The reel row is the same kinematics paid by a line from the tip instead of by the vehicle: a reel that lets the grapple follow a ballistic vehicle over a symmetric window of T must pay the line in and back out by the excursion shown and reach the peak line speed. Who pays is a design choice; the bill is a_rel × T.

What the rigid rotor ignores

Scope

  • A rigid rotor on a circular orbit, a point-mass Earth, and a vehicle exactly at the apex of its arc, matched to the tip at the match instant. Off-apex vehicles, climbing arcs and lateral offsets are out of scope: the page does not extrapolate to them.
  • No atmosphere. At the canon tip altitude drag over a few seconds is small but not zero; it is not computed. Below the altitude flagged above, the "ballistic vehicle" idealisation is wrong and the page says so.
  • No grapple reach, no latch mechanics, no reel dynamics, no tether elasticity, no damping. The dynamic load factor of a catch is a different question (physics/tether_dynamics.py).
  • The 3–10 s window, the ±10 m envelope and the ~1 m/s tolerance are quoted as the program's requirements, with their lines, never as results of this page.
