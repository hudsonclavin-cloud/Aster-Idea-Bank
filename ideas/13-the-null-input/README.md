# The Null Input
> Skyhook's `rev_a.json` says `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg: null` and its finance model prices fiber *launch*, never fiber *purchase*. This sweep adds that line to the Rev A chain (gated at $0/kg to the published $8,741M / Y17 / −$3,883M) and prints a quote-day card: at $300/kg (the MP-001 marketplace proxy, not a quote) the conservative case gains $183M of capex nobody has counted, keeps its Y17 payback, its construction-launch threshold falls from $1,674 to $1,508/kg, and the program-capex substitution that closed the case at $0 stops closing above $156/kg of fiber. With no price it prints no fiber line.

**Kind:** study
**For:** Skyhook
**Run:** from the bank root, `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/fiber_sweep.py` (gate, sweep, thresholds, markers; no card), `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/fiber_sweep.py --quote 300` (the quote-day card at $300/kg; `--multiplier 1.3` for a finished-tether markup; bare `--quote` reads `rev_a` and exits 2 because the key is null; `--json`, `--no-sweep`, `--prices`, `--help`). `example.txt` is saved output of four runs. `ideas/13-the-null-input/index.html` runs the same chain in the browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, fetches nothing; `?p=300` preloads a price, labelled as such). Tests: `python3 -m unittest discover -s ideas/13-the-null-input -p 'test_*.py'` (40 tests, standard library, about 4 s), `node --test ideas/13-the-null-input/model.test.mjs` (11 tests, reconciles the JS port to the Python output) and `node ideas/13-the-null-input/ui-check.mjs` (50 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/build_data.py /path/to/skyhook` re-derives the ladder and marker snapshots and `data.js`; `--check` says whether they are stale.
**Made by:** Tareweight

## What it is

A Python 3 script (and a page that runs the same arithmetic) that answers one question the Skyhook program has flagged since July and cannot yet answer: *what does a fiber quote do to the case?* The repository's own documents say why it cannot. `params/rev_a.json` carries `"fiber_cost_per_kg": null` with the note `"WEAKEST INPUT — no vendor quote; obtain from Toyobo/Avient/Toray"`; `docs/DECISION_LOG.md` item 8 adds that "the financial model currently prices fiber *launch*, not fiber *purchase*, so a quote adds capex not yet in the model"; `finance/skyhook_finance.py` indeed has no purchase line (the word does not occur in it). Three vendor asks went out on 2026-07-02 for a 1 t / 100 t / 600 t price ladder (`docs/outreach_log.md:27-29`); at `34935cb` the handoff still lists Toyobo and FibrXL under "Waiting".

`fiber_sweep.py` does four things, in this order, and stops at the first that fails:

1. **Re-derives the finance chain.** It reads the workbook's three ladder columns from `snapshots/ladder.json` (derived from `tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx`, Assumptions rows 3–16) and the canonical parameters from `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json`, and mirrors `finance/skyhook_finance.py` line for line: EQ-6 fiber mass, launch capex × hardware mass factor, program/fleet capex, reboost electricity, the 15/30/35/20 % capex spread over years 1–4, operations from year 5 with the 0.3 → 0.6 → 1.0 ramp, payback as the first year of positive cumulative cash, NPV over the 15-year window, the closed-form Y15 breakeven customer price.
2. **Gates itself at $0/kg.** Nineteen anchors must reproduce exactly as published: the three workbook triples ($8,741M / Y17 / NPV@10% −$3,883M; $5,772M / Y9; $4,226M / Y7), the `conservative-except-launch@$800` variant ($6,877M / Y14), and the five `rev_a.json` mass ratios and fiber masses (121.83× / 609.1 t, 64.30× / 321.5 t, 40.32× / 201.6 t, 295.27× / 1,476.3 t, 3,085.95× / 15,429.8 t) to the decimals the file prints. One miss and it exits 2 before any fiber line.
3. **Adds the missing line and sweeps it.** Fiber purchase capex = price ($/kg) × multiplier × fiber mass (kg) ÷ 10⁶, added to total capex and spread like every other capex dollar. The hardware mass factor is *not* applied: it scales launched mass (hub, arrays, tips), not fiber bought. The sweep runs $0, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000/kg over five rows (the three columns at 2.5 km/s, the cislunar 2.8 km/s and Phase 3 3.5 km/s tiers on the conservative column) and, for the conservative case, recomputes all eight of `finance/sensitivity.py`'s minimal-relaxation thresholds at each price and the fiber price at which each baseline-value single substitution stops closing the case. The repository's three existing fiber prices are drawn as **markers**, never defaults: MP-001's Zylon marketplace proxy ($300 / $259 / $170, "marketplace listing — low confidence", 10 kg MOQ, "**not** a Toyobo quote"), `finance/inspace_fork.py`'s `SURFACE_FIBER_PRICE_PER_KG = 1000` placeholder, and the strategy simulator's `fiberPriceKg: 250 // [Speculative]`.
4. **Prints the quote-day card, or refuses.** `--quote P` prints, per row, the added capex, the before → after total capex, payback, NPV@10 % and Y15 breakeven price; how the seven conservative thresholds move; which singles still close; and the exact `rev_a.json` key to fill. Bare `--quote` reads `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg` and, finding null, exits 2 with what is missing and what would help, mirroring `inspace_fork.require_non_null`. The default run prints no card at all, only hypotheticals labelled as such.

## Why I made it

The open item, in the repository's own words, with where it is written down at `34935cb`:

- `params/rev_a.json` `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg: null`, `_fiber_cost_note` "WEAKEST INPUT — no vendor quote; obtain from Toyobo/Avient/Toray" (snapshotted here as `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json`).
- `README.md:80`, open item 1: "Fiber vendor quote (Toyobo/Avient/Toray) — largest unpriced capex input | No quote".
- `docs/DECISION_LOG.md:60`, item 8: "fiber vendor quote outstanding … note the financial model currently prices fiber *launch*, not fiber *purchase*, so a quote adds capex not yet in the model".
- `MORNING_REPORT.md:48`, anomaly 5: "Fiber *purchase* cost is still absent from all economics … A Toyobo/Avient/Toray quote would add capex not currently counted anywhere"; `:64` "the entire financial edifice prices no fiber purchase".
- `handoff.md:37-38`: "Waiting (no action needed): Four vendor replies (Rocket Lab / Toyobo / FibrXL / Impulse)"; `docs/outreach_log.md:27-29`: the asks were "Pricing + lead time, 1t/100t/600t ladder" for Zylon (Toyobo, Avient) and T1100G (Toray).

So the quote, when it comes, lands on a model with no row for it, and the person reading it has nothing that says what it changes. That is the gap: not a number, a *slot*, with the arithmetic around it already proven.

What already exists, and why it is not this. `finance/skyhook_finance.py` is the canonical chain and has no purchase line. `finance/inspace_fork.py` does have one (`fiber_purchase_M`, with `require_non_null` and a $200/$500/$1,000 bracket), but for the DRAFT, non-canonical MXER in-space fork on `params/service_mxer_draft.json`, not for the Rev A suborbital program; it also carries its own `[FLAGGED]` hub-mass and integration-margin assumptions, so its numbers are not the workbook's. `tools/sims/skyhook_no_leo_stress_test.jsx:221` computes `fiberCost_M` from a `[Speculative]` $250/kg default, but that value is never used (see findings). `finance/sensitivity.py` has the thresholds and singles but no price axis to move them along.

Alternatives I weighed. (a) Patch `skyhook_finance.py` and the workbook: out of bounds for this bank, and the right version of that patch is a one-line landing (below) once the arithmetic is agreed. (b) Pick a fiber price and report "the" answer: every price in the repository is a marker with a disclaimer on it, and treating one as the answer is exactly the mistake DECISION_LOG item 7 records for the launch price. The script therefore sweeps, labels every row hypothetical, draws the markers as markers, and refuses the card without a price. (c) Build on `inspace_fork.py`'s existing purchase line: wrong program and wrong canon status. (d) A page only: the thing a maintainer needs on quote day is a command that prints a card into the thread; the page is the same arithmetic for the person reading it on a phone, and is reconciled to the script rather than being a second model. This won because the slot is cheap to build, the gate makes it safe to trust, and the thresholds and stop prices are the part no one would work out by hand on the day.

## How it works

**Inputs, and where every number comes from.** `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json` is `params/rev_a.json` cut to its `orbital`, `kinematics`, `materials`, `mass_ratio` and `economics` blocks by the bank's `snapshot.mjs` (sidecar: commit `34935cb`, `readMatchesCommitted: true`). `snapshots/ladder.json` is derived by `build_data.py` from `tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx` with the standard library only (the `.xlsx` is a zip; `xl/workbook.xml` → sheet relationship → `xl/worksheets/sheet2.xml`), using the row map `finance/skyhook_finance.py` itself uses (`_LADDER_ROWS`, rows 3–16, columns B/C/D) and keeping column A's label and column E's source note per row; its `--sidecar-only` sidecar points at the workbook and records its sha256. `snapshots/markers.json` is derived by the same script from the three marker lines (file, line, matched text and commit recorded per marker; sidecar at `finance/inspace_fork.py`, the other two named in its note). `snapshots/SENSITIVITY_FINDINGS.md`, `snapshots/DECISION_LOG.md` and `snapshots/mp001_market_pricing.md` are whole copies, there so the published figures the gate retypes can be checked against the text they came from (a test does that). `data.js` embeds the three JSON snapshots, the published anchors and every sidecar's commit and sha256 for the page, which fetches nothing. Nothing in this folder was typed from memory; the one place I did (an erf table value in a test) was caught by the test and replaced with a computed value.

**The chain** [Certain as a mirror of `finance/skyhook_finance.py` @ `34935cb`; the schedule is the workbook's planning assumption]. V_c = √(2σ/ρ) with Zylon σ 5.8 GPa, ρ 1,560 kg/m³ (2.7269 km/s); v_orb = √(μ/(R_E + 610 km)) with R_E = 6,371 km as `physics/skyhook_physics.py:40` (7.5563 km/s); EQ-6 M/m = 2√π·x·eˣ²·erf(x), x = (V_t/V_c)·√(SF/(1 − derate)), SF 2.5; fiber t = M/m × 5,000 kg; system capex $M = fiber t × 1,000 × launch $/kg × hardware mass factor ÷ 10⁶; total capex = system + program/fleet capex; marginal $/kg = 2·v_orb·v_tip ÷ efficiency ÷ 3.6 × $/kWh + ops/wear/insurance; upmass = cadence × 365 × payload; cash flow per year = revenue × ramp − capex × spread − (variable × ramp + fixed ops) from year 5; payback = first year with cumulative > 0, searched to year 40; NPV = Σ CF_t/(1 + r)^t over 15 years; Y15 breakeven price = (total capex + 11 × fixed ops + variable × Σramp) ÷ (upmass × Σramp), Σramp = 0.3 + 0.6 + 9 × 1.0 = 9.9 for the conservative column. Tiers are discovered the way `load_tiers_from_params` does (any `kinematics` sub-block with a `v_tip_kms`), and a non-reference row carries the module's own PARTIAL TIER MODEL caveat: only tip speed changes. `tests/test_finance.py` in Skyhook accepts this chain within 5 %; this deposit holds it to the published rounding, and, when the checkout and openpyxl are present, to 1e-9 of the live module (that test ran and passed here).

**The purchase line.** `fiber_purchase_M = fiber_t × 1,000 × price × multiplier ÷ 10⁶`, added to total capex, hence spread 15/30/35/20 % over years 1–4, hence inside NPV, payback and the breakeven price. Three consequences are closed forms the tests pin: the cumulative cash at year 15 falls linearly with price, so a single that closes at $0 stops closing at p* = cum₁₅(0) × 1,000 ÷ (fiber_t × m); the launch threshold falls by m ÷ hardware-mass-factor per $/kg of fiber (fiber bought must be paid for by launching fiber_t × 1.8 kg more cheaply); the customer-price threshold *is* the Y15 breakeven price, found here two ways (bisection on payback and the closed form) that agree to 1e-6. Units: $/kg × kg = $, shown in $M; the card's percentage is added ÷ total capex at $0.

**The multiplier** is a knob with no source. A fiber vendor quotes yarn; a tether is woven, jacketed, spliced and cut with waste, and the kilogram the quote prices is not the kilogram EQ-6 counts. 1.00 means "read the quote as the finished-tether price"; the card and the header say so every time. The fiber mass is the canonical Zylon-only EQ-6 mass even though the construction baseline is a Spectra/UHMWPE core with a Zylon outer arm (`materials.construction_baseline`): one price for one mass, labelled.

**Thresholds and singles** reproduce `finance/sensitivity.py`: for each of the eight inputs, bisection (80 iterations, 5× stretch, derate floored at 0, fixed ops at 0) for the value alone that brings payback inside Y15, at the given fiber price; and each conservative input swapped alone to its baseline-column value. One addition: when the base already closes (an option tier can), the threshold is reported as the base value rather than failing the bisection's invariant. A hardware-mass-factor threshold below the optimistic 1.4× is implausible and below 1.0× unphysical; it is printed as the arithmetic says, as the source module prints it, and labelled.

**The page** (`index.html`, `model.js`, `app.js`, `data.js`) runs the same chain in JavaScript. JavaScript has no `erf`; `model.js` carries one (Maclaurin series below x = 3, a continued fraction for erfc above; the Rev A domain is x ∈ [1.45, 2.43]), checked against ten `math.erf` values. `model.test.mjs` reconciles every row, threshold, single and stop price of `reference.json` (`fiber_sweep.py --json --quote 300`) to 1e-9 on money and masses and 1e-6 on bisections, more than 350 numbers, and `test_fiber_sweep.py` checks that `reference.json` is a fresh run, so the page and the script cannot drift apart silently. The page shows the refusal at load, a structured one (`null_input`, the file's own note, what would help), five marker chips that fill the price and label the card "marker, not a quote", the gate table, two charts (relaxation required per lever against price; the stop price of each single as a bar, markers dotted) redrawn at the container's width so the text stays readable at 390 px, the sweep and threshold tables, the markers with their file and line, and the provenance list. The CLI's output carries no date or absolute path, so two runs are byte-identical (a test checks).

## Honest assessment

- [Certain] The chain is `finance/skyhook_finance.py`'s, not an approximation of it: the 19 published anchors reproduce to the printed decimals, `rev_a.json`'s own 686 and 517 fall out of it, and the live module agrees to 1e-9 when imported. The purchase line is one multiplication added where DECISION_LOG item 8 says it is missing, and the three closed forms say what it does without any sweep at all.
- [Certain] Every fiber price shown is hypothetical and labelled; the three repository prices are drawn as markers with their own disclaimers; the card refuses without a price; no marker is a default anywhere in the code (a test greps for that). The multiplier is unsourced and says so on every line it touches.
- [Certain] The option-tier rows inherit `skyhook_finance.py`'s PARTIAL TIER MODEL caveat: cislunar and Phase 3 keep the reference column's price, cadence, launch price and capex, so their rows are the canonical module's tier ladder with a fiber line added, not a cislunar business case.
- [Likely] The useful result is not the capex number but the thresholds and stop prices: that program capex → $5,000M stops rescuing the case at $156/kg of fiber, and launch → $1,500 at $314/kg, is what a maintainer would not have worked out on quote day. ## Where it would land

Skyhook, in two parts. The slot: a `fiber_purchase_per_kg: float | None` field on `ScenarioInputs` in `finance/skyhook_finance.py`, read from a new Assumptions row ("Fiber purchase price ($/kg)", null until quoted) and from `params/rev_a.json` `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg`, with a `fiber_purchase_M()` guarded like `inspace_fork.require_non_null` and added to `total_capex_M()`; `tests/test_finance.py`'s reconciliation is unchanged while the field is null, and a new test asserts the three closed forms (stop price, launch-threshold slope, price threshold = breakeven) so the line cannot be added in the wrong place.
