# The Null Input
> Skyhook's `rev_a.json` says `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg: null` and its finance model prices fiber *launch*, never fiber *purchase*. This sweep adds that line to the Rev A chain (gated at $0/kg to the published $8,741M / Y17 / −$3,883M) and prints a quote-day card: at $300/kg of Zylon PBO (the MP-001 marketplace proxy, not a quote) the conservative case gains $183M of capex the Rev A model has not counted, keeps its Y17 payback, its construction-launch threshold falls from $1,674 to $1,508/kg, and the program-capex substitution that closed the case at $0 stops closing above $156/kg of fiber. With no price, or a quote for another fiber, it prints no fiber line.

**Kind:** study
**For:** Skyhook
**Run:** from the bank root, `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/fiber_sweep.py` (gate, sweep, thresholds, markers; no card), `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/fiber_sweep.py --quote 300` (the quote-day card at $300/kg; `--multiplier 1.3` for a finished-tether markup; `--material uhmwpe|carbon|other` refuses a quote for a fiber other than Zylon PBO; bare `--quote` reads `rev_a` and exits 2 because the key is null; `--json`, `--no-sweep`, `--prices`, `--markers ''`, `--help`). `example.txt` is saved output of five runs. `ideas/13-the-null-input/index.html` runs the same chain in the browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, fetches nothing; `?p=300` preloads a price, labelled as such). Tests: `python3 -m unittest discover -s ideas/13-the-null-input -p 'test_*.py'` (48 tests, standard library, about 8 s), `node --test ideas/13-the-null-input/model.test.mjs` (13 tests, reconciles the JS port to the Python output) and `node ideas/13-the-null-input/ui-check.mjs` (64 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). `python3 ideas/13-the-null-input/build_data.py /path/to/skyhook` re-derives the ladder and marker snapshots and `data.js`; `--check` says whether they are stale.
**Made by:** Tareweight

## What it is

A Python 3 script (and a page that runs the same arithmetic) that answers one question the Skyhook program has flagged since July and cannot yet answer: *what does a fiber quote do to the case?* The repository's own documents say why it cannot. `params/rev_a.json` carries `"fiber_cost_per_kg": null` with the note `"WEAKEST INPUT — no vendor quote; obtain from Toyobo/Avient/Toray"`; `docs/DECISION_LOG.md` item 8 adds that "the financial model currently prices fiber *launch*, not fiber *purchase*, so a quote adds capex not yet in the model"; `finance/skyhook_finance.py` indeed has no purchase line (the word does not occur in it). Which quotes are outstanding is in `docs/outreach_log.md` (snapshotted here): on 2026-07-02 Toyobo and Avient were asked for Zylon PBO "Pricing + lead time, 1t/100t/600t ladder" and Toray for T1100G carbon "Availability + pricing, 1t qualification → larger run" (`:27-29`); the 31-Aug reconciliation in the same file (`:5-17`) gave Toyobo a second touch by web form, added FibrXL for **Dyneema SK78 (UHMWPE, not PBO)**, and retired Toray ("Reclassify as retired, not pending"). At `34935cb` `handoff.md:38` lists Toyobo and FibrXL among the four replies "Waiting"; Avient's July row still reads "awaiting response".

`fiber_sweep.py` does four things, in this order, and stops at the first that fails:

1. **Re-derives the finance chain.** It reads the workbook's three ladder columns from `snapshots/ladder.json` (derived from `tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx`, Assumptions rows 3–16) and the canonical parameters from `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json`, and mirrors `finance/skyhook_finance.py` line for line: EQ-6 fiber mass, launch capex × hardware mass factor, program/fleet capex, reboost electricity, the 15/30/35/20 % capex spread over years 1–4, operations from year 5 with the 0.3 → 0.6 → 1.0 ramp, payback as the first year of positive cumulative cash, NPV over the 15-year window, the closed-form Y15 breakeven customer price.
2. **Gates itself at $0/kg.** Nineteen anchors must reproduce exactly as published: the three workbook triples ($8,741M / Y17 / NPV@10% −$3,883M; $5,772M / Y9; $4,226M / Y7), the `conservative-except-launch@$800` variant ($6,877M / Y14), and the five `rev_a.json` mass ratios and fiber masses (121.83× / 609.1 t, 64.30× / 321.5 t, 40.32× / 201.6 t, 295.27× / 1,476.3 t, 3,085.95× / 15,429.8 t) to the decimals the file prints. One miss and it exits 2 before any fiber line.
3. **Adds the missing line and sweeps it.** Fiber purchase capex = price ($/kg) × multiplier × fiber mass (kg) ÷ 10⁶, added to total capex and spread like every other capex dollar. The hardware mass factor is *not* applied: it scales launched mass (hub, arrays, tips), not fiber bought. The sweep runs $0, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1,000, 3,000/kg over five rows (the three columns at 2.5 km/s, the cislunar 2.8 km/s and Phase 3 3.5 km/s tiers on the conservative column) and, for the conservative case, recomputes all eight of `finance/sensitivity.py`'s minimal-relaxation thresholds at each price, the exact fiber price above which each threshold no longer exists, and the fiber price at which each baseline-value single substitution stops closing the case. The repository's fiber prices, nine of them from three sources, are drawn as **markers**, never defaults: MP-001's Zylon marketplace proxy ($300 / $259 / $170, "marketplace listing — low confidence", 10 kg MOQ, "**not** a Toyobo quote", `docs/research/mp001_market_pricing.md:24`); `finance/inspace_fork.py`'s bracket `FIBER_PRICE_BRACKET_PER_KG = (200.0, 500.0, 1000.0)` (`:54`), whose top is the `SURFACE_FIBER_PRICE_PER_KG = 1000.0` placeholder (`:55`), repeated in `params/service_mxer_draft.json:34-39` because that script writes the file (`inspace_fork.py:820`); and the strategy simulator's `fiberPriceKg: 250 // [Speculative]` default (`tools/sims/skyhook_no_leo_stress_test.jsx:54`) with its conservative and optimistic presets, 400 and 150 (`:325`, `:327`).
4. **Prints the quote-day card, or refuses.** `--quote P` prints, per row, the added capex, the before → after total capex, payback, NPV@10 % and Y15 breakeven price; how the seven conservative thresholds move; which singles still close; the material it priced (Zylon PBO, σ 5.8 GPa, ρ 1,560 kg/m³); and the exact `rev_a.json` key to fill. It refuses with exit 2, giving a code, what is missing and what would help, in three cases: bare `--quote` finds `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg` null (`null_input`, mirroring `inspace_fork.require_non_null`); `--material` names a fiber other than PBO, such as FibrXL's SK78 (`material_not_in_canon`); or `--quote` is not a finite non-negative number, or price × multiplier × fiber mass overflows a double (`invalid_input`). It also exits 2 when the gate fails (printing the BAD anchors), on a `--multiplier` that is not finite and positive or a `--prices` entry that is not finite and non-negative (argument errors), and on unreadable inputs. The default run prints no card at all, only hypotheticals labelled as such.

A fiber quote below $2,500/kg costs less than launching that fiber (the conservative construction-launch price). At marketplace-proxy prices the program's "largest unpriced capex input" (README open item 1) is 2 % of conservative capex, and at $1,000/kg it is 7 %.

## Why I made it

The open item, in the repository's own words, with where it is written down at `34935cb`:

- `params/rev_a.json` `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg: null`, `_fiber_cost_note` "WEAKEST INPUT — no vendor quote; obtain from Toyobo/Avient/Toray" (snapshotted here as `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json`).
- `README.md:80`, open item 1: "Fiber vendor quote (Toyobo/Avient/Toray) — largest unpriced capex input | No quote".
- `docs/DECISION_LOG.md:60`, item 8: "fiber vendor quote outstanding … note the financial model currently prices fiber *launch*, not fiber *purchase*, so a quote adds capex not yet in the model".
- `MORNING_REPORT.md:48`, anomaly 5: "Fiber *purchase* cost is still absent from all economics … A Toyobo/Avient/Toray quote would add capex not currently counted anywhere"; `:64` "the entire financial edifice prices no fiber purchase".
- `handoff.md:37-38`: "Waiting (no action needed): Four vendor replies (Rocket Lab / Toyobo / FibrXL / Impulse)". `docs/outreach_log.md:27-29` and `:5-17` say what each fiber reply will price: Toyobo and Avient, Zylon PBO, asked for a 1 t / 100 t / 600 t ladder (Toyobo touched again in August); FibrXL, Dyneema SK78, a UHMWPE, asked in August; Toray, T1100G carbon, retired.

So the quote, when it comes, lands on a model with no row for it, and the person reading it has nothing that says what it changes. That is the gap: not a number, a *slot*, with the arithmetic around it already proven.

What already exists, and why it is not this. `finance/skyhook_finance.py` is the canonical chain and has no purchase line. `finance/inspace_fork.py` does have one (`fiber_purchase_M`, `:219`, with `require_non_null` and the $200/$500/$1,000 bracket), but for the DRAFT, non-canonical MXER in-space fork on `params/service_mxer_draft.json`, not for the Rev A suborbital program; it also carries its own `[FLAGGED]` hub-mass and integration-margin assumptions, so its numbers are not the workbook's. `tools/sims/skyhook_no_leo_stress_test.jsx:221` computes `fiberCost_M` from a `[Speculative]` $250/kg default, but that value is never used (see findings). `finance/sensitivity.py` has the thresholds and singles but no price axis to move them along.

Alternatives I weighed. (a) Patch `skyhook_finance.py` and the workbook: out of bounds for this bank, and the right version of that patch is a one-line landing (below) once the arithmetic is agreed. (b) Pick a fiber price and report "the" answer: every price in the repository is a marker with a disclaimer on it, and treating one as the answer is exactly the mistake DECISION_LOG item 7 records for the launch price. The script therefore sweeps, labels every row hypothetical, draws the markers as markers, and refuses the card without a price. (c) Build on `inspace_fork.py`'s existing purchase line: wrong program and wrong canon status. (d) A page only: the thing a maintainer needs on quote day is a command that prints a card into the thread; the page is the same arithmetic for the person reading it on a phone, and is reconciled to the script rather than being a second model. This won because the slot is cheap to build, the gate makes it safe to trust, and the thresholds and stop prices are the part no one would work out by hand on the day.

## How it works

**Inputs, and where every number comes from.** `snapshots/rev_a.excerpt.json` is `params/rev_a.json` cut to its `orbital`, `kinematics`, `materials`, `mass_ratio` and `economics` blocks by the bank's `snapshot.mjs` (sidecar: commit `34935cb`, `readMatchesCommitted: true`). `snapshots/ladder.json` is derived by `build_data.py` from `tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx` with the standard library only (the `.xlsx` is a zip; `xl/workbook.xml` → sheet relationship → `xl/worksheets/sheet2.xml`), using the row map `finance/skyhook_finance.py` itself uses (`_LADDER_ROWS`, rows 3–16, columns B/C/D) and keeping column A's label and column E's source note per row; its `--sidecar-only` sidecar points at the workbook and records its sha256. Idea 08 (Blue Cells, `ideas/08-blue-cells/ladder_export.py`) built a fuller reader of the same ladder at the same time. Its `snapshots/ladder.json` and this one agree on all 42 cells and on the workbook's sha256 (compared once while fixing this deposit; no test couples the two folders), and a Skyhook landing needs neither, because openpyxl is already the finance module's dependency. `snapshots/markers.json` is derived by the same script from the three marker sources (file, the line of each price, matched text and commit recorded per marker; sidecar at `finance/inspace_fork.py`, the other two named in its note). `snapshots/SENSITIVITY_FINDINGS.md`, `snapshots/DECISION_LOG.md`, `snapshots/mp001_market_pricing.md` and `snapshots/outreach_log.md` are whole copies, there so the published figures the gate retypes and the vendor facts the material refusal states can be checked against the text they came from (tests do both). `data.js` embeds the three JSON snapshots, the published anchors and every sidecar's commit and sha256 for the page, which fetches nothing. Nothing in this folder was typed from memory; the one place I did (an erf table value in a test) was caught by the test and replaced with a computed value.

**The chain** [Certain as a mirror of `finance/skyhook_finance.py` @ `34935cb`; the schedule is the workbook's planning assumption]. V_c = √(2σ/ρ) with Zylon σ 5.8 GPa, ρ 1,560 kg/m³ (2.7269 km/s); v_orb = √(μ/(R_E + 610 km)) with R_E = 6,371 km as `physics/skyhook_physics.py:40` (7.5563 km/s); EQ-6 M/m = 2√π·x·eˣ²·erf(x), x = (V_t/V_c)·√(SF/(1 − derate)), SF 2.5; fiber t = M/m × 5,000 kg; system capex $M = fiber t × 1,000 × launch $/kg × hardware mass factor ÷ 10⁶; total capex = system + program/fleet capex; marginal $/kg = 2·v_orb·v_tip ÷ efficiency ÷ 3.6 × $/kWh + ops/wear/insurance; upmass = cadence × 365 × payload; cash flow per year = revenue × ramp − capex × spread − (variable × ramp + fixed ops) from year 5; payback = first year with cumulative > 0, searched to year 40; NPV = Σ CF_t/(1 + r)^t over 15 years; Y15 breakeven price = (total capex + 11 × fixed ops + variable × Σramp) ÷ (upmass × Σramp), Σramp = 0.3 + 0.6 + 9 × 1.0 = 9.9 for the conservative column. Tiers are discovered the way `load_tiers_from_params` does (any `kinematics` sub-block with a `v_tip_kms`), and a non-reference row carries the module's own PARTIAL TIER MODEL caveat: only tip speed changes. `tests/test_finance.py` in Skyhook accepts this chain within 5 %; this deposit holds it to the published rounding, and, when the checkout and openpyxl are present, to 1e-9 of the live module (that test ran and passed here).

**The purchase line.** `fiber_purchase_M = fiber_t × 1,000 × price × multiplier ÷ 10⁶`, added to total capex, hence spread 15/30/35/20 % over years 1–4, hence inside NPV, payback and the breakeven price. Four consequences are closed forms the tests pin. The cumulative cash at year 15 falls linearly with price, so any case that closes at $0 stops closing at p* = cum₁₅(0) × 1,000 ÷ (fiber_t × m), and every stop price scales as 1/m. The launch threshold falls by m ÷ hardware-mass-factor per $/kg of fiber (the fiber bought must be paid for by launching fiber_t × 1.8 kg more cheaply). It therefore reaches the 5× stretch's floor, and stops existing, at (L*(0) − L₀/5) × hmf ÷ m. The customer-price threshold *is* the Y15 breakeven price, found here two ways (bisection on payback and the closed form) that agree to 1e-6. Units: $/kg × kg = $, shown in $M; the card's percentage is added ÷ total capex at $0.

**The material and the multiplier.** Every fiber mass here is the canonical EQ-6 mass for `materials.primary_fiber`, Zylon PBO (σ 5.8 GPa, ρ 1,560 kg/m³), even though the construction baseline is a Spectra/UHMWPE core with a Zylon outer arm (`materials.construction_baseline`): one price for one mass, and the header, the card and the page all say which. Only the Toyobo and Avient replies would price that fiber. FibrXL's will be for Dyneema SK78, a UHMWPE with a different strength and density, and `rev_a.json` carries no EQ-6 inputs for it: `materials.alternative_fibers` has `Dyneema_SK99_GPa: 3.6` with no density, nothing for SK78, and T1100G's density only in a free-text note. So `--material uhmwpe` (or `carbon`, `other`; the page has the same selector) refuses with `material_not_in_canon` rather than multiply an SK78 price by a PBO mass. The default is `pbo`, so a user who forgets the flag still gets the material named on the card. The **multiplier** is a knob with no source. A fiber vendor quotes yarn; a tether is woven, jacketed, spliced and cut with waste, and the kilogram the quote prices is not the kilogram EQ-6 counts. 1.00 means "read the quote as the finished-tether price"; the card and the header say so every time.

**Thresholds, vanish prices and singles** reproduce `finance/sensitivity.py`. For each of the eight inputs, bisection (80 iterations, 5× stretch, derate floored at 0, fixed ops at 0) finds the value alone that brings payback inside Y15 at the given fiber price; each conservative input is also swapped alone to its baseline-column value. Two additions. When the base already closes (an option tier can), the threshold is reported as the base value rather than failing the bisection's invariant. And for each input, the **vanish price** is the fiber price above which no value within the stretch closes the case. It is the stop price of the stretch's far end, found by the same bisection on price over $0–$20,000/kg. At ×1.00 it is $2,114 for launch and the hardware factor, $4,606 for derate, $6,394 for program capex and $7,550 for payload; above $20,000 for price and cadence (the closed form puts them at $57,836 and $55,748); and never for fixed ops, which cannot close the case even at zero. The CLI prints the vanish prices under the threshold table; the page lists them in the chart legend and the threshold note, and ends a relaxation-chart line at its vanish price when that falls inside the chart's range (launch and the hardware factor). A hardware-mass-factor threshold below the optimistic 1.4× is implausible and below 1.0× unphysical. It is printed as the arithmetic says, as the source module prints it, and labelled.

**The page** (`index.html`, `model.js`, `app.js`, `data.js`) runs the same chain in JavaScript. JavaScript has no `erf`; `model.js` carries one (Maclaurin series below x = 3, a continued fraction for erfc above; the Rev A domain is x ∈ [1.45, 2.43]), checked against ten `math.erf` values. Above 1e21 JavaScript prints numbers in exponent notation, so the formatter takes exact digits from `BigInt`, which is what Python's `format` prints (checked by hand once at 6.09e299; the test checks that the digits are `BigInt`'s). `model.test.mjs` reconciles every row, threshold, vanish price, single and stop price of `reference.json` (`fiber_sweep.py --json --quote 300 --markers -`, stored without indentation; the marker rows are the same `summarize()` at nine more prices and are left out to keep the fixture at 38 KB) to 1e-9 on money and masses and 1e-6 on bisections, more than 350 numbers. `test_fiber_sweep.py` checks that `reference.json` holds exactly a fresh run's values, so the page and the script cannot drift apart silently. The page shows the refusal at load, a structured one (`null_input`, the file's own note, what would help). It also has a material selector that refuses non-PBO quotes, nine marker chips that fill the price and label the card "marker, not a quote", and the gate table. Two charts follow: the relaxation each lever needs as price rises, where a line whose vanish price falls inside the chart's $3,000 range ends exactly there, and the stop price of each single as a bar, with markers dotted. Both are redrawn at the container's width so the text stays readable at 390 px. Then come the sweep and threshold tables, the markers with their file and lines, and the provenance list. The CLI's output carries no date or absolute path, so two runs are byte-identical (a test checks).

## Where it would land

Skyhook, in two parts. **The slot.** The price lives in one place, `params/rev_a.json` `economics.fiber_cost_per_kg`, read by `finance/skyhook_finance.py` through the physics module's `PARAMS`, as it already reads tiers and materials. The workbook does not get a second copy. If its Assumptions sheet shows the price, the row mirrors `rev_a.json`, and a reconciliation test fails when the two differ, the same pattern finding 2 asks for on the cislunar keys. `tests/test_finance.py`'s reconciliation is unchanged while the value is null. A new test asserts the four closed forms (stop price, launch-threshold slope, the threshold's vanish price, price threshold = breakeven), so the line cannot be added in the wrong place. **The study.** `finance/fiber_price_sweep.py` would import `load_ladder_from_workbook`, `summarize` and `sensitivity.relaxation_threshold` instead of re-deriving them. `finance/FIBER_PRICE_FINDINGS.md` would carry the table above and the markers section and be regenerated when the quote lands.

What must change to ship for real. The snapshots go: the module reads `params/rev_a.json` and the workbook live, and this folder's `build_data.py` xlsx reader (like idea 08's) becomes unnecessary because openpyxl is already the module's dependency.
