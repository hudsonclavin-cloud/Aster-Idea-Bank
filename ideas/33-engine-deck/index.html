        How this works

        The thrust chamber is ideas 31 and 32, copied unmodified: chemical equilibrium in an infinite-area combustor, then isentropic expansion through the nozzle (shifting or frozen) to the area ratio, with idea 32's divergence efficiency λ. At every throttle setting the page re-runs both at the new chamber pressure, at the same mixture ratio and geometry, so the gas chemistry changes with throttle as it should.

        Delivered, not ideal. Real engines fall short of the ideal (idea 32 measured 2.1 to 9.0 % in Isp across twelve engines). Two factors carry that here: c*del = ηc* c*, which sets the mass flow ṁ = pcAt/c*del, and CF,vac,del = ηCF λ CF,vac. A preset is calibrated so both reproduce its published thrust and Isp at one condition (SP-8120 Table I). Above 1 they are calibration factors, not efficiencies: the table's pressures and throat areas are not all on one basis. Thrust at ambient pa is then F = Fvac − paAe, while the nozzle flows full by SP-8120's criterion; past it the thrust is refused.

        The turbopumps. A pump needs ṁ Δp/(ρ ηp), with the densities idea 31 measured. A turbine gives ṁt ηt Δhs, where Δhs is the isentropic enthalpy drop over its pressure ratio: idea 31's shifting equilibrium for gas it can compute (fuel-rich hydrogen-oxygen, for instance), NASA SP-8110's tabulated gas where idea 31 must refuse (fuel-rich kerosene would deposit graphite), and ideal hydrogen for an expander. A gas generator burns a little propellant to drive the turbine, and its exhaust bypasses the chamber (some engines dump it overboard, some, like the F-1 per SP-8120 Table I, duct it along the nozzle extension): the page solves for the flow that balances the pumps, and brackets the engine's Isp between dumping it with no thrust and expanding it completely. In a staged-combustion or expander engine the turbine sits between the pumps and the chamber, so a higher turbine pressure ratio needs higher pump pressures: the page solves for the pressure ratio that balances, or says the cycle cannot close. Every pressure-ladder ratio, efficiency and inlet temperature comes from the reference engine you choose (NASA SP-8107's 1973 tables, or the RL10A-3-3A cycle in NASA TM-107318), held fixed over the throttle range.

        How far to trust it

          Confidence classes: measured is a value from a measured or published source; derived is computed here from such inputs; assumed is your input or a choice. Every number on the page traces to the kernel (engine.js), ideas 31 and 32's kernels, a snapshot, or a cited NASA document (see the README). This is an idea-bank prototype; it has not been through Aster's multi-agent math audit.
