# Engine Deck
> Move the throttle and the altitude of a whole liquid-rocket engine (F-1, H-1, J-2, Atlas booster, RL10, a throttling sled engine, or the chamber and nozzle you built in ideas 31 and 32) and read thrust, specific impulse, mass flow, where the nozzle stops flowing full, and the turbopump power balance of its gas-generator, staged-combustion or expander cycle. Against NASA's own figures it predicts sea-level thrust from altitude thrust to within 0.3 %, a 39 % throttle point to +0.32 % in thrust and +3.65 % in Isp, and the RL10A-3-3A's expander pressure ratio as 1.4038 against 1.402. Turbines are its weak link: their power comes out 14.6 % under to 17.1 % over SP-8107's published figures.

## What it is

Idea 33, module 3 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 31 burns the propellant, idea 32 expands it through a nozzle; this module makes it an engine: something with a throttle, an altitude, a mass flow, and turbopumps that have to be paid for.

It opens on the F-1, every input from NASA SP-8120's Table I (via idea 32's transcription) and every turbopump figure from NASA SP-8107. You choose:

- **an engine**: six real ones (F-1, H-1, J-2, LR89-NA-7 Atlas booster, RL10A-3-3, and the YLR113-AJ-1 throttling sled engine), each calibrated to its own published thrust and Isp, or **idea 32's handoff** (paste, or open on its `#h=` link), whose chamber and nozzle are recomputed with ideas 31 and 32's kernels and checked against the handoff before anything is used. A nozzle that idea 32 did not size can be sized here for a vacuum thrust;
- **the delivered performance**: a c\* efficiency and a thrust-coefficient efficiency (calibrated values, ideal, or typed), and shifting or frozen nozzle flow;
- **the cycle**: a gas generator with the F-1's, H-1's, Atlas booster's or J-2's (two turbines in series) parameters, staged combustion with the SSME's (1973 design values, fuel-rich; oxidizer-rich if you type a preburner temperature), an expander with the RL10A-3-3A's, or none; and, for closed cycles, a turbine inlet temperature of your own;
- **the operating point**: throttle 10 to 120 % of nominal chamber pressure and altitude 0 to 86 km (1976 standard atmosphere) or vacuum, by slider, typed value, or by tapping or dragging either chart.

The page shows, each with a confidence chip: thrust and specific impulse at that point (engine-level, bracketed for a gas generator whose exhaust may or may not give thrust), mass flow split into oxidizer, fuel and gas generator, exit against ambient pressure with SP-8120's separation verdict and the altitude below which it separates at this throttle, vacuum performance, the ideal before losses, and **the lowest throttle that still flows full at sea level**. Two charts (Isp or thrust against altitude, and against throttle) shade the separated region. The **power balance** shows the pumps against the turbines, the gas-generator flow or the turbine pressure ratio and pump discharge pressure a closed cycle needs, each turbine's drop and power, and a third chart of that across the throttle range, with the throttle settings where the cycle cannot close shaded. **Against the published record** runs every check below live, with every SP-8107 pump and turbine row. **Import/Export** of the series handoff carries idea 32's handoff (and so idea 31's) inside it.

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 33: "A whole engine: thrust and Isp against altitude and throttle, mass flows, and the pump-fed cycle power balance (gas generator, staged combustion, expander) with real engines as presets compared to their published figures", handing on an engine deck. Module 34 (Stack) needs thrust, Isp, mass flow, mixture ratio and densities at the engine level; module 35 (Max Q) needs thrust against ambient pressure and throttle. Idea 32's README told this module what to take: "C<sub>F</sub> at any ambient while flowing full is `thrust_coefficient_vacuum_<model>` − `area_ratio` × p<sub>a</sub>/`chamber_pressure`, valid below `separation_onset_ambient_pressure_<model>`; throttling changes p<sub>c</sub>, so 33 re-runs idea 31's chamber from `inputs` and this kernel's `analyze()`". That is what it does.
- **Idea 32 left a gap here.** Its real engines delivered 2.1 to 9.0 % less Isp than the shifting ideal, and it marked as [Speculative] "whether Table I's specific impulses are engine or thrust-chamber values". With SP-8107's pump flows this module answers it: not one basis (below).
- **Aster's propulsion number is an assumption.** `src/v2/porkchop/launch-vehicles.ts:56-57` at `62ddb17`: `SCREENING_ISP_S = 320`, "DEC-13-5: representative of 300-350 s storable bipropellant class". An engine deck is the step from a chamber's ideal Isp to what an engine delivers, including what a gas generator throws away.
- **PartForge says it does not have this.** `engine-study.html` at `0974a62` lists among the domains of an engine design system an *Orchestrator* that "Connects the domain models so one model's outputs become the next model's inputs, in consistent units" (`:714-716`, "Not in PartForge"), and *Flow & performance* (`:720-722`, "Not in PartForge"); throttling is under "Things it does not model at all" (`:273`, there as a transient; this module is steady-state only).

Alternatives weighed. (1) *An engine deck as a table of published numbers with interpolation*: honest but dead; it cannot throttle an engine the table does not list or take idea 32's nozzle. (2) *A full component-level cycle model with pump and turbine maps* (the RL10 report's ROCETS kind): the right tool, but maps are proprietary or chart-only and a transient-capable model is more than a session can build and prove. (3) *Typical textbook efficiencies and cycle parameters*: forbidden here (no remembered numbers) and unfalsifiable. (4) **Built:** the chamber and nozzle re-run at every throttle point on ideas 31 and 32's kernels; delivered performance as two calibration factors fitted to one published point per engine; a steady power balance whose every parameter is computed from one published engine's figures (SP-8107's 1973 tables, NASA TM-107318's RL10A-3-3A cycle); and every comparison NASA's numbers allow, run live and pinned by tests.

## How it works

**Thrust chamber at a throttle point** (`point()`). Idea 31's `rocket()` at chamber pressure τ·p<sub>c</sub> (same mixture ratio), idea 32's `flow()` and `stationAtArea()` to the area ratio, idea 32's divergence efficiency λ (SP-125 chart or (1 + cos α)/2). Delivered: c\*<sub>del</sub> = η<sub>c\*</sub>·c\*, C<sub>F,vac,del</sub> = η<sub>CF</sub>·λ·C<sub>F,vac</sub>; ṁ = p<sub>c</sub>A<sub>t</sub>/c\*<sub>del</sub>; C<sub>F</sub>(p<sub>a</sub>) = C<sub>F,vac,del</sub> − ε p<sub>a</sub>/p<sub>c</sub>; F = C<sub>F</sub> p<sub>c</sub> A<sub>t</sub> = F<sub>vac</sub> − p<sub>a</sub>A<sub>e</sub>; Isp = F/ṁ. Idea 32's `separation()` (SP-8120's criterion) refuses the thrust where the nozzle separates; `separationAmbient()` gives the ambient pressure at which it starts, and the 1976 atmosphere (PartForge's `isa()`, via ideas 02 and 32) the altitude. The chamber-and-nozzle solution is cached per chamber pressure, so altitude sweeps are cheap; a 5 × 12 deck takes about 35 ms.

**Calibration** (`calibrate()`). For a preset, η<sub>c\*</sub> = p<sub>c</sub>A<sub>t</sub>/(ṁ c\*) with ṁ = F/Isp, and η<sub>CF</sub> = (F/(p<sub>c</sub>A<sub>t</sub>) + ε p<sub>a</sub>/p<sub>c</sub>)/(λ C<sub>F,vac</sub>), from SP-8120 Table I's thrust, Isp, first-listed chamber pressure and printed throat area at one condition (altitude, or sea level where that is all it prints). They reproduce that point exactly. Their product is the Isp ratio to the shifting ideal: F-1 0.954, H-1 0.957, J-2 0.962, LR89-NA-7 0.925, RL10A-3-3 0.979, YLR113-AJ-1 0.917. Split, four of them exceed 1 (F-1 c\* 1.058, H-1 1.035, RL10A-3-3 1.021, J-2 C<sub>F</sub> 1.013): impossible for an efficiency, so the page calls them calibration factors and flags them. They exceed 1 because the table's pressures, throat areas and Isp are not on one basis (the F-1's 1126 psia is at the injector; its throat is at 983), which idea 32 found too.

**Pumps** (`pump()`): power = ṁ Δp/(ρ η<sub>p</sub>), head Δp/(ρ g<sub>0</sub>), incompressible, with idea 31's measured densities (NIST, via idea 31). The published check uses SP-8107's own heads: power = w H/η.

**Turbine drive gas.** (a) *Idea 31's equilibrium*: the gas-generator or preburner mixture ratio that burns at the turbine inlet temperature (`gasAtTemperature()`, marching out from stoichiometric in steps of ln 1.5, then false position), its HP state, and the turbine's isentropic drop as an SP equilibrium at the outlet pressure (`turbine()`), refusing where water or graphite would condense. (b) *SP-8110 Table III's tabulated gas* (γ, c<sub>p</sub>, R for LOX/RP-1, LOX/LH2, N2O4/A-50 and others, linear in temperature, never extrapolated): Δh<sub>s</sub> = c<sub>p</sub>T(1 − PR<sup>−(γ−1)/γ</sup>). Fuel-rich kerosene uses (b), because idea 31 refuses it (graphite would deposit). (c) *Ideal hydrogen* for an expander: NASA Glenn's H2 c<sub>p</sub> at the inlet (idea 31's excerpt; fit starts at 200 K, refused below) in the same closed form. Turbine power = ṁ<sub>t</sub> η<sub>t</sub> Δh<sub>s</sub>.

**Cycles** (`cycle()`), every parameter computed from the reference engine's published figures by `cycleReference()`, never typed:

| Cycle | Reference and what is taken from it | Solved for |
|---|---|---|
| Gas generator | F-1, H-1, MA-5 booster, J-2 (SP-8107): pump discharge / chamber pressure, pump inlet pressures and efficiencies, turbine inlet temperature and pressure / chamber pressure, pressure ratio, efficiency; the J-2's oxidizer turbine in series at 5.0/7.0 of the gas | ṁ<sub>gg</sub> = Σ ṁ<sub>i</sub>w<sub>i</sub> / (η Δh − Σ x<sub>i</sub>w<sub>i</sub>), where w<sub>i</sub> = Δp<sub>i</sub>/(ρ<sub>i</sub>η<sub>i</sub>) and x<sub>i</sub> is the gas generator's draw on each pump (its mixture ratio from idea 31, or the main ratio for a tabulated gas, [assumed]) |
| Staged combustion | SSME at emergency power (SP-8107, "Not operational, but presented for comparative purposes"): turbine outlet / chamber, fuel pump discharge / preburner, oxidizer main and boost-stage ratios and the boost share, both turbines' inlet temperatures, efficiencies and flows per unit fuel flow | the turbine pressure ratio at which turbine power = pump power, with the pump discharge rising with it (scan in steps of 5 %, then false position); "does not close" with the best ratio reached |
| Expander | RL10A-3-3A (TM-107318 Table 6.1.1): turbine inlet temperature (the jacket's heat pick-up is not modelled), turbine outlet / chamber, fuel pump / turbine inlet (jacket and ducts), oxidizer pump / chamber, bypass, stage-weighted pump efficiency | the same, in closed form for ideal hydrogen |

**Engine level.** A closed cycle puts every kilogram through the chamber, so the engine is the chamber. A gas generator adds ṁ<sub>gg</sub> to the flow; its exhaust's thrust is not modelled, so engine Isp is bracketed between F/(ṁ + ṁ<sub>gg</sub>) (dumped with no thrust) and (F + ṁ<sub>gg</sub>√(2c<sub>p</sub>T<sub>out</sub>))/(ṁ + ṁ<sub>gg</sub>) (expanded completely) [Likely an upper bound: c<sub>p</sub> at the hottest point of that expansion].

**Published-figure checks** (all run live on the page and pinned by tests): `ambientLawCheck()` (F(sl) = F(alt) − 101 325 Pa × A<sub>e</sub> with SP-8120's printed exit area), `throttleCheck()` (YLR113-AJ-1), `ispBasisCheck()` (SP-8120 thrust over SP-8107 pump flows), `pumpCheck()`, `turbineCheck()`, `ggFlowCheck()`, `scPressureRatioCheck()`, `expanderCheck()`.

**Data.** `snapshots/turbo-data.js`: every row of SP-8107 Tables I-III (NTRS 19750012398), SP-8110 Table III (NTRS 19740026132) and the RL10A-3-3A cycle state of TM-107318 Table 6.1.1 (NTRS 19970010379, Binder, Tomsik & Veres 1997), **hand-transcribed** from pages rendered at 150-220 dpi, because the text layers garble numbers (SP-8107's F-1 oxygen discharge reads "t600", the RL10A-3-3 turbine inlet "106" with its minus sign lost, the SSME oxidizer turbine's pressure ratio "159"; Table 6.1.1 has no text layer). `snapshots/turbo-sources.txt`: the verbatim text-layer lines (written by `oracle/build_sources.py`), in which the tests find the legible numbers. Units as printed, converted with exact definitions (1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, g<sub>0</sub> = 9.80665, 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 hp = 550 ft·lbf/s, 1 Btu/lb = 2326 J/kg). ## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The chamber and nozzle are ideas 31 and 32's, unmodified,** which reproduce NASA CEA to parts per million; this module adds no chamber or nozzle physics, so its ideal numbers inherit theirs, and idea 31's kernel now also reproduces CEA on turbine drive gas (above).
- **[Certain] Altitude: F(sl) = F(alt) − p<sub>sl</sub>A<sub>e</sub> reproduces SP-8120 Table I's sea-level thrust for the three engines that print both,** F-1 −0.02 %, H-1C/D +0.27 %, LR87-AJ-5 +0.24 %, and the sea-level Isp at the same mass flow +0.08 %, +0.18 %, −0.10 %. So "alt" in Table I is vacuum to that precision, and the deck's ambient law holds on real hardware.
- **[Likely] Throttle: calibrated at the top of its range (595 psia, 150 000 lbf, 240 s at sea level), the deck predicts the YLR113-AJ-1 sled engine at 235 psia as 50 162 lbf (+0.32 %) and 202.1 s (+3.65 %) against the printed 50 000 lbf and 195 s.** One engine, whose table gives ranges whose ends are taken to correspond; the Isp gap at deep throttle is the expected direction (efficiencies fall as an engine throttles, and they are held here).
- **[Certain] The F-1's lowest throttle that still flows full at sea level is 52.7 % of its chamber pressure by SP-8120's criterion** (H-1 38.4 %, Atlas LR89 46.0 %, YLR113 32.8 %; the J-2 and RL10 separate at sea level at any throttle), a design number this page computes and the tables do not print. [Likely] as a statement about the real engine: the criterion is SP-8120's empirical guide applied to the 1-D exit pressure.
- **[Certain] Pumps: flow × head / efficiency reproduces SP-8107's pump powers within ±3 % on 17 of 22 rows** (median −0.23 %; range −5.3 % MB-3 oxygen to +9.0 % MA-5 sustainer RP-1, the latter a source inconsistency below). The page's incompressible head Δp/ρ overstates SP-8107's printed head for liquid hydrogen at high pressure: **+15.2 % for the SSME's fuel pump, +4.0 % for the J-2's**, so pump power at those pressures is overstated by as much.
- **[Certain as numbers] Turbines are the weak link.** On SP-8110's tabulated gas the model gives **−14.6 % to +17.1 %** of SP-8107's turbine powers (median −4.2 %, 8 turbines); on idea 31's gas, where it applies (O2/H2), −27.2 % to −3.4 % (4 turbines), agreeing with SP-8110's gas within 0.8 % on the same turbines.
