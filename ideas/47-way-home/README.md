# Way Home
> Every way home from the asteroid, flown through the planets, with what the propellant made there can push to the Earth. On the chain, 7.1 kg of hydrogen and oxygen take 8,973 kg of Apophis home: leaving on 2028-10-29 with a 2.02 m/s burn, the craft rides Apophis's own 2029-04-13 pass and arrives at 5.86 km/s (12.53 km/s at 125 km). The return burn matches JPL's NHATS to 0.28 m/s (median of 61 legs), Apophis's 2029 pass matches JPL's record to 1.7 km, and against OSIRIS-REx's flown return the patched conic is 2.65 % high.

## What it is

Idea 47 is module 17 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 46 made propellant at the asteroid. This page finds the ways home and hands module 48 the Earth arrival.

- **Choose the craft.** The chain is idea 46's committed handoff: Apophis, a stay ending 2028-10-10 06:53 UTC, 7.11 kg of liquid hydrogen and oxygen at O/F 7.94 and 423.3 s, 592.7 kg dry, boiling at idea 46's passive rates. Or describe a craft: one of the six bodies idea 40 flies (Apophis, Bennu, Ryugu, Didymos, Eros, 2000 SG344), a stay's end, propellant, Isp, dry mass and boil-off. Or import any handoff idea 46 exports.
- **Set the window.** How long you are willing to wait, the shortest and longest flight, up to two revolutions about the Sun, and what to choose by: the most material home, the least Δv, or the slowest arrival that can still get home.
- **Set the ledger.** Boil-off passive or held by idea 46's coolers; Aster's Δv margin; the first correction (by default the departure burn's own 3σ execution error, idea 40's Lucy model); how much material to bring.
- **Set the arrival.** Hand v∞ on to module 48 as it is, or brake to NHATS's 12 km/s entry limit; the entry altitude; the perigee to aim at; whether to fly the return through the planets.
- **Read the result.** Tiles (departure, arrival, flight, the burn flown and patched-conic, the Δv the propellant gives, the material home, v∞ and declination, entry speed, mass at the Earth, what a two-body asteroid would say), a bar of where the propellant goes, a confidence chip on every number, and every refusal, with the 31 carried from ideas 40 to 46.
- **Three charts:** the porkchop (departure by flight time, coloured by Δv, arrival v∞ or material, with the line along which you ride the asteroid's own Earth pass; tap a cell, arrow keys move, Enter refines); the cheapest return against the Δv the propellant still gives, by departure date; the return seen from the ecliptic pole.
- **Check it against reality:** ten rows computed live from JPL's NHATS, JPL's close-approach record and OSIRIS-REx's flown return.
- **Export the handoff** for module 48 (copy, download or link).

Idea 46's README (*Where it would land*): module 47 takes `propellant`, `dry_mass`, `site.stay_end_utc` and the boil-off rates.
- **Aster asked for it.** `strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:22` names **K1**, the "return-leg porkchop (NEA → Earth): arrival v∞ and declination at Earth. Mining is a round trip, and Aster only plans the outbound leg today", and `:62` calls it the "highest thesis value per unit cost" and the prerequisite of K2 (the Skyhook catch, module 48). K1 says "any delivered-mass cell must be a structured refusal" because launch curves do not apply; here the returned mass comes from the propellant the chain made, and is refused when there is none.
- **What a person decides with it.** When to leave (waiting costs hydrogen: idea 46's passive rate empties the chain's hydrogen in 77 days); whether to power the coolers on the way home (the chain's material rises from 8,973 to 12,524 kg); whether a return is worth flying at all (the chain's 7 kg cannot brake to 12 km/s: refused); which window the porkchop's cheapest cell hides (on the chain the 6,300-cell grid finds no return the propellant can pay for; the ride line does).

Alternatives weighed.
1. *Two-body from Aster's elements* (what Aster's porkchop and idea 39 do). Rejected: by 2029 the return starts from where the asteroid is, years after the element epoch, often past an Earth encounter. Against NHATS, two-body Apophis legs are off by 12.3 km/s (median).
2. *JPL's ephemeris for each body.* Covers only bodies fetched, and would not test the chain's own model. **Built: idea 40's integrator** on Aster's elements, which JPL's own records then check.
3. *Patched conics only.* Rejected as the final number: flown through the planets, the chain's return misses by 13,500 km of altitude, and the re-aimed burn is 18 % smaller than the patched conic's. **Built: both**, with the flown one paying the ledger.
4. *A navigation covariance analysis of the return* (as idea 40 did outbound). Cut for this session: the margin and one execution-error correction stand in, and a refusal says so.

## How it works

**The asteroid** (`ephemeris()`). Aster's elements (epoch 2026-05-01, idea 40's `catalog-elements.js`) are flown day by day through idea 40's forces: the Sun, Mercury to Neptune, the Moon, the Earth with its J2, the Sun's relativity (`coast.js` `propagate()`, unmodified). A state between days is one short propagation from the nearest daily node, so the same instant is always reached the same way. Its Earth encounters below Aster's 0.05 au are found by a daily scan and golden section to 0.5 s (`encounters()`; the reality table's Apophis row refines its time to 1 ms).

**One return** (`cell()`). Idea 39's `lambert()` (Izzo, unmodified) from the asteroid at departure to the Earth's centre at arrival (idea 39's Chebyshev fit of Aster's Horizons file). The burn is v₁ − v_asteroid, the arrival v∞ is v₂ − v_Earth, its declination asin(v∞z/v∞) from the ICRF equator, and the entry speed √(v∞² + 2μ⊕/(R⊕ + 125 km)) with Aster's μ⊕ and DE440's R⊕ 6,378,136.6 m (also Aster's `bodies.ts:45`). The 125 km and the 12 km/s limit are NHATS's ("Earth atmospheric entry speed less than or equal to 12 km/s at an altitude of 125 km", CNEOS caveats page). Refused as selections, as idea 39 does: arcs within 5° of 180°, arcs unbound about the Sun, and (new) departures from inside the Earth's sphere of influence.

**The porkchop and the choice** (`grid()`, `rides()`, `refine()`). 90 departures from the stay's end by 70 flight times by default, every revolution family asked, scored by the objective. When the asteroid passes the Earth inside the window, the line where the arrival is that pass is searched too: riding the asteroid home. Both seeds are refined by Nelder–Mead in (departure, arrival), and the better kept.

**Flown through the planets** (`approach()`, `bplaneOf()`, `fly()`). The chosen arc is flown from the asteroid with idea 40's forces to its closest approach to the Earth (2-hour scan, then golden section). The approach hyperbola is read from the closest-approach state, or, when that dives within 10 Earth radii, from the last state outside them; from it come v∞, the incoming asymptote S, B = b(S × h) with B·T and B·R (T in the ecliptic, idea 40's convention) and the time of perigee. The departure burn is then re-aimed by Newton on a finite-difference Jacobian (1 mm/s steps, backtracking) so that the flight passes at the aim perigee (125 km by default), on the uncorrected flight's B-plane angle and perigee time, to 1 km.

**The ledger** (`ledger()`, `materialMax()`). Idea 46's fuel and oxidizer wait, each boiling at its passive rate (or held at zero), from the stay's end to the departure. The burn is the flown burn (or the patched conic's) × (1 + Aster's 10 % margin) plus one correction: 3σ of Lucy's "30 mm/s + 4 %" execution error, through idea 40's `EXEC.lucy`. The correction's propellant burns with the departure burn; the `navigation` refusal says how long its fuel would last if kept for later (the chain's 0.029 kg of hydrogen: 2.8 days). Propellant used = M₀(1 − e^(−Δv/vₑ)) with idea 46's vₑ, burned at idea 46's mixture ratio (left-over oxygen rides home as dead mass), so the most material is m = burnable/(1 − e^(−Δv/vₑ)) − dry − propellant. With braking (NHATS's "maneuver to control the atmospheric re-entry speed during Earth return", lowering v∞ far out, which matches its numbers), a second burn after the cruise's boil-off, solved by bisection. Idea 45's digging, carried inside idea 46's handoff (246.3 kg of regolith a day for 90 days, less 11.3 kg of water: 22,158 kg), caps the material.

**Importing idea 46** (`importSplit()`). Checked without re-running idea 46 (that needs ideas 31 and 32): the envelope and units, propellant = fuel + oxidizer, O/F, dry total = craft + tanks + coolers, and idea 46's own Δv estimate from vₑ and the masses, all to 1e-6. Idea 46's 31 refusals travel with `from`. A null propellant (idea 46's engine refusal) still gets a porkchop, with the ledger refused.

**Reusing earlier modules.** Byte-identical snapshots (tested): idea 39's `lambert.js`; idea 40's `coast.js` and its eight data files. Derived: idea 46's committed handoff wrapped as a script (JSON-equal, tested), and two numbers of ideas 39 and 40's handoffs.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain as measured] The model against JPL.** The return burn agrees with NHATS (JPL's patched conics on JPL's ephemerides) to 0.28 m/s median over 61 legs, which is NHATS's printing precision; the arrival v∞ to 0.66 m/s and the declination to 0.002°. The worst, 5.5 m/s, are Apophis legs leaving in 2036 to 2037, years after its 2029 pass: Aster's 2026 elements flown through that pass part from JPL's orbit 220. Apophis legs leaving in 2029, before or after the pass, agree to 0.01 to 0.50 m/s. Apophis's 2029 pass itself is within 1.7 km of JPL's record and 0.081 s after it in time, its v∞ within 0.001 m/s.
- **[Certain as measured] Against JPL's own states, sharper than NHATS prints (review, `oracle/review-oracles.py`).** NHATS rounds to 1 m/s; JPL Horizons states with lamberthub's Gooding (1990) solver (no code shared with ideas 39 and 40) reproduce NHATS to 0.22 m/s median, 0.50 max. Against them this page is within 3.7 mm/s on the 46 Bennu, Ryugu and 2000 SG344 legs, 0.10 m/s on Apophis leaving before 2030, and up to 6.0 m/s on Apophis leaving in 2036–37 (the integrated orbit parts from JPL's after the 2029 pass). The chain's patched conic on JPL's Apophis (623 m from this page's) is 2.4572 m/s, as here. Through an independent n-body model (Horizons' Sun, planets and Moon, DE440 GMs, the Earth's J2, the Sun's relativity, scipy's DOP853) the re-aimed burn passes at 124.2 km (here 123.7) and the patched one at 13,507.9 km (here 13,508.4), within 0.3 s of the page's times. Without the Sun's relativity the same burn passes at 149.7 km: that is how fine the targeting is.
- **[Certain as measured] The model against a flown return.** OSIRIS-REx left Bennu with 265.8 m/s; the patched conic between JPL's states says 272.8 m/s (+2.65 %, 6.1° off) and an arrival v∞ 0.30 % low. [Likely] That gap is what the patched conic leaves out (the planets over 2.4 years, the trajectory corrections, the targeting of the flyby), and the size to expect on Bennu-class returns here; the review did not separate those terms. The comparison uses Horizons' states directly (2021 to 2023 is outside this page's ephemerides), so it tests the method, not the page's ephemeris.
- **[Certain as arithmetic, on idea 46's numbers] The chain goes home riding Apophis.** Leaving 2028-10-29 (18.8 days after the stay, the material optimum: waiting longer saves Δv but boils hydrogen), 167 days of flight, a 2.02 m/s burn flown through the planets (2.46 m/s patched conic; uncorrected the flight passes 13,508 km up) brings the craft past the Earth at 123.7 km on 2029-04-13 20:18 UTC, 1.5 hours before Apophis's own closest approach, at v∞ 5.86 km/s and declination 30.8°. With 2.33 m/s to pay (margin and correction), 7.11 kg of propellant (6.59 kg after the wait) pushes 8,973 kg of material: 40 % of what idea 45 dug. Least Δv instead (leaving 2028-11-13, 1.95 m/s) brings only 6,695 kg, because the wait boils hydrogen. With the coolers holding, 12,524 kg.
- **[Certain] What that does not mean.** "Home" is an Earth flyby at 12.53 km/s at 125 km, above NHATS's 12 km/s; braking to it needs about 1.35 km/s with margin, which the chain cannot pay (refused); the cheapest braked return in the window, leaving 2029-02-10 on a 74-day arc, needs 1.0 km/s in all, also out of reach. Nine tonnes of rock arriving at 12.5 km/s needs capture or a heat shield the craft does not have: module 48's and 49's question, and the `material_structure` refusal says no container or shield mass is counted.
- **[Likely] Idea 46's prediction was wrong.** Its README says the chain's "about 50 m/s" means module 47 "will have to refuse a return". It does not, because Apophis comes to the Earth on its own.
- **[Speculative] The return's navigation.** No covariance analysis: Aster's 10 % and one execution-error correction (0.111 m/s: 3σ of a 2.02 m/s burn) stand in.
