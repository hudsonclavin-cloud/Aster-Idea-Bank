        How this works

        Where the asteroid really is

        Aster's catalog gives each asteroid's orbital elements at 2026-05-01. Drawn as a fixed ellipse (two-body, as Aster's porkchop does), Apophis drifts tens of thousands of kilometres from JPL's orbit by 2028 (idea 39 measured it) and millions after its 2029 pass by the Earth. So this page asks idea 40's integrator: the elements are flown through the Sun, the planets, the Moon, the Earth's flattening and relativity, day by day. The table below compares the 2029 pass it predicts with JPL's own record.

        The porkchop

        For every departure date and flight time, idea 39's Lambert solver finds the arc from the asteroid to the Earth's centre. The burn at the asteroid is that arc's velocity minus the asteroid's; the Earth sees the arc's velocity minus its own (v∞), and the atmosphere at 125 km sees √(v∞² + 2μ/r). Arcs within 5° of 180° (the plane hangs on small offsets), arcs unbound about the Sun and departures from inside the Earth's sphere of influence are refused, as idea 39 does. When the asteroid itself passes the Earth, the cheapest way home is to ride it: a nudge of a few m/s turns its flyby into an arrival, and the page searches along that line too, then refines (Nelder–Mead in departure and arrival time).

        Flown through the planets

        The chosen arc is flown from the asteroid through idea 40's forces. Uncorrected it misses (the Earth's pull bends it, the planets tug it). The page then re-aims the departure burn by Newton's method so the flight passes the Earth at the aim perigee (125 km by default, NHATS's entry altitude), on the same side and at the same time the uncorrected flight would, reading the approach hyperbola's B-plane. That burn, not the patched conic's, is the one the mass ledger pays. Flown through an independent integrator (the review's: JPL's planets, Moon and Sun, scipy's DOP853), the chain's re-aimed burn passes at 124.2 km against this page's 123.7 km; leaving out the Sun's relativity alone would move it 26 km.

        The mass ledger

        Idea 46's propellant waits, boiling at its passive rates (or held at zero by the coolers), until the departure. It burns at idea 46's mixture ratio, so when the hydrogen runs short the oxygen left over rides home as dead mass. The departure Δv carries Aster's 10 % margin and one correction the size of the burn's own 3σ execution error (Lucy's 30 mm/s + 4 %, through idea 40). Tsiolkovsky then says how much material the propellant can push: m = burnable / (1 − e^(−Δv/vₑ)) − dry − propellant. What idea 45 dug caps it. Braking to the entry limit, NHATS's way, is a second burn on arrival, after the cruise's boil-off. The correction's propellant is counted with the departure burn; kept for later, its hydrogen would boil away within days at the passive rate, which the navigation refusal states.

        What it is checked against

        JPL's NHATS study computes round trips to near-Earth asteroids with patched conics and JPL's own ephemerides. All of its return legs for the bodies here that NHATS lists are recomputed (NHATS prints whole m/s); the largest gaps are Apophis's legs after its 2029 pass, where Aster's elements and JPL's orbit solution part ways, and the same legs drawn two-body show why the integrator is needed. OSIRIS-REx's actual return from Bennu, reconstructed by JPL, is the check against reality: a patched conic against a flown spacecraft. The numbers are in the table above.
