Where it lands

Pick a tier and a ladder column, then place the two numbers the program is still waiting on from the outside world: construction-launch $/kg and the rocket-leg $/kg. The page reads what the committed decision grid says there. It owns no cost model. It interpolates the two fields the grid says it may, adds the leg the one way the grid publishes, steps the rest to grid nodes, shows tether figures only as params/rev_a.json publishes them, and refuses what the grid or the canon does not cover.

The plane

Rocket leg across, construction launch up, both on the grid's log axes. Colour is the one leg-dependent quantity the grid publishes, delivered + leg; the lines are its iso-costs. Drag the crosshair or tap a point (on a touch screen a drag must start on the crosshair's ring, so a swipe elsewhere just scrolls the page), or use the sliders and arrow keys. Nothing here is a margin: the lane multipliers are listed below as text and are never applied.

      Read a newer grid

      Choose a decision_grid.json from a newer Skyhook checkout. Nothing is uploaded; the file is read in your browser. A grid with another schema_version, a different composition rule or malformed points is refused and the snapshot stays.

      An Aster Idea Bank prototype. Every number on this page is read from one of its three snapshots (the decision grid, the params/rev_a.json excerpt, the workbook ladder), interpolated by the grid's declared method, or added by the grid's published rule. Where the data runs out the page says so. Skyhook's Rule 1 canon, VM verification and pytest suite have not been applied to this page.
