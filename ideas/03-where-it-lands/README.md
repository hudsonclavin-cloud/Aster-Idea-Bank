# Where It Lands
> The break-even frontier rebuilt as a pure reader of Skyhook's committed `finance/decision_grid.json` (450 points, built at `27229e2`): it interpolates the two fields the grid says it may, applies the one addition the grid publishes, steps payback and NPV to the nearest node, and refuses everything else. At the rev_a.json Falcon 9 anchor ($3,245/kg) the reference tier's conservative column reads $410.59/kg delivered against a $595.34/kg Y15 breakeven price, payback in year 18.

**Kind:** explorable
**For:** Skyhook
**Run:** open `ideas/03-where-it-lands/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no build, no network). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/03-where-it-lands/model.test.mjs` (Node built-ins, about 1 s) and `node ideas/03-where-it-lands/ui-check.mjs` (headless Chromium through the globally installed Playwright, about 15 s; `--shots DIR` saves full-page screenshots). `node tools/check.mjs ideas/03-where-it-lands` runs both, because the folder's `check.mjs` spawns the UI check. To regenerate the snapshots from a Skyhook checkout: `python3 ideas/03-where-it-lands/build_data.py --skyhook /path/to/skyhook`, then the three `tools/snapshot.mjs … --sidecar-only` lines in its docstring.
**Made by:** Plumbline

## What it is

A phone-friendly reading panel and plane for the decision grid that Skyhook's `finance/export_grid.py` writes and nothing in the repo yet reads. You pick a tier (reference 2.5 km/s, cislunar revenue-gated 2.8 km/s, phase-3 growth 3.5 km/s; the tip speeds come from the `params/rev_a.json` excerpt) and a ladder column (Conservative, Baseline, Optimistic; the derate from the workbook's Assumptions sheet), then place the two numbers the program is still waiting on from the outside world: construction-launch $/kg on the grid's exported log axis ($100 to $3,500) and a rocket-leg $/kg on its display axis ($300 to $15,000). Chips put the slider on `rev_a.json`'s anchors ($3,245 Falcon 9 dedicated, $629 SpaceX internal marginal, $7,000 rideshare) and on the workbook's native launch prices ($2,500 / $1,500 / $800).

The page then shows, in this order:

- **Cost side.** `delivered_total_per_kg`, log-linear interpolated between the two bracketing grid nodes (both nodes, their values and the weight are printed), then `+ rocket leg = delivered + leg`, the grid's single composition rule.
- **The comparability warning**, verbatim, as a card *between* the panes.
- **Price side.** `breakeven_price_per_kg`, the Y15 breakeven customer price, interpolated the same way, beside the workbook's own $500/kg customer-price assumption (a price next to a price).
- **Stepped, never interpolated.** Payback year, NPV@10%, mass ratio, fibre mass, total and system capex from the *nearest* node, with that node's launch price printed, and the whole node verbatim (all 16 fields) under a disclosure, with the insurance status attached to the ops + wear + insurance line.
- **The plane.** Rocket leg across, construction launch up, both log; colour is `delivered + leg`, the one leg-dependent quantity the grid publishes, with iso-cost lines, hairlines at the launch anchors, and a crosshair you drag (mouse, touch or arrow keys).
- **Along the launch axis.** The three columns' breakeven price as straight segments between nodes on a log axis (which *is* the declared interpolation), and payback year stepped at the log midpoints between nodes.
- **Caveats the grid carries**: the partial-tier caveat (shown, verbatim, whenever the tier is not the reference), the insurance status, the lane multipliers as text only, and the grid's notes.
- **Read a newer grid.** A file picker reads a `decision_grid.json` from a newer checkout in the browser; another `schema_version`, a composition rule that is not the published addition, or malformed points are refused and the snapshot stays.

Where the data runs out the page says so instead of showing a number: a launch price off the exported axis (the $7,000 rideshare chip, or anything typed outside $100 to $3,500), a leg off the display axis, a null payback ("not reached within 40 years", quoted from the grid's notes), a tier the `rev_a.json` excerpt cannot label, or a grid that fails validation.

## Why I made it

`handoff.md:30` is Skyhook's first open item: "Rebuild the frontier's interactive numbers against `finance/skyhook_finance.py` per tier — the growth-mode $/kg are still the old wrong model (bannered as such in the tool)." The banner is at `tools/sims/skyhook_frontier.html:104-106` ("The interactive plane below still shows the superseded growth-mode figures; read the corrected panel, not the 3.5 numbers, until the model is rebuilt against the repo finance model") and the footer at line 158 repeats it. Since that handoff the repo gained the answer's first half: `finance/export_grid.py` (docstring, lines 5-7: "after D10 the frontier computes NO economics — it reads this file and interpolates. Everything the UI shows must therefore be carried here, and everything carried here must keep its caveat attached") and `finance/decision_grid.json` (schema 2.0.0, 450 points, 27 KB of metadata about what a consumer may and may not do with them). At `34935cb` the only files that mention `decision_grid.json` are the exporter and its test; the consumer the docstring describes does not exist. This is that consumer.

What I weighed:

- **Patch the frontier in place**, swapping its `evalPoint` for grid lookups. Rejected: the frontier's plane is a lane-margin heatmap, and a margin needs the lane multipliers (`LANES` at line 167, 2.2 / 3.5 / 6.0), which the grid exports under `unvalidated_assumptions` with the instruction that they are "exported as labeled metadata, never applied to any number here" (MP-001 section 2 records no public OTV rate cards). A faithful consumer cannot draw that heatmap, so the plane had to change, not just its numbers.
- **Call the finance model live** (port `skyhook_finance.py` to JS). Rejected twice over: a second implementation of the cost model is exactly the drift the exporter was written to end, and the model reads the workbook with openpyxl, which a `file://` page cannot do.
- **A table of the 450 points.** Honest but not a decision tool; the frontier's value is seeing where the two unknown numbers have to land. Kept as the "nearest node, verbatim" disclosure.
- **This**: a reader that owns no economics, makes the grid's own rules (two interpolatable fields, one addition, step the integers, keep the caveats attached, never apply the multipliers) into the shape of the UI, and prints enough of its working (nodes, weights, declared error, node launch) that a reviewer can recompute every figure from the snapshot by hand.

## How it works

**Data.** Three modules under `snapshots/`, each a classic script and a CommonJS module, each with a `tools/snapshot.mjs` provenance sidecar at Skyhook commit `34935cb`:

- `decision_grid.js`: `finance/decision_grid.json` embedded **verbatim** between `/*<json>*/` and `/*</json>*/` markers (the sidecar's `sourceSha256`, `3787eb7a…`, is the sha256 of exactly that text; a test checks it). The grid was generated 2026-09-11 at Skyhook commit `27229e2` from `params/rev_a.json` with md5 `6e84f7ad…` and the workbook with md5 `5ccdee4d…`; the live `rev_a.json` at `34935cb` still hashes to that md5 (LF-normalised, as `export_grid.provenance_md5` does), so the grid is not stale against canon.
- `rev_a.excerpt.js`: the `_meta`, `economics`, `kinematics`, `mass_ratio`, `materials` and `market_thresholds` keys of `params/rev_a.json`, values unchanged. Tier tip speeds, the launch anchors, the cislunar gate numbers and the mass-ratio ladder come from here and nowhere else (Rule 1).
- `ladder.js`: the workbook's Assumptions sheet A2:E16 (`tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx`), parsed from the xlsx zip by `build_data.py` with `zipfile` and `xml.etree` (the sheet uses inline strings and plain numbers, no formulas). Row keys mirror `finance/skyhook_finance.py` `_LADDER_ROWS` (lines 56-71). The page reads row 3 (strength retention, so the column label can say 30% / 15% / 0% derate, the repo's own `1 − retention`), row 5 (native launch prices, the workbook chips), and row 10 (customer price $500, "held flat across scenarios deliberately").

**Validation (`validateGrid`).** Before anything is read: `schema_version === "2.0.0"`; the fifteen metadata keys present; the launch axis a log-spaced `model_dimension` and the leg axis a `display_axis`; a `delivered_plus_leg_per_kg` rule whose operator is `add` over exactly `leg_cost_per_kg` and `delivered_total_per_kg`; `interpolation_fidelity.fields` covering both fields the reader interpolates; every point carrying the thirteen numeric fields plus a `payback_year` that is a positive integer or null, inside the declared axis, with no duplicate launch node and `axes.n` nodes per (tier, column) series; and a `reference` tier present. A grid that fails is never read, on the page or in the loader.

**Interpolation (the only arithmetic of its kind).** For launch L between nodes L₀ < L < L₁, w = ln(L/L₀) / ln(L₁/L₀) and value = v₀ + w (v₁ − v₀). This is the method the exporter measured ("log-linear interpolation in construction_launch_per_kg, probed at log-space interval midpoints") and it is allowed only for the fields listed under `interpolation_fidelity.fields`, which the reader takes from the grid rather than hard-coding: `delivered_total_per_kg` (declared worst case 0.0608 %) and `breakeven_price_per_kg` (0.0611 %, the grid's `interpolation_max_rel_error`). Asking for any other field returns a refusal, not a number (`notes[4]`: "Interpolating payback_year will produce non-integer years — round or step it"). On a node the value is the node's, exactly. The inverse (`launchAtValue`) solves the same segment for L given a value and is used only to draw iso-cost lines.

**The one addition.** `delivered_plus_leg_per_kg = leg_cost_per_kg + delivered_total_per_kg`, read from `composition_rules`; if the rule found is not that addition the reader refuses rather than apply it. The leg is the display-axis position (the rule's own `leg_cost_per_kg_source` text is shown under the figure: "NOT a model output — leg economics live in finance/leg_cost.py, a separate model that is not integrated with this one").

**Stepping.** Payback year, NPV@10%, mass ratio, fibre mass, capex and the four-line cost breakdown are taken from the nearest node in *log* launch distance (the axis is log-spaced), with an exact midpoint going to the lower node, and the node's launch price printed beside them. A null payback is rendered as "not reached within 40 years", the number lifted from the grid's own note.

**Labels.** Tier names and tip speeds from the `rev_a.json` excerpt (`kinematics.v_tip_kms`; `kinematics.<tier>.v_tip_kms` for the two option blocks); a tier key the excerpt lacks is refused and labelled as such. Column derate = 1 − workbook retention. Launch anchors from `economics` and the workbook; each is tested against the exported axis and the off-axis ones ($7,000 rideshare) carry the refusal text.

**The plane.** The heatmap is one `ImageData` per (tier, column, size, scheme), cached: each pixel row is one interpolated `delivered` at that launch; each pixel adds its leg. Colour is the dataviz skill's single-hue sequential blue ramp on a log scale fixed across all tiers ($364 to $18,834/kg, the grid-wide range of leg + delivered). Iso-cost lines are solved exactly (leg = C − delivered(L), sampled along the launch axis). It is deliberately not a margin plane: with the multipliers off the table, what the grid can say about the leg is only this sum, and the lines make the honest point that over most of the exported range the leg, not the tether, sets the delivered cost.

## Tests

`model.test.mjs` (20 tests, Node built-ins, about a second):

- **The snapshot is what it says.** The JSON text embedded in `decision_grid.js` hashes to the sidecar's `sourceSha256` and parses to the object the page uses; schema 2.0.0, `git_commit` `27229e2…`, 450 points, the three tier keys and three columns, 50 launch nodes that are the exporter's log-spaced values rounded to cents; the other two sidecars at the same commit. When a Skyhook checkout is present (skipped otherwise): the embedded text equals the live `finance/decision_grid.json` byte for byte, the live `params/rev_a.json` LF-normalised md5 equals the grid's `rev_a_md5`, and the excerpt's keys deep-equal the live file.
- **Validation refuses** schema 1.0.0, a missing or non-additive composition rule, a field without a fidelity entry, a point with a missing field or a fractional payback, a node outside the axis, a duplicate node, a short series, and a grid with no reference tier.
- **Interpolation.** At every node, both fields return the node exactly (900 checks); at every log midpoint w = ½ and the value is the neighbours' mean, at every log quarter point w = ¼ (two fields, 441 intervals); the allowed set is exactly the grid's two fidelity-tested fields and five other fields refuse; the grid's `interpolation_max_rel_error` is the max of its per-field figures and under its 1 % limit; launches 99.99, 3500.01, 7000, 0, −5, NaN and Infinity refuse with the axis bounds in the message; the inverse round-trips to 1e-9 on all nine series (702 checks).
- **Reconciliation to `params/rev_a.json`** (read from the excerpt, not retyped): the cislunar gate of $686 is bracketed by the $659.63 (payback 15) and $709.27 (payback 16) nodes; the interpolated breakeven at $800 is $516.80, within 0.05 % of the canon ~$517; the grid's `mass_ratio_x` and `fiber_mass_t` reproduce the integrated two-arm ladder for all five published rungs (121.83× / 609.1 t, 64.30× / 321.5 t, 40.32× / 201.6 t, 295.27× / 1,476.3 t, 3,085.95× / 15,429.8 t) to 0.005× and 0.05 t; the workbook's conservative retention equals 1 − `materials.derate_fraction`.
- **Reconciliation to the published workbook results** (`docs/DECISION_LOG.md:47` and `:51`, `finance/SENSITIVITY_FINDINGS.md:10`): at each column's native launch price ($2,500 / $1,500 / $800, read from the ladder, Assumptions B5 for the conservative one) the two bracketing nodes both carry the published payback (17 / 9 / 7), their `total_capex_M` bracket $8,741M / $5,772M / $4,226M, and the conservative nodes' NPV brackets −$3,883M.
- **The one addition**, as Skyhook's `tests/test_export_grid.py::test_applying_the_rule_reproduces_the_1_0_0_field` checks it: delivered + leg − leg equals delivered to 1e-9 for legs 300 / 1,900 / 15,000; off-axis legs refuse; a `multiply` rule refuses; an off-axis leg refuses only the composed figure, not the reading.
- **Stepping**: nearest in log distance, midpoint to the lower node, one part in 10⁹ either side flips it; stepped values are the node's, never blended; the node's four-line breakdown sums to its delivered total; a null payback reads "not reached within 40 years" with the note quoted.
- **Markers and multipliers**: six markers, five on axis, the $7,000 rideshare refused with the axis in the message; the lane multipliers come back as the strings "2.2", "3.5", "6.0" with `applied: false`; `model.js`, `app.js` and `index.html` contain no literal 2.2, 3.5 or 6.0 and never multiply or divide an economic point field (a regex over the thirteen field names).
- **The reading and the words**: the full default reading; the warning verbatim; the partial-tier caveat absent on the reference tier and verbatim elsewhere; the panes differ by about 44 % at the conservative rung as the warning says; and this README quotes the numbers the code computes (516.80, 659.63, 709.27, 121.83, 27229e2, 34935cb, 410.59, 595.34, 0.0611 %, $8,741M, 450 points).

The tests have teeth. Mutations I ran, each restored afterwards: a linear instead of log-linear weight fails 4 tests; the composition as leg − delivered, 2; nearest node by linear distance, 1; allowing any field to be interpolated, 1; clamping an off-axis launch instead of refusing, 1; flipping one digit of one payback in the embedded grid (the $709.27 cislunar node, 16 to 15), 3; the schema constant, 1; a `3.5` literal typed into `app.js`, 1; the null-payback wording, 1; midpoint ties to the upper node, 1.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] Every number on the page traces to the snapshot** by one of three routes: a node value, the declared log-linear interpolation between two printed nodes, or the published addition. The tests cover all 450 nodes, every interval, and the inverse.
- **[Certain] The snapshot is the committed file.** Byte-identical to `finance/decision_grid.json` at `34935cb`, which is the grid built at `27229e2`; the live `rev_a.json` still hashes to the grid's `rev_a_md5`. Skyhook's own `test_export_grid.py::test_committed_grid_is_not_stale` is what proves the committed grid matches the model today; I could not run it here (pytest and the workbook-reading path need the repo's environment), so "not stale against canon" above means the provenance hashes agree, not that I rebuilt the grid.
- **[Certain] This page applies no lane multiplier, draws no margin and computes no economic quantity the grid does not publish.** The static test would fail on a typed `3.5`; the structural test would fail on a `*` applied to any point field.
- **[Certain] The reconciliations hold**: $516.80 against ~$517, the $686 gate bracketed by paybacks 15 and 16, the mass-ratio ladder to 0.005×, and the workbook's $8,741M / Y17 / −$3,883M (and the other two columns) bracketed by the nodes around their native launch prices. They are brackets, not equalities, because $2,500, $1,500 and $800 are not grid nodes and the grid forbids interpolating capex and NPV.
- **[Likely] The interpolated values are within the grid's declared error of the model.** That is the exporter's measurement (0.061 % worst case at interval midpoints), not mine; I reproduce its method, not its probes.
- **[Likely] The plane reads correctly as "mostly leg".** Over the exported axes the tether's delivered cost moves by $136/kg (reference / Conservative, $285 to $421) while the leg axis spans $14,700, so iso-cost lines are nearly vertical. That is the grid's content, not an artefact, but it does make the plane less dramatic than the frontier's margin heatmap it replaces.
- **[Speculative] Whether Hudson wants a leg plane at all.** The proposal's landing note left it to him: retire the lane-margin heatmap, or wait for sourced OTV rate cards. This deposit takes the first position provisionally and keeps the plane; the reading panel stands on its own if the plane goes.
- **Rough edges.**
  - The nearest-node rule (log distance, ties low) is mine; the grid says "round or step it" and does not say how. The page prints the node it chose so the choice is visible.
  - Labels on the plane (anchor hairlines, iso-cost tags) can touch each other at some crosshair positions; nothing is hidden, but it is not always tidy.
  - The file loader validates structure, not provenance: it cannot hash the `rev_a.json` the new grid was built from, so it only compares the grid's `rev_a_md5` with the snapshot's and warns on a difference.
  - "6.0" is how the lane multiplier appears in the grid's text; JSON parsing makes it 6, so the page formats whole-number multipliers with one decimal to match the source.
  - No persistence: the page opens on the anchor every time.
- **Gates not passed.** Skyhook's Rule 1 canon review (this page quotes canon from a snapshot, not the live file), VM verification, and the pytest suite (`tests/test_export_grid.py` would be the place for the consumer-side assertions below; nothing has been added there). Idea-bank fidelity only.
