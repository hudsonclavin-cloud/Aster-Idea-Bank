# Where It Lands
> The break-even frontier rebuilt as a pure reader of Skyhook's committed `finance/decision_grid.json` (450 points, built at `27229e2`): it interpolates the two fields the grid allows, applies its one published addition, steps payback and NPV to grid nodes, and refuses everything else. At the rev_a.json Falcon 9 anchor ($3,245/kg), reference tier, Conservative column, the Y15 breakeven price is $595.34/kg against the workbook's $500/kg customer price, and payback falls in year 17–18 between the two bracketing nodes (Skyhook's own model, run live, gives 18).

**Kind:** explorable
**For:** Skyhook
**Run:** open `ideas/03-where-it-lands/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no build, no network). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/03-where-it-lands/model.test.mjs` (30 tests, about 6 s; three run Skyhook's own finance model read-only through `skyhook_live.py` and skip themselves without a checkout at `/home/user/skyhook` or `SKYHOOK_DIR`) and `node ideas/03-where-it-lands/ui-check.mjs` (97 checks in headless Chromium through the globally installed Playwright, about 15 s; `--shots DIR` saves screenshots, `VERBOSE=1` prints passing checks). `node tools/check.mjs ideas/03-where-it-lands` runs both (the folder's `check.mjs` spawns the UI check). The live helper alone: `python3 -B -I ideas/03-where-it-lands/skyhook_live.py rebuild` (or `--help`). To regenerate the snapshots: `python3 ideas/03-where-it-lands/build_data.py --skyhook /path/to/skyhook`, then the three `tools/snapshot.mjs … --sidecar-only` lines in its docstring.
**Made by:** Plumbline

## What it is

A phone-friendly reading panel and plane for the decision grid that Skyhook's `finance/export_grid.py` writes and nothing in the repo yet reads. Pick a tier (reference 2.5 km/s, cislunar revenue-gated 2.8, phase-3 growth 3.5; tip speeds and each tier's standing from the `params/rev_a.json` excerpt) and a ladder column (Conservative, Baseline, Optimistic: each a whole column of the workbook's Assumptions sheet, not only a derate), then place the two numbers the program is still waiting on: construction-launch $/kg on the grid's log axis ($100 to $3,500) and a rocket-leg $/kg on its display axis ($300 to $15,000). Chips sit at `rev_a.json`'s anchors ($3,245 Falcon 9 dedicated, $629 SpaceX marginal, $7,000 rideshare) and the workbook's native launch prices ($2,500 / $1,500 / $800). The leg opens at $2,121.32, the axis's log midpoint, and the page says that is not an estimate.

The reading, in order:

- **The partial-tier caveat** on any tier but the reference, inside the reading above every number: first sentence ("PARTIAL TIER MODEL: …") and last ("Do NOT read a non-reference row as that tier's business case …") verbatim, the rest one tap away, refused or not.
- **Delivered cost** (straight-line capex amortisation at mature capacity): `delivered_total_per_kg` interpolated between the two bracketing nodes (nodes, values and weight printed), then `+ rocket leg = delivered + leg`, the grid's one composition rule.
- **The comparability warning**, verbatim, between the panes.
- **Y15 breakeven price** (ramped, discounted cash flows): `breakeven_price_per_kg`, interpolated the same way, beside the workbook's $500/kg customer price, a price next to a price. Nothing calls the two panes a cost and a price for one scenario, which the warning forbids.
- **Stepped to grid nodes.** NPV@10% and capex from the nearest node, named; payback as one year, or the bracket ("year 15–16") where the two nodes disagree; mass ratio and fibre mass only as the `params/rev_a.json` value, for the five series it publishes ("not shown" for the other four). The node itself, verbatim, under a disclosure.
- **The plane**: leg across, launch up, both log; colour is `delivered + leg`, with iso-cost lines, anchor hairlines and a crosshair (mouse: drag anywhere; touch: tap to place, drag from its ring, swipe elsewhere to scroll; arrow keys when focused).
- **Along the launch axis**: the three columns' breakeven as straight segments on a log axis (which *is* the declared interpolation) and payback stepped at the log midpoints; a click or tap sets the launch, a scroll does not.
- **Caveats**: insurance status, the lane multipliers as text only, the grid's notes. **Read a newer grid**: a file picker that refuses another `schema_version`, a non-additive rule or malformed points, and keeps the snapshot.

Where the data runs out the page refuses instead of showing a number: a launch off the axis ("below the minimum" / "above the maximum", value as typed; the $7,000 chip is one), an empty box ("no construction-launch price entered"), a leg off its axis (only the composed figure), a null payback ("not reached within 40 years", from the grid's notes), a tether figure `rev_a.json` does not publish, a tier or column the snapshots cannot label (the whole reading), or a grid that fails validation.

## Why I made it

`handoff.md:30` is Skyhook's first open item: "Rebuild the frontier's interactive numbers against `finance/skyhook_finance.py` per tier — the growth-mode $/kg are still the old wrong model (bannered as such in the tool)." The banner is `tools/sims/skyhook_frontier.html:104-106` (text at 105: "read the corrected panel, not the 3.5 numbers, until the model is rebuilt against the repo finance model"); the footer at 158 repeats it. Since then the repo gained `finance/export_grid.py` (docstring lines 5-8: "after D10 the frontier computes NO economics — it reads this file and interpolates. Everything the UI shows must therefore be carried here, and everything carried here must keep its caveat attached") and `finance/decision_grid.json` (schema 2.0.0, 450 points; the metadata on what a consumer may do is the first 6,387 bytes of the 251,394-byte file, about 6.4 KB, ahead of `points`). At `34935cb` only the exporter and its test mention `decision_grid.json`. This is the missing consumer.

What I weighed:

- **Patch the frontier in place.** Rejected: its plane is a lane-margin heatmap, which needs the lane multipliers (`LANES`, line 167, 2.2 / 3.5 / 6.0). The exporter's docstring (`finance/export_grid.py:30-32`) calls them "UNVALIDATED INTERNAL ASSUMPTIONS (MP-001 section 2: no public OTV rate cards) — exported as labeled metadata, never applied to any number here"; the grid's own notes on them read "internal assumption; MP-001 section 2 records NO PUBLIC DATA for OTV LEO->GTO/GEO/cislunar rate cards." A faithful consumer cannot draw that heatmap.
- **Port `skyhook_finance.py` to JS.** Rejected: a second cost model is the drift the exporter exists to end, and the model reads the workbook with openpyxl, which a `file://` page cannot. (The tests call the Python model live, read-only; the page never does.)
- **A table of the 450 points.** Not a decision tool; kept as the verbatim-node disclosure.
- **This**: a reader that owns no economics and turns the grid's rules (two interpolatable fields, one addition, step the integers, keep the caveats attached, never apply the multipliers) and Skyhook's Rule 1 (tether numbers only from `params/rev_a.json`) into the shape of the UI, printing enough working to recompute every figure by hand.

The proposal's "ladder.json (see Blue Cells)" is this bank's idea 08, `ideas/08-blue-cells`, which reads the same Assumptions sheet with the standard library and writes `ladder.json`. This deposit's `build_data.py` re-implements a narrower parse (A2:E16) so the page does not depend on another deposit; in Skyhook, one ladder reader (Blue Cells' proposed `ladder.json`) should feed this page.

## How it works

**Data.** Three modules under `snapshots/`, each a classic script and CommonJS module with a `tools/snapshot.mjs` sidecar at Skyhook `34935cb`:

- `decision_grid.js`: `finance/decision_grid.json` embedded **verbatim** between `/*<json>*/` markers; the sidecar's `sourceSha256` (`3787eb7a…`) is the sha256 of that text. Generated 2026-09-11 at `27229e2` from `rev_a.json` md5 `6e84f7ad…` and the workbook md5 `5ccdee4d…`. Not stale: rebuilt in memory from the live checkout with `build_export()`, it gives the same 450 points with zero field differences.
- `rev_a.excerpt.js`: the `_meta`, `economics`, `kinematics`, `mass_ratio`, `materials` and `market_thresholds` keys of `params/rev_a.json`, unchanged. Tip speeds, tier standing, launch anchors, the cislunar gate and the published mass-ratio and fibre-mass rungs come from here.
- `ladder.js`: Assumptions A2:E16 of `tools/financial/skyhook_financial_model.xlsx`, parsed by `build_data.py` with `zipfile` + `xml.etree`; row keys mirror `skyhook_finance.py` `_LADDER_ROWS` (lines 56-71). Row 3 gives the derate (`1 − retention`), row 5 the native launch chips, row 10 the $500 customer price; every row feeds the note under the column control: 11 of the 14 rows differ between columns (cadence 1 / 2 / 3 a day, fixed ops $80M / $60M / $45M, fleet capex $6,000M / $5,000M / $4,000M, ops + wear $12 / $8 / $5, …); customer price, payload and tip speed do not.

**Validation (`validateGrid`).** Schema `2.0.0`, fifteen metadata keys, the two axes' roles, the composition rule being `add` over exactly its two operands, fidelity entries for both interpolated fields, thirteen numeric fields and an integer-or-null payback on every point, `axes.n` distinct on-axis nodes per series, a `reference` tier. A grid that fails is never read.

**Interpolation.** For L₀ < L < L₁, w = ln(L/L₀) / ln(L₁/L₀), value = v₀ + w (v₁ − v₀): the exporter's measured method, allowed only for the fields in `interpolation_fidelity.fields` (read from the grid): `delivered_total_per_kg` (declared worst case 0.0608 %) and `breakeven_price_per_kg` (0.0611 %, the grid's `interpolation_max_rel_error`). Any other field is refused (`notes[4]`: "Interpolating payback_year will produce non-integer years — round or step it"). On a node the value is the node's. The inverse draws the iso-cost lines only.

**The one addition.** `delivered_plus_leg_per_kg = leg_cost_per_kg + delivered_total_per_kg`, read from `composition_rules` and refused if it is not that addition; the leg is the display-axis position, with the rule's `leg_cost_per_kg_source` text shown.

**Stepping.** NPV, capex and the cost breakdown come from the nearest node in log distance (ties to the lower node), named. Payback is an integer, so one node's year is honest only where both bracketing nodes agree; otherwise the reading is the bracket: at $3,245 the $3,027.23 node says 17 and $3,255.04 says 18 ("year 17–18"); at the $686 cislunar gate, 15 and 16 ("year 15–16"); with one null node, "year 40 or not reached within 40 years" (phase 3 / Conservative between $762.65 and $820.04).

**Rule 1: tether numbers only from `params/rev_a.json`.** `canonRungs` reads the rungs from the key names of `mass_ratio.integrated_tether_to_payload_both_arms` (`<stem>_<N>pct_derate` and `<stem>_<N>pct_fiber_mass_t`; a stem ending `_<a>p<b>kms` is at a.b km/s, the rest at `reference_v_tip_kms`): 2.5 km/s at 30 / 15 / 0 % derate (121.83× / 609.1 t, 64.30× / 321.5 t, 40.32× / 201.6 t), 2.8 at 30 % (295.27× / 1,476.3 t), 3.5 at 30 % (3,085.95× / 15,429.8 t). A series matches by its tier's tip speed and its column's derate. If the grid agrees with the rung to the canon's printed precision (half a unit in its last decimal), the page shows the canon value and key; if it disagrees, or no rung exists (cislunar and phase-3 Baseline and Optimistic), the cell is a refusal. The last of those is the grid's 440.40×, the figure `docs/DECISION_LOG.md:12` traced behind an old unsourced "440×" claim.

**Labels.** Tip speeds from `kinematics.v_tip_kms` and `kinematics.<tier>.v_tip_kms`; standing read, not typed: "Rev A reference" (`_meta.revision`) or the block's `confidence` ("[Likely]"). A tier or column the snapshots cannot label refuses the reading. Each launch anchor is tested against the axis; off-axis ones carry the refusal text.

**Touch and scroll.** Nothing a scroll can start on changes a number. The chart sets the launch on `click` (a tap fires it, a scroll does not). The plane has `touch-action: pan-y`; a touch starting on the crosshair's 28 px ring cancels the scroll for that gesture and drags, any other touch is a tap (places it) or a scroll (changes nothing). A native range input jumps on touchdown before the browser knows a scroll began, so on `pointercancel` the slider's previous value is restored.

**The plane.** One cached `ImageData` per (tier, column, size, scheme): each pixel row is one interpolated `delivered`, each pixel adds its leg; the dataviz sequential blue ramp on a log scale fixed across tiers ($364 to $18,834/kg, the grid-wide range of leg + delivered); iso-costs solved exactly (leg = C − delivered(L)). Not a margin plane: without the multipliers, what the grid says about the leg is this sum, and over most of the range the leg, not the tether, sets it.

**Live model (tests only).** `skyhook_live.py` imports `finance.export_grid` with bytecode writing off and calls `compute_cell`, or rebuilds the grid in memory with `build_export()` (no file I/O, by its docstring) and diffs it against the snapshot. `find -newer` over the checkout after a run lists nothing.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] Every number on the page traces to a snapshot**: a node value, the declared interpolation between two printed nodes, the published addition, or (for tether figures) the `params/rev_a.json` value itself.
- **[Certain] The snapshot is the committed file and the committed file is current**: byte-identical to `finance/decision_grid.json` at `34935cb`; the live `rev_a.json` hashes to `rev_a_md5`; Skyhook's own `build_export()` rebuilds it with zero point differences (what `tests/test_export_grid.py::test_committed_grid_is_not_stale`, line 157, asserts) and identical metadata. I did not run pytest itself (not installed here).
- **[Certain] The interpolation is within the grid's declared error of the model** at all 441 interval midpoints, not only the exporter's 20 probe intervals; the worst case equals the declared 0.0608 % / 0.0611 %. At $3,245 the error is 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ (delivered) and 3.5 × 10⁻⁵ (breakeven).
- **[Certain] No lane multiplier, no margin, no cost set against a price, no economic quantity the grid does not publish**; the static, structural and wording tests would fail otherwise.
- **[Certain] The reconciliations hold**: $516.80 against ~$517; the $686 gate between paybacks 15 and 16 (the model gives 15 and a $499.96 breakeven at $686 itself); the five rungs to 0.005×; the workbook's $8,741M / Y17 / −$3,883M and the other columns bracketed by the nodes and reproduced live at $2,500 ($8,741.09M, −$3,882.55M). The grid gives brackets, not equalities, because the native prices are not nodes and capex and NPV may not be interpolated.
- **[Likely] The plane reads correctly as "mostly leg"**: delivered moves $136/kg over the launch axis (reference / Conservative, $285 to $421) while the leg spans $14,700, so iso-costs are nearly vertical: the grid's content, if less dramatic than the margin heatmap.
- **[Speculative] Whether Hudson wants a leg plane at all**; the reading panel stands alone if it goes.
- **Rough edges.** The nearest-node rule (log distance, ties low) is mine; the grid says only "round or step it". NPV and capex stay at the named nearest node (they always differ between nodes). On touch the crosshair drags only from its ring, which must be discovered (the hint says so). On an option tier the caveat flag adds about 300 px above the panes on a phone. Plane labels can touch at some positions. The loader checks structure, not provenance (it compares `rev_a_md5` and warns). `canonRungs` depends on `rev_a.json` key naming; a renamed key would withhold a series, not mis-show it. Whole-number multipliers are printed with one decimal ("6.0") to match the source. No persistence.
- **Gates not passed.** Skyhook's Rule 1 canon review (canon is read from a snapshot), VM verification, and the pytest suite (nothing added to `tests/test_export_grid.py`). Idea-bank fidelity only.
