# Bell
> Expand idea 31's chamber gas through a nozzle you shape (area ratio, a Rao bell or a cone, where it fires) and read specific impulse and thrust coefficient against altitude, whether NASA SP-8120's criterion says the flow separates, the contour, and the throat and exit for a thrust. It matches NASA CEA 3.3.4 to 2 parts in 10 million in vacuum Isp over 540 nozzle stations, and against twelve real engines in NASA's 1976 nozzle monograph every published Isp is 2.1 to 9.0 % below the shifting ideal (median 5.3 %), which is the number to carry forward.

**Kind:** explorable tool
**For:** Aster + PartForge
**Run:** open `ideas/32-bell/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, `file://` works, no network; it also opens on idea 31's `#h=` link). Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/32-bell/bell.test.mjs` (21 tests, about 0.5 s) and `node ideas/32-bell/ui-check.mjs` (61 checks in headless Chromium; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). ## What it is

Idea 32, module 2 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 31 burns the propellant; this module turns that hot gas into thrust.

It opens on the F-1, every input taken from NASA SP-8120's Table I (LOX/RP-1 at mixture ratio 2.27 and 1126 psia, area ratio 16, a bell 80.3 % of a 15° cone long, sea level, 1,522,000 lbf), so nothing on the first screen is a remembered "typical" value. From there you set:

- **the chamber**: one of idea 31's ten propellant presets, a mixture ratio and a chamber pressure (computed by idea 31's own kernel, copied unmodified), or an idea 31 handoff imported by paste or `#h=` link;
- **the nozzle**: area ratio (1.5 to 400, logarithmic slider or typed), a Rao parabola bell of 60 to 100 % length or a cone of any half-angle to 45°, where it fires (vacuum, an altitude in the 1976 standard atmosphere, or a typed ambient pressure), an optional thrust to size for, and, under *Flow models*, whether the frozen composition is taken at the chamber or the throat and which model the sizing and the handoff use.

The page shows, each with a confidence chip:

- **specific impulse** at the ambient and in vacuum, and the **thrust coefficient**, twice: with **shifting** equilibrium (the upper bound) and with **frozen** composition (the lower bound), after the contour's divergence loss, with the ideal-nozzle value beside it;
- the **exit state** (pressure, temperature, Mach number, velocity), and c\* for each model (frozen at the chamber freezes the throat too, so its c\* is lower);
- a **separation verdict** by SP-8120's criterion: flows full, marginal (within SP-8120's 20 %), or separated, with the ambient pressures (and standard-day altitudes) at which it turns marginal and separates. A separated nozzle's full-flowing Isp is refused, and the truth is bracketed between it and the nozzle cut off at the separation plane;
- the **divergence efficiency** λ, the **parabola angles** θ<sub>n</sub> and θ<sub>e</sub> (with SP-125's own chart's reading beside SP-8120's), and, for a thrust, the **throat and exit diameters, length, mass flow and wetted area**;
- the **contour**, drawn to scale (or per throat radius with no thrust), with the 15° cone of the same area ratio dashed over it;
- a **chart** of Isp against altitude (0 to 50 km, with the ideally expanded nozzle dashed and the separated and marginal altitudes shaded) or against area ratio at the ambient (the separated area ratios shaded); tap or drag it to set the altitude or area ratio, arrow keys work too;
- **two buttons**: the area ratio whose exit pressure equals the ambient, and the largest that still flows full here with SP-8120's 20 % margin;
- **twelve real engines** from SP-8120 Table I, modelled at the table's own conditions beside the published Isp, each with a *Load* button that puts it on the page, and four the module refuses with the reason;
- **Import/Export** of the series handoff, with idea 31's handoff carried inside it.

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 32: "Nozzle flow: isentropic expansion (frozen and, via 31, shifting), area ratio, thrust coefficient against ambient pressure, over/under-expansion and flow separation, a bell contour", handing on "nozzle (ε, CF(pa), Isp(pa), throat and exit size for a thrust)". Module 33 (Engine Deck) needs exactly that, and its throttle sweep needs to re-run the chamber, which is why this handoff carries idea 31's inside it.
- **PartForge says it does not have this.** `engine-study.html` at `0974a62` lists "Internal flow, nozzle flow or losses" under *Things it does not model at all* (`:268`), and its *Flow & performance* domain ("Models how flow through the chamber and nozzle turns theoretical performance into delivered performance, including losses", `:720-721`) is marked "Not in PartForge". Its ledger row L-02 (`:651`) asks for theoretical performance with a solver version and database hash; idea 31 supplied the chamber half, this module the nozzle half, with the same hash.
- **Aster's propulsion number is an assumption.** `src/v2/porkchop/launch-vehicles.ts:56-57` at `62ddb17`: `SCREENING_ISP_S = 320`, "DEC-13-5: representative of 300-350 s storable bipropellant class". This module computes the storable class's ideal Isp at a stated area ratio and sets it beside five flown N2O4/Aerozine 50 engines (below).

Alternatives weighed. (1) *The textbook ideal-rocket nozzle with one constant γ*: one formula, but it needs a γ typed from memory and hides the frozen/shifting spread, which is up to 15 s here and is the honest uncertainty of any ideal number. (2) *A method-of-characteristics contour and divergence loss* (Rao's own method): the right tool for a bell, but a correct axisymmetric MOC with a transonic start line is more than a session can build *and* prove; I cut it and read Rao's results off NASA's published charts instead, saying so. (3) *Porting CEA's rocket module*: not allowed (oracles are never sources). (4) **Built:** one-dimensional isentropic expansion on idea 31's equilibrium kernel, shifting and frozen, solved for an area ratio by safeguarded Newton steps, with NASA's published design rules (SP-8120's separation criterion and margin, SP-125's parabola construction, cone length and efficiency, SP-125's bell efficiency chart, SP-8120's parabola-angle chart) as data, each quoted or digitized from the fetched scans, and checked against CEA, the 1996 RP-1311 printouts, SP-125's worked design and twelve flown engines.

## How it works

**Chamber.** Idea 31's `rocket()` (snapshot `snapshots/mixture.js`, byte-identical to idea 31's, with its `thermo.inp` excerpt): equilibrium chamber, infinite-area combustor, iterated sonic throat. Arriving by handoff, `chamberFromHandoff()` recomputes the chamber from the handoff's inputs with that kernel and refuses (`invalid_input`) if the handoff's c\*, chamber temperature or entropy differ by more than 1 part in 10<sup>8</sup>: a handoff made with other data, or edited, cannot be expanded here. A chamber idea 31 refused arrives as that refusal ("Refused upstream by 31-mixture: …"). Stand-alone, the page builds the chamber handoff with idea 31's own `makeHandoff()`, so both paths feed the nozzle the same object.

**Expansion** (Gordon & McBride, NASA RP-1311 Part I, chapter 6, written from the equations; CEA is the oracle). Constant entropy from the chamber. *Shifting:* each station is an SP equilibrium (`equilibrate(…, {mode: 'SP'})` of idea 31) at its pressure, with idea 31's graphite/water stability test at every station. *Frozen:* the composition at the freeze point (chamber, as CEA's default `n_frz = 1`, or throat, `n_frz = 2`) is fixed; each station's temperature solves s(T, p) = s<sub>freeze</sub> by Newton steps on ln T with the frozen c<sub>p</sub>; species under 10<sup>-10</sup> mole fraction are left out of the frozen mixture (their fits may stop at 300 K), and the target entropy and enthalpy are recomputed for the kept mixture at the freeze point so the isentrope starts exactly there. Frozen at the chamber, the throat is frozen too: Newton on ln(p<sub>c</sub>/p) until u = a<sub>frozen</sub> to 10<sup>-13</sup>, which gives a lower c\*. Every station: u² = u<sub>0</sub>² + 2(h<sub>0</sub> − h); mass flux G = ρu; area ratio ε = G<sub>t</sub>/G; Mach = u/a (equilibrium sound speed for shifting, frozen for frozen); I<sub>vac</sub> = u + p/G; C<sub>F,vac</sub> = I<sub>vac</sub>/c\*. The station for a chosen ε: Newton on x = ln(p<sub>c</sub>/p) with d ln ε/dx = (M² − 1)/(γM²), started from the constant-γ area–Mach estimate, bracketed between the throat and any overshoot, bisecting when a step leaves the bracket or lands on a refused state, converged to |ln(ε/ε<sub>target</sub>)| < 10<sup>-12</sup>.

**Against ambient.** C<sub>F</sub>(p<sub>a</sub>) = λ C<sub>F,vac</sub> − ε p<sub>a</sub>/p<sub>c</sub> and Isp(p<sub>a</sub>) = c\* C<sub>F</sub>(p<sub>a</sub>), λ applied to the vacuum C<sub>F</sub> because that is how SP-125 Figure 4-12 defines it ("percent ideal C<sub>F</sub>"; SP-125 also says a 15° cone's "vacuum thrust coefficient … will be 98.3 percent of the ideal"). The ambient comes from PartForge's 1976 US Standard Atmosphere `isa()`, verbatim (`snapshots/isa.js`, cut out of idea 02's snapshot by `build-data.mjs` and checked byte for byte against it), −5 to 86 km; above, the page offers vacuum. Isp in seconds divides by that file's `ISA_G0` = 9.80665.

**Separation.** NASA SP-8120 §2.1.2.1.3 (Schilling's fit, its ref. 24): the wall separates where its pressure falls to p<sub>a</sub> · 0.583 (p<sub>a</sub>/p<sub>c</sub>)<sup>0.195</sup>; the text layer reads `0.583 (Pamb/P)°"195`, the scan (printed page 17) reads 0.195, checked by rendering. SP-8120 §3.1.2.1.3: "If the exit wall pressure is within 20 percent of the separation pressure, reduce the expansion ratio": that is *marginal* here. The older 0.4 rule it quotes is reported beside. The ambient at which a given exit pressure starts to separate is the closed-form inversion p<sub>a</sub> = (p<sub>e</sub> p<sub>c</sub><sup>0.195</sup>/0.583)<sup>1/1.195</sup>. The one-dimensional exit pressure stands for the exit wall pressure (a bell's wall pressure is somewhat lower) [Likely]. Separated: refused, bracketed between the full-flowing value (the pressure deficit acts on the whole skirt) and a nozzle cut at the separation plane (the skirt beyond contributes nothing).

**Digitizing** (`oracle/digitize/`). PDF pages rendered with `pdftoppm` (SP-8120 p. 29 at 400 dpi, SP-125 p. 100 at 300 dpi). The scans are rotated about 0.5° *and* sheared, so every gridline is located in the pixels and fitted as its own straight line; a pixel's value is interpolated between its neighbouring gridlines along its own row and column (SP-8120 Figure 5(b) has only ticks: there the two axis lines give the skew and the ticks a least-squares log scale, residuals ≤ 1.2 px and 0.05°). Curve centres are read column by column (columns where label text adds runs are skipped) and smoothed by robust cubic fits in ln ε, RMS 0.013 to 0.034°; λ curves by quartic fits in L<sub>f</sub>, RMS 0.004 to 0.011 percentage points. Checks: Figure 4-12's own shortened-cone curve reproduces ½(1 + cos(atan(tan 15°/L<sub>f</sub>))) to within 0.023 percentage points everywhere; both angle charts read SP-125's worked example (ε 14, 80 %: printed 27.4° and 9.8°) as 27.48° and 9.80°.

**Sizing.** A<sub>t</sub> = F/(C<sub>F</sub>(p<sub>a</sub>) p<sub>c</sub>) at the design ambient with the design flow model; ṁ = p<sub>c</sub>A<sub>t</sub>/c\*; D<sub>e</sub> = D<sub>t</sub>√ε. Refused where the design ambient separates or C<sub>F</sub> ≤ 0.

**Real engines** (`snapshots/sp8120-table1.js`). SP-8120 Table I, hand-transcribed from the scan (sideways, no usable text layer; the tests check the transcription for internal consistency: A<sub>t</sub> against D<sub>t</sub>, A<sub>e</sub> against D<sub>e</sub>, A<sub>e</sub>/A<sub>t</sub> against both). For each engine idea 31 can burn: chamber at the table's mixture ratio and (injector) pressure, expansion to its ε, λ from its length (L<sub>f</sub> from its throat diameter and throat-to-exit length; lengths outside 60-100 % use the chart's nearest end), "alt" taken as vacuum, "sl" as 101,325 Pa. The table's thrust at the same condition gives a delivered C<sub>F</sub> = F/(p<sub>0</sub>A<sub>t</sub>) (p<sub>0</sub> the nozzle-stagnation or throat pressure where the table gives one) and c\* = Isp g<sub>0</sub>/C<sub>F</sub>.

**Envelope and refusals** (`code`, `reason`, `what_would_help`, plus `applies_to`): area ratio outside 1.5 to 400 (what CEA was compared over; `out_of_envelope`); a shifting station where graphite or water would condense; a frozen station below its species' fits; altitude outside −5 to 86 km; a negative pressure; a bell length outside 60 to 100 %; a bell area ratio outside 5 to 90 (no parabola angles; the cone still works); a cone outside 0 to 45°; a separated nozzle's ambient Isp; sizing at a separating design ambient or with a non-positive thrust; an edited, foreign or upstream-refused handoff. Chamber pressures are idea 31's (0.1 to 300 bar).

## Honest assessment
