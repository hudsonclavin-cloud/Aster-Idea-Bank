      How this works

      The chamber is idea 31's: chemical equilibrium by Gibbs minimisation on NASA Glenn's data, an infinite-area combustor and an iterated sonic throat. Arriving from idea 31, the page recomputes that chamber with idea 31's own kernel (copied unmodified) and refuses the handoff if the numbers it states are not the ones that kernel gives.

      The expansion. Downstream of the throat the gas expands at constant entropy. With shifting equilibrium every station is a fresh equilibrium at its pressure (recombination keeps releasing energy, the upper bound); with frozen composition the mixture leaving the chamber (or the throat) keeps its makeup (the lower bound); real nozzles fall between, nearer frozen when the gas is thin and fast. Each station's velocity comes from the enthalpy it has lost, its area from the mass flux, and the station whose area is the chosen multiple of the throat's is found by Newton steps on the pressure (Gordon & McBride, NASA RP-1311, chapter 6).

      Thrust against ambient. In vacuum the nozzle gives Ivac = ue + peAe/ṁ; at ambient pressure pa it loses paAe/ṁ, so CF(pa) = CF,vac − ε pa/pc, a straight line. That line is only true while the flow fills the nozzle. NASA SP-8120 gives the criterion this page uses: the wall separates where its pressure falls to pa·0.583 (pa/pc)0.195, and an exit pressure within 20 % of that is marginal. Past it the page refuses the full-flowing number and brackets the truth between it and a nozzle cut off at the separation plane (no bracket when the separation pressure is at or above the throat pressure: that nozzle would not run at all).

      The contour. A bell is Rao's thrust-optimised parabola as NASA SP-125 lays it out: a 1.5 Rt arc into the throat, a 0.382 Rt arc out of it to the point N at angle θn, and a parabola to the lip at angle θe; its length is a fraction of a 15° cone's. The angles are read off SP-8120 Figure 5(b); SP-125's own chart of the same angles is shown beside them, because the two disagree by up to a degree. The divergence efficiency λ (the share of the ideal CF left after the exhaust leaves at an angle) is (1 + cos α)/2 for a cone and is read off SP-125 Figure 4-12 for a bell.

      How far to trust it

      • Against NASA CEA 3.3.4, same data and species, at 26 conditions (ten propellants, 0.1 to 300 bar) and 546 stations from area ratio 1.5 to 400 (540 computed and compared, 6 refused): vacuum specific impulse to 1 part in 10 million shifting and frozen at the chamber, 2 in 10 million frozen after the throat; temperature, Mach number and molar mass to 5 ppm or better. It refuses at exactly the five stations where CEA with every product deposits graphite, and wherever a frozen expansion would cool below the fits of the species it carries (300 K for the 27 species whose data start there; CEA extrapolates). [Certain]
      • Against the 1996 RP-1311 printouts (legacy CEA and data of its day): vacuum Isp within 0.06 %, CF 0.04 %, exit temperature 0.28 %, for both shifting and frozen flow. [Certain]
      • Against twelve real engines (SP-8120 Table I, sixteen operating points): every published specific impulse is below the shifting ideal (after the bell's divergence efficiency), by 2.1 to 9.0 % (median 5.3 %), and between 4.2 % below and 1.9 % above the frozen ideal (median 0.9 % below). That gap is the boundary layer, finite-rate chemistry, incomplete combustion and (for turbine-driven engines) the turbine's own flow, none of which this module models. [Certain that it is 2 to 9 %; Likely as to its causes]
      • Separation is an empirical fit (Schilling, via SP-8120), which SP-8120 itself calls a guide; the page uses the one-dimensional exit pressure for the exit wall pressure, which a bell does not quite have. [Likely]

      Confidence classes: measured is a value read from a measurement or a measured source; derived is computed here from measured inputs or read off a published chart; assumed is your input. Every number on the page traces to the kernel (bell.js), idea 31's kernel, a snapshot, or a cited NASA document.
