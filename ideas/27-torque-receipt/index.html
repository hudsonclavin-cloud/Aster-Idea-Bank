Torque Receipt

What a tightening torque actually promises for one bolt, worked from PartForge's own tables: the preload band with NASA-STD-5020B's ±25 % (lubricated) or ±35 % (as received) scatter, bolt stress at the top of that band against proof, yield and UTS, the two-pitch protrusion rule, and PartForge's own thread check. Every line is computed, never passed: a torque is not a measurement of preload.

What this does not check

  • Joint separation and slip. Both need joint stiffness (the bolt's share of an external load, C). PartForge's evaluation dropped separation for its one pressure fixture, a radial piston seal; NASA TM-106943 is the source the table names for building it next. The in-service line therefore uses C = 1, an upper bound.
  • Torque tolerance, preload loss and temperature. The PartForge table carries only the ±25 / 35 % scatter, so nothing else is applied.
  • The part itself. The stack and engagement are what you type or what the fixture declares; no mesh is measured here.
  • Fatigue, prying, bending, or the head and nut bearing faces.

An Aster Idea Bank prototype for PartForge, not a release: it has not been through PartForge's geometry gate, its data-provenance pipeline or a skeptic review. Spaceflight factors from NASA-STD-5020B are used as the table uses them, a citable floor, not a requirement an amateur rocket must meet.
