# Max Q
> Fly idea 34's rocket off the pad through the 1976 atmosphere over a rotating round Earth, with the Saturn V's wind-tunnel drag, thrust that grows as the air thins, a pitch kick, a gravity turn, a throttle bucket and staging, and read max Q, Mach 1, every cutoff and where the delta-v went. Flown as Apollo 17 with one number fitted, its first stage reaches centre cutoff within 0.9 m/s and 1.2 km of NASA's reconstruction; max Q comes 6.7 s early and 4.4 % weak, and the open-loop upper stages end 183 m/s short of the flight's orbit.

## What it is

Idea 35, module 5 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Ideas 31 to 33 made an engine, 34 made a rocket of engines; this module flies the rocket out of the atmosphere.

It opens on **Saturn V AS-512 (Apollo 17)**, idea 34's vehicle with every kilogram from NASA's flight report, flown from Cape Canaveral on the flight's own azimuth (91.504 deg) with the pitch-over starting when the flight's did (12.9 s range time). You can:

- **change the flight**: the launch site (Aster's Cape Canaveral or Vandenberg, with their azimuth corridors) and azimuth, the pitch-over's start, length and kick angle, the upper stages' steering (gravity turn, a held pitch, or a linear pitch programme), a throttle bucket through max Q (inside the engine's deck from idea 33), the drag curve (the Saturn V's forebody coefficient, its power-off total, or none), the reference diameter and a factor on the coefficient, the coast at each staging, and the Earth's rotation (off, to see what it gives);
- **solve** the kick for a first-stage cutoff flight-path angle (for AS-512, the flight's 20.429 deg from Table 4-2: the one number fitted to the flight), and the upper-stage pitch for a level burnout;
- **choose the F-1's flow**: idea 33's engine as the chain computes it, or scaled to the flow the flight averaged (idea 34's measurement, +2.0 %).

The page shows, each with a confidence chip: max Q (value, time, height, Mach), Mach 1, burnout height, speed and flight-path angle, the orbit it is in (or a refusal saying it is not), the speed the spinning Earth gives on the pad, and the losses; per cutoff the time, height, inertial and ground speed, flight-path angle, mass and that stage's losses. **Through the air** charts dynamic pressure, altitude against downrange, speed, acceleration or Mach, with Apollo 17's reconstructed points on top (tap, drag or arrow-key to read). **Where the delta-v went** is a waterfall from idea 34's ideal delta-v to the burnout speed, measured two ways. **Against Apollo 17** sets 26 numbers beside the flight report. **Handoff** exports the ascent for module 36 and imports idea 34's vehicle (or this page's own flight).

(3) *Generic drag (a constant C_D, or a textbook curve)*: no source to cite, and the series forbids remembered numbers. (4) **Built:** a gravity turn with one fitted number, on drag measured in wind tunnels for the vehicle being flown, against the same flight report idea 34 transcribed, so every number on the first stage is a prediction except the one fitted, and the gap at the end is what guidance would have to buy.

## How it works

**The vehicle** (`vehicleFromHandoff`). Idea 34's handoff, its ledger (`snapshots/stack.js`, unmodified) re-run here on the handoff's inputs, refused unless the liftoff mass, payload, ideal delta-v and burn time match to 1e-8. Engines come from the handoff's numbers and are refused unless they hold together (Isp = F/ṁ; F<sub>SL</sub> = F<sub>vac</sub> − p<sub>0</sub>A<sub>e</sub>, which idea 32's linear CF(p<sub>a</sub>) makes exact; the deck's 100 % row = F<sub>vac</sub>) and, for a preset, equal idea 34's own record of it (`snapshots/data-json.js`, computed by idea 34's committed kernel), or for an imported engine, the idea 33 handoff it carries. A vehicle that calls itself AS-512 keeps the name only if it is the flight report's. Idea 33's engine model itself is not loaded (it is idea 34's job, and 330 KB): an imported engine's numbers are taken as idea 34 verified them.

**Earth and air.** A sphere of Aster's equatorial radius (6,378,136.6 m, `vendor/naif/pck00010.tpc` line 3362), GM 398,600.4418 km³/s² (`delta-v.ts:3`), rotating at pck00010's 360.9856235 deg/day (`BODY399_PM`, over 86,400 s: 7.2921 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s); no J2, no oblate surface (module 37). The 1976 US Standard Atmosphere is PartForge's `isa()` (via ideas 02 and 32, verbatim); above its table's top (84,852 m geopotential, 85,999.95 m geometric) density and pressure are taken as zero. No wind.

**Forces** (`deriv`). r'' = −μr/|r|³ + (F u − D v̂<sub>rel</sub>)/m, m' = −ṁ, with v<sub>rel</sub> = v − ω×r. Thrust F = n k [F<sub>vac</sub>(τ) − p<sub>a</sub>A<sub>e</sub>]; at throttle τ the vacuum thrust is the deck's and the engine's flow scales with the deck's chamber flow (the gas generator's share at other throttles is not in idea 33's handoff: proportional, assumed); k is the flow scale (1, or the F-1's flight-average flow, Isp unchanged). Where p<sub>a</sub> exceeds the nozzle's separation onset (idea 33's criterion) the flight is refused at that point. Drag D = q S C<sub>A</sub>(M), q = ½ρ|v<sub>rel</sub>|², M = |v<sub>rel</sub>|/a, S = π d²/4 with d = 33 ft for the Saturn V. C<sub>A</sub> is Walker's Figure 28 (NASA TM X-53770, 1968), digitized here (below): the forebody coefficient by default, the power-off total as a bound on base drag; held flat outside Mach 0.5 to 7.8 (the page says how much drag was booked there: 1.1 m/s on AS-512). No lift: at angle of attack only the axial force acts.

**Steering**, in a plane fixed in inertial space at liftoff through Earth's centre and the pad, containing the launch azimuth (no yaw steering). Vertical to t<sub>p</sub>; the thrust then tilts toward downrange by δ over Δt<sub>k</sub> (10 s, assumed) and holds until the in-plane Earth-relative velocity has turned as far (found by bisection inside the step, to 10⁻⁶ s); then it follows that velocity (the gravity turn). Upper stages: the same gravity turn, a fixed pitch above the in-plane horizontal, or a pitch linear in time from θ<sub>0</sub> at upper-stage ignition to θ<sub>1</sub> at the last cutoff (whose time is known in advance: upper stages do not throttle). An earlier version followed the Earth-relative velocity in three dimensions: the Coriolis drift during the vertical rise turned the heading by several degrees and AS-512 reached 38.7 deg of inclination instead of 28.5; the plane fixed that.

**Integration** (`fly`, `integrate`). Classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta in the inertial frame, 0.2 s steps below the atmosphere's top and 1 s above, every step landing exactly on the next event: a jettison, an engine cutoff (idea 34's burn clocks), a throttle edge, a steering phase, burnout (constant flow between events, so its time is exact). Events at one instant are all applied. Between stages the spent stage's dry and remaining mass drops, then a ballistic coast (AS-512: 5.13 and 5.56 s, outboard cutoff to the next mainstage, from idea 34's event times). The ascent ends at the first burn followed by a restart of the same stage (AS-512: the S-IVB's first burn); later burns are handed on. Max Q is refined by a parabola through the three samples around the peak, Mach 1 by linear interpolation.

**Where the delta-v went.** Integrated with the state: the ideal delta-v ∫F<sub>vac</sub>/m dt (exactly idea 34's rocket equation, checked), back pressure ∫p<sub>a</sub>A<sub>e</sub>/m dt, and, against the **Earth-relative** speed, gravity ∫−g·v̂<sub>rel</sub>, drag ∫D/m, steering ∫(F/m)(1 − u·v̂<sub>rel</sub>) and a small centrifugal gain ∫v̂<sub>rel</sub>·(−ω×(ω×r)) (Coriolis is normal to v<sub>rel</sub> and drops out): ideal − losses + centrifugal = ground speed at burnout. Against the **inertial** speed the same identity holds with v̂ in place of v̂<sub>rel</sub>, from the pad's ω R cos φ (408.7 m/s at 28.5 deg). Both close to under a millimetre per second (tests). The split differs: on AS-512 the first stage loses 1,212 m/s to gravity and nothing to steering against the ground, but 565 m/s to gravity and 675 m/s to "steering" against inertial space, because the ground carries the rocket east while it climbs straight up. The page shows both.

**Orbit** (`orbitOf`): two-body elements from the burnout state with Aster's GM; perigee and apogee as heights above the sphere. A perigee below the atmosphere's top is a refusal ("not in orbit"), as is escape, and so is a trajectory that leaves the atmosphere and falls back into it.

**Solvers.** Grid bracketing, then the Illinois variant of regula falsi on the function value: the kick for a first-stage cutoff angle (0.01 to 30 deg), and θ<sub>0</sub> for a level last burnout (−20 to 60 deg). Each refuses when nothing in its range works, saying what the range gives.

**The digitized drag curve** (`snapshots/saturn-v-axial-force.js`, procedure in `oracle/digitize_fig28.py`). Walker's figure is a 300 dpi 1-bit scan printed sideways: rendered, rotated, an affine pixel-to-data map fitted to the major grid lines (largest residual 1.6 px: 0.0054 in Mach, 0.0017 in the coefficient), the dark runs crossing a column 0.02 Mach either side of each station listed, and the run on the solid fairing picked by eye on zoomed crops (41 forebody and 37 total stations). The script overlays the table on the figure; I inspected it. Reading uncertainty [Likely]: ±0.01 above Mach 1.5, ±0.02 below, where the fairing runs through the data symbols beside the dashed design-criteria curve (NASA TM X-53517, not on NTRS). The two curves were read independently; their difference is the power-off base drag, which cannot be negative, and the test that checks this found one station where my readings crossed (Mach 7.5), fixed by reading three more stations.

**The AS-512 trajectory** (`snapshots/as512-trajectory.js`), hand-transcribed from MPR-SAT-FE-73-1 (the PDF idea 34 used, same sha256): Table 2-2's event times (pitch and roll from 12.9 s, tilt arrest 160.1 s), Table 4-1 (Mach 1 at 67.5 s and 8.0 km; max Q 3.36 N/cm² at 82.5 s and 13.1 km; the maximum Earth-fixed velocities), Table 4-2 (every cutoff's time, height, space-fixed velocity, flight-path angle, heading, range), Table 4-4 (parking orbit), section 4.1 (azimuths), section 4.2.1 (the report's Mach and q used measured weather to 58.3 km) and Appendix A (13.06 km and a 33.2 m/s wind at max Q). Four smudged ACTUAL entries are NOMINAL + (ACT−NOM), checked against the legible digits.

**Handoff schema (version 1, `from: "35-max-q"`).** The series envelope; `handoff.json` is the sample: idea 34's committed sample (idea 33's sample engine, seven on a first stage, one J-2 above, assumed masses) from Cape Canaveral due east, a 1 deg kick at 10 s, a 4 m diameter, the upper stage pitched linearly to 0 deg from a start solved for a level burnout (9.15 deg): burnout at 370.6 km and 8,591.0 m/s in a 371 × 4,861 km orbit at 28.50 deg, max Q 28.84 kPa (every choice assumed, to show the chain).

## Honest assessment

All gaps are model − flight (flight: MPR-SAT-FE-73-1 ACTUAL columns), with the F-1 at the flight-average flow and the kick fitted to Table 4-2's outboard-cutoff flight-path angle (20.429 deg, kick 1.357 deg) unless said.

- **[Certain] The mechanics are right.** Closed forms, idea 34's exact ledger and an independent integrator in another frame with another atmosphere all agree to the figures in Tests.
- **[Certain as numbers] The first stage, one number fitted, eleven predicted.** At centre cutoff (139.30 s range time): speed −0.92 m/s (2,090.9 against 2,091.8), height +1.19 km, flight-path angle +0.155 deg, range −0.54 km. At outboard cutoff: speed −6.7 m/s, height +1.17 km, range −0.63 km, ground speed −16.7 m/s against Table 4-1's maximum Earth-fixed velocity. Mach 1 −2.92 s and −0.57 km. **Max Q −4.4 % (32.12 against 33.60 kPa), 6.7 s early (75.8 against 82.5 s) and 2.06 km low.**
- **[Certain as numbers] With idea 33's F-1 unscaled** (kick re-fitted, 0.832 deg): the S-IC burns 3.91 s longer than the flight and at centre cutoff is 126.9 m/s slow and 1.27 km low; max Q −8.3 % and 4.1 s early; Mach 1 +0.24 s. The flight-average flow (idea 34's measurement) closes most of that, which says the S-IC's gap was the engine's flow, not the trajectory.
- **[Likely] Max Q comes early because a gravity turn is not the flight's steering.** The S-IC flew a tilt programme (Table 2-2: pitch from 12.9 s to tilt arrest at 160.1 s), not zero angle of attack, through measured weather (the report's Mach and q used it to 58.3 km) and a 33.2 m/s wind from 314 deg at max Q, a 34.8 m/s tailwind at 12.2 km (Appendix A). A gravity turn fitted to the cutoff angle pitches over later and harder, so it is lower and slower at the same time. [Speculative] The tailwind lowers the flight's own q; this page has the standard atmosphere and no wind.
- **[Certain as numbers] The upper stages do not reach the flight's orbit open loop.** With the linear programme solved for a level burnout (35.1 deg down to 0), S-II cutoff is 37.8 km high and 161 m/s slow, and S-IVB cutoff 45.4 km high and **182.8 m/s slow** (7,619.5 against 7,802.3 m/s, 215.9 km): perigee −286 km, not in orbit, and the page says so. Other open-loop programmes trade height for speed: in a scan of end angles from 5 to 12 deg (start solved for a level burnout each time) the burnout is level at 182 to 135 km and 104 to 58 m/s short of circular speed there, never in orbit. [Likely] Most of that is what the flight's iterative guidance bought; module 36 will measure it. [Certain] Part is the chain: idea 33's J-2 runs at mixture ratio 5.5 where the S-IVB flew about 5.0 (idea 34), so the S-IVB's first burn lasts 121.6 s against 137.4, and the S-II at 5.5 throughout misses the flight's shift to 4.8.
