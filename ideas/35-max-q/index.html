    How this works

    The vehicle is idea 34's. Its stages, burns, jettisons, engine cutoffs and every kilogram come in its handoff, and its own ledger is re-run here and must give the handoff's numbers before anything flies. The engines are idea 33's: vacuum thrust, mass flow, exit area, a throttle deck and the ambient pressure at which the nozzle's flow separates.

    The flight. A point mass over a spherical Earth (Aster's radius and GM) that rotates at the rate in NASA/NAIF's pck00010, integrated with fourth-order Runge-Kutta in an Earth-centred inertial frame fixed at liftoff, landing exactly on every event. Thrust is n[Fvac − paAe], so it grows as the air thins; drag is q S CA(M) along the Earth-relative velocity, with q = ½ρv2 and ρ, pa and the speed of sound from the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere (zero above 86 km, where that table ends). CA is the Apollo/Saturn V's measured in six wind tunnels (Walker, NASA TM X-53770, 1968), digitized from his Figure 28: the forebody coefficient, or the power-off total as a bound on base drag.

    Steering. Straight up until the pitch-over, then the thrust tilts toward the launch azimuth by the kick angle and holds until the Earth-relative velocity has turned as far; from there it follows that velocity (zero angle of attack: the gravity turn), all in a plane fixed in space at liftoff. Upper stages continue the gravity turn, hold one pitch above the horizon, or pitch linearly from one angle to another. This is open loop: nothing aims at an orbit. Module 36 adds guidance.

    Where the delta-v went. Along the flight the page integrates the ideal delta-v (vacuum thrust over mass: exactly idea 34's rocket equation), and what was lost to gravity (the component of g along the velocity), drag, steering (thrust not along the velocity) and back pressure (paAe). Measured against the Earth-relative speed, ideal − losses + a small centrifugal term = Earth-relative speed at burnout; against the inertial speed, ideal − losses = inertial speed gained since the pad, which already moved at ωR cos(latitude). Both budgets close to under a millimetre per second; the split between gravity and steering depends on which speed you measure against, and the page shows both.

    How far to trust it

      Confidence classes: measured is from a measured or published source (the flight report, the wind tunnel); derived is computed here from such inputs; assumed is your input or a choice. Every number on the page traces to the kernel (ascent.js), idea 34's kernel and handoff, the snapshots in snapshots/, or your input.
