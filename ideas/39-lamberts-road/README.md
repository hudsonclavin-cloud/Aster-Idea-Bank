# Lambert's Road
> Pick any of 61 real near-Earth asteroids from Aster's catalog and a range of dates, and read a porkchop of Lambert transfers (direct, one or two revolutions) you can click, refine and hand on: Earth from JPL Horizons, the asteroid from Aster's own elements, Izzo's solver matching two independent libraries to 7 × 10⁻¹³ and Aster's DLA cell to 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s. Against JPL's own mission-design maps it is 1.9 m/s off in departure v∞ (median, Apophis) and 6.5 m/s (Bennu); arrivals past an Earth encounter are where two-body propagation fails (Apophis after 2029: 6 km/s off), and the page refuses them, including 1,844 of the 41,905 best windows in Aster's screening cache. Idea 38's closing departure, flown on uncorrected, passes Apophis at 287,000 km; re-aiming it costs 3.3 m/s.

**Kind:** explorable tool
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/39-lamberts-road/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, `file://` works, no network; it also opens on a `#h=` link carrying idea 38's or this page's handoff). Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/39-lamberts-road/lambert.test.mjs` (19 tests, about 1.3 s; three of them run idea 38's committed kernel) and `node ideas/39-lamberts-road/ui-check.mjs` (69 checks in headless Chromium; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Regeneration, none needed to run anything: `node ideas/39-lamberts-road/build-data.mjs <dir of fetched files>` (Aster's checkout plus the five JPL responses named in the sidecars: three hourly Horizons windows and two mission-design maps), `node ideas/39-lamberts-road/build-sample.mjs`, `node ideas/39-lamberts-road/study-encounters.mjs` (about 1 min), `node ideas/39-lamberts-road/oracle/build-cases.mjs` then `python3 -I ideas/39-lamberts-road/oracle/lambert_oracle.py` (hapsira 0.18.0 and lamberthub 1.0.0 in a throw-away venv; 7 s).
**Made by:** Perihelion

## What it is

Idea 39, module 9 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Ideas 37 and 38 took one departure asymptote as given: Aster's own DLA-oracle cell for Apophis (departure 2026-07-02, 677.177 days, one revolution, left branch). This module computes that cell and every cell around it, for any body in an excerpt of Aster's catalog, and hands the chosen transfer to module 40. You can:

- **choose the target** (61 bodies: 23 mission targets and named bodies, Apophis, Bennu, Ryugu, Itokawa, Eros, Didymos, Kamo`oalewa, 1998 KY26, 2024 YR4 among them, and 38 more: the 40 lowest-C3 well-determined bodies in Aster's screening cache, two of which, Apophis and 2000 SG344, are already named), **the departure dates** (2026 to 2040, the span of Aster's Earth ephemeris), **the flight times**, **the revolution families** (direct, up to one, up to two, one only), **what to colour and choose by** (C3, Aster's ΔV stack one-way or sample-return, arrival v∞), **the launch site** for Aster's DLA band and **the grid** (80 × 60 to 200 × 140);
- **read the porkchop**: contours; every cell the page would refuse is hatched and left out of the grid's best (red: the arrival lies past an Earth encounter or inside Earth's sphere of influence; dark: DLA beyond the chosen site's ceiling; grey: within 5° of 180° or unbound about the Sun); the encounter itself drawn as a line; the grid's best reliable cell, Aster's cell and idea 38's departure date marked; tap or click a cell, move with the arrow keys, Enter (or the button) refines it to the local minimum (Nelder–Mead);
- **read the chosen window**: departure and arrival in UTC and TDB, C3, departure v∞, RLA, DLA with Aster's GREEN/AMBER/RED band, arrival v∞, injection ΔV and Aster's whole ΔV stack, the transfer orbit, Earth's distance and the solar elongation at arrival, each with a confidence chip; the refusals that apply, its own and those carried from ideas 33 to 38;
- **see the transfer** from the ecliptic pole: Earth's orbit, the asteroid's, the transfer arc, and idea 38's departure flown uncorrected when one is imported;
- **check it live against the oracles**: Aster's DLA cell (with this page's Earth and with Aster's), Aster's screening-cache best window for the target (and whether this page refuses it), JPL's own selected mission-design windows for Apophis and Bennu, JPL's integrated orbit of the asteroid near the arrival (Apophis and Eros), and Earth against Horizons between Aster's daily records in three windows (2026, 2035 and 2037);
- **import** idea 38's handoff (paste, file or `#h=` link) and **export** this one (copy, download, link).

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 39: "Heliocentric transfer to a real near-Earth asteroid from Aster's catalog: Lambert solutions, a porkchop, the chosen window", taking a departure date range and a target, handing on v∞ out, v∞ in and the dates. Idea 38's README ends: "module 39 (Lambert's Road) imports `handoff.json` and starts the transfer from `hyperbolic_departure_state` at its epoch with `v_infinity`". Idea 37's says "a new day needs module 39's transfer for it".
- **It closes the chain's loop.** Ideas 37 and 38 flew an asymptote copied from `tools/slice12-research/data/dla-oracle-m1-vectors.json` (62ddb17). Nothing in the chain had computed it. This module recomputes it from the ephemerides and checks the copy, and it carries what 38 actually flew (a departure 9.9 h before the epoch the asymptote was computed for) on to the asteroid.
- **Aster's own items it serves.** BRIEFING §1: the porkchop (`src/v2/porkchop/grid-compute.ts`), the screening cache (`tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json`), Slice 18's drift truth (`tests/fixtures/v2/nea-drift-truth-2026-2046.json`), the dormant **C0** kernel ("Chebyshev vs daily Hermite accuracy-per-byte experiment on real Horizons vectors"), and the house rule that a kernel that cannot refuse does not ship.

Alternatives weighed. (1) *Port Aster's TypeScript Lambert solver.* Rejected: reconciliation with Aster would then test Aster against itself, and BRIEFING's INV-024 asks Aster for oracles, not ports. (2) *A universal-variable (Battin/Vallado) solver.* Simpler for one revolution, awkward for multi-revolution branches, which the chain's cell needs. (3) **Built: Izzo's 2015 method**, written here from the paper's equations, with Aster's pinned cells as the reconciliation target and two outside libraries as oracles, one of them a different method (Gooding 1990). For Earth: (a) Aster's daily records and Hermite would be 230 KB here; (b) a coarser Hermite table measured 25 km and 0.2 m/s off at 4-day spacing; (c) **built: Chebyshev series** fitted to Aster's records, 74 KB, which also answers C0 (degree 24 since the review: the degree 18 first built lost to Aster's Hermite after 2030, see Honest assessment). For the asteroid, an n-body propagator was out of scope (module 40's); instead the page detects where two-body propagation stops being true and refuses there.

## How it works

**Lambert** (`lambert()`, after D. Izzo, *Revisiting Lambert's problem*, Celest. Mech. Dyn. Astr. 121, 2015; known to me from the paper and its open implementations, not fetched here, arXiv being blocked from this sandbox). Chord c, semi-perimeter s, λ = ±√(1 − c/s) (negative past 180°, judged prograde about the frame's z axis as Aster's `lambert-multi-rev.ts` and `izzo.ts` do), non-dimensional time T = √(2μ/s³)·t. T(x) in three forms: Lancaster–Blanchard; Lagrange's for 0.01 < |x − 1| < 0.2; Battin's hypergeometric series ₂F₁(3, 1; 5/2; ·) within 0.01 of the parabola. Roots by Householder's third-order iteration from Izzo's initial guesses (to 10⁻¹³, then the residual |T(x) − T| ≤ 10⁻¹⁰ T is required for "converged"). For M revolutions, T_min(M) by Halley's method on dT/dx = 0, no solution below it, and two roots above: *left* (nearer x = −1, poliastro's and hapsira's high path) and *right*. Velocities from Izzo's γ, ρ, σ. A transfer angle of exactly 0 or 180° (no plane) is refused.

**Earth** (`snapshots/earth-cheb.js`, made by `build-data.mjs`). Aster's `src/v2/data/horizons-inner-solar-system-2026-2040.json`: Horizons' geocentre (399) about the Sun's centre, ICRF, TDB, 5,479 daily records. Fitted by least squares, positions and velocities together, with Chebyshev series of degree 24 on 32-day segments (the last segment shorter), coefficients stored as whole metres: 12,864 numbers against the 32,874 Aster interpolates. Velocity is the series' derivative. Worst residual at any daily record: 0.070 km and 1.08 mm/s (December 2035, at a segment boundary). Between records, against Horizons fetched hourly in three windows and measured at build over every hour (`snapshots/horizons-earth-windows.js`):

| window (hourly epochs) | this page's Chebyshev | Aster's daily Hermite |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-01 to 08-01 (1,465) | 14.7 m, 0.47 mm/s | 90.3 m, 3.22 mm/s |
| 2035-11-27 to 2036-01-28 (1,489) | 69.7 m, 1.75 mm/s | 65.9 m, 2.35 mm/s |
| 2037-01-20 to 03-20 (1,417) | 64.1 m, 1.68 mm/s | 60.7 m, 2.16 mm/s |

The degree-18 fit first built (9,786 numbers) was 0.126 km and 3.15 mm/s in 2026 but 0.449 km and 11.1 mm/s in the 2035 window and 0.444 km and 10.9 mm/s in the 2037 one, five times Aster's Hermite in velocity: the review measured that and raised the degree. `hermiteEphemeris()` reproduces Aster's Hermite for reconciliation.

**The asteroid** (`bodyState()`), Aster's `keplerian.ts` formula for formula: GM_SUN 1.32712440018 × 10²⁰ m³/s², mean anomaly, Newton on Kepler's equation (start at M, or ±π above e 0.8; tolerance 10⁻¹²), perifocal to ecliptic, then one rotation by the J2000 obliquity (84,381.448″) to ICRF. Elements from Aster's catalog (`snapshots/neo-excerpt.js`, with Aster's anchor state and its screening-cache best window per body).

**A cell** (`cell()`): Earth at departure, the asteroid at departure + flight time, Lambert for each revolution family asked. Departure v∞ = v₁ − v_Earth, C3 = |v∞|², DLA = asin(v∞z/|v∞|) in ICRF with no rotation and RLA = atan2(v∞y, v∞x) (Aster's `dla.ts`, null below 1 m/s), the band from Aster's `feasibility.ts` for the chosen site, on the porkchop and for the chosen window alike (Cape: GREEN to 28.5°, AMBER to 57°, RED beyond; Vandenberg 70°/90°). Arrival v∞ = v₂ − v_asteroid. Aster's ΔV stack from `delta-v.ts`: injection √(C3 + 2μ/r) − √(μ/r) at 6,578.137 km and μ 398,600.4418 km³/s², rendezvous = arrival v∞, the same again for a sample return, 150 m/s station-keeping, 10 % margin. With several families allowed, a cell shows the one the chosen quantity prefers (Aster's porkchop rule is least C3).

**Where two-body propagation stops being true** (`encounters()`, `targetRefusals()`). The asteroid's distance to Earth is scanned daily from the element epoch to each arrival and every local minimum refined by golden section. An arrival is refused (`target_ephemeris`) past any Earth encounter closer than 0.05 au, the distance Aster's catalog ingestion screens encounters at (`closeApproachWindow.distMaxAu`), and (`arrival_geometry`) when the asteroid is inside Earth's sphere of influence at arrival, Laplace's a(μ⊕/μ☉)^(2/5) = 924,647 km from Aster's two GMs. Also refused: a body Aster tags `not-kepler-safe` (no body in this excerpt is; a test tags a copy of Apophis); condition code ≥ 7 (`target_orbit`); a transfer within 5° of 180°, where the plane is set by small offsets (`transfer_plane`, this module's own screening threshold); DLA beyond the chosen site's ceiling (`dla`); a transfer unbound about the Sun (`transfer_orbit`; the coast propagator and the drawing are elliptic only); departures outside the Earth ephemeris (`departure_date`); a grid whose every solved cell is refused (`selection`, saying how many for which reason); and always the patched-conic assumption (`dynamics`). Porkchop cells refused for any of the per-cell reasons (encounter or sphere of influence, DLA, near 180°, unbound) are hatched and left out of the grid's "best" and of `minimum_c3`; the raw minimum is reported beside them.

**Idea 38's departure, carried on** (`importBurnOut()`, `flown()`). The handoff is checked without re-flying idea 38 (that would need its whole kernel chain, 300 KB): v∞'s magnitude, C3, RLA and DLA against its own vector; UTC against TDB (Aster's 69.184 s); the departure state at the last burn's cutoff; v∞ along idea 37's asymptote (10⁻³°) at idea 37's C3. Idea 37's asymptote is recognised as Aster's cell by its vector (to 10⁻⁹ relative), never by its prose. When idea 38 departs: Earth's centre at the cutoff epoch with Earth's velocity plus 38's v∞ is coasted about the Sun (Kepler, `propagate()`), the closest approach to the asteroid found within 60 days of the planned arrival, and Lambert is solved again from 38's epoch to the planned arrival in the same family: the difference is the correction. When it does not depart, its refusals travel and `chain_departure` says the transfer shown is the one the chain set out to fly.

**Handoff schema (version 1, `from: "39-lamberts-road"`).** `handoff.json` is the sample: idea 38's committed sample handoff (idea 34's sample stack, which idea 38 finds 12.8 t of propellant short) with this page's defaults. The transfer is Aster's cell, recomputed; 13 refusals from ideas 33 to 38 travel with this module's `chain_departure` and `dynamics`.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain as numbers] The solver is right.** Aster's pinned cells to 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵, hapsira's Izzo and Gooding's method to 6.4 × 10⁻¹³ over 75 cases in all three forms of T(x), Lambert's theorem and Euler's parabolic time exact, T_min to 10⁻¹².
- **[Certain as numbers] It reproduces Aster.** Aster's DLA cell to 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s with Aster's Earth, 0.135 mm/s with this page's; 61 screening-cache windows to 0.24 mm/s; Aster's anchor states to 6 mm. The difference from Aster is the Earth interpolation and nothing else.
