Lumpy

Idea 42 parked the spacecraft beside the asteroid and refused one thing: its asteroid was a point. This page gives it a shape. It fills a published shape model with uniform density and computes the exact gravity of that solid, inside and out. It then reads off what a lander and an orbiter need: surface gravity and slope, escape speed, the places where spin and gravity balance, and which close orbits the lumps destroy. OSIRIS-REx's own orbit about Bennu is the test of how right it is.

        How this works

        The shapes

        Apophis: the radar shape of Brozović et al. (2018), 2,000 vertices and 3,996 plates about 25 m across, as the OSIRIS-APEX project delivered it to NAIF (a DSK, unchanged). Bennu: the OSIRIS-REx laser-altimetry model v021 at 12.6 m (Daly et al. 2020), a grid of 6 × 33 × 33 vertices; every second grid vertex is kept here (3,072 plates, 25 m), which moves gravity by at most 0.3% just outside the body (measured against the full model in the tests). Each is checked closed and wound outward, and the origin moved to the uniform-density centre of mass (5.2 m from the DSK's origin at Apophis, 1.3 m at Bennu).

        The gravity of a polyhedron

        A solid of uniform density bounded by flat plates has an exact potential (Werner and Scheeres 1997): a sum over its edges, each with a dyad built from the two plates' normals and a logarithm of distances, and over its plates, each with its normal's dyad times the solid angle it subtends. The same sums give gravity, its gradient, and an inside test for free: the solid angles add to 4π inside and to 0 outside. Density is GM / (G V). The node tests hold this to an independent code that integrates along edges instead (Tsoulis's method, the polyhedral-gravity package) and to the closed form for a box.

        The surface

        At each plate's centre: gravity; the acceleration a grain at rest feels, which is gravity less the spin's centrifugal pull (and, for a tumbler, the Euler term); the slope, the angle between the inward normal and that acceleration (over 90° the ground overhangs its own down); the geopotential, gravity's potential plus the spin's, as a height above its area mean; and the speed that escapes from the energy in a non-rotating field, straight up and along the spin. Statistics are weighted by plate area.

        Spin

        Bennu turns about its pole every 4.30 h (NAIF's bennu_v17.tpc, which includes its YORP spin-up). Apophis tumbles (Pravec et al. 2014): this page flies Euler's equations for a torque-free rigid body with Pravec's inertia ratios 0.61 and 0.965 from the nearest of NAIF's CK samples (every 20 days); against the CK's own samples it holds to under one arcsecond over ten days. After the April 2029 Earth encounter the spin is unknown and the page refuses.

        Equilibria and the Roche lobe

        In a body that turns steadily, a particle can sit still where gravity balances the centrifugal pull, near the synchronous radius (GM/ω²)^(1/3). They are found by Newton's method in the body frame and classified from the linearised motion in the turning frame: saddles have a real pair of eigenvalues, centres an oscillating pair (here every centre is unstable, through a complex quartet). The lowest of their energies bounds the rotational Roche lobe: surface below it is energetically trapped. A tumbler has no such frame and no such points.

        Close orbits

        Each start is a circular orbit at √(GM/r) on the node of the spin equator, at the chosen inclination, flown under the shape's gravity as it turns (idea 40's Dormand-Prince integrator). It ends as an impact (inside the shape), an escape (unbound beyond four Brillouin radii), or survives. A point mass would keep every one of them forever, so every impact or escape here is the lumps' doing. Radii run from just outside the Brillouin sphere to four times it; where idea 42's sunlight passes 5% of gravity the radius is refused (sunlight and the tide are idea 42's, which flies them).

        What it does not do

        Uniform density only: at Bennu the measured field differs (J2 3.7% stronger than this model, surface gravity by up to ±3% and slopes by ±1° per Scheeres et al. 2020). The shapes are 25 m plates: nothing at a lander's scale. The orbit scan leaves out sunlight and the tide, and the Orbital B test uses idea 42's flat-absorber sunlight, not the navigation team's ten-plate model. No spin after Apophis's 2029 encounter; no other bodies' shapes.
