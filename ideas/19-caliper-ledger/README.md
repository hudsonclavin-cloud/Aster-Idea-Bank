# Caliper Ledger
> Type the readings from the machined part against its PartForge FLIGHT declaration and get IN, OUT, UNDECIDED or NOT MEASURED per feature, compared exactly (a 6.70 hole is inside 6.60 ± 0.10, though floating point says 6.6 + 0.1 = 6.699999999999999), with the mesh kept in its own column and the record bound to the declaration by sha256.

**Kind:** tool
**For:** PartForge
**Run:** open `ideas/19-caliper-ledger/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/19-caliper-ledger/ledger.test.mjs` (Node built-ins, under a second) and `node ideas/19-caliper-ledger/ui-check.mjs` (headless Chromium through the globally installed Playwright, about 10 s; `check.mjs` runs it, after `build-data.mjs --check`). Rebuild the snapshot: `node ideas/19-caliper-ledger/build-data.mjs /home/user/Partforge`.
**Made by:** Thimble

## What it is

A bench page for the person holding the micrometer. Every measurement PartForge makes today is of the **mesh**: its flight-grade CMM casts rays at the tessellated model and reports that the geometry matches its own FLIGHT declaration. The interface control drawing then prints an empty last column, *Verified on the article*, and says "Fill the last column from inspection of the real part" (`index.html:6766`, at PartForge `0974a62`). Nothing in the app accepts a reading of the real part. This page does.

1. **Pick the declaration.** One of the seven flight templates PartForge ships (`tests/templates/*.scad`, snapshotted here), or paste or open your own `.scad`. The FLIGHT block is read with PartForge's own `parseFlight()`, copied verbatim. A file with no FLIGHT block, a malformed one, or one that declares nothing measurable is refused, with what would help; no placeholder rows appear.
2. **Put your instruments on the shelf** once: kind, unit (mm or in), the resolution you read off it, and, if you know it, its stated accuracy. A reading cannot be entered without one.
3. **Measure, feature by feature.** Every critical extent, OD, bore and *each hole* of a pattern is a card with its band (`146.150 … 146.250 mm`, and in inches rounded inward), where its tolerance came from (`tool default`, `user`, or `cited: …`), and an entry: readings (one box each, Enter for the next), or GO/NO-GO pins, plugs or rings with what each did. Each FLIGHT gauge (the O-ring groove GO ring, the NO-GO walls) is a card that records what the physical gauge did. The chip turns IN, OUT, UNDECIDED or stays NOT MEASURED, and says why. A sticky bar gives the part verdict: INCOMPLETE until every feature has a verdict and the operator and date are recorded; NONCONFORMING as soon as one feature is OUT; UNDECIDED if a gauge pair cannot settle a size; ALL IN only when all of it holds.
4. **Optionally import PartForge's report** (the browser's *Measurement report (.json)* or the JSON `cli.mjs check` prints). Its numbers fill an *as modelled* line on each card, labelled "the mesh, not this part". They never fill a reading, and a report for another part, or for another revision of this one, is refused.
5. **Keep the record.** Download an as-built JSON (everything typed, every verdict, the FLIGHT block's sha256, the file's sha256, operator, date) or a Markdown table in the ICD's column order with *Verified on the article* filled. A record can be resumed later, in another browser, but only against the same FLIGHT block. Drafts are kept in the browser per declaration; nothing is sent anywhere.

## Why I made it

PartForge's own evaluation names this as the gap. `docs/roadmap-evaluation.md:266` ("What the 50 missed", #4): "As-built / first-article inspection entry. Type the caliper and CMM readings from the **machined** part back in and diff against the model. This is the missing half of the whole CMM story: every measurement in the app today is of the *mesh*." Its skeptic adds (`:353`) that as-built entry "is a prerequisite for 45/46/47/48 and H, not a bonus idea", and the verdict on idea 37 (photo to dimensions) is to drop it but "keep the 2-d caliper-entry subset" (`:104`, `:235`; `:740` asks for "a 'type the caliper reading' input carrying dim_src"). The app's documents ask for the same thing in their own words: `tierEvidence()` lists "As-built inspection of every interface dimension, recorded against this drawing" for every tier-B/C part (`index.html:4795`), and the tier-A manufacturing sheet's status line says release "requires … as-built inspection of every dimension below" (`:6685`). I verified each of these lines in the checkout before building; the proposal's line numbers held.

What I weighed:

- **A Markdown filler for the ICD's last column.** Cheapest, and wrong: it types text into a cell with no verdict, no instrument and nothing tying it to the revision it describes. The value is in refusing to call a number "measured" without saying with what, and in binding it to one declaration.
- **A CMM-report importer** (CSV from a lab machine). No sample exists in the repository, and the club's inspection plans, read out of the templates, are micrometers, calipers, pins and gauge rings. A typed ledger serves the shop that exists.
- **An AS9102 Form 3 generator.** The evaluation itself warns (`:853`) that "dressing the table as AS9102 Form 3 invites a student to submit model numbers as first-article inspection results". So this is plainly labelled a ledger, not an FAI report, and the model column is fenced off.
- **Photo to dimensions.** Dropped by the evaluation (idea 37) for good reasons; the caliper-entry subset it kept is this.

This one won because the honest core is small and checkable (an exact comparison against a declared band, bound by a hash) and because everything else it needs already exists in PartForge and can be reused verbatim: the parser, the report row shape, the templates.

**What I changed from the judged proposal, and why.**
- *PartForge's parser, not a mirror.* The proposal planned a mirror parser to be swapped for the app's at landing. The DECL block is pure and marked, so I copied it verbatim (`build-data.mjs`, by marker) and the ledger already reads declarations the way the app does.
- *Every reading, not the mean.* The proposal listed mean, range and deviation before the verdict. A mean-based IN passes the oval OD above; the app's own CMM gates on extremes. The mean is shown for information only.
- *A fourth verdict, UNDECIDED, and GO/NO-GO for sizes.* The proposal had IN / OUT / NOT MEASURED and treated only FLIGHT gauges as clear/blocked. The templates' own plans inspect most holes with GO/NO-GO pins, and a pin pair that does not sit on the limits (the fin's) can prove neither IN nor OUT; saying so needs a word.
- *10:1 on the larger of resolution and stated accuracy*, plus near-limit and off-step flags. Resolution alone lets a 0.01 mm caliper with a ±0.02 mm accuracy pass on ±0.05. The evaluation (`:740`) asked for a lint of "the stated uncertainty against the process tolerance and the declared fit clearance"; this checks it against the declared tolerance only (no fit clearance), and the instrument recorded with each reading plays the role of the `dim_src` it asked for.
- *Position is not judged.* The proposal listed `pos_tol_mm` among the inputs; the evaluation's own objection (a radial offset compared as if it were a position zone, and no datum frame) stands, so every hole and bore says "not assessed" instead.
- *Completeness* counts every row, gauges included, and needs the operator and a valid date; the proposal said every tolerance-carrying feature.
- *Exact decimals and inch entry* (with inch limits rounded inward) were not in the proposal; the float trap at 6.60 + 0.10, on a real template row, is why.
- *All seven templates* are snapshotted (the proposal's data list named four, its pitch seven; 56 KB).
- *Quotations as they stand.* The proposal quotes "keep the 2-day caliper-entry subset"; the evaluation says "2-d caliper-entry subset" in its table (`:104`) and "2-day provenance subset" in its prose (`:733`). Its `cli.mjs check --asbuilt` landing does not exist; it is listed below as a change PartForge would need, not as a feature.

## How it works

**The declaration** is read by `snapshots/partforge.js`, which `build-data.mjs` derives from the checkout: index.html's `DECL-BEGIN … DECL-END` block (lines 4646–4859 at `0974a62`, found by marker; it holds `parseFlight()`, `mdSafe()`, `provenanceSummary()`, `flightCounts()`, the code `tests/flight-decl.test.mjs` runs) and `headless/partforge.mjs` lines 90–121 (`round4`, `FEATURES`, `measurementRows()`), both **verbatim**, plus the seven templates as strings, each checked against its own sidecar's sha256. So the ledger sees exactly the entries the app sees, including what the app drops (a missing axis, a non-positive tolerance) and demotes (a `source` tolerance with no reference becomes `default`). `ledger.js` then makes one row per measurable feature: each `critical` extent, `od`, `bore`, **each hole** of a `holes` pattern (circle patterns expanded the way `measureHoles()` does at `index.html:3983`, with `cmmFrame()`'s sign flip for axis y at `:3792`; positions are labels only), each `revolve` profile and each `gauge`. Loads are analysis rows and are listed as not inspected.

**Exact sizes.** Every size is an integer number of picometres (1e-9 mm) held as a BigInt. A typed reading is parsed as a decimal (a comma counts as the decimal point, because some phone keypads offer only a comma; at most 6 decimals and 40 characters, and at most 50 readings per feature: more is refused, never cut short); a declared number from the FLIGHT JSON goes through `String()`, which returns the shortest decimal that round-trips, i.e. the decimal the file spells. Inches convert at exactly 25.4 mm. So the band of the bulkhead's rod holes, 6.60 ± 0.10, is exactly 6.500 … 6.700 mm, and a 6.700 reading is inside it; in IEEE doubles 6.6 + 0.1 = 6.699999999999999 and a naive comparison rejects it. 5.7539 in is exactly 146.14906 mm (OUT of 146.150 … 146.250 by 0.00094); 5.7540 in is 146.1516 (IN), where floating point gives 146.15159999999997. Limits printed in inches are rounded **inward** (lower up, upper down) at 5 decimals, so nobody reading an inch instrument against the printed limit can pass a size the millimetre band rejects: 146.15 mm prints as 5.75394 in, 146.25 mm as 5.75787 in, and 12.65 mm as 0.49804 in where rounding to nearest would print 0.49803.

**Readings verdict.** IN if *every* reading lies in [nominal − tol, nominal + tol], limits included; OUT if any does not. Not the mean: an oval OD read 146.14 and 146.26 against 146.20 ± 0.05 has mean 146.2000, mean − nominal 0.0000, range 0.120, worst margin −0.010, and is OUT. PartForge's mesh CMM gates bores and ODs the same way, on the smallest and largest size it finds (`cmmVerdicts()`, `index.html:3849`). The mean, range, smallest … largest, mean − nominal and worst margin are shown for information. This is simple acceptance: no allowance for the instrument is subtracted from the band, and the page says so.

**Three flags**, none of which changes a verdict:
- *Discrimination* when the instrument's figure (the larger of resolution and stated accuracy) times ten exceeds the tolerance band (2 × tol). For ±0.05 the band is 0.10 mm: a 0.01 mm caliper is exactly a tenth and is not flagged; a 0.02 mm one is, and so is a 0.01 mm caliper whose stated accuracy is ±0.02, and a 0.0005 in caliper (0.0127 mm). This is the 10:1 shop rule of thumb, labelled as such on the page; I cite no standard for it.
- *Near limit* when a reading is within that figure of a limit: a better instrument could reverse the verdict.
- *Off step* when a reading is not a whole number of resolution steps (146.213 on a 0.01 caliper): the resolution or the reading is wrong.

**GO/NO-GO.** A pin or plug of size *s* that enters a hole proves the size is at least *s*; one that stops proves at most *s*. A ring or snap gauge that passes over an outside size proves at most *s*; one that stops proves at least *s* (bores and holes are inside sizes; ODs and critical extents outside). The members give an interval. IN if the interval lies inside the band; OUT if it meets the band in at most a point (a GO pin at the lower limit that stops, a NO-GO at the upper limit that enters); UNDECIDED otherwise, with the reason. Contradictory results get no verdict. The templates' own inspection plans supply a reconciliation: bulkhead rod holes "6.50 GO / 6.70 NO-GO", coupler pin holes "2.10 GO / 2.30 NO-GO", centering-ring bore "101.65 GO / 101.75 NO-GO" and M4 holes "4.40 GO / 4.60 NO-GO", motor-retainer bore "98.55 GO / 98.65 NO-GO" all bound their declared bands exactly; the fin's "tab holes with 4.38 GO / 4.60 NO-GO pins" does not (the band is 4.400 … 4.600, so a hole between 4.38 and 4.40 passes this pair and is outside it), and the ledger says UNDECIDED, "0.020 mm below the lower limit 4.400".

**FLIGHT gauges and profiles** are attribute rows: the declaration says whether gauge module `m()` must clear or be blocked (parseFlight maps `blocked`/`contact`/`touch` to `interfere`); you record what the physical gauge did and *what was used*. Without the "what was used", no verdict.

**The part.** NONCONFORMING if any row is OUT (however much is still unmeasured); otherwise INCOMPLETE if any row has no verdict or the operator or a valid date is missing; otherwise UNDECIDED if any row is; otherwise ALL IN. Every row counts, gauges included, not only tolerance-carrying ones. The status also carries caveats: simple acceptance, and how many of the declared tolerances are tool defaults (bulkhead: 10 of 11, counted per declared entry by PartForge's `provenanceSummary()`), because IN against a tool default is IN against a number nobody has stood behind.

**The binding hash** is SHA-256 (implemented in `ledger.js`, tested against the FIPS 180-2 examples and `node:crypto`, so the page needs no secure context) over the exact lines from the one holding `FLIGHT-BEGIN` to the end of the one holding `FLIGHT-END`, located with parseFlight's own regular expression, after normalising CRLF to LF (a Windows checkout must not look like another revision; Aster's OQ-17-7 is the same lesson). For the templates, and any file where only the two marker lines contain those words, it equals `sed -n '/FLIGHT-BEGIN/,/FLIGHT-END/p' part.scad | sha256sum`:

| Template | Rows (holes) | FLIGHT block sha256 (first 16) |
|---|---|---|
| bulkhead | 21 (10) | `ea41c20b0c1ba268` |
| coupler | 7 (3) | `b37baa8301f8b814` |
| centering-ring | 12 (8) | `03e1dd72ad51f6ef` |
| motor-retainer | 12 (8) | `2bb3dfd25a65d4d8` |
| nosecone | 6 (0) | `64a85dd3ba7e83be` |
| fin | 11 (2) | `9c049d1ca9a39fb2` |
| av-sled | 21 (16) | `4f6a16c85ff1f1c3` |

An edit outside the block (a NOTE, a module) leaves the hash alone; a one-digit edit inside it (146.20 → 146.21) changes it. The record also carries the whole file's sha256, which does see outside edits.

**The model column.** `importReport()` accepts the envelope `cli.mjs check` prints (`{tool:'check', value:{part, flight:{measurements:[rows]}}}`), its `value`, or the browser's `measurementReport()` (`index.html:6816`: `{app:'PartForge', part, measurements: lastGate.flightRows, …}`), whose flightRows go through PartForge's own `measurementRows()`, so both shapes reach the same rows. The report's `part` must equal the `.scad`'s PART line (`firstPartName()`, `index.html:7166`); then every declared row must find its report row by kind, name and order of occurrence, with the same nominal and tolerance (after the same `round4`), and the same number of holes; any missing, extra or changed row refuses the whole import as `wrong_revision`. The rows land in a separate map. The readings are not passed to the function at all.

**The record.** `exportLedger()` writes format `caliper-ledger/1`: part, `declaration_sha256`, `file_sha256`, source (template file and commit, or pasted), operator, date, remarks, status, the instruments as typed, and per row the band as exact decimals, the entry as typed, the verdict, its reason, flags, statistics and the model value if any. `importLedger()` refuses a record whose `declaration_sha256` differs. `toMarkdown()` writes the ICD's header, `| Interface | Nominal (mm) | Nominal (in) | Tol (mm) | Tolerance from | As modelled | Verified on the article |` (copied from `icdSheet()`, `index.html:6768`, and checked against it by a test), using the ICD's own row labels and its 4-decimal `inch()` for the nominal column, then a readings table, what was not assessed, the declaration's inspection plan and the caveats. Free text people type goes through PartForge's `mdSafe()`.

## Honest assessment

What works:
- [Certain] The comparison is exact: decimal arithmetic in picometres, limits included, inches at exactly 25.4 mm, and tests pin the cases where floating point gets it wrong.
- [Certain] The declaration is read by PartForge's own parser and the report rows by PartForge's own `measurementRows()`, both verbatim at `0974a62`; `build-data.mjs --check` fails if either drifts.
- [Certain] A record is bound to one FLIGHT block: the hash is reproducible with `sed | sha256sum`, and a record or report from another revision is refused, not merged.
- [Certain] Nothing from the model ever becomes a reading. `importReport()` is never handed the readings, and both test suites check that nothing changed.
- [Certain] No verdict without an instrument and a resolution, or, for a gauge row, without saying what was used; no part verdict but INCOMPLETE without an operator and a date.
- [Likely] The 10:1 rule as used here is the common shop convention (often called the rule of ten), and I have applied it to resolution or stated accuracy, not to a measurement uncertainty, which nobody at a club bench computes. It is a rule of thumb; I cite no standard.

What is rough:
- [Certain] **Hole position is not assessed.** The declaration's `pos_tol_mm` needs a datum frame to mean anything on an article, and PartForge's evaluation already notes the app compares a radial centre offset against it rather than a Y14.5 diametral zone (`roadmap-evaluation.md:653-659`, `:699`). Each hole and bore card says so; so does the record.
- [Certain] Simple acceptance: no guard band. The near-limit flag tells you when that matters; the verdict does not change.
- [Certain] GO/NO-GO members are treated as exactly their nominal size; gauge-maker's tolerance and wear are not modelled.
- [Likely] Form and size are not separated: a GO pin proves the largest pin that enters, a caliper a local two-point size, and the ledger records both as "size". An oval hole read with a caliper at two angles is caught; a tapered one read at one depth is not.
- [Certain] The "tap drill" hint is a heuristic on the feature's name.
- [Certain] The instrument shelf records what you type, not whether the instrument is calibrated, and has no calibration-due date.
- [Certain] The model column is tested only against an **invented** report. No measurement report exists in the PartForge checkout, and producing one needs `openscad-wasm`, which is not installed here. The import follows the shapes in the code (`measurementReport()`, the `check` envelope, `measurementRows()`), not a file the app has written.
- [Speculative] That it is quick enough at a bench. It is phone-first, one card per feature with Enter-to-next and "Next to measure", but no machinist has used it; touch was exercised in headless Chromium with emulated touch, and only Chromium was tried (`file://` and HTTP).
- [Certain] Pasted files are not size-limited beyond what the browser holds; a 50 KB `.scad` is kept in `localStorage` so a reload does not lose it.

Findings about the source repo (PartForge at `0974a62`; reported, not fixed):
- [Certain] `tests/templates/fin.scad:38` (and its copy in `index.html:2220`) inspects the tab holes "with 4.38 GO / 4.60 NO-GO pins", but the same file declares them 4.50 ± 0.10 (`fin.scad:30`), lower limit 4.40. The GO pin is 0.02 mm under the limit, so a 4.38–4.40 hole passes the plan and fails the declaration. The other five GO/NO-GO pairs in the templates' plans sit exactly on their limits.
- [Certain] `tests/templates/bulkhead.scad:32` declares the two "terminal block #8-32 tap drill" holes as FLIGHT features at 3.45 ± 0.10, and its own plan (`:43`) inspects them "with a 2B thread plug gauge after tapping". On the finished article the 3.45 diameter no longer exists, so a ledger of the finished part cannot verify those rows except in process. The coupler's plan does it the other way ("tap drill with a 3.40 pin before tapping"). [Likely] The FLIGHT schema has no notion of an in-process dimension, which is the underlying gap.
- [Certain] `tests/templates/av-sled.scad:24` checks the rod holes (declared 6.60 ± 0.15, so 6.45 … 6.75) "with a 6.35 mm rod: must slide", a single member 0.10 mm below the lower limit, and the boss holes (4.00 ± 0.15, so 3.85 … 4.15) "with a 3.9 mm pin", a single member with nothing on the upper limit. Neither can show conformance to the declared band (the ledger records both as UNDECIDED); the first is a functional fit check, not an inspection of the declared size.
- [Likely] `mfgSheet()` (`index.html:6699-6702`) prints the mesh's OK/FAIL in a column headed **Result** on the tier-A manufacturing sheet, beside **Modelled**, with no column for the article. The ICD (`:6768`) separates *As modelled* from *Verified on the article*; the shop sheet does not, and a reader can take "Result OK" as an inspection result. The evaluation names the same risk for another idea (`roadmap-evaluation.md:853`).
- [Certain] Neither `measurementReport()` (`index.html:6816`) nor the `check` envelope carries a hash of the FLIGHT block, so a report can only be bound to a revision by part name and row-by-row comparison, as done here. `firstPartName()` (`:7166`) returns `'part'` for any file without a PART line, so two unnamed parts share a name.
- [Certain] `roadmap-evaluation.md:659` cites `index.html:3726` for the radial position comparison and `:5634/5636` for printing "pos ±x"; at `0974a62` they are `:3964` and `:6640/6642`, more of the stale line numbers the briefing lists.

Gates this has not been through: PartForge's **skeptic review** (none), its **data provenance pipeline** (the only data are copies of `tests/templates/`; no table rows are used), and its **geometry gate** (not applicable: no geometry is produced); `tests/run.mjs` does not run these tests; the verbatim blocks are from one commit; no engineer has reviewed the verdict rules or signed a record.

## Where it would land
