# Catch
> Six ways to stop at the Earth, flown for one arrival: idea 47's 9.6 t of Apophis at 5.86 km/s has no propellant left, so only the air can stop it; a direct entry has a 5.69° corridor but peaks near 40 g, and a skip entry at -6.50° comes down in two passes at 8.6 g. The flight model reproduces Stardust's reconstructed 32.89 g to -0.34 % and Genesis's 27.0 g to +0.27 %, and an independent integrator to 1e-4.

**Kind:** explorable tool
**For:** Aster + Skyhook

Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/48-catch/catch.test.mjs` (9 tests, about 10 s) and `node ideas/48-catch/ui-check.mjs` (69 checks in headless Chromium, about 25 s; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). ## What it is

Idea 48 is module 18 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 47 brought the asteroid's material home. This page stops it, and hands module 49 (Hot Return) the state at the edge of the atmosphere.

- **Pick the arrival.** The chain: idea 47's committed handoff (Apophis's material, 9,565 kg, v∞ 5.858 km/s, perigee aimed at 123.7 km, no propellant left). Or your own v∞, mass, propellant and Isp along the chain's asymptote. Or one of three capsules that came home from deep space with nothing but their shape: Stardust (2006), Genesis (2004), OSIRIS-REx (2023), at their published entry states.
- **Pick the vehicle.** Four shapes with NASA aerodynamics: an Apollo-derived capsule (the TransHab aerocapture study's: L/D 0.37), Stardust's and OSIRIS-REx's 60° sphere-cone (C<sub>A</sub> against Mach from LAURA), Genesis's. The mass in the air is the arrival's or the shape's own. Choose the entry angle (empty: mid-corridor) and a constant bank.
- **Set the limits.** Deceleration (Stardust and Genesis's 40 g by default; the TransHab crew study's 5 g is a typing away), heat rate (Stardust's 1200 W/cm²), the target orbit (the TransHab study's 407 km), the lunar flyby altitude and the Moon's distance, the Skyhook grapple tolerance, the Δv margin.
- **Read the six options** in one table: direct entry, skip entry (one pass out, the next lands), aerocapture (one pass out, a burn to stay up), a capture burn at perigee, a lunar flyby, the Skyhook upper tip. Each says feasible or refused, the Δv and propellant it needs against what is aboard, its peak deceleration and heating, its corridor, and opens to its structured refusals.
- **Four charts.** Peak deceleration against entry angle for lift up, sideways and down, with both corridors and the limit (tap to choose an angle, arrow keys move it); where each angle leaves the air (apoapsis after the pass); the chosen pass (altitude, speed, deceleration, heating; a skip entry's two passes end to end with the coast marked); Δv to stop against v∞ for every option (tap to try a v∞).
- **Checked against reality:** six rows computed live from NASA's reports on Stardust, Genesis and OSIRIS-REx.
- **Export the handoff** for module 49 (copy, download or link).

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 48: "Earth arrival options compared: direct entry, aerocapture, lunar gravity assist, propulsive capture, and the Skyhook upper-tip catch (idea 01's kernel)", taking "Earth-arrival v∞" and handing on "capture choice + state". Idea 47's README (*Where it would land*): "Module 48 (Catch) takes `earth_arrival` and the `entry_speed` refusal (direct entry, aerocapture, propulsive capture, the Skyhook catch)". Its handoff ends with that refusal: "Arriving at 12.53 km/s at 125 km, above NHATS's 12 km/s entry limit: as handed on, it needs braking, aerocapture or a catch." This module answers it, and says so in `capture.answers`.
- **Aster and Skyhook asked for the seam.** Aster's `strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md` names **K2**, the Aster → Skyhook interface ("is an inbound payload's Earth-arrival v∞ inside the tether's catch envelope?"), and its master plan the Aster Momentum lens. Idea 01 built K2's energy condition and refused plane and phase for want of a v∞ *vector*; idea 47 now hands one on, so this module decides the plane bound (the arrival's plane is inclined at least the asymptote's declination, 30.8°) and puts the tether beside every other way to stop.
- **What a person decides with it.** Whether a return needs propellant kept for the Earth at all (the chain does not: the air can take it, at 8.6 g); which entry angle to tell module 47 to aim at (here -6.50°, a perigee at 56.4 km, not the 123.7 km flyby it aims at now); how wide the corridor is against the ±0.08° Stardust, Genesis and OSIRIS-REx were delivered to; what each option would cost if it were wanted (a capture burn into 407 km: 6.8 t of propellant for a 9.6 t arrival); when the Moon or the tether becomes worth it (below 1.86 km/s for one flyby; at 3.61 km/s and under 5 t for the Phase 3 tip).

Alternatives weighed.
1. *Closed forms only* (Allen–Eggers for ballistic entry, the Chapman skip relations for lift). Rejected as the answer: they ignore gravity and the Earth's curve, which at 12.5 km/s and 8° change peak deceleration by tens of percent; kept as the integrator's test (agreement 5e-5).
2. *Reuse idea 35's flight integrator.* Its derivative is coupled to engines, staging and steering and is not exported; snapshotting it would bring 120 KB of ascent and still have no lift. Reused instead: its atmosphere (PartForge's `isa()`) and Earth constants, unmodified. The entry equations (gravity, drag, lift at a bank angle, air turning with the Earth) are new here, and an independent integrator in another formulation checks them.
3. *A round Earth for the air.* Built first; it put Stardust's peak at 35.5 g (+8 %) and OSIRIS-REx's peak heating 10 s early. The capsules entered over Utah, where the ground is about 9 km nearer the centre than at the equator; measuring altitude from pck00010's flattened Earth brings both within 0.4 % and 3 s. Built: the spheroid.
4. *A guided entry* (bank reversals to a target). Cut for this session: constant-bank lift-up and lift-down bounds give the classical theoretical corridor; a refusal says a guidance law with dispersions narrows it.
5. *The Moon's real position.* Cut: the best flyby needs the Moon at one place on the incoming leg at one hour, which moves module 47's arrival date. The energy question (can one flyby capture this v∞ at all?) is answered for every geometry; phasing is refused.

## How it works

**Constants, all from snapshots.** Earth GM 398,600.4418 km³/s² (Aster `delta-v.ts`), equatorial radius 6,378,136.6 m and rotation 360.9856235°/day (pck00010), all via idea 35's `earth.js`; polar radius 6,356,751.9 m (pck00010 `BODY399_RADII`, via idea 35's excerpt, `earth-radii.js`); Moon and Earth–Moon GMs (DE440, via ideas 40 and 47); ERFA's Earth rotation angle (via ideas 37 and 47); the 1976 atmosphere and g0 = 9.80665 (PartForge `isa()`, via ideas 02 and 35); Skyhook's Rev A canon and idea 01's kernel (via idea 01). Everything else is in `snapshots/entry-sources.js`, written by `build-data.mjs` from twelve NASA reports and the NSSDCA Moon sheet: each number sits beside the sentence it was read from, and the build found every sentence verbatim in the PDF's text and every number inside its sentence (66 quotes). Stardust's C<sub>A</sub> table was transcribed from the page image (its text layer is incomplete); six of its nine values are also checked against the text layer.

**The pass** (`fly()`). A point mass in geocentric ICRF axes: gravity μr/r³; drag ½ρV²C<sub>D</sub>A/m against the velocity relative to the air (v − ω×r, the air turning about the ICRF z axis); lift ½ρV²C<sub>L</sub>A/m perpendicular to it, in the vertical plane at bank 0 and rolled about the relative velocity by the bank (180° pushes down). Density: the 1976 standard to its table's top at 85,999.95 m geometric, none above (as idea 35). Altitude is measured along the radius above the spheroid. RK4 at 0.1 s from the 86 km crossing (reached on the conic from the 125 km entry interface); peaks refined by a parabola through three samples. Halving the step moves peak deceleration by 1e-6 relative. Deceleration is the aerodynamic acceleration over g0. Heating: Sutton & Graves (NASA TR R-376) define K = q√(R/p<sub>s</sub>)/(h<sub>s</sub> − h<sub>w</sub>) and fit K = 0.1113 for air (their Table II); with the Newtonian stagnation pressure ρV² (in atmospheres) and h<sub>s</sub> = V²/2 on a cold wall, q = 1.7483e-4 √(ρ/R<sub>n</sub>) V³ (W/m²), convective only.

**The arrival** (`hyperbolaEntry()`). Idea 47's v∞ vector and its arrival plane (from its perigee state) are kept; for an entry angle γ at r<sub>EI</sub> = R<sub>E</sub> + 125 km the hyperbola is fixed: h = r<sub>EI</sub>v<sub>EI</sub>cos γ, e² = 1 + v∞²h²/μ², and since the incoming asymptote is (1/e)P + (√(e²−1)/e)Q in its perifocal frame, the periapsis direction is P = S/e − (√(e²−1)/e)(ĥ×S). The state at 125 km, the perigee it needs, B = h/v∞, and the time from entry to perigee (hyperbolic Kepler) follow; the perigee time is idea 47's. The retarget is |ΔB| over the time to go (14 days: OSIRIS-REx's TCM-11), with the margin. Flown capsules start from their published entry speed, angle, the landing site's latitude and the landing ellipse's azimuth.

**The corridors** (`corridorDirect()`, `corridorAero()`). Direct entry: the shallow edge is the shallowest angle that stays in with full lift down; the steep edge the steepest within the deceleration and heat-rate limits with full lift up (a guided capsule rolls between them). Aerocapture: the angles that leave the air at the target apoapsis with lift down (shallow) and lift up (steep); the nominal is bank 90°. All by bisection, to better than 1e-6°. The perigee-raise burn at apoapsis lifts the exit orbit's perigee to the target; skip entry flies the same nominal pass, coasts the exit ellipse to its next entry and flies again until it stays in.

**The other options.** Capture burn at perigee: √(v∞² + 2μ/r<sub>p</sub>) − √(2μ/r<sub>p</sub> − 2μ/(r<sub>p</sub>+r<sub>a</sub>)), into the target circle and into a loose orbit with apogee at the Moon's 384,400 km. Propellant: Tsiolkovsky with the arrival's exhaust speed (idea 46's 4,151 m/s on the chain) and Aster's 10 % margin. Lunar flyby (`lunarBest()`): patched conics, coplanar, the Moon on a circle at NSSDCA's distance at √(μ<sub>EM</sub>/a); the payload crosses the Moon's orbit at √(v∞² + 2μ/a) in any direction; the flyby turns the Moon-relative velocity by up to 2 asin(1/(1 + r<sub>p</sub>w²/μ<sub>Moon</sub>)) (r<sub>p</sub>: the Moon's 1,737.4 km radius plus the flyby altitude, 0 by default: the physical bound); the best direction is searched (720 then a ternary refinement). Skyhook: idea 01's `energy()` per tier (trim = payload speed at the upper tip minus the tip's), the canon's 5,000 kg payload (`mass_ratio.payload_mass_kg_for_tonnage`), the plane bound |δ∞|, idea 01's phase refusal; and, if it caught, the release from the lower tip into the air (idea 01's 6,371 km Earth, stated).

**The choice.** Auto takes the feasible option that burns the least propellant, then the lowest peak deceleration. Lunar and Skyhook are never offered as feasible: their phase (and the tether's plane) cannot be shown here; when their energy works the table says "energy only".

**Importing idea 47** (`importWayHome()`). Checked without re-running idea 47 (its kernel and ephemerides are 400 KB): the envelope and units; v∞ against its vector's length; the declination against the vector; the entry speed against √(v∞² + 2μ/(R+125 km)); the perigee state's energy against v∞; the perigee altitude against the state; mass = dry + material + propellant; vₑ = Isp·g0; TDB against UTC; all to 1e-6. Idea 47's 36 refusals travel with their `from`.

**Handoff schema (version 1, `from: "48-catch"`).** `handoff.json` is the sample (the chain, this page's defaults).

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain as measured] The flight model against flown capsules.** Stardust's peak deceleration 32.78 g against the reconstruction's 32.89 g (-0.34 %), its altitude 137.9 s after entry 31.031 km against radar's 31.03 km (+0.6 m; its Mach there 1.12 against 1.23, because one hypersonic C<sub>A</sub> table is held below Mach 7); Genesis 27.07 g against 27.0 g (+0.27 %). [Likely] Part of that agreement is the reconstructions': they fitted a drag multiplier (Stardust +0.83 %, Genesis -8.1 % between C<sub>D</sub> and density) to radar, so they are themselves models; the drogue-time altitude in particular was the point Stardust's fit was made to match, and the 0.6 m here is partly luck.
- **[Certain as measured] OSIRIS-REx.** Peak deceleration 31.45 g against the pre-flight 32 g (-1.71 %), but the papers on file give no entry heading: posigrade headings from 30° to 150° give 29.96 to 32.96 g, so this row tests little. Convective peak heating 706 W/cm² against the predicted 720 W/cm² (-1.94 %; whether that prediction includes radiation is not stated). Peak heating 50.72 s after entry against 53.854 s seen by the infrared cameras (-3.13 s), with the same heading caveat.
- **[Certain as measured] The flattened Earth matters.** On a round Earth (altitude above 6,378.1 km everywhere) Stardust's peak is 35.55 g (+8.1 %), Genesis's 28.00 g (+3.7 %), OSIRIS-REx's 34.47 g and its peak heating at 43.3 s (10.5 s early). Above Utah the ground is about 9 km nearer the centre; an entry interface defined as a radius (6,503.14 km) is 133 km above it: "or approximately 133 km above Mean Sea Level", as the OSIRIS-REx navigation paper puts it.
- **[Certain as measured] The integrator against an independent one.** Vinh's equations in another frame, another integrator and another atmosphere code agree to 1e-5 on peak deceleration, 1e-4 on heating and heat load and 1e-4° on the corridor edges; the closed forms to 5e-5. The constant-bank lateral case (the chain's direct entry at bank 90°) differs by 2e-4, unexplained but small.
- **[Certain as arithmetic, on the chain's numbers] The chain lands without propellant, by skip entry.** In the Apollo-derived shape (ballistic coefficient 491 kg/m² at 9,565 kg), direct entry works from -11.57° (40 g with lift up) to -5.88° (stays in with lift down), 5.69° wide, 36 times the ±0.08° delivery accuracy; its mid-corridor angle flown at bank 90° reaches 39.5 g and 600 W/cm². Skip entry at -6.50° (bank 90°) leaves the air on a 407 km-apoapsis orbit whose perigee is 19 km under the surface, coasts 60 minutes and comes back at 7.50 km/s relative to the air: 6.1 g and 330 W/cm² on the first pass, 8.6 g and 80 W/cm² on the second, 42.9 kJ/cm² in all (more than the direct entry's 17.8: the gentler path spends longer in the air). Aerocapture's corridor is 2.99° wide but staying up needs a 138 m/s burn (312 kg; none aboard). A capture burn into 407 km needs 5,122 m/s (6,780 kg; 1,443 kg for the dry craft alone); even a loose orbit to the Moon's distance needs 1,734 m/s (3,265 kg). One lunar flyby takes v∞ from 5.858 to 5.698 km/s and captures only below 1.855 km/s. The Skyhook tips need trims of 1,923, 1,623 and 923 m/s, the canon sizes the tether for 5,000 kg, and the arrival's plane is inclined at least 30.8° where Rev A's lower-tip note assumes an equatorial orbit.
- **[Certain] What module 47 must change.** It aims at a 123.7 km perigee: that is a flyby (the air in this model ends at 86 km). Entering at -6.50° needs a perigee at 56.4 km. A correction 14 days out would cost 80 mm/s, 0.18 kg of propellant, and 0 kg arrives; the refusal tells module 47 to aim there instead, which costs its departure burn almost nothing.
- **[Likely] The corridors are upper bounds.** They are the theoretical lift-up/lift-down limits at constant bank. A guided vehicle with navigation, density and aerodynamic dispersions has less (the TransHab study's guided corridor was "at least 0.7 degrees"; a different vehicle and limit, so not compared here).
- **[Speculative] The heating.** Sutton & Graves is convective only; at lunar-return speeds Apollo's "radiative heating is approximately one-third of the total heating rate", and the chain's large nose radius makes radiation larger. No heat-shield mass is computed (module 49).
- **What is rough or cut.** No air above 86 km (its effect is inside the measured gaps above, since NASA's numbers include it, but it is not separated), no winds, no day-of density (OSIRIS-REx's team saw "atmospheric density being much lower than was Earth-GRAM was predicting"). No J2 during a pass. Constant bank, no guidance, no dispersions. The Apollo-derived shape's nose radius is half its diameter, the Apollo report's convention before its spherical-segment adjustment (assumed). Genesis's C<sub>A</sub> is Stardust's and its diameter "approximately 1.5 m".
