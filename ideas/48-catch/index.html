Catch

Idea 47 brought nine tonnes of Apophis home at 5.86 km/s. Something has to stop it. This page flies every way the Earth can catch an arrival: straight into the air, one pass through it and out again, a rocket burn, a swing past the Moon, Skyhook's tether. Each is flown or solved for the arrival you pick, with the limits you set, and the one you choose is handed to module 49 as a state at the edge of the atmosphere.

            How this works

            Flying a pass through the air

            The vehicle is a point mass under the Earth's gravity (Aster's GM), drag along its motion through the air and lift across it, rolled about that motion by the bank angle (0 lifts it up, 180 pushes it down). The air turns with the Earth, so the speed that matters is the speed relative to it. Density is the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere (PartForge's code, as idea 35 flew the ascent) up to its table's top at 86 km, and none above. Altitude is measured from the flattened Earth (pck00010's polar radius): the capsules entered over Utah, where the ground is 9 km nearer the centre than at the equator, and on a round Earth their peak deceleration comes out 8 % high and their peak heating 10 s early. Fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 0.1 s; the peaks are refined by a parabola. Heating is Sutton and Graves's stagnation-point formula for air, convective only.

            The corridors

            Steeper entries decelerate harder; shallower ones skip back out. A direct entry must stay in on its first pass and stay under the limits: its corridor runs from the shallowest angle that stays in with the lift pointed down to the steepest that keeps under the limits with the lift pointed up (a guided capsule rolls between those). An aerocapture must leave again at the right height: its corridor runs between the angle that does so with lift down and the one that does with lift up. A skip entry here flies a constant bank of 90° with no burn: its corridor is the range of angles from which it lands within the limits in up to six passes, and it only actually skips (two passes or more) in the shallow part of that range. The width of a corridor is to be compared with how well the arrival can be aimed: Stardust, Genesis and OSIRIS-REx were held to ±0.08°.

            Arriving from idea 47

            Idea 47 hands on the arrival's v∞ vector and its perigee, aimed at 125 km above the Earth: a flyby. To enter at an angle the perigee must be lower. Keeping the v∞ direction and the plane, the page solves the hyperbola that crosses 125 km at the chosen angle, reports the perigee it needs, and what a correction 14 days out (OSIRIS-REx's last one) costs to move the aim there. The chain arrives with no propellant, so the aim has to be set in module 47. When idea 47's own aim already enters where the chosen option works, nothing is corrected and the entry is flown as aimed.

            The Moon and the tether

            A flyby of the Moon turns the arrival's velocity relative to the Moon by an angle that shrinks as that velocity grows. Patched conics, the Moon on a circle: the best single flyby is searched over every approach direction, and the largest v∞ it can capture follows. A three-body integration agrees to 0.16 %. Skyhook is idea 01's kernel on Rev A's canon: the payload must pass the upper tip at the tip's own speed, inside the tether's plane, at the instant the tip is there, and weigh no more than the payload the tether is sized for. Only speed and mass can be checked; Rev A states no orbit plane and no tether has flown.
