    How this works

    Flown, not modelled. run-chain.mjs runs ideas 31 to 49 in order in Node, each through its own loader and the recipe its own build script uses to make its sample (sample() for 32 to 40; the import, run and handoff calls for 41 to 49), each fed the handoff the previous one just made. It reproduces all 19 committed samples' outputs exactly when given the chain's own choices. The rocket's payload can be closed on the spacecraft: idea 42 sizes the spacecraft for the arrival this very departure produces, the rocket is flown again with that payload, and the loop repeats until the two agree to 0.01 kg. For any asteroid but Apophis, idea 39 is solved first (least delta-v for a sample return over its default window) and its asymptote handed to idea 37.

    What this page computes. The events in time order with the mass each link reports, the delta-v budget and who pays it, the seams (where a link restarts from its own assumption rather than the last link's result, measured), the refusals each link raised and which ones fail to travel on (identities compared link by link; the four a module documents as answered are not losses), and a verdict: the mission closes only if it lands and nothing breaks. The what-if re-runs three budgets the chain already has (idea 42's tanks, idea 47's ledger, idea 49's main parachute) and reads Aster's launch-vehicle curves.

    Against reality. NHATS (JPL) publishes round trips with every term of their delta-v. The chain's two legs flown on NHATS's own dates (idea 39 out, idea 47 back) and summed as NHATS sums them give its total within the gap shown above. Its Earth-departure term uses Aster's injectionDvFromC3 at 400 km, the parking orbit NHATS states.

    Limits. The page does not run the 19 kernels (they are about 1.3 MB of code and 2 MB of data, in their own folders); it reads what the runner computed and the handoffs you bring. It does not re-check an imported link's physics: each module's own page does that, and the table of links opens them.
