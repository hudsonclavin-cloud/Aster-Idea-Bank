1 · What one throw takes

The payload is caught at the lower tip and let go at the upper tip. With nothing dissipated, the tether system loses exactly the orbital energy the payload gains, so that is what reboost has to put back.

2 · Putting it back

Time to restore one throw is energy ÷ (installed power × efficiency × duty), the arithmetic of finance/reboost_bound.py. The founding document's sizing rule: restore one design-payload boost within the inter-catch interval, initially 5–10 days, with 30 % margin.

Logarithmic slider, 10 kW to 100 MW.

3 · The orbit in between

Reboost takes days; the tether's orbit changes at once. The tether system (hub plus fibre) gives up the payload's energy gain, its centre of mass ends lower at the throw point, and half an orbit later the lower tip comes lowest.

The repository names no tip-altitude floor. docs/project_instructions.md:44 puts a HASTOL-class tip at 80–150 km; the Rev A tip is designed at 290 km.
