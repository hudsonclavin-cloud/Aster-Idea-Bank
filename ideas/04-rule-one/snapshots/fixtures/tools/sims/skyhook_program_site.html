A tether supports a tip at Vc with no taper at all; above it, cross-section must grow exponentially toward the hub.

Taper ratio is not tether mass. Taper (EQ 4) is the peak cross-sectional area at the hub divided by the area at the tip — but that peak exists only near the hub; most of each arm is far thinner. The quantity that actually costs money is the tether mass integrated over the whole tapered profile, the Moravec/Forward two-arm mass ratio:

EQ 6M ⁄ mp = 2√π · x · ex² · erf(x)    x = ( vtip ⁄ Vc )·√( SF ⁄ (1−derate) )

At the Rev A reference (2.5 km/s, SF 2.5): 40× at 0 % derate, 64× at 15 %, and a conservative 122× at 30 % — this last figure is the Rule 4 financial base case. The 2.8 km/s cislunar option rises to 295×; the 3.5 km/s Mars-class Phase 3 option reaches ~3,086× and is launch-cost-dominated. That sounds terrible until you remember the tether is reusable thousands of times and is the orbital equivalent of a bridge: bridges also outweigh the trucks that cross them.
