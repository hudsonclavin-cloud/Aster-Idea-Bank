Window

A transfer to an asteroid leaves Earth along one direction in space, the departure asymptote. The parking orbit must contain that direction when the departure burn comes, and it must also pass over the launch pad at liftoff, which turns with the Earth. Most moments of the day give a plane that leaves the pad on an azimuth the range will not allow; the stretches that do are the launch windows. This page finds them, says when to go and on what heading, coasts the parking orbit with the Earth's bulge turning its plane, and hands module 38 the departure point and its epoch. It opens on the Saturn V that flew Apollo 17, departing for Apophis on the asymptote Aster itself computes.

Against Apollo 17 (7 December 1972)

The flight report gives the Saturn V's azimuth at the scheduled liftoff and at the actual one, 2 h 40 min later; the press kit gives the window and its azimuth limits. A direction fixed in space fitted to the two azimuths predicts the rest.

How this works

    
    
      
  • The plane. At liftoff the parking orbit's plane passes through Earth's centre and the pad; at the departure burn it must also hold the asymptote (JPL Publication 82-43, Launch/Injection Geometry, eq. 9). That fixes the launch azimuth at every instant; inclination follows from cos i = cos(latitude) sin(azimuth) (eq. 15). When the asymptote's declination exceeds the pad's latitude, azimuths near east are never reached (eq. 14): Aster's AMBER band, made day-specific.
    • 
      
  • The Earth. The pad's direction in space comes from the IAU 2006 mean sidereal time and precession (coefficients parsed from ERFA's C source), with UTC turned into TT by NAIF's leap-second table. Nutation and UT1 − UTC are left out: against pyerfa's full IAU 2006/2000A model the pad moves along its daily circle by at most 0.2 s and the window edges by under 1 s.
    • 
      
  • The coast. Idea 36's insertion, turned into each plane, is coasted under Aster's GM plus the Earth's J2 (WGS-84) until the asymptote lies the hyperbola's true anomaly ahead (eq. 21): where an impulsive burn at perigee would leave along it. J2 turns the plane westward (about 0.46 deg per revolution, eq. 25), so the launch is aimed east of the asymptote by just enough that the plane holds it when the burn comes (eq. 27, solved numerically).
    • 
      
  • The window. The day is scanned with the handbook's closed form; every edge is refined with the numerical solve to 0.05 s, the vehicle re-flown at the corridor's limiting azimuth.
    • 
      
  • The vehicle. At the chosen moment idea 36's guided ascent is flown again on that azimuth (its first stage on it, guidance aimed at its plane) until the azimuth stops moving; seven moments across each window give the propellant curve.
    • 
      
  • What it is not. No dogleg, no plane change at departure, no drag, no J3 or higher, no Sun or Moon over the coast, no venting; the asymptote is held fixed for the day; the departure burn is impulsive (module 38 makes it finite). The README has the tests, the oracle and every source.
