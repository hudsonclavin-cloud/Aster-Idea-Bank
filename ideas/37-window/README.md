# Window
> Pick a day and a departure asymptote and get the launch windows from the pad, the azimuth, inclination and node at each moment, the parking-orbit coast with the Earth's J2 turning the plane, and the departure point in J2000 for the burn. On Aster's own Apophis asymptote (DLA 43.57°, an AMBER cell Aster prices at zero added Δv) the chain's Saturn V gets one 7 h 27 min window on 2 July 2026, only on azimuths 35° to 55.5°, leaving 56,250 to 62,874 kg of S-IVB propellant against 70,937 kg as idea 36 flew it near due east; that buys only 2,525 to 2,744 m/s against the 3,255 m/s the departure needs, so the page refuses the departure (due east it would buy 2,991). Fitted to Apollo 17's two flown azimuths, the same geometry closes the 1972 window 6.2 min after the press kit's 1:31 am, and against a brute-force scan with the full IAU 2006/2000A Earth model its window edges agree to 0.84 s.

## What it is

Idea 37, module 7 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 36 flew a rocket to a parking orbit in a frame fixed at liftoff with no clock: its handoff says the node is "module 37's". This module puts the flight on the calendar. A transfer leaves Earth along one direction in space (the departure asymptote); the parking orbit must hold that direction when the departure burn comes, and it must pass over the pad at liftoff, which turns with the Earth. Most of the day gives a plane that leaves the pad on an azimuth the range forbids; the rest are the launch windows.

It opens on **the Saturn V AS-512** (idea 36's page default, guided to Apollo 17's parking orbit) **departing for Apophis on 2 July 2026**, on the one asymptote in Aster's fixtures that carries vector components. You can:

- **choose the asymptote**: Aster's Apophis cell, Apollo 17's translunar target direction (fitted from the flight report, C3 negative, so the departure point is refused and only the plane is solved), or your own C3, right ascension, declination and date; drag the declination and watch the windows close in, merge and split (JPL 82-43's Fig. 12, live);
- **set the range and the coast**: Aster's Cape corridor (35 to 120°) or Apollo 17's (72 to 100°) or your own; the longest coast in parking orbit (default 3.11 h, AS-512's own insertion to translunar cutoff); the pad's longitude (Pad 39A, placed from the flight report); J2 on or off;
- **read** the day's windows with their bounding azimuths, and at any moment inside one (slider, tap on the chart, or arrow keys): launch time in UTC and TDB, azimuth, inclination, the node in J2000, the coast, how far J2 makes the launch aim east of the asymptote and how much that moves the window, the departure time and point, the impulsive Δv, the propellant left for the burn with **idea 36's ascent flown again on that azimuth**, and the Δv that propellant can buy against the Δv the departure needs (refused when short);
- **see** the azimuth through the day against the corridor, the propellant across each window, and the geometry on the sky (the pad's daily circle, the plane, the asymptote and the departure point, as JPL 82-43's Fig. 14);
- set the model **against Apollo 17** (six rows) and **hand on** the departure state, its epoch and the propellant to module 38.

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 37: "Launch site to parking orbit to departure asymptote: azimuth, inclination, node timing, the daily window that lines the orbit up with the departure direction (RLA/DLA), J2 regression over the coast", taking the parking orbit and the asymptote, handing on the departure geometry. Idea 36's handoff names the gap: its frame has "no epoch, so the node in J2000 is module 37's".
- **Aster says it does not do this.** `src/v2/core/lambert/feasibility.ts:70-71` (62ddb17): "INV-016d: callers must surface a disclosure that this classifies screening feasibility only, not day-specific launch geometry." `src/v2/SLICE_12_FOUNDING.md:54` gives the reason with Mars Global Surveyor, which needed a dogleg at DLA 36.5° inside Aster's AMBER band "because its window required the southerly azimuth side". `INVARIANTS.md:120` (DEC-13-3) prices AMBER at "zero added ΔV with an explicit disclosure that plane-matching is launch-geometry class". This module is that launch geometry, day by day, with the vehicle flown on each azimuth, so the AMBER price can be read as kilograms. The same founding doc names JPL Publication 82-43 as the canonical reference; it is the one used here.

Alternatives weighed. (1) *The handbook's closed form only* (eqs. 9, 22, 25, 27): fast and exact for the plane, but its J2 bias is a secular rate over a coast estimated from the mean motion; kept for the day scan and shown to be 6.3 s early against (2) **built:** a numerical solve, where idea 36's insertion is placed in each plane and coasted with J2 to the departure point, and the liftoff plane is aimed so that the plane at the burn holds the asymptote. (3) *A rigid ascent* (idea 36's insertion turned into every plane): good to about 1 s at the window edges, but the propellant would be idea 36's at every azimuth, which is the number this module exists to change; so the ascent is re-flown at each azimuth by idea 36's own kernel. (4) *Aster's pck00010 Earth model* (the IAU WGCCRE prime meridian, the Earth orientation already in Aster's checkout): simpler, but measured 16 to 29 s off in launch time (Honest assessment); IAU 2006 with ERFA's coefficients won.

## How it works

**What is reused, unmodified.** Idea 36's kernel (`snapshots/closedloop.js`) and every file it loads (idea 35's `ascent.js`, idea 34's `stack.js`, the AS-512 data, Aster's constants), byte-identical to the committed files (a test): `parseHandoff` re-checks the imported handoff by re-flying it, `targetOrbit` and `flyClosed` fly the guided ascent at each azimuth, `A.fly` the first stage, `A.validatePlan` the corridor. Nothing of theirs is re-implemented.

**Time.** UTC to TT with NAIF's leap-second table (`naif0012.tls`, TAI − UTC from 1972, TT − TAI 32.184 s), refused before 1972. TDB = TT (the periodic part, under 2 ms, ignored as Aster does); TDB seconds past J2000 by Aster's own formula (`utc-to-tdb.ts`), which the tests reproduce. UT1 = UTC (|DUT1| < 0.9 s, not modelled).

**Earth orientation** (IAU 2006, coefficients parsed by `build-data.mjs` from ERFA's C source `era00.c`, `gmst06.c`, `pfw06.c`, `obl06.c`, `fw2m.c`): Earth rotation angle, Greenwich mean sidereal time, and the bias-precession matrix ICRF/J2000 → mean equator and equinox of date (Fukushima-Williams angles). The pad sits at right ascension GMST + longitude in that mean frame of date (MOD). Nutation is not modelled; the oracle measures what that costs.

**Geometry** (JPL Publication 82-43, Vol. I Part 2, section III.C, printed pages 9 to 17, transcribed in `snapshots/jpl-82-43-launch-geometry.txt`). The plane through Earth's centre, the pad u and the asymptote S has normal u × S; its azimuth over the pad is that of the direction toward S (eq. 9 in vector form; a test checks it equals eq. 9 itself, quadrant rule included, over 1,980 cases); of the two directions along the plane, the one inside the corridor is flown. Inclination cos i = cos φ sin Σ (eq. 15), node (16), range angle (17, 18), the split-azimuth limit sin Σ = cos δ / cos φ (14), the asymptote's true anomaly cos ν∞ = −1/(1 + C3 r/μ) (21).

**The ascent in a plane.** Idea 36's insertion is reduced to an in-plane description (arc from the pad θ1, radius, radial and transverse speed, time from liftoff) and placed in any plane through the pad; placing it in its own flown plane reproduces idea 36's state turned by the pad's right ascension to 1 cm (idea 36's plane misses the pad by 7 × 10⁻⁸ deg, 8 mm).

**The coast and the J2 aim.** From insertion, RK4 at 10 s steps under Aster's GM plus J2 about the MOD pole (WGS-84 J2 = 1.08262998905 × 10⁻³ at 6,378.137 km, quoted in NAIF's `geophysical.ker`), until the asymptote lies ν∞(r) ahead of the vehicle along its motion: where an impulsive burn at the hyperbola's perigee would leave along S (eq. 22 with θ2 = ν_I = 0), the first such point at least the minimum coast after insertion. J2 turns the plane westward over the coast, so the liftoff plane is aimed at S turned east about the pole by ψ, ψ found by secant so that the plane at the departure point holds S to 10⁻⁹ deg (the handbook's biased launch, eq. 27, solved instead of estimated).

**The re-flown vehicle.** At the chosen moment the solve gives an azimuth; idea 36's ascent is flown again on it (first stage on that azimuth, guidance aimed at the plane of that azimuth over the pad, the imported perigee and apogee); its insertion changes θ1 and the insertion time, so the solve runs again, until the azimuth moves less than 10⁻⁹ deg (two or three flights). The propellant left in the guided stage is what the departure burn gets. The first stage flies the plane's azimuth, as the Saturn V's did (its tilt programme ran in the plane of the flight azimuth); it is not re-optimised per azimuth (Honest assessment gives what that costs).

**What the propellant buys** (added by the independent review). dv = ve ln(m0 / (m0 − mp)): m0 the mass at insertion and mp the propellant left, both from the re-flown ascent, and ve the guided stage's engine vacuum exhaust velocity from idea 33's deck as idea 36 flies it (J-2: 4,146 m/s). With no gravity loss this is an upper bound, and the impulsive Δv is a lower bound on the need, so when the first is below the second the departure is refused for that launch (`departure_propellant`; a shortfall under 2 % is called marginal). The yardstick is checked on Apollo 17's own translunar burn: report Table 16-7 gives the real vehicle at 139,750 kg at S-IVB ignition and 65,711 kg at cutoff, which at 4,146 m/s is 3,129 m/s, 0.9 % under the 3,155 m/s impulsive minimum that Table 4-5's C3 needs from the chain's insertion radius. The burn did reach that C3, so the yardstick reads at least 0.9 % low.

**The day's windows.** The day is scanned every 2 min with the handbook's closed form (azimuth biased by eq. 25 over a coast from the range angle at the mean motion) from 1 h before the date to 48 h after its start; each edge is bisected to 0.05 s on the numerical solve, with the vehicle re-flown at the corridor's limiting azimuth. A window belongs to the date it opens on; one that never closes is the whole day.

**Pad 39A** (the flight report gives no coordinates). Table 4-3's S-IC/S-II separation point, 28.580 N (geodetic), −79.637 E, lies 94.7 km down range on the 91.504° flight azimuth: walked back on a sphere of Aster's radius it puts the pad at 28.6058 N geodetic, −80.6054 E, uncertain by about 0.003° (the 0.3 km cross range, side not stated, and the sphere): about 0.7 s of launch time. The plane geometry needs the geocentric latitude (JPL 82-43's φ_L): 28.4443° on Aster's pck00010 ellipsoid. The latitude the geometry uses is the chain's (Aster's Cape 28.5°, the one idea 35 flew from); the pad's geocentric one moves the Apophis window 0.85 s earlier at the opening and 13.9 s at the close, and the Apollo 17 gap by +11 s (below).

**Apollo 17.** The report gives the flight azimuth at the scheduled liftoff (72.141° at 02:53:00 UT, section 9.3.1; the hold at T−30 s began 02:52:30, section 3.2.2) and at the actual one (91.504° at 05:33:00). A direction fixed in the mean frame of date is fitted to both (Newton, residual 0); the window is then where its plane's azimuth crosses the press kit's limits, 72° and 100°. For a C3 below zero there is no asymptote: the direction is a target vector, the plane is solved and the departure point refused.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The geometry is right.** The vector form equals JPL 82-43's eq. 9 to 10⁻⁹ deg and reproduces its worked numbers (0.460° per revolution, 284.26° against Fig. 14's printed 285°); with the full IAU 2006/2000A Earth model, an independent brute-force scan in Python finds the same window edges to 0.04 and 0.84 s.
- **[Certain as numbers] Leaving out nutation and UT1 − UTC.** Against pyerfa the pad's direction in space is 4.4 to 8.6 arcsec off at six epochs, but along its daily circle by at most 0.20 s of time (the equation of the equinoxes, up to 14 arcsec, cancels against the precession matrix's origin); the rest is a tilt of the pole, which moves window edges by up to 0.84 s here. UT1 − UTC (kept under 0.9 s by the definition of UTC) adds directly and is not modelled.
- **[Certain as numbers] Apophis, 2 July 2026, the chain's Saturn V from Pad 39A** (Aster's corridor 35 to 120°): one window, 02:50:50 to 10:17:53 UTC (7 h 27 min), bounded by 35° at both ends, because the asymptote's declination (43.57°) is beyond the pad's latitude and only azimuths within 55.53° of north or south are reachable (eq. 14; the southern side would need 124.5°, past the corridor). Inclination 59.7° at the opening, 43.7° at the best moment. **Idea 36's ascent re-flown leaves 56,250 kg (at 35°) to 62,874 kg (at 55.3°) for the departure burn, against 70,937 kg as idea 36 flew it on 91.27°: 8.1 to 14.7 t (11 to 21 %) that Aster's AMBER price ("zero added ΔV") does not carry.** The impulsive departure from the 171 km point needs 3,255 m/s. **That propellant buys 2,525 to 2,741 m/s** (2,744 at eq. 14's 55.53°, where the seven-moment curve's 62,874 kg becomes 62,954), **so the departure is refused at every moment of the window, short by 511 to 730 m/s; due east it would buy 2,991, still 264 short.** The chain's Saturn V with Apollo 17's spacecraft cannot depart along this asymptote on this day at all; the AMBER cost widens a 264 m/s shortfall to 511 to 730 m/s (added by the independent review; the yardstick reads Apollo 17's own burn at least 0.9 % low, far less than these gaps). J2 aims the launch 0.224° east of the asymptote and moves the window 53.7 s later (33.5 s at the close); the coast is 62 min.
- **[Certain as numbers] Apollo 17** (MPR-SAT-FE-73-1 and the press kit). A fixed direction fitted to the flown 72.141° and 91.504° puts the 100° limit at 06:37:09 UT: **the press kit closes the window at 06:31 UT, 369 s (6.2 min) earlier**; 72.000° comes at 02:51:57, 62.5 s before the scheduled 02:53:00, consistent with the report's 72.141° then. [Likely] The gap is mostly that the Saturn V's translunar targeting was not a fixed direction: its target moves with the Moon through the window, which a fixed direction cannot follow; with the pad's geocentric latitude (28.444°, the report's geodetic 28.606° on Aster's ellipsoid) instead of the chain's 28.5° the gap is 380 s. (As first written this README used the geodetic latitude as if it were geocentric and gave 348 s; the review corrected it.) Eq. 15 gives 28.536° at 91.504° against Table 4-4's 28.526° (28.481° from the pad's geocentric latitude). The geometry also offers a second daily window (12:59 to 16:29 UT) the press kit does not list ([Likely] daylight landing and other constraints, not geometry). The chain's Saturn V flown at 72.141° keeps 67,958 kg for TLI against 70,931 at 91.504°: [Certain as numbers] the 2 h 40 min hold gave its S-IVB 2,973 kg (91 m/s of ideal Δv: 2,991 against 2,900).
- **[Likely] J2 over AS-512's coast.** From Table 4-4's insertion to Table 4-5's translunar conditions (11,205 s) the J2 coast moves the descending node −1.023° (eq. 25: −1.037°); the flight's moved −0.917°, a gap of 0.106°, but the flight's figure includes the translunar burn (ending at 11,907.64 s), which also took 0.052° off the inclination; the two are not separated here.
- **[Certain as numbers] The handbook against the numerical solve.** The closed form's window opening is 6.33 s before the numerical one on Apophis (its J2 bias is a secular rate over a coast taken at the mean motion); the numerical node regression matches eq. 25 to 0.04 %.
