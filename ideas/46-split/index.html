Split

Idea 45 left a mining plant on the asteroid making water every day. This page turns that water into propellant for the trip home: it splits it in an electrolyser run by the spacecraft's solar arrays, liquefies or compresses the hydrogen and oxygen (or keeps the water to boil in a steam thruster), loses some to boil-off, and asks which mixture ratio is worth burning, going back to idea 31's chamber and idea 32's nozzle to price it.

              How this works

              Splitting the water

              A proton-exchange-membrane cell splits liquid water: H₂O → H₂ + ½O₂, two electrons per molecule. Faraday's law sets the charge (Faraday's constant is Avogadro's number times the elementary charge, both exact in the SI), the cell voltage sets the energy: 2FV per mole of water, divided by the Faraday efficiency (current lost to gas crossing the membrane). The voltage cannot be below the reversible voltage, ΔG/2F, about 1.23 V; above the thermoneutral voltage, ΔH/2F, about 1.48 V, the excess leaves as heat. Both come from idea 31's NASA Glenn data and agree with NIST-JANAF to better than 0.05 mV. The presets are DOE's PEM stack figures (1.9 V today, 1.8 and 1.6 V as targets); the Faraday efficiency defaults to what the ISS oxygen generator's published current and output imply, 98.3 %. Storing gas at pressure, the cell itself does the compressing and pays only the Nernst voltage.

              Power

              The arrays give their power at 1 au times (1 au / r)², with r from idea 45's distance each day, and only while the Sun is up at the site (idea 45's lit hours). There are no batteries. Checked against NEAR Shoemaker and Psyche beyond 1 au, the 1/r² law reads 3.5 to 17 % low; inside 1 au, where the chain sits, hotter cells make it likely optimistic.

              Liquefying, and boil-off

              Gas leaves the cell and is cooled at 1 atm to liquid: NIST's tables give the heat. Hydrogen is also converted from its room-temperature mix (three parts ortho) to para, releasing 527 kJ/kg (NBS 1958) that must be removed at 20 K, or the liquid boils itself away. A 90 K stage takes the gas down to 90 K and liquefies the oxygen; NASA and Creare's 20 K reverse turbo-Brayton cooler does the rest, on its measured turndown curve (631 W just to run, 1642 W for 16.5 W of lift), as many units as needed, 106 kg each. No 90 K unit's efficiency is on file, so it is costed at the 20 K unit's fraction of Carnot. The tanks are spheres sized for everything idea 45 could make and wrapped in MHTB's 45-layer insulation (0.085 to 0.22 W/m² measured between 164 and 305 K); the heat that leaks in boils liquid away, in the dark always, and by day too unless the coolers hold it.

              Compressed gas, and steam

              Gas is stored at room temperature at the chosen pressure (NIST's densities: hydrogen is less dense than an ideal gas, so higher pressure costs more tank per kilogram). Tanks are thin-walled spheres of one of PartForge's flight metals, sized to burst at 1.4 times the pressure (NASA-STD-5001B's metallic ultimate factor, as PartForge carries it). Steam needs no splitting: the water is kept liquid and heated to the heater temperature as it is burned; idea 31 equilibrates the steam and idea 32 expands it. Where the equilibrium expansion would condense, or idea 32 cannot reach the area ratio, the frozen expansion is used and said so.

              The mixture ratio worth burning

              Water carries oxygen and hydrogen at 7.94 to 1 by mass. Engines like the RL10 run near 5 for the best Isp; burning at 5 leaves a third of the oxygen behind. The page burns all the hydrogen and as much oxygen as the ratio asks (venting the rest before it is liquefied), runs idea 31's equilibrium chamber and idea 32's nozzle at each ratio, scales by the RL10A-3-3's measured 444 s over its ideal (NASA SP-8120), and finds the ratio that buys the most Δv for the dry mass. With any real craft that is the water's own 7.94: the Isp lost (about 5 %) is worth less than the extra mass of oxygen, until the propellant outweighs the craft about 90 to 1.
