Tip Match

A payload coming home from an asteroid reaches Earth with some hyperbolic excess speed v∞. Could a rotating tether meet it at the top of its swing? This page checks the one condition that can be checked today, energy, from Skyhook's Rev A constants, and refuses the two it cannot.

What this is, and is not

    
    
      
  • Two-body, Earth-centred, instantaneous. The payload's speed at the upper-tip radius follows from v∞ alone, whatever its perigee; the trim shown is that speed difference, which is the whole velocity mismatch only when the payload moves parallel to the tip (perigee at the upper-tip radius, horizontal, prograde). A lower perigee adds a radial component, so the trim shown is a lower bound. The tether's centre of mass is on a circular orbit at the Rev A altitude, rotating rigidly, and the catch is at the top of the swing.
    • 
      
  • Energy is necessary, not sufficient. The arrival plane (needs the v∞ vector and the tether's orbit plane) and the phase (needs a tether ephemeris and an arrival epoch) are refused above, with what would help.
    • 
      
  • Not modelled: J2, drag, the Moon, gravity-gradient spin modulation, grapple geometry, what catching tonnes at 10 km/s does to the tether, or the momentum deposit a down-mass catch makes.
    • 
      
  • Fidelity: an idea-bank prototype. It has not been through Aster's multi-agent math audit or founding-document process, nor Skyhook's Rule 1 canon review, verification memo or pytest suite.
