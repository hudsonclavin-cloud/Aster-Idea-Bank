# Tip Match
> Give it an Earth-arrival v∞ and it says whether a Skyhook tether tier could meet the payload at its upper tip and what trim Δv remains: the 2.5 km/s reference tip is 0.393 km/s too slow for even a parabolic arrival, the 2.8 km/s cislunar tip 0.093 km/s too slow, and only the 3.5 km/s Phase 3 tip meets a hyperbolic arrival at rest, at exactly v∞* = 3.613 km/s. Of the 128 JPL NHATS returns in Aster's test fixtures, 20 arrive faster than that and 108 slower, the closest 3.8 m/s off (reading their entry speed at 125 km). Plane and phase it refuses, with what would help.

**Kind:** explorable kernel
**For:** Aster + Skyhook
**Run:** open `ideas/01-tip-match/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). CLI: `node ideas/01-tip-match/tip-match.mjs --vinf 3.61 --tier phase3` prints Aster-shaped evidence envelopes; `--nhats --tier phase3` reads the NHATS records instead; `--text` for prose, `--help` for the rest; `example.txt` is saved output. Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/01-tip-match/tip-match.test.mjs` (27 tests, Node built-ins, about 5 s) and `node ideas/01-tip-match/ui-check.mjs` (112 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Data rebuild: `node ideas/01-tip-match/build-data.mjs --aster /home/user/asteroid-mining-planner`.
**Made by:** Grapnel

## What it is

Aster plans the outbound leg to an asteroid. Skyhook plans a rotating tether that catches ascending payloads at its lower tip and throws them from its upper tip. The one quantity the two projects would ever hand each other is an Earth-arrival hyperbolic excess speed, v∞: a payload coming home from an asteroid arrives with it, and the question Aster's kernel list calls K2 is whether that payload is inside the tether's catch envelope. Nothing in either repository computes the inbound case. This does the part that can be done honestly today.

You type a v∞ (the slider spans 0 to 8 km/s; the field accepts 0 up to 72.76 km/s, the fastest any body bound to the Sun can meet Earth; 0 is a parabolic arrival, the slowest anything can arrive from beyond Earth's sphere of influence), pick a tip-speed tier from Skyhook's Rev A canon (2.5 km/s reference, 2.8 km/s revenue-gated cislunar option, 3.5 km/s Phase 3 growth option) and declare a grapple tolerance (default 1 m/s). The page then:

- draws the payload's speed at the upper-tip radius, √(v∞² + 2μ/r_tip), against each tier's tip speed at the top of its swing, v_orb + v_tip, and reports the difference as the trim Δv the payload would have to burn to arrive at rest relative to the tip, in m/s with enough digits that a near-miss never reads as zero;
- states the one v∞ each tier can meet with zero trim, v∞* = √C3, which exists only when the tier's release C3 is positive: none for the 2.5 and 2.8 km/s tiers (their C3 is −8.06 and −1.94 km²/s², so every hyperbolic arrival overshoots them) and 3.613 km/s for Phase 3;
- shows that the lower tip is never an option for an inbound payload (anything from beyond Earth passes 290 km at ≥ 10.94 km/s; the lower tip moves at 5.06 km/s);
- inverts the question: what tip speed would meet *this* v∞ with zero trim, as a multiple of Zylon's characteristic velocity and against the canon's 1.3× mass-growth guardrail;
- lights an "energy-matched" chip only when |trim Δv| is inside your declared tolerance, and says in the same breath that energy is necessary, not sufficient;
- refuses the two other conditions with structured refusals: the arrival **plane** (needs the v∞ vector and the tether's orbit plane; neither exists) and the **phase** (needs a tether ephemeris and an arrival epoch; no tether has flown);
- refuses to take v∞ from Aster's screening cache, because Aster computes no Earth-arrival v∞ and that cache holds outbound-leg quantities only, and says what would supply the real number (K1, the return-leg porkchop);
- shows, separately and labelled as external data, what real return speeds look like: the 128 JPL NHATS minimum-Δv round trips committed as Aster test fixtures, read as Earth-arrival v∞ (a tick per body on the chart, a summary against the selected tier, the five bodies closest to zero trim), with the entry-altitude reading switchable between 125 km and 200 km because the records do not say which applies.

Every output is an Aster `EvidenceEnvelope`: `envelope_version`, `tool`, `value` with per-quantity `confidence` and `sourceIds`, `provenance` carrying the commits of the Skyhook and Aster snapshots, `assumptions`, `validity_envelope`, and for refusals a `code` from Aster's three, a `reason` and `what_would_help`. The CLI prints the same envelopes.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** K2 is a CONCEPT kernel in Aster's `strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:35` (§1 row 16: "the Aster → Skyhook interface: is an inbound payload's Earth-arrival v∞ inside the tether's catch envelope? … Tether constants come ONLY from Skyhook `params/rev_a.json`, never from memory"), and `IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:62-65` (§3 item 2) names it the link between the two flagship projects through "one well-defined interface: Earth-arrival v∞". `strategy/ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:41` carries it as lens L9 Aster Momentum ("connects the rotovator program — Rev A constants locked in its own skill, never quoted from memory"). The bank's own `BRIEFING.md` §4 lists it first among the seams. On the Skyhook side the catch window is defined only for the lower tip and an ascending payload (`docs/glossary.md:11-12`), and the capture envelope (±10 m, ~1 m/s relative velocity, 3–10 s) is a requirement in `docs/project_instructions.md:123-124`, again for a climbing payload. `physics/skyhook_physics.py:143` (`apogee_from_release`) computes the release side only; `physics/tether_dynamics.py:344` (`catch_and_measure`) models a lower-tip catch of an upbound payload; the program site's Monte Carlo (`tools/sims/skyhook_program_site.html:1315`) is an ascending rendezvous. The inverse energy condition did not exist anywhere.

- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *A rendezvous Monte Carlo for the inbound case*, like the site's ascending one. It would need a tether ephemeris, an arrival state vector and dispersion models; none exist, so every input would have been invented. Killed by Aster's rule that a kernel which must invent numbers does not ship.
  - *Feed it from Aster's screening cache.* `src/v2/boundary/lambert-screen-cache.ts:43-49` keeps `c3`, `vInfDep` and `vInfArr` per window, but those are departure from Earth and arrival at the asteroid on the outbound leg. Presenting either as an Earth-arrival v∞ is a fabrication. The page offers that source and refuses it, which is more useful than hiding it.
  - *Feed it from the NHATS fixtures.* Aster's `tests/fixtures/v2/nhats-validation-targets/` and `oq7-nhats-coorbital/` commit JPL NHATS records whose `min_dv_traj.v_arr_earth` is an Earth-entry speed (Bennu's is 11.832 km/s), so an arrival v∞ follows once the entry altitude is fixed. They are real numbers, so the page shows them; but they are JPL's, one minimum-Δv trajectory per body, and the altitude is not in the record. So they appear beside the typed input as a labelled external set with both readings, not as "where v∞ comes from". (The first version of this deposit missed them and said Aster held no Earth-arrival speed at all; review round 1 caught it.)
  - *A Python function in Skyhook's physics module* (`inbound_catch_trim_kms`). That is where it should land, but a deposit here cannot write there, and the Aster side wants the envelope shape. So the kernel is a UMD JavaScript module that both the page and the CLI share, and the README says what the Python port is.
  - *A one-number CLI.* Cheaper, but the chart is the argument: two tier lines that never meet the payload curve, and one that crosses it once, inside the spread of real return speeds, is why "catchable at the reference tier" is false in a way a reader sees at a glance.

  This won because it is the smallest thing that is true: an energy condition from canon constants, a structured refusal for everything else, the nearest real data clearly labelled, and a shape Aster could consume.

## How it works

All tether constants come from `snapshots/rev_a.json`, a byte-for-byte copy of Skyhook `params/rev_a.json` at commit `34935cb` (sidecar: sha256 `d0828c1e…`, read bytes match the committed blob). Aster-side constants come from two `tools/snapshot.mjs` copies at Aster `62ddb17`: `snapshots/aster_physics_constants.ts` (`src/physics/constants/index.ts`) and `snapshots/aster_delta-v.ts` (`src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts`). `build-data.mjs` wraps the canon as `snapshots/rev_a.data.js`, and with `--aster` derives `snapshots/aster.data.js` from the two `.ts` copies and the NHATS fixture files (each file's committed blob checked against the bytes read), so the page can read both from `file://`. Tests check that the wrapper deep-equals the JSON, that the Aster module carries exactly what the `.ts` copies say, and that every file under `snapshots/` still hashes to what its own sidecar recorded. The only physical or requirement numbers typed in code are the 1 m/s default tolerance (one constant in the kernel, cited), three altitudes Aster states only in prose (`build-data.mjs` `CITED`, each with file, line and the quoted words, which the build re-checks against the checkout), and the chart's axis ranges; the CLI's help and the page's prose take tier speeds, radii and ranges from the snapshots. `index.html` carries no constant as static text, and a test greps for that.

**Geometry (all km, [Certain] arithmetic on canon values).** μ = `orbital.mu_earth_m3s2` / 10⁹ = 398,600 km³/s². Altitude h = `orbital.com_altitude_km` = 610, half-length L = `geometry.tether_half_length_km` = 320. The file never states an Earth radius as a key, but it states the upper-tip radius three times in prose ("r_tip=7301 km" in `kinematics._c3_note`, "r=R_E+610+320=7301 km" in `_upper_release_note` and `market_thresholds._note`); that is the radius its own C3 and apogee entries were evaluated at, so the kernel parses r_tip = 7301 from `_c3_note` and derives R_E = 7301 − 610 − 320 = 6371 km, which is the `R_EARTH_KM = 6371.0` at `physics/skyhook_physics.py:40` (snapshotted; a test pins the two together). Hence r_com = 6981, r_low = 6661. If the note is absent the kernel refuses (`not_found`) rather than remembering a radius. The file's `orbital.com_radius_km` = 6988 is deliberately **not** used: it implies R_E = 6378, and a consistent 6378 km Earth (r_com 6988, r_tip 7308) gives C3 = −8.033 km²/s² and an apogee of 85,559 km, not the file's −8.06 and 85,222 (Findings, F1).

**The tip.** v_orb = √(μ/r_com) = 7.5563 km/s (EQ-2). At the top of the swing the tip moves horizontally, prograde, at v_tip,inertial = v_orb + v_tip: 10.0563, 10.3563 and 11.0563 km/s for the three tiers (as `v_upper_release_kms` in `skyhook_physics.py:113`). The lower tip at the bottom moves at v_orb − v_tip: 5.0563, 4.7563, 4.0563 km/s.

**Release energy, the canon's own numbers reproduced.** C3 = v²_tip,inertial − 2μ/r_tip (Skyhook EQ-7, `apogee_from_release`, `skyhook_physics.py:143-166`): −8.0611, −1.9373, +13.0515 km²/s² against the file's −8.06, −1.94, 13.05. For C3 < 0, a = −μ/C3 and the apogee altitude 2a − r_tip − R_E is 85,222.3 and 397,819.4 km against the file's 85,222 and 397,819. For C3 ≥ 0 the apogee is `null` (Skyhook's Python returns `math.inf`; Aster's envelope builder rejects any non-finite number, so the envelope says `release_bound: false` instead).

**The payload.** On its arrival hyperbola the speed at radius r is fixed by vis-viva, v = √(v∞² + 2μ/r), whatever the perigee; it is the perigee speed only if the perigee is at r. At r_tip the parabolic floor (v∞ = 0) is √(2μ/7301) = 10.4494 km/s. So **trim Δv = v_payload − v_tip,inertial**, in km/s and m/s; positive means the payload is faster and a retrograde burn would be needed. At v∞ = 0: +393.1 m/s (reference), +93.1 m/s (cislunar), −606.9 m/s (Phase 3). At v∞ = 3 km/s against the reference tip: payload 10.8715 km/s, trim 0.8152 km/s (a hand-worked example in the tests). The trim is strictly increasing in v∞ and equals trim(0) + √(v∞² + v²_par) − v_par exactly.

**v∞\*.** A tier meets a payload at rest when v²_payload = v²_tip,inertial, i.e. v∞² = C3. So v∞* = √C3 exists only for C3 > 0: Phase 3 gives √13.0515 = 3.6127 km/s. The slope d(trim)/d(v∞) = v∞/v_payload is 0.32675 there, so a ±1 m/s grapple tolerance is ±3.06 m/s in v∞: a 6.1 m/s window, 3.6096–3.6157 km/s. 3.61 km/s is inside at −0.88 m/s, 3.615 inside at +0.76 m/s, 3.616 outside at +1.08 m/s (the tests pin all three; the UI check pins 3.615 and 3.616 on the page).

**Accepted v∞ ([Certain], patched conic).** A payload returning from an asteroid is bound to the Sun, so where it meets Earth its heliocentric speed is below solar escape speed there, and Earth's own speed adds at most head-on. Both are largest at Earth's perihelion, r_p = a(1 − e), so v∞ ≤ √(2μ☉/r_p) + √(μ☉(2/r_p − 1/a)) = 42.478 + 30.286 = 72.764 km/s, with μ☉ = `GM_sun`, `AU` and Earth's a = 1.00000261 AU, e = 0.01671123 (the Standish 1992 row) read from the Aster snapshot. The kernel refuses `out_of_envelope` above that, before any arithmetic, and states the range in every envelope's `validity_envelope`; a circular Earth orbit would give the slightly lower (1 + √2)·v⊕ = 71.9 km/s, so perihelion is the right worst case. Bound arrivals (geocentric C3 < 0, a payload falling back from a high Earth orbit or the Moon) are outside the input domain, since they have no v∞; on a tier's own release orbit, read backwards, such a payload meets the reference and cislunar tips with zero trim, and the refusal, the validity envelope and the page say so.

**Zero-trim tip speed.** The inverse: v_tip,needed = √(v∞² + 2μ/r_tip) − v_orb. At v∞ = 0 it is 2.893 km/s, which is the "escape (C3=0) is reached at ~2.9 km/s" in the Phase 3 note and `market_thresholds.escape_C3_0_tip_speed_kms` = 2.9. At 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 km/s: 2.94, 3.08, 3.32, 4.03, 5.60 km/s. Shown as a multiple of V_c = √(2σ/ρ) = 2.727 km/s (from `materials.tensile_strength_GPa` and `density_kgm3`; the file states 2.73) against the "1.3x mass-growth guardrail" parsed from `mass_ratio._ratio_note`: v∞ = 5 km/s needs 1.48 V_c, beyond it. The page quotes the file's own integrated mass ratios (121.83× at 2.5 km/s, 3085.95× at 3.5 km/s, 30 % derate; EQ-6 with x = (V_t/V_c)·√(SF/(1 − derate))) and its reason for the top tier, the Mars-escape threshold `market_thresholds.mars_escape_tip_speed_kms` = 3.5. It does not claim the guardrail sets the top of the ladder.

**Lower tip.** √(2μ/r_low) = 10.9399 km/s at 290 km, against 5.06 km/s: the gap is ≥ 5.88 km/s for every tier and every v∞ ≥ 0, verdict `not reachable`.

**Match flag.** `energy_matched` iff |trim| ≤ the declared tolerance in m/s. The 1 m/s default is the "relative velocity within ~1 m/s at the moment of capture" requirement at Skyhook `docs/project_instructions.md:124` @ `34935cb`; it is a planning requirement for ascending payloads, not in `rev_a.json`, so it is a user-declared input with `confidence: assumed`. It lives once, as `DEFAULT_TOLERANCE_MS` in the kernel, and the page and CLI read it from there. The trim is printed by `formatSpeedDiff`: m/s with two significant figures below 1 m/s (0.10 m/s, never "0.000 km/s"), two decimals to 10 m/s, one to 1000 m/s, km/s above.

**The NHATS records ([Likely]; external data).** `build-data.mjs --aster` reads all 135 JSON files in the two fixture directories at Aster `62ddb17`, keeps `des`, `fullname` and `min_dv_traj.{v_arr_earth, dec_arr}` as the strings the API returned, lists the 5 API error records (`2003_YN107` and four others: "specified NHATS object not found") and folds the 2 bodies present in both directories (2000 SG344, 2001 GP2), leaving 128. The kernel reads each `v_arr_earth` as the speed at an entry altitude h and inverts vis-viva with Aster's own Earth: v∞ = √(v² − 2μ/(R_E + h)), μ = `EARTH_MU_KM3_PER_S2` = 398,600.4418 km³/s² and R_E = `LEO_PARKING_RADIUS_KM` − 200 = 6378.137 km (`delta-v.ts:3-4`, with the 200 km of `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:981`). The altitude is not in the record: Aster's research note gives NHATS's entry constraint at 125 km (`src/v2/research/slice-11-porkchop/perplexity-jpl-trajectory-browser-nhats.md:42`) and the Trajectory Browser's reentry at 200 km (`:17`); sibling deposit `ideas/05-round-trip-ledger` documents the same ambiguity. At 125 km (escape 11.0719 km/s; every record is above it): v∞ 0.262 km/s (2011 WE) to 4.345 km/s (2014 KF39), median 2.449; Bennu 4.172. At 200 km: 1.211 to 4.503, median 2.719. The 125 km range and median and the 200 km range are the figures idea 05 publishes from the same fixtures, reproduced here by independent code. Each record is rounded to 0.001 km/s, which carries through d(trim)/d(v_arr) = v_arr/v_payload to ±0.53 m/s of trim; the kernel reports that band per body. Against the tiers: every record arrives faster than the reference and cislunar tips (the slowest, 2011 WE, still needs +396.4 and +96.4 m/s); against Phase 3, 20 arrive faster and 108 slower at 125 km (24 and 104 at 200 km), none within 1 m/s, and the closest is 2021 AT2 at +3.79 ± 0.53 m/s (v∞ 3.6243 km/s). Which body is close depends on the reading: at 200 km, 2021 AT2 is +66.8 m/s off and the closest is 2011 BQ50 at +15.5 m/s. The two v∞ sides use their own Earths (Aster's μ and R_E to back v∞ out of an entry speed, Skyhook's on the tether side); v∞ is the quantity handed across, and the two μ differ by 1.1 parts per million.

**Envelopes (Aster `mcp/src/envelope/index.ts`).** The energy envelope has six sources: `rev-a-mu` (repo, measured), `rev-a-design` (repo, assumed: altitude, length, tip speeds, σ/ρ are design choices), `vinf-input` and `tolerance-input` (external, assumed), `aster-helio` (repo, measured: the constants behind the 72.76 km/s bound) and `two-body` (computation, derived). The NHATS envelope (`tip-match.nhats-arrivals`) cites `nhats-fixtures` (external, derived: JPL's computations), `aster-porkchop` (repo, measured) and `nhats-altitude` (repo, assumed: the reading), beside the canon, tolerance and computation refs. Every quantity leaf carries `confidence` and `sourceIds`, as Aster's `assertMixedProvenanceLeaves` demands, and each envelope's `confidence` is the weakest source, `assumed`, by `confidenceMin`. The refusal codes are Aster's three: `insufficient_data` (missing, blank or non-finite v∞; plane; phase), `out_of_envelope` (negative v∞, v∞ above the bound, a bad tolerance, an NHATS set with no record above escape speed; a single record below escape is listed as not converted), `not_found` (unknown tier, including names such as `toString` or `__proto__` that an object lookup would otherwise inherit; the Aster-cache source; a canon without the r_tip note; a missing Aster module; an unknown reading). `validateEnvelope()` re-implements Aster's checks (version, non-empty fields, finite-deep value, resolvable `sourceIds`, weakest-link confidence, refusal shape); the CLI refuses to print an envelope that fails it. The CLI reads `--vinf` and `--tol` only as plain decimals: an empty, blank or non-decimal value reaches the kernel as the string it is and is refused, never read as 0.

**The chart.** Inline SVG sized to its real pixel width (so labels stay 11–12 px on a phone), x = v∞ 0–8 km/s, y = speed at r_tip 9.5–13.5 km/s: the payload curve, three tier lines with direct labels, the current v∞ as a cursor with both markers and the trim bracket, the Phase 3 crossing labelled v∞*, a tick per NHATS body along the foot, and one tooltip listing every series on hover or tap. The trim label tries eight placements (above or below the markers, either side, two offsets) and takes the first that touches no other label and stays inside the drawing, right of the y axis. A typed v∞ beyond 8 km/s is computed and marked "off this axis" rather than drawn outside it. Colours were run through the dataviz palette validator in both modes (light `#2a78d6 #eb6834 #1baf7a #6250d6`, dark `#3987e5 #d95926 #199e70 #9085e9`: all checks pass; the light aqua is under 3:1 and so is always direct-labelled and in the table). Status colours carry an icon and a word, never colour alone. Every render is wrapped: if anything throws, the page shows a refusal card and hides the numbers instead of leaving the last result on screen.

**Earth-radius sensitivity, measured.** Re-deriving everything with R_E = 6378.137 km (a modified canon note; r_com and r_tip both move) shifts the reference C3 by +0.029 km²/s² and the v∞ = 0 trim by about −1 m/s. Moving only r_tip while keeping v_orb at the 6371 km radius, which is what mixing the two radii does, shifts C3 by 0.106; that is the "~0.1" figure in the proposal, and it is the inconsistent version.

## Tests

`tip-match.test.mjs` (27 tests, Node built-ins, about 5 s). What they pin, and to what outside this code:

- **Snapshots.** `rev_a.data.js` deep-equals `snapshots/rev_a.json` and embeds its sidecar verbatim; every file under `snapshots/` still hashes to its own sidecar's `writtenSha256`, names the repository it came from, and was read from a clean checkout; the canon copy's sha256 equals the source's; the sidecar commit is 40 hex.
- **Constants.** r_tip parsed from the canon note is 7301 and equals 6371 + 610 + 320; R_E equals `R_EARTH_KM` regexed out of the `skyhook_physics.py` snapshot; tier speeds equal the three `v_tip_kms` keys; the 1.3× guardrail is found; V_c reproduces `materials.V_c_kms` 2.73. A canon with the note removed, or μ removed, makes `create()` refuse `not_found`; a missing Aster module makes the energy and NHATS envelopes refuse `not_found`, and one without Earth's eccentricity makes the energy envelope refuse naming that key. The Aster module's `GM_sun`, `AU`, Earth a and e, μ and parking radius equal what the test regexes out of the two `.ts` copies; R_E behind the 200 km parking radius is 6378.137; the cited altitudes carry their quotes.
- **Reconciliation with the canon's stated numbers.** C3 to two decimals and apogee to the kilometre for all three tiers; release speed to two decimals for reference and cislunar; the C3 = 0 tip speed rounds to the file's 2.9; rotation period 13.4 min and tip acceleration 1.99 g from the same constants.
- **Findings as tests** (so a refreshed snapshot that no longer shows them flags the README as stale): F1, `com_radius_km` 6988 ≠ 6981; a consistent 6378 km Earth gives C3 −8.03 and apogee 85,559 km, neither the file's; the mixed recipe (6988 with 7301) gives −8.137 and 84,297; only 6371 reproduces −8.06; the file's `v_orb_kms` 7.55 is what 6988 gives while 6981 gives 7.56. F2, the Phase 3 release speed 11.05 equals the rounded 7.55 + 3.5 while recomputation gives 11.06 and the other two tiers use the unrounded v_orb.
- **The inbound numbers.** A hand-worked v∞ = 3 km/s case computed in the test from μ and the radii without calling the kernel (payload 10.8715, trim 0.8152, agreement to 10⁻¹²); the pitch's 0.393, 0.093 and 3.613 within 0.002/0.005; trim at v∞* is zero to 10⁻⁶ m/s; the match flag flips between 0.9 and 1.1 m/s of trim; 3.61 is matched at 1 m/s (−0.877 m/s) but not at 0.5; 3.615 is matched (+0.757) and 3.616 is not (+1.084); the window half-width is 3.06 m/s of v∞ per m/s of trim; trim strictly increasing on a 0.05 km/s grid for every tier and equal to the closed form to 10⁻¹²; lower tip `not reachable` with gap > 5 km/s for every tier and v∞ in {0, 0.5, 2, 5, 8}, parabolic speed at 290 km = 10.94 ± 0.005, reference lower tip 5.056; the zero-trim tip speed inverts exactly, is 3.5 at v∞*, and crosses the guardrail by v∞ = 5; the Earth-radius sensitivity is between 0.02 and 0.04 km²/s² (consistent) and 0.09–0.12 (inconsistent), trim shift under 2 m/s.
- **The v∞ bound.** Recomputed by hand in the test from the Aster constants (72.76 km/s), above the circular-orbit value by less than 1 km/s; just below it is a valid envelope that states the bound; just above it, 300,000, 10¹⁵⁴, 10¹⁵⁵ and `Number.MAX_VALUE` are `out_of_envelope` refusals that validate.
- **Formatting.** 0.103 → "0.10 m/s", 0.0004 → "0.00040 m/s", 1.0836 → "1.08 m/s", 393.116 → "393.1 m/s", 12,509 → "12.509 km/s"; nine values from 10⁻¹² to 999 m/s never print as zero; the review's state (3.613 km/s, 0.05 m/s tolerance) yields "payload arrives 0.10 m/s faster".
- **NHATS.** Bennu inverted by hand in the test with μ and R_E regexed from the `.ts` copy (v∞ 4.172 km/s, trim to 10⁻⁹ m/s); escape at 125 km 11.0719; 135 files, 128 bodies, 5 skipped, duplicates 2000 SG344 and 2001 GP2; v∞ 0.262 (2011 WE) to 4.345 (2014 KF39), median 2.449; Phase 3 20 faster, 108 slower, none within 1 m/s, closest 2021 AT2 at +3.79 m/s with the rounding band equal to its closed form; all 128 faster than the reference and cislunar tips; at 200 km 1.211–4.503 and 24 faster; an unknown reading, a bad tolerance and a set whose only record is below escape speed are refused.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The energy arithmetic.** Two-body vis-viva and EQ-2/EQ-7 on canon constants, reconciled with the file's own C3, apogee, release speed, escape threshold, V_c, rotation period and tip acceleration, and checked against a hand computation and closed forms. The trims (+393.1, +93.1, −606.9 m/s at v∞ = 0) and v∞* = 3.613 km/s follow.
- **[Certain] Under the stated model (circular CoM orbit, rigid rotation, v_orb at r_com), no hyperbolic arrival can be met at rest by the 2.5 or 2.8 km/s tips.** Their release C3 is negative, so their tip speed is below the parabolic speed at the tip radius; the payload is always faster. This is the same fact as "the reference tier is GTO-class, not escape-class" (`rev_a.json` `_c3_note`), read backwards.
- **For a real tether the two tiers differ.** At 2.5 km/s the margin at a parabolic arrival is 393 m/s, 15.7 % of tip speed and five times the ±3 % gravity-gradient modulation of tip speed the glossary quotes (`docs/glossary.md:24`, itself [Likely]; ±75 m/s here), so the conclusion survives that effect: [Likely], close to certain. At 2.8 km/s the margin is 93 m/s, 3.3 % of tip speed, about the size of that modulation (±84 m/s), so spin dynamics could nearly close it: [Likely] at best that a real cislunar-tier tether cannot meet a near-parabolic arrival, and the question deserves Skyhook's dynamics model rather than this two-body bound.
- **[Certain] Bound arrivals are outside the input, not excluded by physics.** A payload falling back on a tier's own release orbit (apogee 85,222 km reference, 397,819 km cislunar) meets the reference or cislunar tip with zero trim, by time reversal of the release.
