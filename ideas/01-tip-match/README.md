# Tip Match
> Give it an Earth-arrival v∞ and it says whether a Skyhook tether tier could meet the payload at its upper tip and what trim Δv remains: the 2.5 km/s reference tip is 0.393 km/s too slow for even a parabolic arrival, the 2.8 km/s cislunar tip 0.093 km/s too slow, and only the 3.5 km/s Phase 3 tip meets a hyperbolic arrival at rest, at exactly v∞* = 3.613 km/s. Plane and phase it refuses, with what would help.

**Kind:** explorable kernel
**For:** Aster + Skyhook
**Run:** open `ideas/01-tip-match/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). CLI: `node ideas/01-tip-match/tip-match.mjs --vinf 3.61 --tier phase3` prints Aster-shaped evidence envelopes (`--text` for prose, `--help` for the rest; `example.txt` is saved output). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/01-tip-match/tip-match.test.mjs` (22 tests, Node built-ins, about 1 s) and `node ideas/01-tip-match/ui-check.mjs` (76 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`).
**Made by:** Grapnel

## What it is

Aster plans the outbound leg to an asteroid. Skyhook plans a rotating tether that catches ascending payloads at its lower tip and throws them from its upper tip. The one quantity the two projects would ever hand each other is an Earth-arrival hyperbolic excess speed, v∞: a payload coming home from an asteroid arrives with it, and the question Aster's kernel list calls K2 is whether that payload is inside the tether's catch envelope. Nothing in either repository computes the inbound case. This does the part that can be done honestly today.

You type a v∞ (0 to 8 km/s; 0 is a parabolic arrival, the slowest anything can arrive from beyond Earth's sphere of influence), pick a tip-speed tier from Skyhook's Rev A canon (2.5 km/s reference, 2.8 km/s revenue-gated cislunar option, 3.5 km/s Phase 3 growth option) and declare a grapple tolerance (default 1 m/s). The page then:

- draws the payload's speed at the upper-tip radius, √(v∞² + 2μ/r_tip), against each tier's tip speed at the top of its swing, v_orb + v_tip, and reports the difference as the trim Δv the payload would have to burn to arrive at rest relative to the tip;
- states the one v∞ each tier can meet with zero trim, v∞* = √C3, which exists only when the tier's release C3 is positive: none for the 2.5 and 2.8 km/s tiers (their C3 is −8.06 and −1.94 km²/s², so every hyperbolic arrival overshoots them) and 3.613 km/s for Phase 3;
- shows that the lower tip is never an option for an inbound payload (anything from beyond Earth passes 290 km at ≥ 10.94 km/s; the lower tip moves at 5.06 km/s);
- inverts the question: what tip speed would meet *this* v∞ with zero trim, as a multiple of Zylon's characteristic velocity and against the canon's 1.3× mass-growth guardrail;
- lights an "energy-matched" chip only when |trim Δv| is inside your declared tolerance, and says in the same breath that energy is necessary, not sufficient;
- refuses the two other conditions with structured refusals: the arrival **plane** (needs the v∞ vector and the tether's orbit plane; neither exists) and the **phase** (needs a tether ephemeris and an arrival epoch; no tether has flown);
- refuses to take v∞ from Aster's screening cache, because that cache holds outbound-leg quantities only, and says what would supply the real number (K1, the return-leg porkchop).

Every output is an Aster `EvidenceEnvelope`: `envelope_version`, `tool`, `value` with per-quantity `confidence` and `sourceIds`, `provenance` carrying the commit of the `rev_a.json` snapshot, `assumptions`, `validity_envelope`, and for refusals a `code` from Aster's three, a `reason` and `what_would_help`. The CLI prints the same envelopes.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** K2 is a CONCEPT kernel in Aster's `strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:35` (§1 row 16: "the Aster → Skyhook interface: is an inbound payload's Earth-arrival v∞ inside the tether's catch envelope? … Tether constants come ONLY from Skyhook `params/rev_a.json`, never from memory"), and `IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md:62-65` (§3 item 2) names it the link between the two flagship projects through "one well-defined interface: Earth-arrival v∞". `strategy/ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:41` carries it as lens L9 Aster Momentum ("connects the rotovator program — Rev A constants locked in its own skill, never quoted from memory"). The bank's own `BRIEFING.md` §4 lists it first among the seams. On the Skyhook side the catch window is defined only for the lower tip and an ascending payload (`docs/glossary.md:11-12`), and the capture envelope (±10 m, ~1 m/s relative velocity, 3–10 s) is a requirement in `docs/project_instructions.md:123-124`, again for a climbing payload. `physics/skyhook_physics.py:143` (`apogee_from_release`) computes the release side only; `physics/tether_dynamics.py:344` (`catch_and_measure`) models a lower-tip catch of an upbound payload; the program site's Monte Carlo (`tools/sims/skyhook_program_site.html:1315`) is an ascending rendezvous. The inverse energy condition did not exist anywhere.

- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *A rendezvous Monte Carlo for the inbound case*, like the site's ascending one. It would need a tether ephemeris, an arrival state vector and dispersion models; none exist, so every input would have been invented. Killed by Aster's rule that a kernel which must invent numbers does not ship.
  - *Feed it from Aster's screening cache.* `src/v2/boundary/lambert-screen-cache.ts:43-49` keeps `c3`, `vInfDep` and `vInfArr` per window, but those are departure from Earth and arrival at the asteroid on the outbound leg. Presenting either as an Earth-arrival v∞ is a fabrication. The page offers that source and refuses it, which is more useful than hiding it.
  - *A Python function in Skyhook's physics module* (`inbound_catch_trim_kms`). That is where it should land, but a deposit here cannot write there, and the Aster side wants the envelope shape. So the kernel is a UMD JavaScript module that both the page and the CLI share, and the README says what the Python port is.
  - *A one-number CLI.* Cheaper, but the chart is the argument: two tier lines that never meet the payload curve, and one that crosses it once, is why "catchable at the reference tier" is false in a way a reader sees at a glance.

  This won because it is the smallest thing that is true: an energy condition from canon constants, a structured refusal for everything else, and a shape Aster could consume.

## How it works

All constants come from `snapshots/rev_a.json`, a byte-for-byte copy of Skyhook `params/rev_a.json` at commit `34935cb` (sidecar: sha256 `d0828c1e…`, read bytes match the committed blob). `build-data.mjs` wraps that JSON and its sidecar as `snapshots/rev_a.data.js` so the page can read it from `file://` (a test checks the wrapper deep-equals the JSON and that every file under `snapshots/` still hashes to what its own sidecar recorded). Nothing physical is typed in code; `index.html` carries no tether constant as static text and a test greps for that.

**Geometry (all km, [Certain] arithmetic on canon values).** μ = `orbital.mu_earth_m3s2` / 10⁹ = 398,600 km³/s². Altitude h = `orbital.com_altitude_km` = 610, half-length L = `geometry.tether_half_length_km` = 320. The file never states an Earth radius as a key, but it states the upper-tip radius three times in prose ("r_tip=7301 km" in `kinematics._c3_note`, "r=R_E+610+320=7301 km" in `_upper_release_note` and `market_thresholds._note`); that is the radius its own C3 and apogee entries were evaluated at, so the kernel parses r_tip = 7301 from `_c3_note` and derives R_E = 7301 − 610 − 320 = 6371 km, which is the `R_EARTH_KM = 6371.0` at `physics/skyhook_physics.py:40` (snapshotted; a test pins the two together). Hence r_com = 6981, r_low = 6661. If the note is absent the kernel refuses (`not_found`) rather than remembering a radius. The file's `orbital.com_radius_km` = 6988 is deliberately **not** used: it implies R_E = 6378 and gives C3 = −8.137 km²/s², not the file's −8.06 (Findings, F1).

**The tip.** v_orb = √(μ/r_com) = 7.5563 km/s (EQ-2). At the top of the swing the tip moves horizontally, prograde, at v_tip,inertial = v_orb + v_tip: 10.0563, 10.3563 and 11.0563 km/s for the three tiers (as `v_upper_release_kms` in `skyhook_physics.py:113`). The lower tip at the bottom moves at v_orb − v_tip: 5.0563, 4.7563, 4.0563 km/s.

**Release energy, the canon's own numbers reproduced.** C3 = v²_tip,inertial − 2μ/r_tip (Skyhook EQ-7, `apogee_from_release`, `skyhook_physics.py:143-166`): −8.0611, −1.9373, +13.0515 km²/s² against the file's −8.06, −1.94, 13.05. For C3 < 0, a = −μ/C3 and the apogee altitude 2a − r_tip − R_E is 85,222.3 and 397,819.4 km against the file's 85,222 and 397,819. For C3 ≥ 0 the apogee is `null` (Skyhook's Python returns `math.inf`; Aster's envelope builder rejects any non-finite number, so the envelope says `release_bound: false` instead).

**The payload.** On its arrival hyperbola the speed at radius r is fixed by vis-viva, v = √(v∞² + 2μ/r), whatever the perigee. At r_tip the parabolic floor (v∞ = 0) is √(2μ/7301) = 10.4494 km/s. So **trim Δv = v_payload − v_tip,inertial**, in km/s and m/s; positive means the payload is faster and a retrograde burn would be needed. At v∞ = 0: +0.3931 (reference), +0.0931 (cislunar), −0.6069 (Phase 3). At v∞ = 3 km/s against the reference tip: payload 10.8715, trim 0.8152 (a hand-worked example in the tests). The trim is strictly increasing in v∞ and equals trim(0) + √(v∞² + v²_par) − v_par exactly.

**v∞\*.** A tier meets a payload at rest when v²_payload = v²_tip,inertial, i.e. v∞² = C3. So v∞* = √C3 exists only for C3 > 0: Phase 3 gives √13.0515 = 3.6127 km/s. The slope d(trim)/d(v∞) = v∞/v_payload is 0.327 there, so a 1 m/s grapple tolerance is a 3.06 m/s window in v∞ (3.61 km/s is inside it at −0.88 m/s; 3.615 is not).

**Zero-trim tip speed.** The inverse: v_tip,needed = √(v∞² + 2μ/r_tip) − v_orb. At v∞ = 0 it is 2.893 km/s, which is the "escape (C3=0) is reached at ~2.9 km/s" in the Phase 3 note and `market_thresholds.escape_C3_0_tip_speed_kms` = 2.9. At 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 km/s: 2.94, 3.08, 3.32, 4.03, 5.60 km/s. Shown as a multiple of V_c = √(2σ/ρ) = 2.727 km/s (from `materials.tensile_strength_GPa` and `density_kgm3`; the file states 2.73) against the "1.3x mass-growth guardrail" parsed from `mass_ratio._ratio_note`: v∞ = 5 km/s needs 1.48 V_c, beyond it.

**Lower tip.** √(2μ/r_low) = 10.9399 km/s at 290 km, against 5.06 km/s: the gap is ≥ 5.88 km/s for every tier and every v∞ ≥ 0, verdict `not reachable`.

**Match flag.** `energy_matched` iff |trim| ≤ the declared tolerance in m/s. The 1 m/s default is the "relative velocity within ~1 m/s at the moment of capture" requirement at `docs/project_instructions.md:124`; it is a planning requirement for ascending payloads, not in `rev_a.json`, so it is a user-declared input with `confidence: assumed`.

**Envelopes (Aster `mcp/src/envelope/index.ts`).** Value envelopes have five sources: `rev-a-mu` (repo, measured), `rev-a-design` (repo, assumed: altitude, length, tip speeds, σ/ρ are design choices), `vinf-input` and `tolerance-input` (external, assumed) and `two-body` (computation, derived). Every quantity leaf carries `confidence` and `sourceIds`, as Aster's `assertMixedProvenanceLeaves` demands, and the envelope's `confidence` is the weakest source, `assumed`, by `confidenceMin`. The refusal codes are Aster's three: `insufficient_data` (missing or non-finite v∞; plane; phase), `out_of_envelope` (negative v∞, bad tolerance), `not_found` (unknown tier; the Aster-cache source; a canon without the r_tip note). `validateEnvelope()` re-implements Aster's checks (version, non-empty fields, finite-deep value, resolvable `sourceIds`, weakest-link confidence, refusal shape); the CLI refuses to print an envelope that fails it.

**The chart.** Inline SVG sized to its real pixel width (so labels stay 11–12 px on a phone), x = v∞ 0–8 km/s, y = speed at r_tip 9.5–13.5 km/s: the payload curve, three tier lines with direct labels, the current v∞ as a cursor with both markers and the trim bracket, the Phase 3 crossing labelled v∞*, and one tooltip listing every series on hover or tap. Colours were run through the dataviz palette validator in both modes (light `#2a78d6 #eb6834 #1baf7a #6250d6`, dark `#3987e5 #d95926 #199e70 #9085e9`: all checks pass; the light aqua is under 3:1 and so is always direct-labelled and in the table). Status colours carry an icon and a word, never colour alone.

**Earth-radius sensitivity, measured.** Re-deriving everything with R_E = 6378.137 km (a modified canon note; r_com and r_tip both move) shifts the reference C3 by +0.029 km²/s² and the v∞ = 0 trim by about −1 m/s. Moving only r_tip while keeping v_orb at the 6371 km radius, which is what mixing the two radii does, shifts C3 by 0.106; that is the "~0.1" figure in the proposal, and it is the inconsistent version.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The energy arithmetic.** Two-body vis-viva and EQ-2/EQ-7 on canon constants, reconciled with the file's own C3, apogee, release speed, escape threshold, V_c, rotation period and tip acceleration, and checked against a hand computation and closed forms. The trims (0.393, 0.093, −0.607 km/s at v∞ = 0) and v∞* = 3.613 km/s follow.
- **[Certain] No hyperbolic arrival can be met at rest by the 2.5 or 2.8 km/s tips.** Their release C3 is negative, so their tip speed is below the parabolic speed at the tip radius; the payload is always faster. This is the same fact as "the reference tier is GTO-class, not escape-class" (`rev_a.json` `_c3_note`), read backwards.
- **[Certain] The lower tip is irrelevant for inbound payloads** under these assumptions: 10.94 km/s versus 5.06.
- **[Certain] The reported trim is the scalar speed difference and a lower bound on the vector mismatch.** The speed at r_tip is fixed by v∞; the direction is not. Only a payload whose perigee is at the upper-tip radius, moving horizontally and prograde at the top of the swing, has this as its whole mismatch. A lower perigee adds a radial component. The page, the validity envelope and the energy card say so.
- **[Likely] Energy-matched at Phase 3 means something.** A 3.5 km/s tether could meet a v∞ ≈ 3.6 km/s payload at rest if plane and phase were arranged, and the ~1 m/s tolerance is a 3 m/s window in v∞. Whether a return leg from a real near-Earth asteroid arrives near 3.6 km/s is exactly what K1 would compute and this cannot.
- **[Likely] The modelling simplifications are the right first ones** (circular CoM orbit, rigid rotation, tip at the top of the swing, v_orb evaluated at r_com as Skyhook's own `apogee_from_release` does). The glossary's gravity-gradient modulation (±3 % of tip speed) alone is ±75–105 m/s, two orders above the grapple tolerance; a real catch is timed, not averaged.
- **[Speculative] What catching a multi-tonne payload at 10–11 km/s does to the tether, and what the momentum deposit is worth.** Not modelled. `tether_dynamics.py` models a lower-tip catch of an upbound payload only; the glossary's dynamic load factor is itself [Speculative].
- **[Speculative] The 1 m/s default tolerance applies to this geometry.** It is written for a climbing payload and a cone-and-latch grapple; an inbound capsule at escape speed may need a different number. It is user-declared here for that reason.
- **Rough.** The envelope confidence is always `assumed` under Aster's weakest-link rule, because tip speed and altitude are design choices and v∞ is typed; informative at the leaf level, uninformative at the top. The slider's 0–8 km/s range and the chart's 9.5–13.5 km/s axis are drawing choices. The v∞* button rounds to 3.613 (0.3 m/s of v∞, 0.1 m/s of trim off the exact crossing), which is why a 0.05 m/s tolerance un-matches it. `as_of` defaults to the day the page is opened. The tooltip is hover and tap only; the table carries the same numbers for keyboard readers. No real person has used the page.
- **Fidelity.** An idea-bank prototype. It has not been through Aster's multi-agent math audit, oracle validation, founding-document DEC process or protected-core review (`src/v2/core/` is protected surface), nor Skyhook's Rule 1 canon process, verification memo or `pytest tests/`.

**Deviations from the judged proposal, and why.**

- The proposal's Earth-radius sensitivity ("shifts C3 by ~0.1 km²/s²") is the inconsistent, r_tip-only shift. Moving both radii gives 0.029 km²/s²; both are measured and tested. The disclosure is kept, with the smaller number.
- The proposal called the 1 m/s tolerance "labelled [Planning]". The manifest's [Planning] label covers Parts VI–VII, not §4.3 (F3). The deposit describes it as a planning requirement, user-declared, not canon.
