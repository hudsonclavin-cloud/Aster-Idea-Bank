# Known Unknowns
> Half of Aster's 41,906-body catalog has condition code U ≥ 7, yet 98.8% passes the low-departure-C3 screen, and while the badly known bodies look cheaper catalog-wide (Spearman ρ(U, min C3) = −0.21), the sign flips inside every one of six H bands (+0.08 to +0.28): it is size, not knowledge.

**Kind:** study
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/17-known-unknowns/index.html` in a browser (plain classic scripts, works from `file://`, no build, no network; the data is a derived snapshot). CLI, against the checkout: `node ideas/17-known-unknowns/known-unknowns.mjs` (reads `/home/user/asteroid-mining-planner` by default, `--aster DIR` for another; `example.txt` is its saved output). Other modes: `--body "1979 XB"` (one body as an Aster-shaped evidence envelope plus the observing-plan refusal, `--json` for the envelopes; exit 2 with a `not_found` refusal for an unknown designation), `--sort H|c3 --top N` (re-select the list from all 20,296), `--u 6 --arc 30` (the what-if filter), `--json`, `--write` / `--check` (rebuild or verify `snapshots/study.data.js`), `--help`. Exit 0 ok, 1 a reconciliation failed or the snapshot is stale, 2 refused, 3 usage. Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/17-known-unknowns/known-unknowns.test.mjs` (23 tests, Node built-ins; about 22 s with the Aster checkout present, about 1 s without, when the four live-checkout tests skip) and `node ideas/17-known-unknowns/ui-check.mjs` (90 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright, about 5 s; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`; `--shots DIR` saves full-page screenshots).
**Made by:** Precovery

## What it is

Aster screens all 41,906 near-Earth bodies in its catalog for minimum departure C3 and shows the result with honesty labels, but nothing in Aster puts that screen next to how well each orbit is known. The catalog carries the orbit-quality fields (JPL's condition code U, the data arc in days, the number of observations used, the derived `qualityRank`), the screening cache carries each body's minimum C3 and its window, and the Slice 18 tier artifact carries each body's screening-fidelity tier. This study joins the three and asks what Aster's family plan calls the Survey lens's question ("What observation most improves confidence?", `strategy/ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:37`): which bodies are cheap to reach on paper and badly known, how many there are, and why.

The page has six parts, all drawn from one derived snapshot:

- **A cross-tab** of minimum-C3 band against U, data arc, observations used or H (a toggle), every body counted once, with row shares as a one-hue heat shade and the counts printed. The C3 band edges are Aster's own constants: 0.1 km²/s² (the near-zero flag), 1 (the porkchop colour floor), 10 (the first porkchop contour) and 25 (the screening threshold).
- **The reversal.** A dot plot of median minimum C3 for U ≤ 2 against U ≥ 7, for the whole catalog and then inside six H bands. Catalog-wide the U ≥ 7 median is 0.993 km²/s² against 3.32 for U ≤ 2; in every H band it is higher (for H 22–24, 2.17 against 1.41). Absolute magnitude drives both: ρ(H, min C3) = −0.58 and ρ(H, U) = +0.56.
- **A what-if filter**: two sliders, "U at most" and "data arc at least", and a count of the 41,422 low-departure-C3 bodies that would pass, with every dropped body assigned to one reason (U too high, arc too short, both, U not reported, arc not reported) and the survivors split by C3 band, over a chart of all ten U limits against the arc limit. At U ≤ 6 and an arc of at least 30 days, 16,381 pass (39.5%). It is labelled a what-if filter, not a tier, because Aster's DEC-17-4 says U is "never a kill switch" and the catalog spec reserves `planning-tier` for Aster policy; both are quoted on the page.
- **The list**: the 20,296 bodies with a low departure C3, U ≥ 7 and a data arc under 30 days or none reported, carried as the top 200 under two rankings (brightest H first; lowest minimum C3) with each body's minimum-C3 window (launch, TOF, arrival, C3, arrival v∞) and labelled flags: Aster's own near-zero-C3 badge and hide toggle, "after <date> encounter" for Slice 18 L1 bodies whose window arrives after the encounter that degrades them (Aster's own sentence), a launch before the Aster commit date, a minimum on an edge of the screening lattice, co-orbital, PHA, not-kepler-safe and the fidelity tier. Rows become two-column cards at phone width.
- **A qualityRank audit** with a two-input calculator: the spec's formula against the stored values for every body (they match), and against the formula string the fixture carries about itself (it disagrees for 1,856 bodies; see the findings).
- **Four refusals**, shaped like an Aster evidence envelope's `refusal` field, for the questions this study cannot answer: an observing plan, U in kilometres, the next window after today, and whether high U means the screening C3 is wrong. And a provenance section with the commit, blobs, sha256s and every constant's source line.

The CLI prints the same study as text from a live checkout, re-selects the list under either ranking at any length, runs the what-if filter, and emits one body as a pair of envelopes: its row (with `value`, `provenance`, `assumptions`) and the observing-plan refusal (`insufficient_data`, with `what_would_help`).

## Why I made it

The judged proposal (A4-07) was to cross condition code and data arc with screening C3 and produce "the list an observing plan would start from" for the Survey lens, which the master plan describes as closing the evidence loop: "Survey closes the loop: it turns `what_would_help` into observation campaign plans." (`strategy/ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:69`; the lens row is `:37`, status CONCEPT). Nothing in Aster does this today: the compare page explains U per body in prose (`src/v2/app/compare/main.ts:294-328`), MCP `get_body` returns the quality fields for one body (`mcp/src/tools/catalog-shared.ts:138-149`), and `search_bodies` filters by designation or name query, `orbitClass` and `screeningStatus` only (`mcp/src/tools/search-bodies.ts:13-19`). No catalog-wide quality × screening table exists.

I verified the proposal before building, and several parts did not survive as written:

- **The headline claim.** The proposal said the user would learn "that accessibility and knowledge are anti-correlated in this catalog, and by how much". Catalog-wide they are (ρ = −0.21 over 41,895 bodies). But when I stratified by H the correlation turned positive in all six bands. The interesting finding is the reversal, so the page leads with it.
- **The ranking.** The proposal ranked the list by minimum C3, ascending. Aster's DEC-19-1 (`src/v2/SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:81-110`) rules that near-zero-C3 windows are flagged and never removed from data, displays or rankings, and 1,686 of the list's bodies are near-zero. Ranked honestly by minimum C3, the first 1,686 rows are all flags ("a flag to inspect the geometry, not a real planning number"). Dropping them would break DEC-19-1, so I kept that ranking with Aster's badge and its view-only hide toggle, and added a brightest-first ranking that is useful for recovery. The page says why.
- **The sliders.** The proposal called them a "what-if gate". DEC-17-4 (`SLICE_17_FOUNDING.md:193-194`) says U is "ranking-penalty + visibility flag; never a kill switch". A gate on U is exactly what that rules out as policy, so the page calls it a counting-only what-if filter, quotes the ruling, and frames the question as what such a gate would cost.
- **A citation.** The proposal put Slice 18's "Q ANSWERED — condition code does NOT predict propagation error" and its structural caveat in `src/v2/SLICE_18_FOUNDING.md`. They are at `STATUS.md:269` (the measurement is `tools/slice18-research/NEA_DRIFT_MEASUREMENT.md:99`). The founding document carries the related DEC-18-2 at `:63`: U "is NEVER converted to kilometres". Both are quoted, each from where it actually is.
- **The data path.** The proposal's "stratum cutter `--must`" is not a tool in this bank. I wrote the derivation myself (`known-unknowns.mjs --write`).
- **What ships.** The proposal planned a separate cross-tab/survivor JSON (~30 KB) and a top-200 excerpt (~40 KB). I ship one derived file of 79 KB: the histograms (the survivor grid is computed from them in the browser, not stored), the stratified table, the audit, the constants and quotes with their lines, and 400 list rows (the top 200 under each ranking). I added the Slice 18 tier artifact as a third input, for the L1 encounter flag.

Alternatives I weighed:

- *Ship per-body data and filter in the browser.* All 41,906 rows with the five fields the page needs measure 0.94 MB as bare JSON tuples (1.4 MB with designations), nearly twice the folder budget. The cross-tabs and the what-if filter only need counts, so the page ships three dense histograms ([C3 band][U][arc / observations / H bin], 2,046 cells) and computes everything else from them. The list ships 400 rows.
- *Rank by Aster's ΔV stack instead of C3.* That would use the cached windows' arrival v∞ (`src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:28-29` books the rendezvous burn as the arrival v∞). But the cache keeps each body's five lowest-C3 cells, not its lowest-ΔV cells, so the ranking would be "ΔV at the C3 minimum", which is easy to misread. I show the arrival v∞ per row and say what it means instead.
- *Rank by the soonest window.* That is the urgency a recovery campaign wants, but the cache cannot answer it (see the refusals), so it is a refusal, not a ranking.
- *A per-body observability column.* There is no magnitude, sky position or elongation anywhere in Aster. Faking one is the thing this bank forbids, so it is the first refusal.

## How it works

**Inputs [Certain].** `known-unknowns.mjs` reads three files from the Aster checkout at commit `62ddb17` (2026-10-25). They are the catalog `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.json` (sha256 `f88028d2…`, 52,969,826 bytes), the screening cache `tests/fixtures/v2/lambert-screen-cache.json` (sha256 `bd27b9bd…`) and the Slice 18 tier artifact `docs/tier-sizing-per-body.json` (sha256 `dff569ab…`). It records each one's committed blob and the blob of the bytes it read. It joins catalog to cache by `bodyId` and catalog to tiers by designation. It fails (exit 1) unless the cache's `metadata.provenance.catalogFixtureSha256` equals the catalog's sha256 (it does), every body joins, every body's C3 band agrees with its cache status, and `minC3` is `bestWindows[0]` for every body. It reads every Aster constant by regex from its source line and records the path and line: near-zero 0.1 (`src/v2/core/constants/near-zero-c3.ts:5`), colour floor 1 (`src/v2/porkchop/colormap.ts:4`), first contour 10 (`src/v2/porkchop/porkchop-view.ts:247`), screening threshold 25 (`metadata.feasibleC3MaxKm2S2`), U ≥ 7 as JPL's "highly uncertain" and U ≤ 2 as Aster's own "tightly determined" (`src/v2/app/compare/main.ts:306` and `:314`), and TDB − UTC 69.184 s (`src/v2/core/units/utc-to-tdb.ts:28`). It reads the 17 passages the page quotes (rulings, labels, Aster's exact UI strings, the refusal-code enum) the same way. It writes `snapshots/study.data.js` (79 KB), which has no timestamps, so a re-derivation can be compared byte for byte.

**C3 bands [Certain].** Band 0 is minC3 < 0.1 (strict, as `isNearZeroC3Degenerate`). Bands 1–3 are [0.1, 1), [1, 10) and [10, 25], with ≤ 25 as in the precompute's `minC3 <= FEASIBLE_C3_MAX` (`tools/build/precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:300`). Band 4 is > 25 and band 5 is no solution. Bands 0–3 are exactly `low_departure_c3`, and the derivation checks that for every body. **Our own bands [labelled as ours everywhere]:** data-arc bin starts 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 14, 21, 30, 60, 90, 180, 365, 1000, 3653 days (1000 is where the qualityRank arc score saturates; 30 is the list's limit); observations 0, 10, 20, 50, 100, 1000; H edges 18, 20, 22, 24, 26. Not-reported values are their own rows, never imputed.

**The what-if filter [Certain as arithmetic].** The population is the 41,422 `low_departure_c3` bodies, near-zero included. For U limit u (0–9) and arc-bin index d, a body passes when U ≤ u and its arc bin is at least d. One with U or arc not reported cannot be shown to pass and is counted apart. The six outcomes partition the population, and a test checks that for all 160 (u, d) pairs. The curves are `survivorGrid`, all computed in the browser from the histogram.

**The reversal [Certain as measured, Likely as explained].** Spearman's ρ is the Pearson correlation of average ranks (ties averaged). Medians are over every body with a minimum C3, near-zero included. Catalog-wide: ρ(U, min C3) = −0.213 (n 41,895), ρ(arc, min C3) = +0.397 (n 41,488), ρ(H, min C3) = −0.579, ρ(H, U) = +0.557. Within H bands:

| H band | bodies | U ≤ 2: n, median min C3 | U ≥ 7: n, median min C3 | ρ(U, min C3) | ρ(arc, min C3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 41,905 | 10,826, 3.32 | 21,825, 0.993 | −0.21 | +0.40 |
| < 18 | 1,088 | 1,078, 6.13 | 5, 20.6 | +0.18 | −0.23 |
| 18–20 | 3,703 | 3,348, 5.13 | 112, 7.77 | +0.16 | −0.19 |
| 20–22 | 6,726 | 3,880, 3.23 | 1,078, 5.05 | +0.20 | −0.21 |
| 22–24 | 8,867 | 1,671, 1.41 | 4,462, 2.17 | +0.08 | −0.05 |
| 24–26 | 11,767 | 607, 0.573 | 9,012, 1.01 | +0.18 | +0.04 |
| ≥ 26 | 9,545 | 162, 0.210 | 7,073, 0.431 | +0.28 | −0.02 |

(209 bodies with a minimum C3 and no H appear only in "all".) The likely reason is discovery selection. Faint, small bodies are found when they pass close to Earth, on Earth-like orbits that screen cheap, and are followed only briefly. I did not measure that mechanism, and the page says so.

**The list [Certain].** Criterion: U ≥ 7 (from the compare page's line), data arc under 30 days or not reported (ours), low departure C3 with near-zero kept (DEC-19-1). 20,296 bodies (48.4% of the catalog). The count from the per-body filter equals the count summed from the histogram, and the derivation fails if they differ. Each row's window is the cached minimum-C3 cell. Arrival = launch + TOF. The L1 flag follows Aster's support boundary (`src/v2/porkchop/support-boundary.ts:44-51`): the departure epoch is the UTC midnight of `launchDate` plus TDB − UTC (the precompute builds departures that way, `precompute-lambert-screen.mjs:141-146`), arrival at or after the encounter's TDB Julian date counts as unsupported, and the badge's tooltip is Aster's own sentence. The offset matters in principle: across all 10,150 L1 bodies the closest cached arrival is 91 s before its encounter (136849, 2027-07-19). It changes no flag here, and the data records that. Flag totals over all 20,296: near-zero 1,686, minimum-C3 launch before 2026-10-25 1,747, on a lattice edge 914, arrival after the L1 encounter 2,040, co-orbital 9, PHA 162, not-kepler-safe 140, H not reported 62, arc not reported 350; tiers L0 2, L1 2,744, L2 17,550.

**qualityRank [Certain].** The spec (`tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.spec.md:158-160`) clamps each score: `clamp01(1 − U/9)`, `clamp01(log10(1 + arc)/log10(1001))`, then `clamp01(0.6·c + 0.4·a)`. Recomputed for all 41,906 bodies it matches the stored value to 4.99 × 10⁻⁷, the stored value being rounded to six decimals. That agrees with Aster's own A.2b audit, which found 0 mismatches against the implementation (`tools/slice9-diagnostic/SLICE_9_A2B_DERIVED_FIELDS_DIAGNOSTIC.md:26-32`). The fixture's `catalog.qualityRankFormula` string, written at `tools/slice9-ingestion/build-nea-catalog.mjs:34`, has only the outer clamp. Read literally, it disagrees for every body with an arc over 1000 days and U not 0 (U = 0 is already capped by the outer clamp): 1,856 bodies, by up to 0.2246 (55P: U not reported, arc 48,407 d, stored 0.4, literal 0.6246).

**The page.** `model.js` is pure (UMD, classic script and `require()`). `app.js` builds the DOM with `textContent` only, draws inline SVG, and reads every number from the study object or the model: the tests fail if a literal 25, 0.1, 69.184 or `7)` appears in `model.js` or `app.js`, or a headline count, the commit or the as-of date appears in `app.js` or `index.html`. The one number typed into `index.html` is the calculator's example input (U 9, arc 94,131 d, the catalog's longest arc), whose outputs are computed. Colours are CSS variables on `:root` redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. The two series (blue for U ≤ 2, orange for U ≥ 7) pass the dataviz validator in both modes (CVD ΔE 24.7 light / 26.8 dark). The heat shade has five classes of row share (up to 5, 15, 35 and 60%, and above; zero is the bare surface), a display choice of mine; the printed share and count carry the value. Heat-cell text uses dark or white ink by fill, at a contrast of 5.39:1 at the lowest. At 390 px the cross-tab scrolls sideways in its own box with sticky row labels, and the list becomes cards. Every place that shows a minimum C3 (the lede, the cross-tab head, the chart axis, the list column, the CLI's sections and `--body`'s `minC3Grid`) names the grid it came from, the cache's 783 × 55 lattice, because DEC-19-2 (`src/v2/SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:112-120`) requires it: Aster's catalog, porkchop and compare grids give different minima for the same body.

## Honest assessment

What works:

- [Certain] Every count on the page and in `example.txt` comes from the committed fixtures at `62ddb17` through code a reviewer can run. A fresh derivation is tested to deep-equal the snapshot, and an independent recount without `model.js` agrees on the list size, the qualityRank audit and the catalog-wide ρ.
- [Certain] The catalog-wide anti-correlation and its reversal within H bands are measured on this catalog. The U ≥ 7 median minimum C3 is lower catalog-wide and higher in all six H bands, and ρ(U, min C3) is positive in all six.
- [Likely] The reversal is a discovery-selection effect (faint bodies are found near Earth and followed briefly). That is the standard reading of such a pattern and fits ρ(H, U) = +0.56, but I did not test it.
- [Certain] Nothing Aster rules out is done. U is never turned into a distance (DEC-18-2), never used as a kill switch (DEC-17-4; the filter only counts), near-zero values are flagged and never dropped (DEC-19-1), every minimum C3 names its grid (DEC-19-2), the fixture's reserved `planning-tier` name is not used, and "low departure C3" is never called "accessible" (the cache boundary's own warning is quoted).

What is rough:

- [Certain] **The list is long and is not a plan.** 20,296 bodies, 48% of the catalog, meet a criterion built from JPL's U ≥ 7 line and my own 30-day arc limit. The page carries only the top 200 under each of two rankings. Neither ranking is a recommendation, and the brightest-first one only uses H as a rough proxy for "easier to recover" without computing any magnitude.
- [Certain] **Each row's window is the cache's minimum-C3 cell, not a mission.** It can sit on a lattice edge (914 rows), launch before the commit date (1,747), arrive after an L1 encounter (2,040), or pair a low departure C3 with an arrival v∞ above 25 km/s (2025 NR197). All of these are flagged or shown, not fixed.
- [Certain] **The 30-day arc limit, the arc, observation and H bands are mine.** Other choices move the counts. The what-if filter exists so a reader can see how much.
- [Certain] **The "as of" date is the Aster commit date (2026-10-25), not today.** It keeps the page deterministic. A reader on a later date sees "launch before 2026-10-25" flags that understate what is past.
- [Likely] The H-band medians for the brightest band rest on 5 U ≥ 7 bodies. The chart draws them hollow and the note says so.
- [Certain] The CLI reads 98 MB of JSON and takes about 3.5 s. The F tests run it four times, which is most of the 22 s.

Findings about the source repo:

[Certain] **The catalog fixture's self-described qualityRank formula is wrong.** `catalog.qualityRankFormula` in `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.json` (written from the string at `tools/slice9-ingestion/build-nea-catalog.mjs:34`) reads `clamp01(0.6 * (… 1 - condition_code / 9) + 0.4 * (… log10(1 + data_arc_days) / log10(1001)))`, with no inner clamps. Gates this has not been through: Aster's founding-document DEC process (the Survey lens is CONCEPT, and the master plan's Wave 2 entry gate, "a named user, data partner, or application deadline that wants it", `strategy/ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:75`, has not fired); INV-028/INV-033 as Aster enforces them (the envelopes here are hand-built in the schema's shape and checked against its fields, not produced by `makeEnvelope`); the MCP eval (`mcp/eval/slice15-eval-pairs.json`); the protected-core process (nothing here edits `src/v2/core`; it only reads constants). The multi-agent math audit and oracle validation do not apply: no orbital mechanics is computed, only counts, ranks and medians over committed data.

## Where it would land

- **Aster MCP, `mcp/src/tools/search-bodies.ts`.** Add optional `conditionCodeMin` / `conditionCodeMax`, `dataArcDaysMin` / `dataArcDaysMax` filters to the zod input schema, name them in `coverage.selection_rule` as the existing filters are, and treat a body with the field not reported as not matching, saying so in `assumptions`. The eval pairs in `mcp/eval/slice15-eval-pairs.json` would need a pair for it, and DEC-17-4's "never a kill switch" needs a ruling on whether a search filter counts as one.
- **The Survey lens's first artefact.** The cross-tab, the reversal and the list as a page or an MCP resource, once the lens gets a founding document. Its refusals become real INV-028 envelopes built with `makeEnvelope`, and its `what_would_help` for an observing plan names the observer model the lens needs (`ASTER_FAMILY_MASTER_PLAN.md:37`: "facility capability data, observation-type value model").
- **The fixture fix.** Correct the string at `tools/slice9-ingestion/build-nea-catalog.mjs:34` and regenerate the header. Because the fixture is hashed into the cache's provenance (`metadata.provenance.catalogFixtureSha256`), that means a cache provenance update too, or a documented exception.
- **To ship for real:** read the fixtures through Aster's own boundary loaders instead of `JSON.parse`, import the constants instead of reading them by regex, move the as-of date to the request time with the cache's own window, and replace the U ≥ 7 / 30-day list criterion with whatever the Survey founding document decides.
