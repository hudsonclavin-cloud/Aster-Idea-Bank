# Hot Return
> The chain's 9.6 t of asteroid flown from idea 48's skip entry to the ground: the radiation idea 48 left out more than triples its first pass's peak heating (776 of 1,104 W/cm²), the heat load reaches 77 kJ/cm², 2.1 times what Stardust's heat shield was built for, so the page names the 10.3 mm that burns away and refuses a thickness, then lands it at 4.5 m/s under 7,541 m² of parachute. The same model puts Genesis's parachuteless fall (+1.5 %), OSIRIS-REx's time on its main (+4 % from where NASA reconstructs it opened) and Stardust's flown recession (-24 %, -4 % on NASA's heat load) beside what was measured, and an independent integrator flies the descent to within 0.01 s.

## What it is

Idea 49 is module 19 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 48 decided how the returning material stops at the Earth and handed on the state at the top of the air. This page flies it the rest of the way and hands module 50 what lands.

- **Pick what comes down.** The chain: idea 48's committed handoff (Apophis material, 9,565 kg in the Apollo-derived shape, a skip entry at -6.50°, two passes an hour apart). Or your own idea 48 export (paste, file or `#h=` link). Or one of three capsules that came home, flown from the entry states idea 48 took from NASA's reports: Stardust (2006, drogue and main as planned), OSIRIS-REx (2023, as flown: its swapped signals left the drogue in its canister until the main's pressure switch fired the mortar, and the drogue pulled the main straight out), Genesis (2004, as flown: no parachute at all).
- **Set the shield and the parachutes.** Count the radiative heating or not (to see idea 48's convective-only picture); PICA's density (Stardust's 266 kg/m³, MSL-grade 274); the parachute sequence (as flown, drogue then main, no drogue, none); the touchdown speed that sizes a new main (OSIRIS-REx's 4.5 m/s by default); the altitude the main's pressure switch fires at; the G-switch threshold; the drogue timer; the ground's altitude.
- **Read what lands and what it went through:** touchdown speed, time and place (refused for the flown capsules: idea 48 starts their entries at their landing targets, so where their flights end is not where they landed), landed mass, peak heating split into convective and radiative, heat load, the stagnation-point recession, the wall temperature (an upper bound), whether a flown heat shield covers this entry (three bars against Stardust's design), the opening loads of each canopy, the main's drag area and what it means in OSIRIS-REx mains. Every tile carries its confidence; every refusal says what would help, including a parachute sequence that never opens (a G-switch that never fires, a timer that runs past the ground, a pressure switch below it); the 42 refusals idea 48 carried come along.
- **Three charts that follow the controls:** heating against time in the air (coasts cut out; tap to read a moment), the way down (altitude and speed with the G-switch, drogue, main and touchdown marked; tap to read), the shield burning away (cumulative recession and wall temperature).
- **Checked against what flew:** 21 rows computed live from NASA's reports on Stardust, Genesis and OSIRIS-REx.
- **Export the handoff** for module 50 (copy, download or link).

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 49: "Re-entry of the sample capsule: entry trajectory, deceleration, stagnation-point heating, heat-shield ablation, parachute", taking "entry state" and handing on "landed mass, peak loads". Idea 48's README (*How it works*, "For module 49"): "`entry_interface`, `vehicle` (with its C<sub>D</sub> table and nose radius) and `screening` to fly the entry in detail, size the heat shield (`mass.heat_shield_kg` is null and refused here) and add the parachutes; the `radiative_heating`, `tps_mass` and `atmosphere` refusals are 49's to answer." Its handoff carries those three refusals. This page answers `radiative_heating` (computed), answers `tps_mass` as far as public data allow (the recession and the mass it takes; the thickness only where a flown shield covers the entry, else a sharper refusal) and carries `atmosphere` on.
- **Aster asked for honest sample-return numbers.** Aster's porkchop prices a sample return's departure burn (`src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:45` at `62ddb17`) but nothing in Aster models the arrival: no entry heating, no heat shield, no parachute, no capsule mass in the delivered-mass budget. The Long Way Round's module 48 opened that seam; this closes it as far as a capsule on the ground.
- **What a person decides with it.** Whether a return's entry fits inside a heat shield that has already flown (Stardust's, which OSIRIS-REx flew build-to-print: if yes, 5.82 cm of PICA and its mass; if no, the page says which of heating, heat load and pressure breaks it); how much shield burns; how much parachute a heavy capsule needs for a given touchdown speed; what an off-nominal sequence costs (OSIRIS-REx without its drogue opened its main at 12.5 g instead of 7; Genesis without either hit at 58 m/s).

Alternatives weighed.
1. *Size the heat shield with a 1-D ablation and conduction model* (the FIAT/CMA class). Rejected: it needs PICA's temperature-dependent conductivity, heat capacity and pyrolysis kinetics, and "Material properties of high-performance ablators are subject to export-control restrictions" (Milos & Chen, AIAA 2009-0262, which validates the PICAv3.3 model but cannot print it). Any numbers I could put in would be from memory or invented. Built instead: the surface recession from Stardust's own arc-jet data (a law, not a property set), and the thickness by heritage only.
2. *An effective heat of ablation (heat load over Q\*).* Rejected: Q\* from the arc jet (q/(ρ ṡ) ≈ 120 MJ/kg) mixes re-radiation, blowing and oxidation and depends on enthalpy, so it does not carry from a 29.5 MJ/kg arc jet to a 50-70 MJ/kg flight. The mass-transfer form (ṁ = B′ q/h₀) does: in diffusion-limited oxidation B′ is fixed by the oxygen at the edge, the same air in both. The two arc-jet points give B′ within 4 % of each other at different heat fluxes and pressures, which is what that physics predicts.
3. *Sutton & Hartung's radiative tables instead of a fit.* Their text layer is OCR with garbled exponents ("1.13013+01"); transcribing ten tables would be the error source. Built: the Suttles curve fit (nine coefficients, read and checked against all 91 sphere cases of the paper's own Table III(a)), with Sutton & Hartung's 60 km table (28 values, each checked on the page image: 12 inside the fit's range, 16 outside it) as the independent check.
4. *Parachute drag coefficients from a handbook.* None on file gives numbers for a 0.8 m DGB or a 7.3 m triconic canopy (NASA SP-8066 rates canopies "high/medium/low C<sub>D</sub>" only). Built: drag areas from flight, one per canopy, each fitted to one flight observable and checked against others (below).
5. *Re-run idea 48 whole on import.* Rejected for size (its Skyhook, Aster and idea 47 data are 115 KB more) and time (a second). Built: the strong check without them: fly idea 48's entry state again with idea 48's own `fly()` and match the loads it screened to one part in a million, so an edited entry state, vehicle or screening is caught.

## How it works

**Reused, unmodified.** Idea 48's kernel `catch.js` (`fly()`, `flyPasses()`, `coastTo()`, `deriv()`, the vehicles with their NASA aerodynamics, the flown capsules' entry states, Sutton & Graves's convective heating) and its six snapshots (PartForge's 1976 atmosphere `isa()`, Aster's GM and Earth radius, pck00010's spheroid and rotation, DE440's GMs, ERFA's Earth rotation angle, idea 48's NASA quotes), byte-identical to idea 48's committed files (a test). Every flight on this page is that `fly()`: a 3-DOF point mass under point-mass gravity, drag and lift at constant bank, air turning with the Earth, altitude above the flattened Earth, RK4 at 0.1 s.

**New numbers, all quoted.** `snapshots/reentry-sources.js` is written by `build-data.mjs` from 17 NASA reports and NIST's page: each number sits beside the sentence it was read from, and the build found every sentence verbatim in the PDF's text and every number inside its sentence (133 quotes; 124 before the review). Read from page images, and marked so: Covington's two arc-jet recession averages (a figure legend: "Measured Average for 580 W/cm2 = 0.018 cm/sec", "... 400 W/cm2 = 0.0115 cm/sec"), Suttles's Table III(a), all 91 sphere cases with the paper's error column (printed sideways over three pages, no text layer; the review transcribed the 66 the builder had not), the Sutton & Hartung 60 km values (their rows are also in the OCR text layer).

**The flight to the ground** (`descend()`). Every pass with idea 48's bank to the ground with no parachute; the G-switch fires where the sensed deceleration falls back through 3 g after the last pass's peak ("As the SRC slows and entry deceleration decreases below 3-g's, the switch will reopen", OSIRIS-REx). From there the vehicle is lighter by the shield it has burned. The drogue opens after the timer (Stardust 15.04 s, OSIRIS-REx 14 s, Genesis 5.6 s); the main at the pressure switch or the main timer, "whichever method indicates first" (Stardust 10.11 psi = 69,706 Pa, about 3.05 km in the 1976 atmosphere; OSIRIS-REx 10,000 ft; Stardust's timer 350.6 s after the drogue, OSIRIS-REx's 363 s after the trigger; Genesis had no pressure switch, its main timer 254.0 s after the drogue). A pressure switch armed when the pressure is already past its setting (below its altitude) fires at once; with no drogue, a main timer that starts with the drogue's still starts when the drogue's timer runs out. A sequence that never opens a parachute (a G-switch threshold above the last pass's peak, or at or below the 1 g of a steady fall; a timer longer than the fall; a main pressure switch below the ground) lands as it is and is refused (`parachute_sequence`), with the speed it hits at. Canopies fill over OSIRIS-REx's filmed times (drogue mortar to full inflation 0.38 s; main bag strip to full inflation 1.14 s), the drag area growing linearly (assumed), flown at 0.01 s; the opening peak includes the instant the canopy is full. Every event is found between 0.1 s samples and the flight is flown again to stop exactly there, so the state handed on is `fly()`'s own. Ground: UTTR for the capsules (OSIRIS-REx's 1,288 m; Stardust's 3.1 km main deploy "1.8 km above ground", 1,300 m; Genesis's 4,200 ft), sea level for the chain (no site is chosen: refused); 0 to 6,000 m when set (`fly()` stops at the spheroid, so lower ground is never reached). **Where it lands.** For idea 48's arrivals with an epoch: latitude and longitude through ERFA's Earth rotation angle at the landing epoch. For the flown capsules (and an idea 48 handoff made from one) idea 48 starts the entry at the landing target's latitude and longitude, heading along the landing ellipse, because the papers give no entry point; the flight then ends about 830-880 km downrange of that target, which is not where the capsule landed, so the latitude and longitude are refused (`landing_site`) and only the distance flown is given.

**The canopies, from flight** (`srcCanopies()`). Drogue: the drag area (capsule and drogue together) that takes Stardust from 31.03 km, where radar saw its drogue open, to 3.428 km, where its main opened, in the 331.3 s radar measured: 0.719 m² (C<sub>D</sub> 1.43 on the 0.8 m canopy's area, the capsule's own drag included). Main: the drag area that lands OSIRIS-REx (49.25 kg after ablation) at the 4.5 m/s its team predicted at 1,288 m: 44.14 m² (C<sub>D</sub> 1.05 on the 7.3 m canopy). For another vehicle, and for Genesis (whose own drogue and main are not on file), the canopies are sized, not designed: the drogue at Stardust's ballistic coefficient under its drogue (drag area × m/45.31 kg), the main for the touchdown speed asked at the ground (2mg/(ρv²), ρ at the ground's altitude). Without a drogue a flown capsule tumbles below Mach 0.85 with Genesis's reconstructed C<sub>D</sub> 0.63; above it, idea 48's C<sub>A</sub> table (held at its Mach 7.15 value below).

**Heating** (`heating()`). Convective: idea 48's Sutton & Graves, q = 1.7483e-4 √(ρ/R<sub>n</sub>) V³, cold wall. Radiative: Suttles, Sullivan & Margolis (NASA TN D-7622): q<sub>R</sub> = e^A1 ρ^(A2+A3V) R<sup>(A4+A5V+A6V²)</sup> V<sup>(A7+A8V+A9V²)</sup> in W/cm² (ρ g/cm³, R cm, V km/s; A1 -69.099 ... A9 0.02659), a fit to radiatively coupled, equilibrium, inviscid stagnation-point solutions over 11-16 km/s, 53.75-68.4 km and 30-450 cm, with "an average deviation of about 3 percent" (the sphere form, K<sub>r</sub> = 1). Outside those ranges it is extrapolated, each sample says so, and the share of radiative heat load from extrapolated samples is reported (a refusal past 50 %). Below 11 km/s the extrapolation runs high against Sutton & Hartung's tables (+10 % at 10 km/s, +81 % at 9.5, 3.2 times at 9 km/s for a 2.3 m nose at 60 km), so the extrapolated share is an overestimate, not a neutral guess. Heat loads by trapezoids on the 0.1 s samples, per pass.

**The shield** (`shield()`). Recession: diffusion-limited oxidation of the char, ṁ = B′ q<sub>conv</sub>/h₀ with h₀ = V²/2 + a²/(γ-1) (the free stream's total enthalpy) and B′ = ρ ṡ h₀/q from Stardust's arc-jet averages on 0.266 g/cm³ PICA at 29.5 MJ/kg: 0.2256 at 400 W/cm² and 0.2 atm, 0.2435 at 580 W/cm² and 0.45 atm, mean 0.2346 ± 0.0090. Counted only while the wall is at 1,600 K or more (arc-jet surface temperatures "ranged from 1600–3100 K, which indicates ... diffusion-limited oxidation", Johnston et al.); radiation heats the wall but is not taken to remove char. Recession = ∫ṁ dt / ρ<sub>shield</sub>. Wall: ε(T)σT⁴ = q<sub>conv</sub> + q<sub>rad</sub> with PICA's emissivity as FIAT models it (0.858 at 250 K to 0.93 at 3,500 K, linear) and CODATA's σ: an upper bound (no conduction, blowing or ablation energy). Mass burned: the stagnation-point mass loss × the shield's wetted area (a 60° sphere-cone's cap and frustum for the capsules; the frontal area for the Apollo-derived shape, a lower bound) × 0.6533, the area-weighted mean of Stardust's measured recession (6-6.5 mm at the apex, about 3 mm at the edge, near-linear); 1.0 is shown as the upper bound. Envelope: PICA's "established limits of 1500 W/cm2 heat flux and 1.0 atm pressure", beyond which the recession is refused (the 1,800 W/cm² arc-jet test shows the law under-predicting there). Thickness: Stardust's 5.82 cm, designed for 1,200 W/cm², 0.5 atm and 36.5 kJ/cm² (bondline 116 °C calculated against 250 °C allowed), carries over to an entry inside all three (OSIRIS-REx flew it "with build-to-print thicknesses"); otherwise refused, with the recession as the lower bound.

**Importing idea 48** (`importCatch()`). The envelope, version and units; the choice must end on the ground (aerocapture, a capture burn or the tether are refused: this page flies entries); the entry state's speeds, air-relative speed, altitude and flight-path angle against its vectors; the vehicle against idea 48's shape of that name; the mass against the ledger; then the strong check: flying the state with idea 48's `fly()` at its bank, 0.1 s and 1,500 m/s stop must give idea 48's screened peak deceleration, peak heating, heat load and number of passes to 1e-6.
