Hot Return

Idea 48 chose how the asteroid's material stops at the Earth and handed on the state at the top of the air. This page flies it the rest of the way: every pass through the atmosphere, the heat the air puts into the shield (by the boundary layer and by the glowing shock in front of it), how much of the shield burns away and whether a shield that has flown covers this entry, then the parachutes, opened by a G-switch, timers and a pressure switch as the real capsules did, down to the touchdown.           How this works

          The flight. Every flight on this page is idea 48's own fly(), snapshotted unmodified: a point mass under the Earth's gravity, drag and lift at a constant bank, through the 1976 standard atmosphere (PartForge's isa()) to 86 km above pck00010's flattened Earth, the air turning with the Earth, 0.1 s steps. A skip entry is flown pass by pass with the coast between. Idea 48's handoff is checked by flying its entry state again and matching the loads it screened to one part in a million.

          Heating. Convective: Sutton & Graves (idea 48's). Radiative, the glowing shock layer, which idea 48 left out: the curve fit of Suttles, Sullivan & Margolis (NASA TN D-7622, 1974) to radiatively coupled equilibrium calculations, fitted for 11 to 16 km/s, 53.75 to 68.4 km and nose radii 0.30 to 4.50 m. Outside those ranges it is extrapolated and the page says how much of the radiative heat came from there. It reproduces the paper's own fit for all 91 of its sphere cases to 5e-4 (the fit itself is within 15 % of their calculations, 3 % on average) and Sutton & Hartung's independent tables (NASA TM-102652) to within 11 % inside its range; below 11 km/s it runs high against them (+10 % at 10 km/s, three times at 9 km/s for a 2.3 m nose), so the extrapolated share is an overestimate.

          The shield. PICA, Stardust's and OSIRIS-REx's ablator. Its char burns away at the rate oxygen reaches it: mass loss = B′ qconv/h0, with B′ = 0.235 taken from Stardust's own arc-jet qualification tests (0.0115 cm/s at 400 W/cm², 0.018 cm/s at 580 W/cm², 29.5 MJ/kg), counted while the wall is at 1600 K or more. The wall temperature is radiative equilibrium with PICA's emissivity, an upper bound. Whether a thickness can be named: only if the entry is no harsher than Stardust's design (1,200 W/cm², 36.5 kJ/cm², 0.5 atm), which OSIRIS-REx flew "with build-to-print thicknesses"; beyond it the page refuses, because PICA's in-depth property model is export-controlled.

          Parachutes. A G-switch fires when the deceleration falls back through 3 g; a timer opens the drogue; a pressure switch (or a second timer, whichever comes first) opens the main. No canopy drag coefficient is published for these capsules, so the drag areas come from flight: the drogue's from Stardust's 331.3 s between drogue and main seen on radar, the main's from OSIRIS-REx's predicted 4.5 m/s touchdown. Canopies fill over OSIRIS-REx's filmed times (0.38 s, 1.14 s), the area growing linearly (assumed). For any other vehicle, and for Genesis (whose own canopies are not on file), the canopies are sized: the drogue at Stardust's ballistic coefficient, the main for the touchdown speed you set. Without a drogue the capsule tumbles below Mach 0.85 with Genesis's reconstructed drag coefficient 0.63. A sequence that never opens a parachute (a G-switch that never fires, a timer that runs past the ground, a pressure switch below it) is refused, with the speed it hits at.

          Checked by an independent integrator for the descent from the G-switch (Earth-fixed frame, scipy DOP853, the ambiance atmosphere) and for the heating along this page's hypersonic flight (Simpson's rule), and by the flights in the table above. The flown capsules' entry points are idea 48's stand-ins at their landing targets, so where their flights end is refused. All numbers are SI inside; times are seconds after the entry interface.
