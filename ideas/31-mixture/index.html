    How this works

    The chemistry. Each reactant brings atoms and an enthalpy. At a fixed pressure and that total enthalpy, the products settle where their Gibbs energy is lowest. The page finds that point the way NASA's CEA does (Gordon & McBride, NASA RP-1311, 1994): one Lagrange multiplier per element, Newton steps on the moles of every species and on temperature, CEA's step limiter and convergence tests. Each species' heat capacity, enthalpy and entropy come from its NASA Glenn 9-coefficient fit (McBride, Zehe & Gordon, NASA/TP-2002-211556), copied verbatim from thermo.inp in NASA's own CEA repository (github.com/nasa/cea at 2f79a64, sha256 fa774657…).

    The species. 43 gas species: every C/H/O/N gas product that NASA CEA puts above one part in ten million anywhere in a survey of ten propellant pairs, 1 to 300 bar, chamber to a thousandfold expansion. Solids and liquids are not modelled. Instead, at every answer the page asks whether graphite, liquid water or ice would be stable; if one would, it refuses rather than show a wrong number. For LOX/RP-1 that happens below O/F 1.13 to 1.15 (1 to 300 bar); for LOX/methane below 0.94 to 1.18, under the preset's range.

    The rocket. The chamber is an infinite-area combustor (no flow speed in the chamber). From the chamber, the gas expands at constant entropy, its composition shifting as it cools, until its speed equals the local sound speed: that is the throat. c* is chamber pressure divided by the mass flux there. The equilibrium γs includes the shifting chemistry (RP-1311 eq. 2.71); the frozen γ does not. The closed-form c* with the chamber γs is shown for comparison only.

    Densities. Liquid densities come from fetched primary sources: NIST's fluid tables at the temperature the thermodynamic data assume for the cryogens; NASA SP-125 (Huzel & Huang, 1971) Table 1-4 at 68 °F for the storables and RP-1 (0.80 to 0.82, the midpoint is used); HSDB via PubChem for ethanol. A blend measured as a blend (98 % hydrogen peroxide) uses its measured value; any other blend gets a volume-additive estimate, labelled derived. With no density on file, the page refuses the density and the bulk density, and keeps the rest.

    How far to trust it

    • Against NASA CEA (version 3.3.4, the same data) at 177 points over ten propellant pairs and 1, 20 and 200 bar: with the same 43 gas species, chamber temperature, molar mass and γs agree to better than one part per million and c* to five; letting CEA use all 158 C/H/O/N gas products and graphite moves c* by less than five parts per million. [Certain]
    • Against the 1996 printout of RP-1311's Example 8 (LOX/LH2, O/F 5.55157, 53.3 bar): chamber temperature 0.16 % lower and c* 0.046 % lower. The 1996 run used the thermodynamic data of its day. [Likely, for the cause]
    • Against NASA/TP-2002-211729's theory table for gaseous oxygen and RP-1 at 175 psia: c* 0.21 to 0.61 % lower. The report does not state its oxygen temperature or data version. [Speculative, for the cause]
    • Against a real chamber. NASA Marshall's 1,000-lbf GOX/RP-1 test chamber measured 91.2 to 92.5 % of this page's equilibrium c* (six runs, Tables 11 and 13 of the same report): equilibrium overstated that engine by 8.1 to 9.7 %. Equilibrium c* is an upper bound; a real injector and chamber deliver less, by an efficiency only testing measures. [Certain, for that engine]

    Confidence classes: measured is a value read from a measurement source; derived is computed here from measured inputs; assumed is your input or a stated assumption.
