## What it is

Idea 31, the first of the twenty modules (ideas 31 to 50) of *The Long Way Round*, the series that flies launch to asteroid and back. It is the first link: the propellant and what it turns into in the combustion chamber.

You pick a preset (LOX/LH2, LOX/RP-1, LOX/methane, N2O4 with MMH, UDMH, Aerozine 50 or hydrazine, LOX with 75 % ethanol, 98 % hydrogen peroxide with RP-1, and the gaseous-oxygen/RP-1 pair NASA Marshall hot-fired), or build your own blend on either side from twelve components by mass percent. Then a mixture ratio (slider, typed value, or tap and drag on the chart) and a chamber pressure (logarithmic slider from 1 to 300 bar, or typed from 0.1 to 300 bar; outside that, where nothing was checked, the chamber is refused). The page shows, each with units and a confidence class:

- **c\***, chamber temperature, molar mass, equilibrium γ<sub>s</sub> and frozen γ, equilibrium c<sub>p</sub>, the throat pressure ratio and temperature, and the textbook closed-form c\* beside the real one;
- the **composition** over the species that carry the mixture's elements (9 for LOX/LH2, 28 for C/H/O, all 43 with carbon and nitrogen), as bars;
- **liquid densities** of each side and the **bulk density** of the load at that ratio, from fetched primary sources, or a refusal when no density is on file;
- the stoichiometric O/F and equivalence ratio;
- a **chart across the mixture ratio** of c\*, chamber temperature, molar mass, γ<sub>s</sub>, bulk density or density × c\*, at your pressure and at a tenth of it (so you see dissociation), with the c\* peak ringed, the stoichiometric line, grey bands where the answer is refused, and, for the GOX/RP-1 preset, NASA's six measured c\* values as dots;
- **Import/Export** of the series handoff (copy, download, a `#h=` link to module 32, paste or open a link to import).

Every preset opens at its own computed c\* peak, not at a remembered "typical" ratio, and at an arbitrary 100 bar (the GOX preset at the report's 175 psia; leaving it returns to 100 bar, a pressure you set is kept).

Alternatives weighed. (1) *Closed-form "ideal rocket" with a fixed γ*: one line, but every input (T<sub>c</sub>, M, γ) would have to be typed from memory, which the series forbids, and it cannot say why LOX/LH2 peaks far fuel-rich. (2) *Frozen-composition or one-reaction (water-gas) model*: cheaper, but wrong by percents at 3,500 K where OH, H and O carry real enthalpy. I built (4). Within it I chose to iterate the real sonic throat for c\* (RP-1311 chapter 6) rather than use the closed form with chamber γ, because c\* is what the series hands on and the throat is where CEA defines it; the closed form is shown beside it (it differs by 0.008 to 0.19 % at the presets' opening points; a test pins both ends).

## How it works

**Data.** `snapshots/thermo-excerpt.js` is a verbatim excerpt of NASA Glenn's `thermo.inp` (McBride, Zehe & Gordon, NASA/TP-2002-211556) from NASA's CEA repository, https://github.com/nasa/cea at `2f79a647` (`data/thermo.inp`, sha256 `fa7746572952d74e249e818a82a35c113829742fb421a308e167185528884363`, identical to the file at tag v3.3.4): 46 product records and 10 reactant records, copied line for line by `build-data.mjs`, which refuses any other file. The kernel parses the fixed-column records itself (RP-1311 Part II Appendix A layout) and evaluates c<sub>p</sub>/R, H/RT and S/R from the 9-coefficient fits. Which species: `oracle/cea_oracle.py` ran NASA CEA with every C/H/O/N product it would consider (158 gas species plus graphite, liquid water and ice) over 10 propellant pairs × 25 O/F × 1, 10, 30, 100 and 300 bar, at the chamber, throat and pressure ratios 10, 100 and 1000, and kept the **43 gas species** whose mole fraction ever exceeded 1 × 10⁻⁷ (`oracle/species-survey.json`). The gas constant is CEA's, 8.31451 J/(mol·K) (`cea/constants.py`, "used internally by CEA"; CODATA 2018 is 8.314462618, and the tests show the difference matters at the ppm level).

**Reactants.** Each component's assigned enthalpy comes from its `thermo.inp` record: a fixed value at a fixed temperature for O2(L) 90.17 K, H2(L) 20.27 K, CH4(L) 111.643 K, RP-1, N2O4(L), MMH and UDMH at 298.15 K; a fit evaluated at 298.15 K for hydrazine, ethanol, hydrogen peroxide and water; gaseous oxygen at 298.15 K from the O2 product fit. Element amounts b⁰ (mol/kg) and enthalpy h⁰ (J/kg) follow by mass fraction. Stoichiometric O/F uses RP-1311's valences (C +4, H +1, O −2, N 0).

**Equilibrium.** Gordon & McBride, NASA RP-1311 Part I (1994): unknowns are one Lagrange multiplier π per element, Δln n (total moles), and Δln T for the assigned-enthalpy (HP) and assigned-entropy (SP) problems; species corrections follow from them (eqs 2.18, 2.24, 2.26-2.28). CEA's control factor limits each step (eqs 3.1-3.3), CEA's tests decide convergence (§3.4), and CEA's starting point is used (§3.5). Then the derivatives (∂lnV/∂lnT)<sub>P</sub> and (∂lnV/∂lnP)<sub>T</sub>, equilibrium c<sub>p</sub>, c<sub>v</sub> and γ<sub>s</sub> = −γ/(∂lnV/∂lnP)<sub>T</sub> (eqs 2.50-2.71). I derived the right-hand sides of the two derivative systems by differentiating ln n<sub>j</sub> = Σa<sub>ij</sub>π<sub>i</sub> − μ°<sub>j</sub>/RT + ln n − ln(P/P°) under the element and Σn<sub>j</sub> = n constraints (the OCR of the scanned report is unreadable at that table); the first attempt had the pressure system's last right-hand side as 0 instead of n and gave (∂lnV/∂lnP)<sub>T</sub> = 0, which the tests now catch 13 ways.

**Condensed phases.** Not modelled. At every converged state the kernel computes μ°<sub>c</sub>/RT − Σa<sub>ic</sub>π<sub>i</sub> for graphite, liquid water and ice (RP-1311's test for adding a condensed species); if it is negative the phase would be stable and the result is refused with `out_of_envelope`. For LOX/RP-1 that starts below O/F 1.13 to 1.15 between 1 and 300 bar; for LOX/methane below 0.94 to 1.18 (under the preset's range). Against CEA's boundary (bisected in `oracle/cea-review.json`) the kernel's is within 0.0003 in O/F for RP-1 and 0.0012 for methane, always leaner: it refuses a sliver more than it must, never less.

**Rocket.** Infinite-area combustor: HP equilibrium at p<sub>c</sub>; then SP equilibria at falling pressure, the composition shifting, Newton on x = ln(p<sub>c</sub>/p<sub>t</sub>) with dx = −(u² − a²)/(nRT(γ<sub>s</sub>+1)) until |u² − a²|/u² < 10⁻⁹ (CEA stops at 4 × 10⁻⁵), with u² = 2(h<sub>c</sub> − h<sub>t</sub>) and a² = nRTγ<sub>s</sub>. c\* = p<sub>c</sub>/(ρ<sub>t</sub>u<sub>t</sub>). The c\* peak is a golden-section search on that function. **Pressure envelope:** `rocket()` refuses a chamber pressure outside 0.1 to 300 bar (`Mixture.PC_ENVELOPE`), the span the CEA fixtures cover; past 300 bar even the ideal-gas equation of state CEA and the kernel share is unchecked. A module that has checked more passes `opts.envelope`; `equilibrate()` has no limit, as module 32 expands far below 0.1 bar.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] It reproduces NASA CEA.** Same data, same method, 249 points across ten propellant pairs and 0.1 to 300 bar (177 in the builder's fixture, 72 in the reviewer's, written with a separate call sequence): chamber state to 1 ppm, c\* to 5 ppm. This is verification (the equations are solved right), not validation.
- **[Certain] The species cut is free.** CEA with all 158 C/H/O/N gas species and the condensed ones moves c\* by under 5 ppm inside the presets' envelopes.
- **[Certain] On the one engine I could check, equilibrium overstated c\* by about 8 to 10 %; [Likely] 8.1 to 9.7 % to the decimal.** NASA Marshall's 1,000-lbf GOX/RP-1 research chamber (NASA/TP-2002-211729, Tables 11 and 13, six runs). The decimals rest on two [Likely] assumptions, oxygen at 298.15 K and a 1 atm ambient: oxygen 15 K colder or warmer gives 8.0-9.7 or 8.1-9.8 %; a 14.4 psia ambient changes nothing at this precision (review). The report's Table 9 puts 13.0 ft/s (0.24 %) of uncertainty on one run's c\*, whose mean there is 0.24 % below Table 11's figure for the same run: small against 8-10 %. [Likely] Equilibrium c\* is an upper bound for an adiabatic chamber with complete combustion; real chambers lose heat and mix imperfectly. That is a small research chamber; flight engines are designed for much higher combustion efficiency, so the gap for them is very likely smaller [Likely], but I have no fetched measurement to put a number on it. Engine figures belong to module 33.
- **[Likely] The 0.16 % chamber-temperature gap to the 1996 Example 8 printout is the thermodynamic data.** The 1996 run used the data of its day; this kernel and CEA 3.3.4 agree to 1 ppm on the 2021 file. I did not fetch the 1996 data to prove it.
- **[Speculative] Why NASA's 2002 theory table sits 0.21 to 0.61 % above.** The report states neither its oxygen temperature nor its data version; RP-1's record in today's file is dated 2000 (Mehta et al., AIAA 95-2962). The gap is stated, the cause is a guess.
- **[Likely] Gas-only is the right cut for a chamber.** Graphite forms only far fuel-rich (O/F < 1.15 for LOX/RP-1), where real gas generators run and where equilibrium is not the right model anyway; the kernel refuses there. It is not the right cut for deep nozzle expansions (water condenses, and down a 1000:1 expansion CEA deposits graphite for LOX/RP-1 up to O/F 1.79; the builder's grid had said 1.75): module 32 inherits the refusal, not a model.
- **[Certain] Densities are measured values with stated spreads, at stated temperatures.** RP-1 is a 2.5 % range in its own source and disagrees by up to 2.7 % with NASA's 2002 report; N2O4's DOE PAC entry disagrees with HSDB and SP-125 by 3.7 %. Storable densities are at 293.15 K while their enthalpies are at 298.15 K (the handoff says so). N2O4 boils at 70 °F (SP-125), so at 298.15 K it is liquid only under pressure. Blend densities other than 95 and 98 % peroxide are ideal-mixing estimates; ethanol and water contract on mixing, so the 75 % ethanol estimate is probably low [Likely].
