# Homeward Porkchop
> The return leg Aster does not plan yet, built on Aster's own two-body pipeline: a 200×70 grid of asteroid-to-Earth legs for six bodies with the push to leave, the Earth-arrival v∞ Skyhook would be handed, its declination and entry speed, and a refusal for every delivered mass. Against the 82 distinct round trips JPL's NHATS returns for its five NHATS bodies, Aster's own fidelity tier, keyed on departure, is a safe gate for the departure burn: the 23 legs it supports reproduce NHATS's departure v∞ to 30.5 m/s, 52 of the 57 it flags miss by more (up to 186 km/s), and the arrival-keyed rule Aster uses outbound would refuse 8 legs that reproduce; on the Earth-arrival v∞ the supported legs agree only to 148 m/s.

**Kind:** prototype
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/homeward.test.mjs` (31 tests, Node built-ins, about 2 s; one re-derivation test skips unless `NHATS_DIR` names a fetch) and `node ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/ui-check.mjs` (118 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright, about 8 s; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Data rebuild, none needed to run anything: `node ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/build-data.mjs /path/to/asteroid-mining-planner` (Aster's files), and `bash ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/oracle/fetch-nhats.sh <empty dir>` then `node ideas/21-homeward-porkchop/build-data.mjs --nhats <that dir> --retrieved <date>` (JPL NHATS, network); `NHATS_DIR=<that dir> node --test …` then re-derives the excerpt and its digest from the fetch.
**Made by:** Backsight

## What it is

Aster's porkchop page answers "when do I leave Earth for this asteroid, and what does it cost". Mining is a round trip, and nothing in Aster plans the trip home. This page does, for six bodies: the five JPL NHATS validation targets Aster keeps in its fixtures (1999 AO10, 101955 Bennu, 2000 SG344, 99942 Apophis, 2001 GP2) and 433 Eros.

Pick a body. The plot's x axis is the day you leave the asteroid (2026-01-01 to 2040-12-01), its y axis the flight time to Earth (30 to 720 days), and each of the 14,000 cells is a zero-revolution Lambert leg from the body's two-body position to Earth's JPL Horizons position. Colour it by **Earth-arrival v∞** (the quantity Aster's kernel K2 would hand to Skyhook) or by **departure v∞ at the body** (the burn to leave). Tap, click or arrow-key to a cell and the panel gives:

- the dates and flight time;
- departure v∞ at the body, Earth-arrival v∞, and the arrival asymptote's declination (equatorial, `asin(vz/|v∞|)`, the convention of Aster's DLA);
- the direct-entry speed at 125 km (NHATS's convention, with NHATS's 12 km/s cap flagged), and the propulsive capture into Aster's 200 km circular parking orbit;
- for Apophis and Eros, how far the two-body state of the body is from JPL's integrated truth at the nearest checkpoint;
- **Delivered mass: refused**, as an Aster evidence-envelope refusal (`out_of_envelope`, the reason, and what would help), on every cell.

Cells whose Earth arrival falls after 2040-12-31 are hatched and refused (`insufficient_data`: the committed Earth ephemeris ends there). Cells that leave the body on or after its Slice 18 "L1" encounter are washed, never hidden (DEC-18-7, `SLICE_18_FOUNDING.md:109`). Each minimum is printed with the lattice it came from, says when it sits on an edge of what was searched, and says when a lower value exists only past the L1 boundary. Every NHATS return leg in the fetched sample for the body is a dot on the plot (the minimum-ΔV round trip's is ringed). A table sets the page's numbers beside JPL NHATS's for the return leg inside each target's minimum-ΔV round trip.

Then the question this page exists to answer for Aster: **can K1 ship on the two-body pipeline Aster already has, gated by the fidelity tier it already has?** A chart puts the 82 NHATS return legs of the five bodies (the minimum-ΔV and minimum-duration trip for each of 180 constraint sets, out of 5,506,319 viable trajectories NHATS counts for these bodies) on one axis of time (years from the body's L1 encounter at departure) and one of error (how far this page's departure v∞ is from NHATS's, log scale), coloured by what the tier rule says, ringed where Aster's outbound (arrival-keyed) rule would refuse the leg, with the worst supported leg drawn as a line. Tap a point to read the leg; Enter or a tap shows it on the porkchop. A table counts it per body.

## Why I made it

**The open item.** K1, the return-leg porkchop, is row 3 of `strategy/IDEA_KERNELS_2026-09.md` §1 (line 22) and item 2 of its near-term shortlist (§3, lines 62-65: "highest thesis value per unit cost … any delivered-mass cell must be a structured refusal, not a number … the prerequisite to K2"). `src/v2/SLICE_19_5_FOUNDING.md:33` parks it for Slice 20+, and `STATUS.md:509` lists "the K1 return leg" among the next candidates. Today the only stand-in is the symmetric-return line in the sample-return stack, `departureDvFromVInf` returning the outbound arrival v∞ (`src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:32-34`).

**What a later idea now covers, and what this one does instead.** Idea 47 (*Way Home*, built 2026-11-01, after this one was begun and held) also plans the trip home: one craft's return from the end of its stay, the asteroid flown through the planets by idea 40's n-body integrator, idea 39's Izzo solver, idea 46's propellant paying the burn, and JPL NHATS reproduced to 0.28 m/s (median of 61 legs). It rejects two-body propagation ("two-body Apophis legs are off by 12.3 km/s (median)") and concludes that "K1 needs Slice 22's n-body propagator, or JPL's ephemerides, first". This deposit does not repeat that. It stays on what Aster has today (two-body Kepler from the catalog elements, the Slice 18 tier artifact, Aster's envelope codes, colour scale and lattice label), which is what K1 would be if it shipped before Slice 22, and measures where that is good enough: the answer is "wherever the tier rule, keyed on departure, says it is", over 82 NHATS legs on five bodies, two of which (1999 AO10, 2001 GP2) idea 47 does not carry. It also covers the whole 2026–2040 span for six bodies at once and keeps K1's own requirement that delivered mass is a refusal, where idea 47 prices material from a declared propellant load. The 12.3 km/s two-body Apophis median reproduces here (12.27 km/s), and the 48 NHATS rows the two deposits share are identical, which a test checks.

**Alternatives weighed.**
- *A study of the departure assumption over all 128 NHATS round trips.* Idea 05 (Round-Trip Ledger) already measured it (ratio 0.214 to 11.627); repeating it adds nothing, and a study gives K1 no surface.
- *Transpile `izzo.ts` and run Aster's own solver.* The bank has no build step, `src/v2/core` is protected, and INV-024 forbids porting oracles; a clean-room solver that must reproduce Aster's oracle vectors is the honest substitute, and is what this deposit does.
- *Jump to K2 (catch envelope).* Idea 01 (Tip Match) already takes an Earth-arrival v∞ as typed input; what was missing is the computed one.
- *The proposal as written: six bodies, ~9,000 cells, solver, Kepler, Hermite, refusals.* Built, scoped (see the deviations below), because every input is committed in Aster and JPL's NHATS records give an external answer to reproduce.
- *When finishing after the hold: replace the solver with idea 39's Izzo `lambert.js`.* Not done: this solver already reproduces Aster's own oracle vectors, and the copy would add 67 KB for no change in any number shown. *Fly the body through the planets as idea 47 does.* Not done: that is idea 47's page, and it would stop measuring Aster's pipeline. *Test the tier rule on the five minimum-ΔV legs alone.* Too few (two supported); every NHATS trajectory the query grid returns was fetched instead.

## How it works

**Data from Aster `62ddb17` (2026-10-25) and from JPL NHATS (fetched 2026-11-02), nothing retyped.** `build-data.mjs` copies every source through the bank's `tools/snapshot.mjs`, which writes a provenance sidecar (repo, path, commit, blob, sha256):
- `catalog-six.json`: the six bodies' records from `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-catalog-slice9.json` (elements in J2000 ecliptic, anchor state, epoch 2026-05-01 TDB); `tier-six.json`: their Slice 18 tier records from `docs/tier-sizing-per-body.json`; `nhats/*.json`: the five NHATS fixtures, whole.
- Constants by regex from verbatim copies of Aster's source: GM☉ 1.32712440018e20 m³/s² (`keplerian.ts:9`), obliquity 84,381.448″ (`units.ts:5`), μ⊕ 398,600.4418 km³/s² and the 6,578.137 km parking radius (`delta-v.ts:3-4`), Earth radius 6,378,136.6 m (`bodies.ts:45`), the colormap (`colormap.ts`), the refusal codes (`mcp/src/tools/envelope-schema.ts`), the 125 km / 12 km/s NHATS entry convention (`src/v2/research/slice-11-porkchop/perplexity-jpl-trajectory-browser-nhats.md:42`). A missing match stops the build. Aster's Lambert oracle file, pinned cells, `c3-grid.test.mjs` and `support-boundary.ts` are copied for the tests.
- Two derived excerpts (`--sidecar-only`, the rule in the sidecar note): `earth-weekly.js`, every 7th of the 5,479 daily Earth records in `src/v2/data/horizons-inner-solar-system-2026-2040.json` plus the last (784), with the interpolation error measured against the other 4,695 and the six daily records of the worst interval; `drift-excerpt.json`, half-yearly checkpoints of JPL's integrated Apophis and Eros states from `tests/fixtures/v2/nea-drift-truth-2026-2046.json` (Slice 18's two-body-drift truth).
- `data.js`, the page's bundle, is built from the snapshots alone; a test rebuilds it and requires byte equality.
- `nhats-legs.js`: every distinct trajectory JPL's NHATS API (v1.3) returns for the five NHATS bodies over idea 47's query grid (Δv 6, 8, 12 km/s × duration 360, 450 d × stay 8, 32 d × launch 2025–2030, 2030–2035, 2035–2040: 180 queries, 91 with trajectories), min-ΔV and min-duration, de-duplicated by body and trajectory id: 82 legs, cut to the ten columns a return leg needs, NHATS's values unchanged. They are NHATS's extremes per constraint set, not all its trips: the excerpt also carries NHATS's own count of viable trajectories per body (`n_via_traj`: 47,757 for Bennu to 3,302,685 for 2000 SG344, 5,506,319 in all) and the fetch date. `oracle/fetch-nhats.sh` fetches, `build-data.mjs --nhats` derives and writes the sidecar by hand (API, query pattern, date, digest of the 180 responses, licence), as `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md` "Data and validation" prescribes. The digest is taken over each response as canonical JSON (keys sorted): the API returns the same content with its keys in a different order on every call, so a digest of the raw bytes (what the builder recorded) could never be reproduced. The review re-fetched the grid twice on 2026-11-02; the 82 rows are byte-identical to the builder's, and the canonical digest of each fetch matches the sidecar. The page loads it as a classic script and never touches the network. (Snapshots carry the provenance sidecars' `copiedAt` of the 2026-10-26 transcript replay, not the original build time.)

**The body [Certain arithmetic].** Two-body Kepler from the catalog elements (Newton on Kepler's equation to 1e-14 rad), perifocal to J2000 ecliptic, then one rotation by the obliquity into ICRF, the same chain as Aster's `propagateKeplerianStateVectors`. Propagated to their own epoch, all six land within 0.4 mm and 1e-12 km/s of the catalog's anchor state: the elements were made from the anchor with this chain.

**The frame, by measurement (INV-021).** That match proves consistency, not the frame. The frame is measured three ways in the tests: rotating each anchor back by −ε puts every body within r·sin(i) of the ecliptic, while unrotated 1999 AO10 sits at |z|/r = 0.294 against sin(i) = 0.045; the daily Earth records reach |vz| = 11.897 km/s (11.893 in the weekly ones), impossible in an ecliptic frame; and at 1999 AO10 the equatorial arrival declination hits NHATS's −18.811° to 0.002° where the ecliptic reading is −20.34°, and the launch declination −11.682° against NHATS's −11.685° where ecliptic gives −33.28°.

**Earth [Certain, measured].** Cubic Hermite on the weekly records (positions and velocities at the nodes; velocity is the derivative of the position cubic, as in Aster's `hermite.ts`). Against all 4,695 daily records left out: at most 204.8 km in position (2030-11-25) and 0.994 m/s in velocity (2030-11-27). The proposal's analytic bound (82 km for a circular 1 au orbit) does not hold for Earth. [Speculative: a recalled explanation, not measured here] the geocentre's monthly swing about the Earth–Moon barycentre adds a fourth-derivative term comparable to the orbital one. For a leg the 205 km moves v∞ by ~2e-5 km/s; the 1 m/s goes straight into the arrival v∞, so the third decimal of every displayed v∞ carries ±0.001 km/s.

**Lambert [Certain against the oracles below].** Universal variables (Bate, Mueller & White, *Fundamentals of Astrodynamics*, 1971, ch. 5; the z/Stumpff form and dT/dz of Curtis, *Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students*, Algorithm 5.2), written from my knowledge of the textbook formulation, not from any code and without the books open here [Likely as to the exact chapter and algorithm numbers], zero revolutions, solved by Newton on z inside a bracket that always holds T(lo) < tof ≤ T(hi), with bisection whenever a step leaves it; stops at 1e-14 relative time. Direction follows Aster: `lambertMultiRev(…, lw=true)` passes `lw` as `prograde` (`lambert-multi-rev.ts:282,291`), meaning the transfer's angular momentum has z ≥ 0 in the input frame (ICRF here). 14,000 cells take 60–230 ms in Node on this machine.

**Per cell.** v∞ at departure = |v₁ − v_body(t_dep)|; Earth-arrival v∞ = |v₂ − v_Earth(t_arr)|; declination = asin(v∞z/|v∞|); entry speed = √(v∞² + 2μ⊕/(R⊕ + 125 km)), the escape term being 122.5871 km²/s²; capture = √(v∞² + 2μ⊕/r) − √(μ⊕/r) at r = 6,578.137 km, which a test shows is Aster's own `injectionDvFromC3(v∞²)` executed from the snapshot's source. The body's own gravity is ignored (no mass or density is in the catalog, so the page cannot size that term).

**The support boundary, flipped [Certain].** Aster marks an outbound cell unsupported when it *arrives* on or after the L1 encounter (`support-boundary.ts:49-50`, recorded as OQ-19-4 at `SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:204-208`), because the body's state is used at arrival. On a return leg it is used at *departure*, so here a cell is unsupported when it leaves on or after the encounter: a vertical line. The L1 sentence on the page is Aster's (`tier-disclosure.ts:110-113`) reworded from "Arrivals after it" to departures, and says so.

**Lattice, minima, colour.** Departures run from the first Earth record to the last minus the shortest flight; flights 30–720 d; 200 × 70. The label is derived with the wording of Aster's `formatJdC3GridLabel` (`c3-grid.ts:42-75`): `200×70 grid · 27.4 d × 10 d steps`. Minima are over supported cells; `edgesOf` names the edge a minimum sits on (first or last departure, shortest or longest flight, the last flight inside the ephemeris, the last departure before the encounter). Colour is Aster's porkchop scale, log-viridis over 1–1000 km²/s² (`colormap.ts`), applied to v∞² of the chosen end, so below 1 km/s every cell is the darkest colour (the legend says so). That breaks the dataviz rule of one hue for magnitude on purpose: it is the scale Aster's users already read. The thin bright streaks across every body's plot are legs whose transfer angle is near 180° (or 0°), where the transfer plane is barely defined and a zero-revolution leg must tilt far out of the ecliptic [Certain as measured at review: on Bennu's grid the 54 cells more than three times their flight-time neighbours have a median transfer angle of 175.3°]; the page now says so.

**Every NHATS return leg in the sample, and the tier rule [Certain as measured on these 82 legs].** Each leg is solved exactly as a grid cell is (`auditNhatsLeg`): the body's two-body state at NHATS's departure date (launch + `dur_out` + `dur_at`, midnight TDB), Earth at arrival, the residuals of departure v∞ (`vrel_dep_neo`), Earth-arrival v∞ and declination. On the Earth-arrival v∞, the quantity K1 hands to K2, the rule does not separate the legs as well (measured at review): the 23 supported legs agree only to 0.148 km/s and 0.94°, all of the excess from Bennu's eight 2036 legs (Bennu's six 2030 legs agree to 0.026 km/s, AO10's nine to 0.040 km/s and 0.30°), and 14 of the 57 past legs do no worse than that. So the tier rule is a safe gate for the departure burn; for the arrival v∞ it is safe only to about 150 m/s near Bennu's encounter. Past the boundary the misses run up to 185.7 km/s (2000 SG344's 17-day legs in 2030, aimed from a position the two-body orbit has lost). The five past legs inside the line are Apophis's two that leave in March 2029, after its small 2028-09-12 pass but before the 2029-04-13 flyby, and three AO10 legs 3.9 years after its 15.6 m/s Venus pass: the rule is conservative, never permissive, on this sample. At 200 km instead of 125 km, AO10's entry speed would be 11.8205, 59 m/s off.
