# Burn Out
> Fly the Earth-departure burn as a real engine flies it, minutes long, with thrust on, mass falling, gravity bending the path and a steering law, the ignition time and a small yaw solved so the hyperbola still leaves along idea 37's asymptote. Read the finite-burn loss against the impulsive figure, how it grows as thrust-to-weight falls, what perigee burns buy back, and how much payload the stage can still send. Replayed on Apollo 17's translunar burn from the flight report, the model reaches the flight's C3 0.11 s before the S-IVB's 351.04 s and 34 kg heavier: it needs 3,161.5 m/s where the flight spent 3,163.1 (−0.05 %). On the chain's AS-512 Apophis departure the J-2 loses 7.71 m/s to the finite burn at T/W 0.85, 213 m/s at 0.15, and 28 m/s at 0.15 if the burn is split over three perigees. The stage still falls 10.9 t of propellant short and can send 30.6 t, not the 50.6 t it carries.

## What it is

Idea 38, module 8 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 37 handed on a departure point: where, on a given day, an **impulsive** burn would leave the parking orbit along the departure asymptote, with the Δv that takes. No engine burns impulsively. This module flies the burn. It opens on **idea 37's page default**: the Saturn V AS-512 (ideas 34 to 36), departing for Apophis on 2 July 2026 along Aster's own asymptote. You can:

- **choose the departure**: idea 37's AS-512 to Apophis; **Apollo 17's translunar burn as flown**, with the engine as the flight report reconstructs it (or at the burn's mean thrust, or on the chain's J-2 deck); or **your own** circular parking orbit, stage, engine and C3;
- **move** the payload on the stage, the **thrust-to-weight** (slider, typed, or a tap on the chart), the **steering law** (along velocity, optimised, or fixed attitude as a spin-stabilised kick stage flies), and the number of **perigee burns** (1 to 4);
- **read** the Δv the burn spends against the impulsive figure (the finite-burn or "gravity" loss, in m/s and kg), the propellant needed against the propellant aboard, burn time, ignition before idea 37's point, burn arc, cutoff altitude, the C3 reached at infinity, the yaw that holds the asymptote, the **payload the stage can send** (finite and impulsive), and where the tank runs dry when it is short;
- **see** the loss against thrust-to-weight for one, two and three burns with this burn marked, the burn in the orbit plane (Earth and altitudes to scale) and the steering through the burn;
- **check** the model against Apollo 17's translunar burn, fifteen rows from the flight report, with the steering you chose;
- **hand on** the state at cutoff in J2000 with its TDB epoch, the v-infinity and the losses to module 39, with idea 37's handoff inside.

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 38: "The Earth-departure burn as a finite burn: gravity loss against thrust-to-weight, perigee burns, comparison with the impulsive Δv from C3", taking the parking orbit, the engine and C3, handing on the hyperbolic departure state. Idea 37's README says so directly: "The departure burn is impulsive; module 38 makes it finite", and its handoff's `impulsive_departure` is labelled "module 38 makes it finite".
- **Aster prices the departure impulsively.** `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts` (62ddb17) turns C3 into an injection Δv by vis-viva at a 6,578.137 km parking radius (BRIEFING.md §1). That is exact for an infinitely strong engine and optimistic for a real one. How optimistic depends on the stage, which Aster's delivered-mass card does not know. This page gives the correction as a number per stage, together with the J2 term that the vis-viva formula leaves out (Findings).
- **Idea 37 left two open items here.** First, its yardstick for what the propellant buys "reads Apollo 17's own burn at least 0.9 % low". Second, it could not separate the J2 coast from the translunar burn in Apollo 17's node change. Both are answered below.

Alternatives weighed. (1) *A loss formula*: a closed-form gravity-loss correction (the burn-arc-squared kind). It is quick, but it holds only for short burns, has no ignition time and no asymptote, and gives nothing to check against a flight. It is kept as a limit instead: the tests show the loss falling as 1/(T/W)² at high thrust (ratios 3.99, 4.00, 4.01 per doubling). (2) **Built: integrate the burn.** RK4 with idea 37's gravity, the cutoff found inside the step, the ignition solved against the asymptote. It is cheap enough (one burn, with its coast out to 20 Earth radii, in 0.3 ms in Node) to solve inside optimisers, payload searches and a thrust sweep. (3) *A true optimal-control solver in the page* (primer vector or collocation). It would give the best steering exactly, but it is fragile and slow to run live. It went to the oracle instead, which measured the page's two-parameter law at 0.03 to 0.6 m/s from that optimum. (4) *Re-flying idea 37's chain to check its handoff* (as 37 re-flies 36). That needs ideas 33 to 36's kernels, 307 KB on top of a folder already near the budget. Rejected for consistency checks done with idea 37's own functions (How it works).

## How it works

**What is reused, unmodified.** Idea 37's kernel (`snapshots/window.js`, byte-identical to the committed file, a test). Its point-mass plus J2 gravity (`accel`), its RK4 coast (`rk4`, `propagate`), its Earth orientation and time scales (`orient`, `parseUtc`), its impulsive departure (`departureOf`), its node and AS-512 placement (`nodeOf`, `as512Coast`). Its data: ERFA's IAU 2006 coefficients, NAIF's leap seconds and WGS-84 J2, Aster's constants, the AS-512 transcriptions of ideas 34 to 37. Idea 37's kernel is created with idea 36's *constants* only (Aster's GM and radius, from the same `earth.js` idea 36 reads). The functions used here touch nothing else, and a test checks they return what idea 37's own fully-loaded instance returns, bit for bit.

**Reading idea 37's handoff without re-flying the chain.** The insertion state is turned into idea 37's frame (the mean equator of date at liftoff, from its own precession matrix) and coasted with its own RK4 at its own step. It must land on the handed departure point within 1 m and 1 mm/s; it lands within 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ m. The epochs must agree with the coast. The impulsive Δv is recomputed with `departureOf`. The asymptote must lie in the plane at the departure point (10⁻⁶ deg). The guided stage's engine must sit in idea 34's vehicle with its thrust, flow and exhaust velocity agreeing with each other and with idea 37's capacity. Any failure is refused as edited. A missing departure point (no window, or a C3 ≤ 0 target) is refused as `insufficient_data` with idea 37's own reason.

**The burn** (`fly`). State r, v, m. r̈ = g(r) + (F/m) u, ṁ = −F/vₑ, with g idea 37's (GM 398,600.4418 km³/s², J2 1.08262998905 × 10⁻³ at 6,378.137 km), RK4 at 1 s (the oracle agrees to 10⁻⁹ s). Thrust comes in segments of constant F and ṁ, so a flown engine's start transient and mixture-ratio shift can be replayed. The cutoff is bisected inside the last step to 10⁻¹² s, when the energy reaches C3/2. **Which energy** matters (Findings). This module counts the energy conserved on the coast afterwards, v²/2 − μ/r + μJ₂R²P₂(z/r)/r³. Its gradient is idea 37's J2 acceleration (a test, numerically), and J2 is symmetric about the spin axis, so this energy stays constant after cutoff and the C3 reached is the C3 at infinity in this gravity model. The two-body C3 at cutoff, idea 37's and Aster's convention, is handed on beside it. The Apollo replay uses the two-body C3, because the report's tables do [Likely].

**Steering.** *Along velocity*: u = v̂, the law that adds energy fastest at every instant. *Optimised*: u turned from v̂ toward the local "up" by δ(t) = a + b (t/T − ½), with a and b searched (Nelder–Mead) for least propellant. *Fixed attitude*: u held in one inertial direction in the plane, the angle searched (a 13-point scan, each from a cold start, then golden section round the best; the review added the scan, because on a weak engine most directions never reach the C3 and the golden section alone refused burns that fly). This is how a spin-stabilised kick stage burns.

**Where to light it.** In idea 37's frame the burn starts before the impulsive point and ends after it. The coast after cutoff is followed with idea 37's RK4 and J2, the step growing as r^1.5, out to 20 Earth radii. Following it on to 200 radii turns the asymptote by a further 6 × 10⁻⁵ deg (a test). The osculating hyperbola's asymptote there must lie along idea 37's. Two unknowns, the ignition time and a constant yaw out of the plane, are solved by Newton with a finite-difference Jacobian and Broyden updates. They put the in-plane and out-of-plane misses below 10⁻¹¹ rad. On the chain's departure the yaw is 0.187° and costs 0.01 m/s; it exists because J2 turns the plane during and after the burn.

**The loss.** The Δv spent, Σ vₑ ln(m₀/m_f) over the burn's thrust segments, less the impulsive Δv. That is idea 37's `departureOf` at its departure point, fed the C3 that gives the same conserved energy there (for the free presets, vis-viva from the orbit at ignition). Its propellant equivalent is m₀(1 − e^(−Δv/vₑ)) against the burn's.

**Perigee burns** (along velocity). Burns 1 to N−1 cut off at energies that split the impulsive Δv from the departure point into equal shares. Between burns the coast runs (idea 37's RK4) to the next perigee after an apogee, found by bisection on r·v. Each later burn starts half its rocket-equation duration before that perigee. The first ignition and the last burn's yaw are solved as above. The same sequence is also solved **in plane only** (no yaw): the loss as if idea 37 had aimed the launch for the longer coast. The loss chart uses that version.

**Payload capability.** The payload P at which the burn reaches the C3 just as the tank empties, by secant. Each trial re-solves the ignition and yaw with the steering parameters held at the nominal solution's. The stage's inert mass is idea 37's insertion mass less the propellant and idea 34's payload (16,453 kg for the AS-512, which includes the 1,165 kg idea 34 books for the coast). The impulsive figure is closed form: P = m_p/(e^(Δv/vₑ) − 1) − m_inert. When no payload works (impulsively, or only once the finite-burn loss is counted) there is no capability number: `payload_capability` is refused (before review a finite-burn shortfall showed as "0 kg"). The ascent is not re-flown, so a lighter payload is assumed to arrive with the same propellant: a lower bound.

**Apollo 17's translunar burn** (`snapshots/as512-tli-burn.js`, hand-transcribed by this module from MPR-SAT-FE-73-1, the same PDF as ideas 34 to 37, sha256 8e543894…). Table 4-4's parking orbit, placed as idea 37 places it, is coasted with J2 for 10,843.94 s to the flown ignition (STDV open, 11,556.6 s, Table 2-2 item 88). The engine runs in three segments:
- *Start*, 2.5 s: section 7.6's measured 182,502 lbf·s over Table 16-7's 201 kg from ignition to mainstage.
- *EMR 5.0 phase*: from the valve shift at 11,649.9 s (item 90, cross-checked against its T6 + 671.2 s) at Table 7-2's thrust and flow, 914.59 kN and 218.11 kg/s.
- *EMR 4.5 phase* before it: whatever remains of section 7.7's measured mainstage impulse (69.59 × 10⁶ lbf·s) and Table 16-9's mainstage mass (73,828 kg) over the flown durations. That gives 813.05 kN at 4,192.0 m/s, within 0.03 % of the 5.0 phase's exhaust velocity, as a J-2 near those ratios should be [Likely].

The cutoff is at Table 4-2's C3 (−1.773218 km²/s², two-body). Section 7.8's cutoff impulse (46,260 lbf·s) is then added along the last thrust direction and the coast run 10.01 s to Table 4-5's TLI conditions. The flight's own Δv is the same rocket equation over the flown durations: 3,163.09 m/s.
