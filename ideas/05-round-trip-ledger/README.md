# Round-Trip Ledger
> Aster's sample-return ΔV stack books the burn home as equal to the arrival v∞. The 128 JPL NHATS min-ΔV round trips already sitting in Aster's own test fixtures put that ratio between 0.214 and 11.627 (median 1.049), which moves the cost card's delivered mass by ×0.56 to ×2.32 at Isp 320 s; the same records reproduce Aster's injection formula to 0.5 m/s once the parking orbit is NHATS's 400 km.

**Kind:** study
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). CLI: `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ledger.mjs` prints the study (`example.txt` is that output); `--body "2000 SG344"` prints one body's two ledgers, `--json results.json --svg example.svg` writes the artefacts, `--help` the rest; exit 1 on a failed reconciliation or a refusal, 2 on usage. Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ledger.test.mjs` (15 tests, Node built-ins, about 1 s) and `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ui-check.mjs` (96 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Data rebuild: `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/build-data.mjs /path/to/asteroid-mining-planner`.

## What it is

Aster's porkchop page shows a vehicle-agnostic ΔV stack: injection from C3 at a 200 km parking orbit, rendezvous equal to the arrival v∞, in sample-return mode a departure burn, 150 m/s of stationkeeping, 10 % margin. The departure line is an assumption: `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:32-34` returns the arrival v∞ as the departure Δv, `:43-45` applies it in sample-return mode under the comment "symmetric-return departure burn", and `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:981` discloses it as "departure = arrival v∞ under the symmetric-return assumption". Nothing in Aster can check that line against itself, because Aster computes no return leg (K1 is a CONCEPT kernel).

Aster's test fixtures, however, hold 135 JPL NHATS object records (`tests/fixtures/v2/nhats-validation-targets/`, 5 files, and `tests/fixtures/v2/oq7-nhats-coorbital/`, 130 files), cached in Slice 10 for the Lambert validation (OQ-4) and the co-orbital drift study (OQ-7). 130 of them carry a `min_dv_traj`: the minimum-ΔV round trip NHATS found for that body, with the outbound C3, the parking-orbit departure Δv, the relative speed at the asteroid on arrival **and on departure**, the Earth entry speed, and both asymptote declinations. Five are API error records ("specified NHATS object not found") and two designations appear in both directories, so there are 128 unique bodies. No test in Aster reads any of them.

This deposit is the comparison. A Node script and a page:

- reproduce NHATS's own injection Δv with Aster's `injectionDvFromC3` for all 130 records (to 0.5 m/s, the records' rounding floor, at a 400 km parking radius; the best-fitting radius on a 1 km grid is exactly R_E + 400), so the injection model is right and the two catalogues differ only by parking-altitude convention (Aster's 200 km is 30–48 m/s higher for the same C3);
- measure the symmetric-return assumption against NHATS's `vrel_dep_neo / vrel_arr_neo` over the 128 bodies: min 0.214 (2008 EL68), median 1.049, max 11.627 (2025 WR7); 26 within ±10 % of 1, 35 off by a factor of two or more;
- convert each ratio into what Aster's cost card would do to delivered mass at its screening Isp of 320 s with its 5 % deterministic margin: ×0.556 (1999 AO10) to ×2.321 (459872);
- back out the Earth-arrival v∞ implied by `v_arr_earth` at 125 km (0.262 to 4.345 km/s), the quantity K1 would compute and K2 would hand to Skyhook, with the rounding sensitivity at the low end and the 200 km alternative convention shown;
- classify NHATS's departure declinations with Aster's own launch-site bands (Cape Canaveral: 56 GREEN, 44 AMBER, 28 RED) and show the Earth-arrival declinations beside them;
- show, for any body, Aster's mode-aware stack built from NHATS's C3 and arrival v∞, with the departure line swappable between the symmetric assumption and NHATS's number, next to NHATS's own four-line ledger, the cost-card consequence, the body's row in Aster's screening cache (labelled as a different objective), and a structured refusal for the return leg Aster does not have. Bodies that are not in NHATS get a `not_found` refusal, not a blank.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** The assumption itself at `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:32-34,43-45`, disclosed at `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:981` and recorded as shipped in `src/v2/SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:262,291` (D10 / DEC-19-5). The Slice 11 research note `src/v2/research/slice-11-porkchop/perplexity-jpl-trajectory-browser-nhats.md:68` asks for exactly this: "For regression tests, use the API's object-detail mode on well-known cases like Apophis or 2000 SG344 and compare against the min_dv_traj and min_dur_traj records rather than only the summary minima." No test does: `grep -rn nhats tests/` finds only a URL check at `tests/phase6-contract-smoke.test.mjs:91-94`, and the two fixture directories are named nowhere under `tests/` or `src/` except `src/v2/SLICE_10_FOUNDING.md` and the one-off harnesses `tools/slice10-research/nhats-validation.mjs` and `coorbital-drift.mjs`. Aster's own `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.test.mjs:52-65` pins the symmetric behaviour as correct (`departureKmps === vInfArr`), which is the right test for the code and no test of the assumption.

- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *Build K1, the return-leg porkchop.* The honest fix, but it needs the Lambert solver, the catalog and the Earth ephemeris wired backwards through the protected core (`src/v2/core/`), a multi-agent math audit and a founding-document DEC. Far beyond a deposit, and the proposal's own landing says K1 is what eventually replaces the line.
  - *A mode-aware stack visualiser.* Slice 19 shipped one (the "difference 0" probe in `SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:45`); redrawing it adds nothing. The stack panel here exists only because its departure line can be replaced by data.
  - *Compare Aster's Lambert C3 with NHATS's.* Done already: OQ-4 and OQ-7 (`SLICE_10_FOUNDING.md:290-318, 369-397`) compared departure v∞ for 5 + 36 bodies and set the 0.1 / 2.0 km/s tolerances. The cache row shown per body here is labelled "different objective" and is not graded.
  - *Measure the one line nobody checked, with data already committed.* This won: every input is a committed fixture or a constant in a committed module, the arithmetic is two-body, and the result is a number Aster can put in its own disclosure today.

## How it works

**Data, all from Aster at commit `62ddb17` (2026-10-25), nothing retyped.** `build-data.mjs` runs `tools/snapshot.mjs` once per fixture file (135 runs: `--pick des,fullname,computed,signature,min_dv_traj` for the 130 records; whole copies of the 5 error files), then folds each directory's excerpts and their tool-written sidecars into one bundle, `snapshots/nhats/validation-targets.json` and `snapshots/nhats/oq7-coorbital.json`. Each file entry keeps the tool's `committedBlob`, `sourceSha256`, `sourceBytes`, `writtenSha256`, `writtenBytes` and `readMatchesCommitted`, and its data verbatim: `writtenSha256` still equals sha256 of `JSON.stringify(data, null, 1) + '\n'` (or of the copied text), which a test re-verifies for all 135. The fields that are identical for every file (repo, commit, mode, picked keys, note, `pathLastChanged`) are hoisted, and `readBlob` is omitted where it equals `committedBlob`. The bundle's own sidecar names the directory, its git tree id (`git rev-parse HEAD:<dir>`), the file count and the bundle's sha256; it is written by `build-data.mjs` in `snapshot.mjs`'s schema because the tool has no multi-file mode. (270 separate files would have been equally honest and cost about 1.1 MB of filesystem block overhead.) `snapshots/delta-v.ts`, `launch-vehicles.ts` and `feasibility.ts` are byte-for-byte tool copies with sidecars; `snapshots/lambert-screen-cache.excerpt.json` is the 128 matching rows of the 34 MB cache (seven fields each plus the cache metadata) with a `--sidecar-only` sidecar. `nhats-data.js` is the aggregate of the snapshots as a classic script so the page can read it from `file://`; a test rebuilds it from the snapshots with the same function and requires deep equality.

**Constants** come out of the TypeScript copies by regex (`build-data.mjs` `tsNumber`/`tsSite`, numeric separators stripped): μ = 398,600.4418 km³/s², parking radius 6,578.137 km, stationkeeping 0.150 km/s, margin 0.10 (`delta-v.ts:3-6`); screening Isp 320 s, 5 % deterministic margin, 150 m/s stationkeeping, g0 9.80665 (`launch-vehicles.ts:57-63`); Cape Canaveral iMin 28.5°, ceiling 57°, Vandenberg 70°/90° (`feasibility.ts:29-50`). A missing constant makes the build throw and the kernel refuse `not_found`. The only numbers typed in code are four altitudes in `ledger.js` `CITED`, each with its line: 200 km LEO (`main.ts:981` "200 km circular LEO (r = 6578.137 km)", `INVARIANTS.md:191` INV-016c), NHATS's 400 km parking orbit (research note `:46`), its 125 km entry altitude (`:42`), the Trajectory Browser FAQ's 200 km (`:17`), and the 12 km/s entry cap (`:42`). R_E = 6,578.137 − 200 = 6,378.137 km, the WGS84 equatorial radius (a test pins it), so the NHATS radii are 6,778.137 and 6,503.137 km.

**Injection, reproduced [Certain].** Aster's `injectionDvFromC3(c3, μ, r) = √(c3 + 2μ/r) − √(μ/r)` (`delta-v.ts:18-26`, ported line for line). At r = 6,778.137 km it reproduces NHATS's `dv_dep_park` for all 130 records to a maximum of 0.514 m/s; the records carry three decimals, so 0.5 m/s is the rounding floor. Bennu by hand: √(17.629 + 2μ/6778.137) − √(μ/6778.137) = 3.9608 → 3.961, the record. Scanning the radius from R_E + 100 to R_E + 800 km in 1 km steps, the best fit is R_E + 400 exactly; at Aster's own 6,578.137 km the formula is 30.1 to 48.3 m/s above NHATS. NHATS's own ledger closes: `dv_total − (dv_dep_park + vrel_arr_neo + vrel_dep_neo + vrel_arr_earth)` is at most 1.0 m/s over 130 records, `v_dep_earth = √c3` to 0.9 m/s, and `vrel_arr_earth` is 0.000 in every record (direct entry, no capture burn). So NHATS's total is injection + rendezvous + departure: no stationkeeping, no margin.

**The stack [Certain port].** `asterStack(c3, vInfArr, mode, departureOverride?)` is `buildDeltaVStack` (`delta-v.ts:36-58`) with one addition, an optional departure value in sample-return mode: injection + rendezvous (= v∞_arr) + departure (0 one-way; v∞_arr, or the override) + 0.150 = subtotal; margin = 10 % of the subtotal; total. With the override equal to v∞_arr it is the shipped stack; with NHATS's `vrel_dep_neo` it moves by 1.1 × (dep − arr). The page's stacked bar and rows carry the five lines in the reference palette's first five categorical slots (validated with the dataviz palette script in both modes: all gates pass; three light slots are under 3:1 contrast, so every value is also printed in the row beside its swatch).

**The cost-card consequence [Certain arithmetic on Aster's own rule].** `main.ts:437-451` builds the spacecraft budget as rendezvous + stationkeeping + margin + departure in m/s, with the margin 5 % of the deterministic manoeuvres only (rendezvous, and departure in sample-return), and `deliveredMassKg` (`launch-vehicles.ts:288`) multiplies the payload at C3 by exp(−Δv/(g0·Isp)). Replacing the departure by NHATS's therefore changes delivered mass by exp(−1.05 (dep − arr) × 1000 / (9.80665 × 320)): 1999 AO10 (arr 0.351, dep 2.106) gives ×0.556; 459872 (arr 3.241, dep 0.725) gives ×2.321; the median is ×0.991; 22 bodies are below ×0.9 and 11 above ×1.1. One-way mode has no departure line, so the factor is 1 there and the page says so.

**The spread [Certain over these records].** Ratio `vrel_dep_neo / vrel_arr_neo` over the 128 unique bodies: min 0.214, q1 0.827, median 1.049, q3 1.812, max 11.627; 51 below 1, 76 above, one exactly 1; departure − arrival runs −2.516 to +1.755 km/s, median +0.026. Counting the two duplicated records twice, as the judges did, the median is 1.060. The largest ratio, 2025 WR7's 0.686 / 0.059, is sensitive to the records' third decimal (0.0585 to 0.0595 moves it between 11.5 and 11.7); the page says so. The histogram uses 13 geometric bins from 2^−2.5 to 2^4 so a bin edge sits exactly at 1 and ×2 and ÷2 are the same distance from it; the symmetric assumption is drawn as a reference line.

**Earth arrival [Likely].** The research note says NHATS constrains "Earth atmospheric entry speed ≤ 12 km/s at 125 km altitude" (`:42`); reading `v_arr_earth` as the speed at 125 km and inverting vis-viva gives v∞ = √(v² − 2μ/6503.137). Every record lies above the escape speed there (11.0719 km/s) and under the 12 km/s cap, so nothing is refused: 0.262 km/s (2011 WE) to 4.345 km/s (2014 KF39), median 2.449. The low end is fragile: 2011 WE's 11.075 km/s is 3.1 m/s above escape, and ±0.0005 km/s of rounding moves its v∞ between 0.240 and 0.282 km/s. The Trajectory Browser FAQ's 200 km convention (`:17`, `:78`) would read 1.211 to 4.503 km/s; the page offers both and says the record cannot tell which the API means. A `v_arr_earth` below escape returns an `out_of_envelope` refusal, never NaN.

**Declinations [Likely frame].** `dlaBand` is `feasibility.ts:60-85` ported: GREEN for |δ| ≤ iMin (inclusive), AMBER to the ceiling, RED beyond. NHATS `dec_dep` from the Cape: 56 / 44 / 28; from Vandenberg (polar corridor, ceiling 90°): 115 / 13 / 0. `dec_arr`, the Earth-arrival asymptote, against the Cape bands: 60 / 42 / 26, shown only to give it a scale. The checkout nowhere states the frame of NHATS's declination; Aster's DLA is equatorial by measurement (INV-021), so the comparison holds only if NHATS's is too.

**The page.** `ledger.js` is pure (no DOM, no clock); it loads as `window.Ledger` and with `require`. `app.js` draws everything from it and types no number but histogram bin edges. Histograms are inline SVG from the same `svgHistogram` the CLI uses for `example.svg`, drawn at the mount's real pixel width (labels stay 11 px on a phone) and redrawn on resize; bars keep a 2 px surface gap and a 4 px rounded data end; full-height hit targets on top serve hover, tap (touch keeps the tooltip until the next tap) and keyboard focus. Light and dark follow `prefers-color-scheme` through tokens on `:root` and inside the SVG. The full table scrolls sideways inside its own box on a phone; the page never does. Names from the fixtures are inserted as text, never HTML.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The injection model is right, and the catalogues differ only by parking altitude.** Aster's formula reproduces 130 NHATS departure burns to the rounding floor at NHATS's 400 km, and the data pin that altitude to a kilometre. Aster's 200 km convention (INV-016c) is 30–48 m/s higher for the same C3; both disclose their altitude.
- **[Certain over these 128 records] The symmetric-return assumption is not a small-error approximation.** Half the bodies are within ±25 % of 1, a quarter are off by a factor of two or more, and the direction is not systematic (51 below, 76 above). The median is close to 1, so a population statistic would hide it; a per-body screen cannot.
- **[Certain] The consequence is cost-card scale, not noise.** At Aster's own Isp and margin rule the delivered mass moves by ×0.56 to ×2.32; for 33 of 128 bodies by more than 10 %. The stack total moves by 1.1 × (dep − arr), up to 2.8 km/s.
- **[Likely] `v_arr_earth` is the entry speed at 125 km.** The research note's constraint altitude and the data (every record above escape there, all under the 12 km/s cap) agree, but the API documentation is not in the checkout. The 200 km reading is shown beside it. The implied v∞ values are the first real inputs the K1/K2 interface (and the bank's Tip Match deposit) could take: 0.26–4.35 km/s.
- **[Likely] NHATS's declinations are equatorial.** Not stated anywhere in the checkout; the band counts mean nothing if they are ecliptic.
- **[Speculative] What NHATS's min-ΔV trajectory says about Aster's cheapest cell.** They optimise different things (total round-trip ΔV with ≥ 8-day stays and a 2020–2045 launch window, versus outbound C3 on a 2026–2040 one-way grid), 74 of the 128 NHATS trajectories launch before 2026, and the cache's cheapest window is a different date for every body. The page shows the cache row only as context and never compares totals. Measuring the assumption is sound; using these records to pick Aster's return burn for a given cell is not.
- **Rough.** The NHATS field semantics (which altitude, which frame, what `vrel_arr_earth` means) are read from Aster's research note, not from the API documentation. The records carry three decimals, so ratios of small arrival speeds (2025 WR7's 0.059) are uncertain in their second significant figure. The histogram bin edges are drawing choices. The declination bands apply Aster's launch-site corridor to an arrival asymptote, which is a scale, not a model. `results.json` carries the statistics and seven extreme bodies, not every row (the table is in `example.txt`). The bundle sidecars are written by `build-data.mjs`, not by `tools/snapshot.mjs` (the per-file sidecars inside them are the tool's). No real person has used the page.
- **Fidelity.** An idea-bank prototype. It has not been through Aster's multi-agent math audit, oracle validation, founding-document DEC process or protected-core review; the study reads `src/v2/porkchop/*.ts` and fixtures only and changes nothing in Aster.

**Findings about the source repo** (observed at Aster `62ddb17`; not fixed there):

- **F1. Five of the 130 `oq7-nhats-coorbital` fixtures are API error records**, not trajectories: `2003_YN107.json`, `2006_JY26.json`, `2008_KT.json`, `2012_FC71.json`, `2013_RZ53.json` hold `{"error": "specified NHATS object not found"}`. `src/v2/SLICE_10_FOUNDING.md:370` counts "Not in NHATS: 5", so this is known, but anything that iterates the directory expecting `min_dv_traj` must skip them, and the proposal for this deposit (and two of its three judges) counted 130 bodies.
- **F2. Two designations are duplicated across the fixture directories**, `2000_SG344.json` and `2001_GP2.json`, with value-identical records whose JSON keys are in a different order (and `signature` keys are in two orders across the set). Unique bodies are 128, not 130 or 133.
- **F3. Fixture age.** NHATS object records `computed` 2021-04-19 to 2026-05-24; 74 of the 128 min-ΔV trajectories launch before 2026 and 15 after 2040, so only 39 fall inside Aster's 2026–2040 grid (`SLICE_10_FOUNDING.md:369-371` recorded 36 / 5 / 89 at the time). Fine for validating formulas; stale as an accessibility catalogue.
- **F4. Two parking/entry conventions in one research note.** `perplexity-jpl-trajectory-browser-nhats.md:17,78` give the Trajectory Browser's 200 km injection and 200 km reentry; `:42,46` give NHATS's 125 km entry and 400 km parking orbit. The fixture data settle the parking orbit (400 km) and cannot settle the entry altitude. If Aster ever quotes NHATS beside its stack, the 200 km LEO disclosure at `main.ts:981` should say the two differ by 30–48 m/s.
- **F5. Gap: the frame of NHATS's `dec_dep`/`dec_arr` is stated nowhere in the checkout** (`grep -rn dec_dep` outside the fixtures returns nothing). Any future DLA comparison needs it established, by measurement as INV-021 requires or from the API documentation.
- **F6. The fixtures are referenced only by one-off harnesses.** `tools/slice10-research/nhats-validation.mjs:122-130` and `coorbital-drift.mjs:194-232` read `min_dv_traj`, but nothing under `tests/` does; `tests/v2-coorbital-disclosure.test.mjs` does not reference `oq7-nhats-coorbital` (the proposal said it did). 135 committed files with no test behind them.
- **F7. Coupling to know about.** `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.test.mjs:53` asserts `sampleReturn.departureKmps === vInfArr`; replacing the symmetric line (K1) must change that test and the disclosure string at `main.ts:981`, which `delta-v.test.mjs:73` also pins.
