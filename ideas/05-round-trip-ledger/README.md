# Round-Trip Ledger
> Aster's sample-return ΔV stack books the burn home as equal to the arrival v∞; on the 128 JPL NHATS min-ΔV round trips already in Aster's test fixtures (human-mission trajectories: at most 450 days in total, at least 8 days at the asteroid, launch 2020–2045) that ratio runs 0.214 to 11.627 (median 1.049), which on those trajectories moves the cost card's delivered mass by ×0.56 to ×2.32 at Isp 320 s; the same records reproduce Aster's injection formula to 0.514 m/s, inside their own rounding, once the parking orbit is NHATS's 400 km.

**Kind:** study
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, works from `file://`, no network). CLI: `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ledger.mjs` prints the study (`example.txt` is that output); `--body "2000 SG344"` prints one body's two ledgers (`2000_SG344` and surrounding spaces also work), `--json results.json` writes the statistics, `--svg <file>` the ratio histogram, `--help` the rest; exit 1 on a failed reconciliation or a refusal, 2 on usage (an empty `--body` included). Tests, from the bank root: `node --test ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ledger.test.mjs` (17 tests, Node built-ins, about 2 s) and `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/ui-check.mjs` (103 checks in headless Chromium with the globally installed Playwright; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Data rebuild: `node ideas/05-round-trip-ledger/build-data.mjs /path/to/asteroid-mining-planner`.
**Made by:** Tare

## What it is

Aster's porkchop page shows a vehicle-agnostic ΔV stack: injection from C3 at a 200 km parking orbit, rendezvous equal to the arrival v∞, in sample-return mode a departure burn, 150 m/s of stationkeeping, 10 % margin. The departure line is an assumption: `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:32-34` returns the arrival v∞ as the departure Δv, `:43-45` applies it in sample-return mode under the comment "symmetric-return departure burn", and `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:981` discloses it as "departure = arrival v∞ under the symmetric-return assumption". Nothing in Aster can check that line against itself, because Aster computes no return leg (K1 is a CONCEPT kernel).

Aster's test fixtures, however, hold 135 JPL NHATS object records (`tests/fixtures/v2/nhats-validation-targets/`, 5 files, and `tests/fixtures/v2/oq7-nhats-coorbital/`, 130 files), cached in Slice 10 for the Lambert validation (OQ-4) and the co-orbital drift study (OQ-7). 130 of them carry a `min_dv_traj`: the minimum-ΔV round trip NHATS found for that body, with the outbound C3, the parking-orbit departure Δv, the relative speed at the asteroid on arrival **and on departure**, the Earth entry speed, and both asymptote declinations. Five are API error records ("specified NHATS object not found") and two designations appear in both directories, so there are 128 unique bodies. No test in Aster reads any of them.

These are human-mission round trips under NHATS's own rules, which the records sit against (Aster's research note `src/v2/research/slice-11-porkchop/perplexity-jpl-trajectory-browser-nhats.md`): total duration at most 450 days (`:40`; the records run 202 to 450, and 11 bodies use all 450), stay at least 8 days (`:41`; 101 of the 128 bodies, 103 of the 130 records, stay exactly 8), departure from a 400 km parking orbit (`:46`), entry at most 12 km/s at 125 km (`:42`), launch 2020–2045 (`:31`, `:44`). Every number below is measured on those trajectories. Aster's own cells are different trajectories: for 127 of the 128 bodies the screening cache's cheapest-C3 window arrives at the asteroid faster than NHATS's trajectory (median +2.41 km/s; Bennu 5.215 km/s against NHATS's 1.501).

This deposit is the comparison. A Node script and a page:

- reproduce NHATS's injection Δv with Aster's `injectionDvFromC3` for all 130 records at a 400 km parking radius, worst 0.514 m/s, each inside its own rounding bound (0.523 m/s at most); the best 1 km fit is exactly R_E + 400 km, so the model is right and the catalogues differ only by parking altitude (Aster's 200 km is 30–48 m/s higher);
- measure the symmetric-return assumption against NHATS's `vrel_dep_neo / vrel_arr_neo` over the 128 bodies: min 0.214 (2008 EL68), median 1.049, max 11.627 (2025 WR7); 26 within ±10 % of 1, 54 (42 %) within ±25 %, 35 off by a factor of two or more;
- convert each ratio into what Aster's cost card would do to delivered mass on that NHATS trajectory, at its screening Isp of 320 s with its 5 % deterministic margin: ×0.556 (1999 AO10) to ×2.321 (459872). What it would do at an Aster cell is not measured here;
- back out the Earth-arrival v∞ implied by `v_arr_earth` at 125 km (0.262 to 4.345 km/s), the quantity K1 would compute and K2 would hand to Skyhook, with its rounding sensitivity and the 200 km alternative;
- classify NHATS's departure declinations with Aster's launch-site bands (Cape Canaveral: 56 GREEN, 44 AMBER, 28 RED), Earth-arrival declinations beside them;
- show, for any body, Aster's mode-aware stack from NHATS's C3 and arrival v∞, its departure line swappable between the symmetric assumption and NHATS's number, beside NHATS's four-line ledger, the cost-card consequence, the body's screening-cache row (a different objective, both arrival speeds shown) and a structured refusal for the return leg Aster does not have. Bodies not in NHATS get a `not_found` refusal, not a blank.

## Why I made it

- **The open item.** The assumption itself at `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:32-34,43-45`, set by Slice 11's DEC-7 (`src/v2/SLICE_11_FOUNDING.md:113-117`, "Departure ΔV (sample return) = v∞_arr (matches outbound)"), disclosed at `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:981` and recorded as shipped in `src/v2/SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:262,291` (D10 / DEC-19-5). A prior audit checked it and judged it "disclosed … Not a defect" (`tools/audit/sanity-69472a3/ASTER_AUDIT_REPORT.md:727-733`); this study agrees it is disclosed and asks the next question, how large it is. The Slice 11 research note (`:68`) asks for exactly this: "For regression tests, use the API's object-detail mode on well-known cases like Apophis or 2000 SG344 and compare against the min_dv_traj and min_dur_traj records rather than only the summary minima." No test does: `grep -rn nhats tests/` finds only a URL check at `tests/phase6-contract-smoke.test.mjs:91-94`, and the two fixture directories are read only by the Slice 10 one-off harnesses (F6). Aster's own `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.test.mjs:52-65` pins the symmetric behaviour as correct (`departureKmps === vInfArr`), which is the right test for the code and no test of the assumption.

- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - *Build K1, the return-leg porkchop.* The honest fix, but it needs the Lambert solver, catalog and Earth ephemeris wired backwards through the protected core (`src/v2/core/`), a multi-agent math audit and a founding-document DEC: far beyond a deposit, and K1 is what eventually replaces the line.
  - *A mode-aware stack visualiser.* Slice 19 shipped one (the "difference 0" probe, `SLICE_19_FOUNDING.md:45`). The panel here exists only because its departure line can be replaced by data.
  - *Compare Aster's Lambert C3 with NHATS's.* Done already: OQ-4 and OQ-7 (`SLICE_10_FOUNDING.md:290-318, 369-397`) compared departure v∞ for 5 + 36 bodies and set the 0.1 / 2.0 km/s tolerances. The cache row shown per body here is labelled "different objective" and is not graded.
  - *Measure the one line nobody measured, with data already committed.* This won: every input is a committed fixture or a constant in a committed module, the arithmetic is two-body, and the result is a number Aster can put in its own disclosure today, with NHATS's constraints beside it.

## How it works

**Data, all from Aster at commit `62ddb17` (2026-10-25), nothing retyped.** `build-data.mjs` runs `tools/snapshot.mjs` once per fixture file (135 runs: `--pick des,fullname,computed,signature,min_dv_traj` for the 130 records; whole copies of the 5 error files), then folds each directory's excerpts and their tool-written sidecars into one bundle, `snapshots/nhats/validation-targets.json` and `snapshots/nhats/oq7-coorbital.json`. Each file entry keeps the tool's `committedBlob`, `sourceSha256`, `sourceBytes`, `writtenSha256`, `writtenBytes` and `readMatchesCommitted`, and its data verbatim: `writtenSha256` still equals sha256 of `JSON.stringify(data, null, 1) + '\n'` (or of the copied text), which a test re-verifies for all 135. Fields identical for every file are hoisted, and `readBlob` is omitted where it equals `committedBlob`. The bundle's sidecar (directory, git tree id, file count, sha256) is written by `build-data.mjs` in `snapshot.mjs`'s schema, because the tool has no multi-file mode. `snapshots/delta-v.ts`, `launch-vehicles.ts` and `feasibility.ts` are byte-for-byte tool copies with sidecars. Two derived excerpts carry `--sidecar-only` sidecars: `lambert-screen-cache.excerpt.json` (the 128 matching rows of the 34 MB cache, seven fields each as one array per row, plus the cache metadata with its screening window) and `porkchop-grid.excerpt.json` (the `GRID_PARAMS` block of `src/v2/app/porkchop/main.ts:194-201`, verbatim). `nhats-data.js` is the aggregate of the snapshots as a classic script so the page can read it from `file://`; it stores each trajectory and cache row as an array against one field list, `ledger.js` turns them back into objects, and a test rebuilds the aggregate from the snapshots, requires deep equality and checks every rebuilt trajectory against the bundle's record.

**Constants and typed numbers.** Aster's constants come out of the TypeScript copies by regex (`build-data.mjs` `tsNumber`/`tsSite`): μ = 398,600.4418 km³/s², parking radius 6,578.137 km, stationkeeping 0.150 km/s, margin 0.10 (`delta-v.ts:3-6`); screening Isp 320 s, 5 % deterministic margin, 150 m/s stationkeeping, g0 9.80665 (`launch-vehicles.ts:57-63`); Cape Canaveral iMin 28.5°, ceiling 57°, Vandenberg 70°/90° (`feasibility.ts:29-50`). A missing constant makes the build throw and the kernel refuse. Every window date is read: the screening-cache window 2026-01-01 to 2040-12-31 from the cache metadata, the porkchop grid's departures 2026-01-01 to 2040-01-01 from the `GRID_PARAMS` excerpt. The rounding half-unit, 0.0005 km/s, is read off the records (three decimals; a test changes one record to two decimals and sees it widen to 0.005). Numbers typed in the kernel, CLI and page: the `CITED` values in `ledger.js`, each with its file:line (Aster's 200 km LEO, `main.ts:981` and `INVARIANTS.md:191`; NHATS's 400 km parking orbit `:46`, 125 km entry altitude and 12 km/s cap `:42`, the Trajectory Browser's 200 km `:17`, the 450-day cap `:40`, the 8-day stay `:41`, the 2020–2045 launch window `:31`, `:44`); the `ANALYSIS` choices (±10 % and ±25 % ratio bands, the ×2 factor, the ×0.9 / ×1.1 mass bands, the parking-radius scan R_E + 100 to R_E + 800 km in 1 km steps); the quartile fractions; the five rounded terms in NHATS's sum; histogram bin edges and SVG layout pixels. R_E = 6,578.137 − 200 = 6,378.137 km, the WGS84 equatorial radius (a test pins it), so the NHATS radii are 6,778.137 and 6,503.137 km.

**Injection, reproduced [Certain].** Aster's `injectionDvFromC3(c3, μ, r) = √(c3 + 2μ/r) − √(μ/r)` (`delta-v.ts:18-26`, ported line for line). At r = 6,778.137 km it reproduces NHATS's `dv_dep_park` for all 130 records to a maximum of 0.514 m/s. The records' rounding allows ±0.5 m/s on `dv_dep_park` plus ±0.0005 km²/s² on c3 carried through d(Δv)/d(c3) = 1/(2 v_perigee), about 0.02 m/s, so each record has its own bound (0.523 m/s at most, at the smallest c3) and all 130 are inside theirs. Bennu by hand: √(17.629 + 2μ/6778.137) − √(μ/6778.137) = 3.9608 → 3.961, the record. Scanning the radius from R_E + 100 to R_E + 800 km in 1 km steps, the best fit is R_E + 400 exactly; at Aster's own 6,578.137 km the formula is 30.1 to 48.3 m/s above NHATS, 94× the worst difference at 400 km (48.294 against 0.514 m/s). NHATS's own ledger closes: `dv_total − (dv_dep_park + vrel_arr_neo + vrel_dep_neo + vrel_arr_earth)` is at most 1.0 m/s over 130 records (five rounded numbers allow 2.5), `v_dep_earth = √c3` to 0.9 m/s (inside each record's bound), and `vrel_arr_earth` is 0.000 in every record (direct entry, no capture burn). So NHATS's total is injection + rendezvous + departure: no stationkeeping, no margin.

**The stack [Certain port].** `asterStack(c3, vInfArr, mode, departureOverride?)` is `buildDeltaVStack` (`delta-v.ts:36-58`) with one addition, an optional departure value in sample-return mode: injection + rendezvous (= v∞_arr) + departure (0 one-way; v∞_arr, or the override) + 0.150 = subtotal; margin = 10 % of the subtotal; total. With the override equal to v∞_arr it is the shipped stack; with NHATS's `vrel_dep_neo` it moves by 1.1 × (dep − arr). The stacked bar uses the reference palette's first five categorical slots (validated in both modes; three light slots are under 3:1 contrast, so every value is also printed beside its swatch).

**The cost-card consequence [Certain arithmetic on NHATS's own trajectories].** `main.ts:437-451` builds the spacecraft budget as rendezvous + stationkeeping + margin + departure in m/s, with the margin 5 % of the deterministic manoeuvres only (rendezvous, and departure in sample-return), and `deliveredMassKg` (`launch-vehicles.ts:288`) multiplies the payload at C3 by exp(−Δv/(g0·Isp)). Replacing the departure by NHATS's therefore changes delivered mass by exp(−1.05 (dep − arr) × 1000 / (9.80665 × 320)): 1999 AO10 (arr 0.351, dep 2.106) gives ×0.556; 459872 (arr 3.241, dep 0.725) gives ×2.321; the median is ×0.991; 22 bodies are below ×0.9 and 11 above ×1.1. These are NHATS's duration-capped trajectories; Aster's cheapest cell for the same body arrives faster (median +2.41 km/s), and its own departure v∞ is unknown until K1, so the factor at an Aster cell is not measured. One-way mode has no departure line, so the factor is 1 there and the page says so.

**The spread [Certain over these records].** Ratio `vrel_dep_neo / vrel_arr_neo` over the 128 unique bodies: min 0.214, q1 0.827, median 1.049, q3 1.812, max 11.627; 51 below 1, 76 above, one exactly 1, a lean above 1 that an exact two-sided sign test puts at p ≈ 0.033; departure − arrival runs −2.516 to +1.755 km/s, median +0.026. The bands are closed intervals (`withinOf1`: a ratio of exactly 1.1 counts as within ±10 %, which `|r − 1| ≤ 0.1` gets wrong in floating point). Counting the two duplicated records twice, as the judges did, the median is 1.060. The largest ratio, 2025 WR7's 0.686 / 0.059, is sensitive to the records' third decimal (0.0585 to 0.0595 moves it between 11.5 and 11.7); the page says so. The histogram uses 13 geometric bins from 2^−2.5 to 2^4 so a bin edge sits exactly at 1 and ×2 and ÷2 are the same distance from it; the symmetric assumption is drawn as a reference line.

**Earth arrival [Likely].** The research note says NHATS constrains "Earth atmospheric entry speed ≤ 12 km/s at 125 km altitude" (`:42`); reading `v_arr_earth` as the speed at 125 km and inverting vis-viva gives v∞ = √(v² − 2μ/6503.137). Every record lies above the escape speed there (11.0719 km/s) and under the 12 km/s cap, so nothing is refused: 0.262 km/s (2011 WE) to 4.345 km/s (2014 KF39), median 2.449. The low end is fragile: 2011 WE's 11.075 km/s is 3.1 m/s above escape, and ±0.0005 km/s of rounding moves its v∞ between 0.240 and 0.282 km/s. The Trajectory Browser FAQ's 200 km convention (`:17`, `:78`) would read 1.211 to 4.503 km/s; the page offers both. Which altitude the API means is not settled in this deposit; the two-body check described under Honest assessment sides with 125 km. A `v_arr_earth` below escape returns an `out_of_envelope` refusal, never NaN.

**Declinations [Likely frame].** `dlaBand` is `feasibility.ts:60-85` ported: GREEN for |δ| ≤ iMin (inclusive), AMBER to the site's `dlaCeilingDeg`, RED beyond. NHATS `dec_dep` from the Cape: 56 / 44 / 28; from Vandenberg (polar corridor, ceiling 90°): 115 / 13 / 0. `dec_arr`, the Earth-arrival asymptote, against the Cape bands: 60 / 42 / 26, shown only to give it a scale. The checkout nowhere states the frame of NHATS's declination; Aster's DLA is equatorial by measurement (INV-021), so the comparison holds only if NHATS's is too. Not settled here; the same two-body check sides with equatorial.

**The page.** `ledger.js` is pure (no DOM, no clock) and loads as `window.Ledger` or with `require`. `app.js` draws everything from it and types no number but bin edges and layout; the constraints note under the tiles is built from the records and `CITED`. Histograms are inline SVG from the same `svgHistogram` the CLI's `--svg` uses, drawn at the mount's pixel width (11 px labels on a phone) and redrawn on resize, with band labels in their own row above the plot and full-height hit targets for hover, tap and keyboard focus. Definition lists give labels 12 rem to 38 % at desktop width and stack on a phone. Light and dark follow `prefers-color-scheme` via tokens on `:root` and in the SVG. Only the full table scrolls sideways, in its own box. Names from the fixtures are inserted as text, never HTML.

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The injection model is right, and the catalogues differ only by parking altitude.** Aster's formula reproduces 130 NHATS departure burns inside each record's rounding bound at NHATS's 400 km, and the data pin that altitude to a kilometre. Aster's 200 km convention (INV-016c) is 30–48 m/s higher for the same C3; both disclose their altitude.
- **[Certain over these 128 NHATS trajectories] The symmetric-return assumption is not a small-error approximation there.** 54 of the 128 bodies (42 %) are within ±25 % of 1, 35 are off by a factor of two or more, and the direction leans above 1 (76 against 51, sign-test p ≈ 0.033) while the median is 1.049. A population statistic would hide that; a per-body screen cannot.
- **[Certain arithmetic on NHATS's own trajectories] The consequence there is cost-card scale, not noise.** At Aster's own Isp and margin rule the delivered mass moves by ×0.56 to ×2.32; for 33 of 128 bodies by more than 10 %. The stack total moves by 1.1 × (dep − arr), up to 2.8 km/s. These trajectories are NHATS's: at most 450 days in total (11 bodies use all of it), at least 8 days at the asteroid (101 of 128 sit on the minimum), a 400 km parking orbit, entry at most 12 km/s at 125 km, launch 2020–2045.
- **[Speculative] The factor at an Aster cell.** Not measured.
