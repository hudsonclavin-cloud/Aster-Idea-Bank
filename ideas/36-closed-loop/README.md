# Closed Loop
> Choose an orbit and fly idea 35's rocket to it with the Space Shuttle's Unified Powered Flight Guidance, beside open-loop pitch programmes, then shake both with Monte Carlo dispersions sized from Apollo 17's flight report. Guided to the parking orbit Apollo 17 reached, the chain's Saturn V gets there within 6 mm and burns 32,897 kg of S-IVB propellant where the flight burned 30,006 (+9.6 %), leaving 2,891 kg less for the translunar burn; UPFG comes within 0.96 kg of an independent optimiser's best and within 0.008 s of the flat-Earth linear-tangent optimum; at AS-512-sized dispersions the closed loop hits the target in 100 of 100 flights and the same steering replayed open loop reaches orbit in 66.

**Kind:** explorable tool
**For:** Aster
**Run:** open `ideas/36-closed-loop/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, `file://` works, no network; it also opens on idea 35's `#h=` link). Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/36-closed-loop/closedloop.test.mjs` (18 tests, about 8 s) and `node ideas/36-closed-loop/ui-check.mjs` (74 checks in headless Chromium; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Regeneration, none needed to run anything: `node ideas/36-closed-loop/build-data.mjs`, `node ideas/36-closed-loop/oracle/build-cases.mjs`, `python3 -I ideas/36-closed-loop/oracle/guidance_oracle.py` (numpy and scipy in a throw-away venv; about 20 minutes), `node ideas/36-closed-loop/build-sample.mjs`.
**Made by:** Lambda Dot

## What it is

Idea 36, module 6 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 35 flew a rocket out of the atmosphere open loop and said so: its Saturn V ends 183 m/s short of the orbit the real one reached. This module closes the loop.

It opens on **Saturn V AS-512 (Apollo 17)**: idea 34's vehicle, idea 35's first stage exactly as idea 35 flies it (F-1 at the flight-average flow, the kick fitted to the S-IC cutoff angle), and guidance from 42.9 s after S-IC cutoff, when the flight's iterative guidance began, aimed at the parking orbit the flight reached (166.6 x 167.2 km at 28.526 deg, Table 4-4). You can:

- **choose the target**: perigee, apogee, inclination and whether the plane crosses the pad heading north or south of east (presets: Apollo 17's orbit, Aster's 6,578.137 km parking radius); a target the vehicle cannot reach, or an inclination below the launch latitude, is refused with the shortfall;
- **change the guidance**: when it starts and how often it re-solves (0.5 to 4 s), and the first-stage kick it inherits (to see a lofted or flattened first stage recovered, or not);
- **read** the orbit reached, how far from the target at cutoff (radius, speed, flight-path angle, plane), the guided burn and the propellant left for later burns, idea 35's open-loop programme on the same plan, and the climb (altitude, thrust pitch and yaw, speed, guidance's time-to-go against the time actually left) with Apollo 17's cutoffs on top;
- **shake it**: 50 to 400 Monte Carlo flights, each flown closed loop and open loop (the nominal guided flight's attitude history replayed on the clock with a timer cutoff) on the same draws of thrust and flow, specific impulse, propellant and dry mass per stage, and air density; at AS-512's own deviations from its prediction or scaled to MSFC's pre-flight S-IC band; every sigma editable. Read the orbit scatter, the propellant-left histogram and the reserve it calls for;
- **see what the reserve pays for**: each dispersion alone at minus and plus one sigma, two-sided, so the ones that cost propellant whichever way they go show;
- set the guided AS-512 **against Apollo 17** (16 rows) and **hand on** the parking orbit, the insertion state and the propellant left for idea 38's departure burn.

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 36: "Ascent guidance to a target orbit (linear-tangent / powered explicit guidance) against open-loop pitch programmes, with Monte Carlo dispersions", taking the vehicle and a target orbit, handing on the parking orbit achieved with its scatter. Idea 35's handoff ends with the refusal this module answers (`applies_to: 'guidance'`: "Open-loop steering: the orbit reached is what this pitch plan gives, not a target orbit ... Module 36 (Closed Loop) flies the same vehicle to a chosen orbit with guidance"), and its README names the number to close: the open-loop S-IVB cutoff 182.8 m/s short.
- **Aster plans from a parking orbit it assumes.** `src/v2/porkchop/delta-v.ts:4` at `62ddb17`: `LEO_PARKING_RADIUS_KM = 6_578.137`, from which every injection delta-v in the porkchop is computed. This module flies a real vehicle to that orbit, says what is left in the tank when it gets there, and how much of that a guided vehicle must hold back for dispersions; or refuses when it cannot get there.

Alternatives weighed. (1) *The Saturn V's own Iterative Guidance Mode*: the right law for AS-512, but its equations live in MSFC documents (the Astrionics System Handbook, the guidance equation reports) that I did not fetch: UPFG's primary source was on NTRS and readable, and the flight report gives IGM only a start time and an accuracy. (2) *A numerical predictor-corrector* (shoot the remaining flight each cycle): the most accurate, but (my estimate, not measured) tens of times slower, which would have cut the Monte Carlo to a handful of flights. (3) **Built:** UPFG, the Shuttle's guidance, transcribed from its primary source (NTRS 19740004402) and checked equation by equation; linear-tangent like IGM, explicit and cheap (a whole guided AS-512 ascent, first stage included, 239 guidance cycles, flies in about 13 ms), so 100 dispersed flights, each flown twice, take about a second. (4) *Open loop as "idea 35's programme only"*: it does not reach orbit for AS-512, so under dispersions it would say nothing; replaying the guided flight's own attitude history on the clock is the fair open-loop twin, the same steering without the feedback.

## How it works

**What is reused, unmodified.** Idea 35's kernel (`snapshots/ascent.js`, byte-identical to the committed file, a test) flies the first stage (`fly(..., {burns: 1})`), and supplies the vehicle import (`vehicleFromHandoff`, `parseHandoff`, which re-check ideas 34 and 33), the equations of motion (`deriv`: point-mass gravity on Aster's GM over a rotating sphere, the 1976 atmosphere, thrust less p<sub>a</sub>A<sub>e</sub>, Walker's drag), the RK4 integrator (`integrate`), `orbitOf`, `stateOf`, `launchPlane`, its solvers and `makeHandoff`. Idea 34's ledger (`snapshots/stack.js`) gives the masses. Nothing physical is re-implemented.

**The upper-stage loop** (`flyClosed`). Idea 35's `fly()` loop, re-walked so that a guidance cycle can change the thrust direction and a guided cutoff can end a burn: stage drops, staging coasts, shed mass, jettisons and engine cutoffs on each burn's clock, RK4 steps landing on every event, the nozzle-separation, crash and fall-back checks after every step. With idea 35's own upper-stage programme it reproduces `fly()` **to the last bit** (the linear and hold programmes on AS-512 and idea 35's committed sample; and the same nozzle-separation refusal for AS-512's upper stages in a gravity turn), which is the evidence that the walk is idea 35's. Two checks are this module's and apply only to guided and replayed flights: falling 10 km below the first-stage cutoff height ends the flight as "falls back", and so does re-entering the atmosphere (idea 35 flies on through a re-entry and reports it at the end, and in idea 35's mode so does this loop). The last ascent burn may use its whole stage's propellant when guided (the S-IVB's 30,006 + 73,828 kg on AS-512); what it does not burn is handed on to the later burns.

**Holding a command.** Each cycle's commanded inertial thrust direction u is flown through idea 35's `'linear'` steering mode in the plane through Earth's centre containing r and u, with the pitch above the local horizontal turned down at the rate the local vertical sweeps through that plane, (v . down)/|r|, so u stays fixed in space to first order. Between first-stage cutoff and the start of guidance the attitude at cutoff is held (the Saturn V's "tilt arrest").

**Guidance: UPFG standard ascent** (Brand, Brown and Higgins, Draper Laboratory, June 1973; NTRS 19740004402, sha256 `290a9607...75d715d0`; the equations used are transcribed with page numbers in `snapshots/upfg-1973.txt`). Target: cutoff radius r<sub>d</sub>, speed v<sub>d</sub>, flight-path angle 0 (insertion at perigee) and the plane, as UPFG's i<sub>y</sub>, the negative of the orbit normal. Each cycle (`upfgPass`):
1. v<sub>go</sub> -= the sensed velocity change since the last cycle (Δv minus gravity's trapezoid over the cycle).
2. Time-to-go (4.3): the remaining phases from the current burn on, each constant-thrust with τ = m/ṁ, the current one's τ from the sensed thrust acceleration (v<sub>ex</sub>/a); |v<sub>go</sub>| is spent phase by phase and the last is cut at τ(1 - e<sup>-L/v<sub>ex</sub></sup>). Phases come from idea 34's ledger: on AS-512, S-II with five engines to its centre-engine cutoff, four to depletion, the 5.56 s staging coast, the S-IVB to its ullage-case jettison and on.
3. Thrust integrals L, J, S, Q, H, P (4.4), computed as moments about each phase's start shifted to the horizon; a test shows they equal both quadrature of their definitions and the document's two-step accumulation (in which the printed sign of one term is blurred; the definitions fix it).
4. Turning rate (4.5): λ = unit(v<sub>go</sub>); r<sub>go</sub> = r<sub>d</sub> - (r + v t<sub>go</sub> + r<sub>grav</sub>) with its downrange component freed so that λ . r<sub>go</sub> = S; λ̇ = (r<sub>go</sub> - Sλ)/(Q - SJ/L); the command i<sub>F</sub> = unit(λ - (J/L)λ̇); the thrust-rotation biases from φ = acos(i<sub>F</sub> . λ).
5. Gravity (4.7) along the "special coasting trajectory" r<sub>c1</sub> = r - r<sub>thrust</sub>/10 - v<sub>thrust</sub>t<sub>go</sub>/30, v<sub>c1</sub> = v + (6/5) r<sub>thrust</sub>/t<sub>go</sub> - v<sub>thrust</sub>/10, propagated for t<sub>go</sub> by idea 35's integrator coasting (steps of at most 30 s): its "Kepler routine". The coefficients are derived in a test.
6. Desired state (4.8): r<sub>d</sub> along the predicted cutoff position, v<sub>d</sub> at γ<sub>d</sub> in the plane; v<sub>go</sub> = v<sub>d</sub> - v - v<sub>grav</sub> + v<sub>bias</sub>.
The first call iterates to convergence (UPFG's "prethrust"); later cycles make one pass. **This module's additions**, not in the document: the predicted cutoff position is projected onto the target plane before r<sub>d</sub> is taken from it (Figure 5-9a takes r<sub>d</sub> along r<sub>p</sub> as it is, which leaves the out-of-plane position untargeted); the steering freezes once the cutoff is under two cycles away; the engine stops when the orbital energy reaches the target's (a velocity cutoff, as AS-512's S-IVB did: "terminated by a guidance velocity cutoff command", section 7.3), found by bisection inside the step; if guidance finds the target out of reach in flight it holds attitude to depletion (a real vehicle would retarget or abort), and if it is out of reach when guidance starts the flight is refused with the shortfall. **What guidance knows:** the true position, velocity and mass and the propellant left in the burning stage (perfect navigation and gauging), the sensed thrust acceleration, and idea 34's nominal model for everything after the current burn.

**Target geometry** (`targetOrbit`). Insertion at perigee of the orbit with the perigee and apogee heights asked, r<sub>d</sub> = R + h<sub>p</sub>, v<sub>d</sub> from vis-viva on Aster's GM. The plane passes over the pad at liftoff: sin β = cos i / cos φ, β the inertial azimuth at the pad, north or south branch; refused when |cos i| > cos φ. Idea 35's launch azimuth is kept (for AS-512 the flight's 91.504 deg); guidance yaws the difference out. The page also shows the azimuth over the ground that heading needs at insertion speed, atan2(v sin β - ωR cos φ, v cos β).

**Open loop.** (a) Idea 35's `fly()` with its own plan (for AS-512, the linear programme solved for a level burnout): unchanged. (b) Under dispersions, `replayLaw`: the nominal guided flight's commands on the clock and a timer at the nominal cutoff, flown by the same loop (undispersed it lands exactly on the guided state; the test allows 1 mm).

**Dispersions** (`dispersionTable`, `disperse`, `monteCarlo`). Per stage: thrust and flow together (idea 35's flow scale), specific impulse (vacuum thrust and its deck at fixed flow, a per-stage copy of the engine), propellant (every burn), dry mass; and air density as a factor on drag. Default one-sigma sizes are how far AS-512 itself strayed from MSFC's prediction (`snapshots/as512-guidance.js`, transcribed from MPR-SAT-FE-73-1): S-IC flow 0.16 %, Isp 0.14 %, propellant 0.14 % (section 5.1); S-II flow 0.19 %, Isp 0.05 % (6.3), mass at ignition 0.11 % (16.2); S-IVB flow 0.527 % and Isp 0.14 % (Table 7-1), LOX load 0.027 % (7.9); dry masses 0.9 % (16.2); density 4 % (A.5.3). A vehicle with other than three stages takes the S-IC's for its first, the S-IVB's for its last and the S-II's between. One flight is one draw, so these are **assumed**. The alternative scale (`msfcScale`): the factor, 4.089, that makes three standard deviations of the S-IC burn time (from idea 34's ledger by central differences on the flow and propellant rows, σ = 0.416 s at 1x) equal the mean half-width of MSFC's pre-flight band, (5.99 + 4.22)/2 s (section 5.1). Normal draws by Box-Muller from a seeded mulberry32. Each flight flies the first stage once (`fly`, burns 1) and the upper stages twice. The guided reserve is three standard deviations of the propellant left, assuming normality; with 100 flights the 0.135 % tail is not measured, and the page says so.

**Sensitivities** (`sensitivities`). Each dispersion alone at -1σ and +1σ, the change in propellant left (and in the open loop's perigee), and the root-sum-square of the half-differences (a linear covariance).

**The AS-512 transcription** (`snapshots/as512-guidance.js`), from the PDF ideas 34 and 35 used (same sha256), read as page images where the text layer is unreliable: Table 2-2 items 36 to 41 (IGM phase 1 at 204.1 s range time, 42.9 s into time base 3, which starts at S-IC outboard cutoff), the S-IVB first burn (138.8 s, a guidance velocity cutoff, 3.7 s shorter than predicted; ECO 702.65 s), section 9.3 (at first S-IVB cutoff the LVDC's velocity was 0.20 m/s below and its radius 30.8 m above the operational trajectory's), and the deviations above. Tests check every figure that can be cross-checked: lbf against percent, ft against m, the time-base arithmetic against idea 35's Table 4-2.

**Handoff schema (version 1, `from: "36-closed-loop"`).** The series envelope; `handoff.json` is the sample: idea 35's committed sample handoff (idea 34's sample stack), guided to Aster's parking orbit at 28.5 deg, with a 100-flight Monte Carlo at AS-512-sized dispersions (seed 1): 199.911 x 200.089 km at 28.5000 deg, 4,930 kg left in stage 2, 100 of 100 closed-loop flights on target, 65 of 100 open-loop flights in orbit (every choice assumed, to show the chain).

## Tests

`closedloop.test.mjs`, 18 tests, Node built-ins, about 8 s:

- **Snapshots.** Idea 35's kernel and every data file it needs are byte-identical to the committed files in `ideas/35-max-q`; every sidecar's sha256 matches; the parking radius is parsed from Aster's text.
- **Transcription.** IGM's start minus its time-from-base is Table 4-2's S-IC cutoff (idea 35's independent transcription); the S-II cutoffs agree between the two tables; every Table 7-1 deviation is reconstructed from its predicted and reconstructed columns, in pounds and in percent; the S-II thrust percent from its pounds; 0.66 ft/s and 101.0 ft against 0.20 m/s and 30.8 m; the S-IVB burn's -4.1 + 0.4 = -3.7 s.
- **UPFG against its document.** The thrust integrals against midpoint quadrature of their definitions (200,000 points, three phases with a coast) and against section 4.4's two-step accumulation transcribed in the test; the shortfall when the phases run out. The coasting trajectory's coefficients (1/10, 1/30, 6/5, 1/10) make its mean and time-weighted position error against a cubic powered path zero.
- **The loop is idea 35's.** On AS-512 with idea 35's linear and hold programmes, and on idea 35's committed sample, `flyClosed` reproduces `fly()`'s final position, velocity, mass, losses and every cutoff time exactly (`deepEqual` on the vectors); with a gravity-turn upper stage both refuse (the J-2 nozzle separates as the stage sinks).
- **Oracle, closed form** (`oracle/guidance_oracle.py`, output `oracle/guidance-oracle.json`, inputs `oracle/cases.json`). Over a flat Earth in uniform gravity (a 10<sup>12</sup> m sphere, idea 35's own kernel built over it), the minimum-time burn to an altitude with no vertical speed and a given horizontal speed has a thrust direction (1, b - ct) normalised: the linear tangent, from the optimality conditions. The Python oracle solves it by shooting (scipy DOP853 at rtol 10<sup>-12</sup>): 54.098 s, from 40.09 deg pitch to 4.94 deg. A direct optimisation over a 24-node piecewise-linear pitch with no form assumed must not beat it, and does not (54.264 s; SLSQP stops there, rank-deficient). The kernel's UPFG on the same burn: cutoff at T* + 0.0076 s with a 2 s cycle and + 0.0016 s with 0.5 s (tolerances 0.012 and 0.004 s), altitude within 0.03 m and vertical speed within 0.05 m/s of the target; its first command is 40.84 deg against the optimum's 40.09 (UPFG's small-angle bias terms; later cycles correct it; tolerance 1 deg). A **steep** case (climb 20 km in the same time: the optimum sweeps 125 deg) is outside what UPFG's small-angle bias terms carry; the oracle solves it (71.722 s) and the test asserts the kernel does not claim success on it.
- **Oracle, replay.** The guided AS-512 upper stages from the start of guidance, re-flown in Python on the kernel's commands with the oracle's own integrator, event schedule and mass bookkeeping from idea 34's vehicle data: measured agreement: S-II depletion time 5 × 10<sup>-12</sup> s, cutoff position 2.3 µm, velocity 1.9 × 10<sup>-6</sup> m/s, mass 10<sup>-9</sup> kg, and the oracle's mass after S-II separation equals the kernel's (tolerances about 40 times the measured: 10<sup>-4</sup> m, 10<sup>-5</sup> m/s, 10<sup>-6</sup> kg). Its cutoff energy is the target's to 5 × 10<sup>-4</sup> m<sup>2</sup>/s<sup>2</sup>.
- **Oracle, optimum.** The least S-IVB propellant that reaches the same target from the same state with the same stages, by SLSQP over piecewise-linear inertial pitch and yaw on K = 8, 16 and 24 nodes (each warm-started from the last; SLSQP stops at its iteration limit with the constraints met to 10<sup>-11</sup>): 32,896.041, 32,896.039 and 32,896.039 kg. ## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The guidance law is UPFG and it works.** Transcribed from the primary source and tested against it; over a flat Earth it lands on the true optimum (0.008 s); on AS-512 an independent integrator re-flying its commands agrees to millimetres and a direct optimiser beats it by only 0.96 kg of 32,897.
- **[Certain as numbers] Guided to Apollo 17's parking orbit** (all gaps model - flight; MPR-SAT-FE-73-1):
  - S-II cutoff 3.6 km low (169.0 against 172.6 km), 63.5 m/s slow (6,926.6 against 6,990.1), flight-path angle 0.088 against 0.254 deg, 11.4 s early (548.3 against 559.66 s range time). Guidance shapes this: it is a prediction.

- **[Certain as numbers] UPFG has an envelope.** Its thrust-rotation biases are small-angle expansions. - **What is rough or cut.** *Navigation:* perfect (refusal `navigation`): no platform drift, no accelerometer error. *Guidance knowledge:* the true mass and the burning stage's propellant are known (perfect gauging); later stages from the nominal model. *Steering:* the command is held fixed in space between cycles (zero-order hold), no attitude dynamics, no control lag. *IGM itself:* not built (UPFG is its descendant, not it); no "chi-tilde" terminal mode, no artificial tau through the mixture-ratio shift (there is no shift in idea 33's J-2). *Cutoff:* exact (bisection on energy), no thrust tail-off impulse. *Dispersions:* normal, independent, no winds (idea 35 has none), no mixture-ratio or engine-out cases. *Not built:* the launch window and node in J2000 (module 37), the departure burn (38), a reserve from the 0.135 % tail itself (it needs thousands of flights; the page offers 400). *Size:* 560 KB as the checker counts (672 KB by `du -sk`, which rounds each of the 40 files up to whole disk blocks), over its 500 KB budget and under its 650 KB limit: idea 35's kernel and data are 220 KB of it, and the sample handoff carries idea 35's whole (51 KB) as the series asks.
