Closed Loop

Choose an orbit and let the rocket aim for it. Above the air the upper stages are flown by the Space Shuttle's Unified Powered Flight Guidance, which re-solves a linear-tangent steering law every two seconds from where the vehicle is and what its engines are doing, and cuts the engine when the orbit's energy is reached. Set it beside open-loop pitch programmes, then shake the rocket: thrust, flow, propellant, dry mass and air density drawn at random, the closed loop and the open loop flown through the same dispersions. It opens on the Saturn V that flew Apollo 17, aiming for the parking orbit that flight reached.

    Each flight draws every dispersion below from a normal distribution, flies the first stage open loop (idea 35), then flies the upper stages twice on the same draws: closed loop (guidance on a nominal model of the vehicle, sensing only its own acceleration) and open loop (the nominal guided flight's attitude history replayed on the clock, cut off by a timer). Reproducible from the seed.

      The dispersions (one sigma) and where each size comes from

      Each default is how far Apollo 17 itself strayed from MSFC's prediction (MPR-SAT-FE-73-1), used as one standard deviation: one flight is one draw, so these sizes are assumed. "Scaled to MSFC's S-IC band" multiplies every one by the factor that makes three standard deviations of the first stage's burn time equal the report's pre-flight three-sigma band (+5.99/−4.22 s). Edit any of them.

      What the reserve pays for

      Each dispersion alone at minus and plus one sigma (the size chosen above): how much more or less propellant the guided stage has left at cutoff than on the nominal flight. A quantity that loses on both sides is not linear, and a linear covariance would miss it.

      Against Apollo 17

      The guided Saturn V AS-512 against what NASA reconstructed (MPR-SAT-FE-73-1, Tables 4-2 and 4-4, sections 7.3 and 9.3). The orbit is the target, so agreement there is the guidance working; the S-II cutoff, the S-IVB burn and what is left for the translunar burn are predictions. Times are range time (liftoff at 0.24 s).

      Handoff

      One JSON object: idea 35's ascent handoff whole (and so 34's vehicle and 33's engines), the target and guidance settings, the parking orbit reached and the state at insertion (Earth-centred inertial, fixed at liftoff), how far from the target, the propellant left for the departure burn, the open-loop comparison, the Monte Carlo scatter if you ran one, and every refusal; each number with SI units, a confidence class and a source.

      How this works

      The first stage is idea 35's, unchanged. Its kernel flies the vehicle off the pad through the 1976 atmosphere with the pitch kick and gravity turn of its plan; nothing aims at an orbit there, as on the Saturn V, whose first stage flew a fixed tilt programme. At first-stage cutoff the attitude is held until guidance starts (for AS-512, 42.9 s later, when the flight's iterative guidance began: Table 2-2).

      Guidance. Unified Powered Flight Guidance (Brand, Brown and Higgins, Draper Laboratory for NASA, 1973) aims for a cutoff radius, speed, flight-path angle and plane. Each cycle it predicts how long the remaining stages must burn to supply the velocity still to be gained (the rocket equation over each stage, from idea 34's masses), integrates the thrust acceleration over that time, chooses a thrust direction that turns linearly in time (the linear-tangent law, optimal in uniform gravity) so that the burn also ends at the right height, estimates gravity's share along a coasting arc that shadows the powered one, and corrects. It senses only the vehicle's thrust acceleration and velocity change; its model of what is still to burn is the nominal one. The command is held fixed in space between cycles. When the cutoff is under two cycles away the steering is frozen, and the engine stops when the orbit's energy reaches the target's.

      Open loop. Idea 35's linear pitch programme, solved for a level burnout, is flown unchanged (for AS-512 it does not reach orbit). Under dispersions the open loop is the nominal guided flight's own attitude history replayed on the clock, with a timer cutoff: the same steering, without the feedback.
