Stack

Put engines under propellant and stack the stages. Change the engines, the loads, the dry masses or the payload and read the ideal delta-v of every burn, how long each burns, how hard it pushes, and how big its propellant is. It opens on the Saturn V that flew Apollo 17, every kilogram from NASA's flight report, and sets this module's numbers beside what that rocket actually did.

      How this works

      Engines are idea 33's. A preset (F-1, H-1, J-2, Atlas LR89 booster pair, RL10) is built by idea 33's kernel, unmodified: NASA SP-8120's chamber and nozzle, calibrated to its published thrust and Isp, with the gas-generator or expander cycle NASA SP-8107 or TM-107318 describes. An imported engine is re-computed from its handoff and refused unless the numbers match. Each gives a vacuum and a sea-level thrust, an engine-level mass flow (chamber plus gas generator) and so an engine-level Isp, the mixture ratio at the engine's inlets, and the propellant densities idea 31 measured.

      A burn is cut into segments by its events: an engine shut down early (the Saturn V's centre engines), or something dropped mid-burn (the S-II's aft interstage, the launch escape tower, the S-IVB's ullage rocket cases). Within a segment every engine is the same engine, so the exhaust velocity c = F/ṁ is constant and the rocket equation is exact: Δv = c ln(mstart/mend), time = propellant / (n ṁ). The ledger: every kilogram of a stage leaves the vehicle one way: burned, lost without thrust (thrust build-up, frost, purges, venting between burns), jettisoned, or dropped at separation with the dry stage. Liftoff mass is their sum plus the payload, and nothing is left at the end but the payload.

      Ideal, not flown. The headline delta-v uses each engine's vacuum Isp at the engine level (a gas generator's exhaust giving no extra thrust, idea 33's lower bound, which is where the F-1 matches its flight data). The first stage is also given at its sea-level Isp (a floor: the atmosphere thins as it climbs) and gas-generator stages at idea 33's upper bound. Gravity, drag and steering losses are module 35's; the Apollo 17 comparison shows how big they were. Tank sizes are propellant volumes at idea 31's densities (no ullage).

      How far to trust it

        Confidence classes: measured is a value from a measured or published source (here, the flight report's masses); derived is computed here from such inputs; assumed is your input or a choice. Every number on the page traces to the kernel (stack.js), idea 33's kernel and its sources, the flight report transcription (snapshots/as512-flight.js), or your input. This is an idea-bank prototype; it has not been through Aster's multi-agent math audit.
