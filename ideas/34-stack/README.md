# Stack
> Stack stages of idea 33's engines (F-1, H-1, J-2, Atlas booster, RL10, or your own) under propellant and a payload and read each burn's ideal delta-v, burn time, thrust-to-weight and propellant volume, with the Saturn V that flew Apollo 17 as a preset whose every kilogram comes from NASA's flight report. Built from those masses, the stack lifts off within 27 kg of the report's 2,917,843 kg; idea 33's F-1 matches the five flight engines to +0.14 % in thrust and +0.03 % in flow, the S-IC's burn comes out 2.4 % long, and the S-IVB's J-2, which flew at mixture ratio 5.0 against idea 33's 5.5 point, comes out 11.8 % strong.

**Kind:** explorable tool
**For:** Aster + PartForge
**Run:** open `ideas/34-stack/index.html` in a browser (classic scripts, `file://` works, no network; it also opens on idea 33's `#h=` link). Tests from the bank root: `node --test ideas/34-stack/stack.test.mjs` (16 tests, about 0.5 s) and `node ideas/34-stack/ui-check.mjs` (74 checks in headless Chromium; `check.mjs` runs it for `tools/check.mjs`). Regeneration, none needed to run anything: verbatim text layer `python3 -I -B ideas/34-stack/oracle/build_sources.py <19730025089.pdf>`, sidecars `node ideas/34-stack/oracle/write_sidecars.mjs`, sample handoff `node ideas/34-stack/build-sample.mjs`.

## What it is

Idea 34, module 4 of the twenty in *The Long Way Round* (ideas 31 to 50, launch to asteroid and back). Idea 31 burns the propellant, 32 expands it, 33 makes it an engine; this module makes engines into a rocket.

It opens on **Saturn V AS-512 (Apollo 17) as flown**: three stages, every mass from NASA's flight evaluation report (MPR-SAT-FE-73-1, 1973), with idea 33's F-1 and J-2 under them. You can:

- **edit any stage**: its engine (idea 33's F-1, H-1, J-2, LR89-NA-7 booster pair, RL10A-3-3, or an engine imported from idea 33), the number of engines (stepper), the propellant it burns (typed or slider), its dry mass and what it leaves unburned, or **scale its dry mass from a real stage's inert fraction** (AS-512's S-IC 7.35 %, S-II 9.20 %, S-IVB 12.68 %, each (dry + unburned) / (dry + unburned + propellant burned)); add a stage on top (up to five) or remove one;
- **switch the in-flight events** between "as scheduled" (the S-IC and S-II centre-engine cutoffs, the S-II's aft interstage and the launch escape tower jettisoned mid-burn, the S-IVB's ullage cases, its two burns and the hydrogen it vents between them) and "none" (the same masses, every engine to depletion, everything carried to separation): on AS-512 the events are worth 93.9 m/s;
- **set the payload** by typing, by slider, or by dragging, tapping or arrow-keying the payload chart;
- **pick a target delta-v** (the ideal delta-v this model gives Apollo 17's own stack to its 170.5 km parking orbit, 9,177 m/s, or to translunar injection, 12,300 m/s, or one you type) and read the most payload this stack gives it, or a refusal saying why it cannot (including when that payload would not lift off: the refusal then names the most the stack can lift and the delta-v left at it).

The page shows, each with a confidence chip: liftoff mass, thrust-to-weight at liftoff, total ideal delta-v (with the first stage at its sea-level Isp as a floor and gas-generator exhaust expanded as a ceiling), burning time, payload fraction; per stage the delta-v of each burn, burn time, thrust-to-weight at ignition and acceleration at cutoff, mass ratio, oxidizer and fuel mass and volume, and inert fraction. **Through the burns** charts ideal velocity, acceleration or mass against burning time, with AS-512's measured cutoff accelerations and masses on the same clock. **Payload against delta-v** draws the trade curve and greys where the stack no longer lifts off. **Against Apollo 17** runs every comparison below live. **Handoff** exports the vehicle for module 35 and imports idea 33's engine (or this page's own vehicle).

## Why I made it

- **The series needs it next.** `queue/SERIES_LONG_WAY_ROUND.md`, row 34: "A rocket: stages, engines, propellant loads, tank volumes from the propellant densities, dry masses, payload; staged Δv; real vehicles as presets with sourced masses", taking engine decks and handing on a vehicle. Idea 33's README said what to take: "use the engine-level `thrust_*`, `specific_impulse_*`, `mass_flow*` and `engine_mixture_ratio`; tank volumes from `oxidizer_density` and `fuel_density`; the engine's mass must come from module 34's own sources." This module does all of that, and supplies the mass as whole-stage dry masses from the flight report rather than as engine masses (below).
- **Aster's delivered mass is a single-stage rocket equation at an assumed Isp.** `src/v2/porkchop/launch-vehicles.ts:288` at `62ddb17`: `payloadKg * Math.exp(-dvSpacecraftMps / (G0_MPS2 * SCREENING_ISP_S))`, with `SCREENING_ISP_S = 320` at `:57` (DEC-13-5, "representative"). Its launch-vehicle payloads are NASA LSP C3→payload anchors (`:68-180`), interpolated and refused beyond the curve. A staged ledger on engine-level Isp is the physics under such a curve; the payload-against-delta-v chart here is that curve computed rather than tabulated, with a refusal where the stack cannot reach.
- **PartForge's evaluation wants a vehicle mass budget, but not a duplicate of OpenRocket.** `docs/roadmap-evaluation.md:101` at `0974a62` ("Vehicle mass budget + CG travel ... build the 2-d mass_budget slice only") and `:270` (emit OpenRocket/RocketPy entries "putting real machined masses into the flight sim instead of typed estimates"). The ledger here (every kilogram leaves by one named road) is that slice for a liquid vehicle.

Alternatives weighed. (1) *A table of vehicles with their published delta-v*: dead, and there is no such table with sources. (2) *Generic structural ratios and a textbook staging optimiser*: forbidden here (no remembered numbers) and unvalidated. (3) *A vehicle from a press kit or encyclopedia* (stage dry and wet masses): secondary sources disagree in the third digit and give no flight to check against. (4) **Built:** one real vehicle whose every kilogram was **reconstructed after flight** by NASA, so the module's own ledger, burn times, accelerations and velocities can each be checked against the same report, with idea 33's engines unmodified. I searched NTRS for the Saturn V and Saturn IB flight evaluation reports (AS-503, AS-509 to AS-512, SA-513, SA-206 to SA-208 are there) and chose AS-512 for its legible scans and because its F-1s run at SP-8120's 1,522,000 lbf rating, the point idea 33 calibrates on (Table 5-2: "PREDICTED 1522" for every engine).

## How it works

**Engines** (`engineFromPreset`, `engineFromHandoff`). Idea 33's kernel, unmodified (`snapshots/engine.js` on ideas 31 and 32's kernels): `engineSpec(id)` (NASA SP-8120 Table I, calibrated), `point()` in vacuum and at 101,325 Pa, `cycle()` with idea 33's own reference for that engine, `engineLevel()`. A record holds, per engine (idea 33's per-chamber values times the chambers; the LR89-NA-7 has two): vacuum thrust, its gas-generator upper bound, sea-level thrust (or idea 33's separation refusal), engine-level mass flow (chamber plus gas generator), engine-level Isp in vacuum and at sea level (idea 33's lower bound: turbine exhaust giving no thrust), the mixture ratio at the engine's inlets, idea 31's propellant densities, exit area and separation-onset pressure (for module 35's F(p<sub>a</sub>) = F<sub>vac</sub> − p<sub>a</sub>A<sub>e</sub>), and a vacuum throttle deck at 40 to 110 %. An imported idea 33 handoff is parsed by idea 33's `parseHandoff()` (which re-checks idea 32's nozzle and idea 31's chamber), rebuilt from its inputs (throat area, efficiencies, cycle reference, rich side), and refused unless its `thrust_vacuum`, `mass_flow` and `specific_impulse_vacuum` are reproduced to 10<sup>-8</sup> with a shifting or a frozen nozzle.

**A burn** (`evaluate`). A stage has a dry mass (dropped at separation, engines included), a remaining mass (residuals, gases: dropped with it) and one or more burns; a burn has the propellant its engines burn, mass shed without thrust at its start (thrust build-up, frost, purges), mass lost after it (venting before the next burn), jettisons at a time after its start, and engine cutoffs at a time. Events split the burn into segments. Every engine in a stage is the same engine, so within a segment c = F/ṁ is constant and the rocket equation is exact: Δv = c ln(m<sub>start</sub>/m<sub>end</sub>), time = propellant/(n ṁ). An engine cutoff changes time, not Δv; a jettison changes Δv. An event after the propellant is gone is not applied: a cutoff is ignored and a jettison rides to separation, each with a warning. Liftoff mass is the sum of every stage's parts plus the payload; after the last separation what is left is the payload, which the tests check. Thrust-to-weight at liftoff uses the first stage's sea-level thrust; at other ignitions, vacuum thrust over m g<sub>0</sub>; acceleration at cutoff is n F<sub>vac</sub>/m.

**Tanks.** A stage's propellant is split at the engine-level mixture ratio; volumes at idea 31's measured densities (liquid oxygen at its normal boiling point, RP-1 at 298 K, liquid hydrogen at its normal boiling point). These are propellant volumes, not tank volumes: no ullage, no residual geometry, no tank mass.

**Payload** (`sweepPayload`, `solvePayload`). Delta-v falls monotonically with payload; the payload for a target is found by bisection to 10<sup>-6</sup>, refused when even an empty stack falls short, and refused when the payload it finds would leave liftoff thrust-to-weight at 1 or below (the refusal gives the most payload the first stage lifts, n F<sub>sl</sub>/g<sub>0</sub> less the empty stack, and the delta-v left at it). **Inert fraction** of a stage is (dry + unburned) / (dry + unburned + propellant burned), the definition `dryForFraction()` inverts, so scaling an AS-512 stage's own propellant at its own fraction gives back its own dry mass.

**The AS-512 vehicle** (`fromAS512`). From `snapshots/as512-flight.js`, hand-transcribed from MPR-SAT-FE-73-1 (NTRS 19730025089, PDF sha256 `8e543894…2eaa42`). The vehicle starts at holddown-arm release (0.24 s, 2,917,843 kg); every row of Table 16-9 (Flight Sequence Mass Summary) from there to the S-IVB's second cutoff is assigned to one road: S-IC burn propellant = "MAINSTAGE" (2,076,017 kg), shed = frost, purges, the centre engine's thrust decay and expended propellant, and the upper stages' frost (1,807 kg); S-IC dropped at separation = Table 16-1's DRY STAGE (130,342) plus the small interstage (616) as dry, the rest (33,805, residuals, gases, thrust decay after cutoff, retro-rocket propellant) as remaining. S-II: propellant 449,797; shed 637; jettisons the aft interstage (3,908 kg at 192.9 s, Table 2-2) and the launch escape system (4,129 kg; Table 2-2 gives no time, "*Data not available", so it is placed with the interstage, **assumed**); dry = Table 16-3's DRY STAGE plus the S-II/S-IVB interstage dry (39,633), remaining 5,927. S-IVB: burn 1 30,006 kg (ullage cases, 61 kg, at 572.4 s), 1,165 kg lost in the coast (fuel venting, APS, burner), burn 2 73,828 kg; dry and remaining from Table 16-7 at second cutoff (11,273 and 3,801). Payload = vehicle less S-IVB at second cutoff = 50,637 kg (instrument unit 2,027 plus spacecraft 48,609, Table 16-5). Burn clocks start at each mainstage (0.24, 166.33, 565.22, 11,559.10 s); the centre cutoffs are at 139.30 and 461.21 s (Tables 2-2, 4-2).

**Handoff schema (version 1, `from: "34-stack"`).** The series envelope; `handoff.json` is the sample: idea 33's committed sample engine (LOX/RP-1, 10 MPa, area ratio 16, 1 MN at sea level, F-1 gas-generator cycle) imported and re-computed, seven of them under 400 t of propellant, one idea-33 J-2 under 100 t, dry masses scaled from AS-512's S-IC and S-IVB inert fractions, 10 t of payload (every mass **assumed**, chosen to show the chain): 556.3 t at liftoff, thrust-to-weight 1.283, 10,733 m/s of ideal delta-v (reproduced in review by an independent Python recompute from the report's rows and idea 33's committed outputs).

## Honest assessment

- **[Certain] The ledger reproduces the flight's masses.** Built from Table 16-9's rows, AS-512 lifts off at 2,917,816 kg against the report's 2,917,843 (−27 kg, the report's own row rounding), and reaches S-IC cutoff −24 kg, S-II cutoff −20, S-IVB first cutoff −12, second cutoff 0 (the last is where the payload is defined).
- **[Certain] Idea 33's F-1 matches the flight at the engine level:** against Table 5-2's five engines reduced to standard sea level (mean 1,519.8 klbf, 5,729.8 lbm/s, 265.24 s), thrust +0.14 %, flow +0.03 %, Isp +0.11 %. The engine-level Isp that matches is idea 33's **lower** bound (turbine exhaust giving no thrust beyond the calibration); the upper bound is 2.8 % off. This settles which end of idea 33's bracket a stage should use for the F-1.
- **[Certain as numbers] In flight the F-1 ran harder than its nominal point:** the S-IC burn (the flight's 2,076,017 kg at idea 33's flow, centre engine out at 139.06 s) lasts 164.87 s against the flight's 160.96 (+2.43 %), and the acceleration at outboard cutoff (four engines over the model's cutoff mass, 24 kg under the flight's, at 66.5 km) is 37.03 m/s² against Table 4-1's 37.95 (−2.44 %). Read as engine numbers: to burn that propellant in the flown time each F-1 averaged 2,651.7 kg/s, **2.0 %** more than idea 33's 2,599.8 (a row of the live table since review), and the cutoff acceleration needs **2.5 %** more thrust than idea 33's. (The builder wrote "equal and opposite, both about 2.4 % by cutoff"; the burn time measures the average flow over the burn, not the flow at cutoff.) [Likely] cause: the report's Figure 5-3 shows the stage flow rate creeping up through the burn before the centre cutoff (read off the plot, roughly 2 %); why (pump inlet pressure growing with the acceleration head is the usual explanation) the report does not say here, so that part is [Speculative]. Idea 33 holds chamber pressure fixed.
- **[Certain as numbers] The J-2 is right only where it was calibrated.** On the S-II's high-mixture-ratio phase (section 6.3, five engines at ESC +61 s) thrust −0.58 %, flow −0.85 %, Isp +0.28 %; S-II burn −2.89 % (the flight shifted to mixture ratio 4.8 at 489.2 s, cutting flow, which idea 33 cannot follow) and acceleration before the centre cutoff −1.0 %. The S-IVB's J-2 ran at mixture ratio 5.0 (Tables 7-1, 7-2: 205,797 lbf, 427.7 s), so idea 33's 5.5-point J-2 is **+11.8 % in thrust, +13.1 % in flow, −1.15 % in Isp** there: its burns come out 11.5 % and 14.2 % short and its cutoff accelerations 11.0 % and 12.3 % high. The S-IVB numbers on this page are therefore idea 33's J-2, not the S-IVB's, and the page says so.
- **[Likely] The rocket equation is not the trajectory.** The S-II's ideal delta-v (4,654.8 m/s) exceeds the space-fixed velocity the flight gained between S-IC and S-II cutoff (Table 4-2, 4,243.2 m/s) by 411.6 m/s: mostly gravity and steering loss while climbing, but not all of it: idea 33's J-2 Isp is 0.28 % above the high-EMR phase's (about 13 m/s of the 411.6), and the flight's shift to mixture ratio 4.8 is not followed (the page's note said "not a model error" until the review). At the flight's own Isp the S-IVB's near-horizontal first burn gives 809.7 m/s against 812.2 gained (−2.5 m/s, the thrust build-up and tail-off impulse this module counts as shed); the second burn 3,157.9 against 3,040.5 (+117.4 m/s of gravity loss leaving orbit, assuming the speed at second ignition equals the insertion speed, Table 4-4, e = 0.0000). The S-IC's gain is not compared: the report gives no space-fixed velocity at liftoff and Aster's constants carry no Earth rotation rate. These are module 35's to explain; they are the losses its trajectory must reproduce.
- **[Certain as numbers] Tank volumes follow the engine's mixture ratio, not the loading:** splitting each stage's loaded propellant at the engine-level ratio gives fuel volumes S-IC +1.37 %, S-II −0.65 % and S-IVB **−13.2 %** against the loaded split (Tables 16-1, 16-3, 16-5; loaded oxidizer/fuel 2.315, 5.261 and 4.472 against the engines' 2.270, 5.302, 5.302). [Likely] The S-IVB loads extra hydrogen for venting in orbit (1,041 kg lost in the coast, Table 16-9) and its restart at the 4.5 position (PDF page 109); a stage designed here at the engine's ratio would be short of hydrogen.
- **[Likely] Screening payloads.** With the parking-orbit target (this model's own ideal delta-v for Apollo 17's climb, 9,177 m/s, losses included because the flight paid them) the AS-512 stack carries 149.9 t if the S-IVB spends everything getting there.
